## 2. Drivetrain losses when maps are missing (our main blocker)

| Source | Method | Values |
|---|---|---|
| BETOS [CODE `M1_Veh_Prop.py`, `M91_Prediction.py`] | Constant efficiencies, symmetric for regen (`/η` driving, `×η` braking) | gear 0.94 (Mareev et al.), motor 0.95, power electronics 0.98. Code comment: "Alternative Efficiency Map to be defined, yet the Calculation is done with fixed value" |
| FASTSim 3 [CODE `electric_machine.rs`] | Motor efficiency = **1-D curve vs fraction of rated output power**, not a torque–speed map. Curve can be rescaled to a new peak efficiency (`set_eff_fwd_max` multiplies every point by eff_max/old_max) | – |
| T3CO class 8 BEV demo [CODE `Demo_FY22_vehicle_model_assumptions.csv`, row "Class 8 Sleeper cab high roof (BEV, 2025)"] | Same 1-D curve, plus one constant transmission efficiency | power fraction [0, .02, .04, .06, .08, .1, .2, .4, .6, .8, 1] → eff [.83, .85, .87, .89, .90, .91, .93, .94, .94, .93, .92]; trans_eff 0.97. **No source for the curve is given in the file.** |
| KIT thesis [PAPER §4.2, p. ~71–72, Eq. 4.17] | 1-D efficiency curves vs relative tractive power for motor, transmission and axle, each pre-calculated from 2-D torque–speed maps and calibrated with FASTSim | Motor curve from Basma et al. 2021a, Wolff et al. 2021, Brooker et al. 2015; transmission and axle from VECTO and Tansini et al. 2019. Curve values only in Figure 26 (graphic, not extracted) |
| Wolff 2021 [PAPER] | Motor maps generated by a machine-design tool (MEAPA) per machine; not scaled from measurement. Inverter not modelled | gear 0.99 (fixed gear / top gear), 0.96 (lower and planetary gears), final drive 0.97. Average machine efficiency 87–95 % depending on topology. No validation against measurement |
| NREL/Microgrid Labs [PAPER] | FASTSim bus variant; motor efficiency from a torque–speed map derived from NREL Fleet DNA | No values in text |

What this tells us for HDEV:
- **Nobody has solved this better than us with no data.** Every tool either uses constants (BETOS: same family as our failing V-A) or one generic curve.
- **KIT's validation shows the same Urban weakness we found.** Their diesel model vs EEA certified values has "usual deviations below 5 %"; the largest deviation is Urban Delivery, which they attribute to static efficiency maps [PAPER p. ~77]. Example row from Table S16 (units not given in the extract): UD, group 4, low payload, simulated 25.20 vs reported 29.00.
- **[JUDGEMENT]** The 1-D curve vs load fraction is the step between our constants (failed) and full maps (data not available). It makes losses rise at low load, which is where our Method C missed (Urban). This is the speed/load-dependent loss test already planned in the 7 Nov closeout (§9 step 3). It needs a sourced curve: KIT Figure 26 is the best candidate (needs the PDF, see §8).

## 3. Regen

| Source | Rule |
|---|---|
| KIT [PAPER Eq. 4.20] | Battery power ≥ −P_rated; rest goes to mechanical brakes |
| FASTSim [CODE `electric_machine.rs` l.236–252] | Regen capped by motor `pwr_out_max` and by what the battery can accept |
| T3CO demo [CODE] | `max_regen` = 0.9 |
| BETOS [CODE `M6_Veh_Dyn.py`] | Dynamic model: regen torque halved when target deceleration < −1 m/s². But the model that produces kWh (`M91_Prediction.py`) has **no regen cap at all** |

HDEV already plans regen limits from EU standard data (22 Oct decision). KIT's "cap at rated power" is the simplest sourced fallback.

## 4. Rolling resistance (our open issue 5)

- KIT [PAPER Eq. A.11, partly garbled in extract]: VECTO-style per-axle load term `(m_i·g / (w_i·F_ISO))^(β−1)` with F_ISO = 37,500 N, a tyre-wear factor 0.88 (Kies 2018), and an ISO 28580 temperature correction with k_t = 0.05 and T_ISO = 25 °C (Greiner et al. 2018). One formula is used everywhere.
- BETOS [CODE]: linear Crr·m·g, Crr 0.005 ("Discussion with MAN").
- **[JUDGEMENT]** Both are internally consistent; HDEV is the only one mixing two forms (driver model linear, consumption m^β). KIT backs the m^β form for both.
- Check before use: the k_t value and whether β = 0.9 need the original PDF (extract was garbled).

## 5. Auxiliaries, HVAC, battery thermal

| Source | Model / values |
|---|---|
| KIT [PAPER Eq. 4.24, A.13–A.16] | Base aux 1,800 W constant (Tansini 2019; Heidt 2019; Zhao 2013). HVAC = ΔT·α_cab / COP, α_cab 50–155 W/K day cab, 65–201 W/K sleeper (Magnino et al. 2024, speed-dependent), T_cab 20 °C, heater cap 5,500 W ("own assumption"), HVAC COP vs temperature shown only as a graph (Fig. S21, no source given; COP_C 2.5 is the BTMS value, not HVAC). ## 6. Battery sizing and the battery ↔ mass loop

| Source | Sizing rule | Mass loop |
|---|---|---|
| KIT [PAPER §4.3.2] | E_bat = E_req / (0.92 · r_soc); usable 92 % (Wassiliadis 2022); r_soc = min(1, α·FEC^β), a power law that shrinks the SoC window as cycling rises (NMC and LFP coefficients only in Fig. 30). E_req can be per trip, per stretch between stops, per day or per schedule. Only cyclic ageing in sizing | Not stated in extract |
| NREL/Microgrid Labs [PAPER Eq. 9] | Nelder-Mead minimising a weighted penalty: SoC shortfall (battery), uphill acceleration time (motor), missed speed trace, component mass. Sized on the **most energy-intensive logged day** at the most extreme TMY3 day. No degradation, no SoC window given | Yes, kg/kWh and kg/kW updated each iteration |
| carculator_truck [CODE `model.py` `override_range`] | Battery sized to a target range per VECTO mission: Urban delivery 150 km, Regional 400 km, Long haul 800 km | Yes |
| T3CO [CODE `optimize`] | pymoo minimises TCO subject to range, acceleration and grade constraints | Yes, but see §9 |
| BETOS [CODE `M4_Veh_Static.py`] | Battery is an input (500–572 kWh scenarios) | Yes: 165 Wh/kg pack (König et al.), motor 0.5 kg/kW and PE 1/12.8 kg/kW (Fries et al.), gearbox mass from Naunheimer |
| Baek 2020 [PAPER] | Sweep of battery sizes, keep max annual profit. Constant battery conversion efficiency, no value given; no validation | Qualitative only |

For HDEV:
- KIT's equation is the most complete sourced version of what Bob's own baseline does (×1.1 weather ÷ 0.8 buffer), with the buffer tied to ageing instead of a fixed 20 %.
- Everyone sizes on the worst day or a target range, not the average (consistent with our earlier note).
- None of them treat end-of-life capacity as a sizing check the way the Ressourceindsamling tender does (see gap analysis 26 Oct).

## 7. Validation: how good are they?

- **No tool or paper read here validated BET energy against measured data with a stated error.**
  - KIT: diesel vs EEA <5 % typical; BET only compared with manufacturer and study values (1.1–1.5 kWh/km for 4×2 tractors).
  - Wolff 2021: no validation; results are relative only.
  - NREL/Microgrid Labs: "more accurate than manufacturer figures in nearly all cases", no number given.
  - BETOS: simulated only (GPS speed → simulated energy).
- **[JUDGEMENT]** HDEV's leave-one-out test against 10 EEA-certified BETs (7 Nov) is already more than any of these did for battery-electric trucks. That is a selling point, stated carefully: it is agreement with VECTO-certified values, not with measured energy.
- KIT's Monte Carlo (N = 500, PERT distributions on P_rated, CdA, crr, mass from EEA) is a method we could copy for confidence bands (matches the planned Monte Carlo).

## 8. Features they have that HDEV lacks

1. **Payload-capacity opportunity cost** (T3CO `opportunity_costs.py`): values lost payload from heavier BET using a payload probability distribution. Also on our COWI gap list.
2. **Downtime cost** for charging dwell and maintenance (T3CO).
3. **Battery thermal management power** (KIT).
4. **Temperature-dependent rolling resistance** (KIT; we have an ISO 28580 test, not in the engine).
5. **Pre-computed consumption table** (eFLIPS): kWh/km looked up from a 4-D table of grade, ambient temperature, load level and mean speed. **[JUDGEMENT]** A possible fallback for the year simulation if the per-day timing test is too slow (12 Oct decision). Note: eFLIPS silently uses nearest-neighbour when a point is outside the table; we would flag that.
6. **Charging C-rate curve vs SoC** from a cell teardown (BETOS, Wassiliadis et al. ID.3) instead of a fixed charger power.
7. **Ageing-linked SoC window** in sizing (KIT).
8. **Real truck driving data:** TUM's 10 Hz GPS of 150 N3 trucks, 2.15 million km (Zenodo 16411298). Speed and stop data only, no measured energy. **[JUDGEMENT]** Useful to test our driver model's speed profiles against real driving, not for energy validation.

## 9. Bugs or weak spots I saw in their code (don't copy)

As I read the code; not confirmed by running it.
- BETOS `M6_Veh_Dyn.py` and `M91_Prediction.py`: grade force uses `np.sin(np.arctan(slope) / 100)`. If slope is in %, the correct form is `np.sin(np.arctan(slope / 100))`. At 2 % the written form gives about 0.011 rad instead of 0.020 rad.
- BETOS notebook sensitivity: scales the whole driving energy by payload ratio (19.3 t ± 20 %), i.e. energy ∝ payload, not ∝ total mass.
- BETOS kWh model (`M91`) contains no aux term and no regen cap.
- T3CO 2.0.0 `input_data/vehicle.py` l.135: battery mass = `ess_base_kg + ess_kg_per_kwh / ess_max_kwh` (divides; multiplication expected). Same pattern for motor and fuel-cell mass.

### 12.1 KIT Figure 26 (the load-dependent efficiency curves): no exact values available
- Figures 26, 28, 29, S20 and S21 are embedded raster images (checked with `pdfimages`, page 95 = 1179×316 px). No numbers are printed with them. Any values read off them would be a digitised estimate, not a sourced value.
- Shape only (described, not quantified): motor efficiency rises steeply from the lowest load, peaks at around a quarter of rated power, then falls slightly towards full load. Transmission and axle rise quickly at very low load, then stay flat.
- Where the motor curve comes from [PAPER p. 70, footnote 7]: a blend of Basma et al. 2021a, Wolff et al. 2021 and Brooker et al. 2015, built by "pre-calculating" 1-D curves from 2-D torque–speed maps over the VECTO missions. Transmission and axle: VECTO and Tansini et al. 2019.
- **Brooker 2015 [PAPER]:** FASTSim's motor curve shape is "based on sampling simulated operating points on a Nissan Leaf efficiency map". So the FASTSim-family curve (and very likely T3CO's demo curve) has a **passenger-car motor** origin. FASTSim validation: most vehicles within 5 % of EPA test data, almost all within 10 %, light-duty focus.

**[JUDGEMENT] Consequence:** neither KIT nor T3CO gives a sourced, exact truck curve. KIT's *method* is reproducible with data we already have: build the 1-D curve (efficiency vs fraction of rated power) by running VECTO's own generic maps (bus-generic IEPC, E2 example, EU standard-value motor map) over VECTO cycles. Every number then traces to VECTO files.

### 12.2 Rolling resistance temperature term: do NOT copy KIT's version
- KIT Eq. A.11 [PAPER p. 157]: crr = f_wear · Σ(m_i/m_total · crr_ISO,i · (m_i·g / (w_i·F_ISO))^(β−1)) / (1 − k_t·(T_amb − T_ISO)), with k_t = 0.05 "(Greiner et al. 2018)", T_ISO 25 °C, f_wear 0.88, F_ISO 37,500 N, κ 0.9 (β is not defined; presumably κ).
- Greiner et al. 2017 [PAPER Eq. 24–25]: the ISO 28580 conversion is cR25 = cR·(1 + k_t·(T − 25)), and "according to ISO 28580 k_t = 0.008 for car tyres". It converts a drum measurement at another room temperature to 25 °C.
- Our 22 Oct winter test already used ISO 28580 eq. 10 with K_t = 0.006 for truck tyres (load index > 121), flagged as outside ISO's 20–30 °C range.
- Problems with KIT's version: (1) k_t = 0.05 is about 6× the ISO value for car tyres and 8× our truck value, and is not what Greiner states; (2) with the minus sign, cold air *lowers* crr (at −5 °C the divisor is 2.5), the opposite of their own text. **[JUDGEMENT]** Likely a typo in the thesis; HDEV's ISO-based form stays.
- The wear factor 0.88 (Kies 2018) is new to us; not checked (Kies not fetched).

### 12.3 HVAC cab heat transfer: traced to source
- Magnino et al. 2024 [PAPER p. 5]: "no detailed analysis regarding the energy need for truck cab heating has been found", so they "define an ad-hoc simplified model". The 50–155 W/K (day cab) and 65–201 W/K (sleeper) range, varying with speed, comes from their refs [54] Lustbader et al. 2012 (SAE 2012-01-2052, sleeper-cab thermal management) and [55] NREL CoolCab (fy10osti/47415), plus [56] (fin heat transfer). Derivation is in their Supplementary Material (not fetched).
- Magnino uses it as heat demand directly: ΔEC = α·(20 °C − T_out)/v_avg per month, no COP. They size the battery assuming the whole trip at −30 °C, usable capacity 90 %.
- KIT adds COP curves (Fig. S21: graph only, described as following "general patterns and scales of modern air-source heat pumps", no source) and a 5.5 kW heater cap (own assumption).
- **[JUDGEMENT]** Usable for HDEV only as a flagged, cited range (an ad-hoc model, not a measurement). The primary data is NREL CoolCab: worth fetching before relying on it.

### 12.4 KIT battery sizing: exact values (Figure 30 has a table)
E_bat = E_req / (z_usable · r_soc), r_soc = min(1, α_soc · FEC_req^β_soc), z_usable = 92 % (Wassiliadis et al. 2022). Ageing basis: Karlsson et al. 2023a (cyclic ageing only).

| Chemistry | α_soc | β_soc | FEC → SoC window (table in Fig. 30) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ni-rich (NMC, NCA) | 59.66 | −0.54 | 2,000 → 1.0; 3,000 → 0.8; 5,000 → 0.6; 10,000 → 0.4; 40,000 → 0.2 |
| Fe/Mn-based (LFP) | 133.93 | −0.62 | 3,000 → 1.0; 4,000 → 0.8; 6,000 → 0.6; 10,000 → 0.4; 40,000 → 0.2 |

Check: 59.66 × 2000^(−0.54) ≈ 0.98, consistent with the table. FEC_req comes from total energy throughput over the schedule.

### 12.5 Regen rules found (adds to §3)
- Rothgang 2015 [PAPER Table, p. ~6]: electric braking up to 0.8 m/s² deceleration, rest mechanical; drivetrain 90 %, battery 96 %, charging infrastructure 93 % (constants). No energy validation; most of the paper is cell selection, ageing and BMS design.
- Route_Dynamics (Eggleton et al.) [PAPER Table]: regen factor 0.6 (from Gallet et al. 2018), drivetrain 0.89, crr 0.01, Cd 0.6. 24 Seattle routes, same bus: 0.9–1.9 kWh/km. No validation found in the text.

### 12.6 Other notes
- Route_Dynamics uses LiDAR *surface* model (highest hit) elevation and then has to remove bridges/vehicles by smoothing. **[JUDGEMENT]** For HDEV's Danish grade, the DHM *terrain* model is the right layer, but bridges/tunnels still need a check.
- Salati 2015: CFD, trailer add-on devices cut drag by over 10 % in head wind. Not relevant to the drivetrain question; possible future "aero device" option.
- **TUM truck dataset (corrects §8 and §10):** Zenodo v2 is **54 N3 trucks, 4 German fleets, 12 months, 1.26 million km**, 9.7 GB, licence **ODC-ODbL 1.0** (share-alike for derived databases; check before using it in the product), code at github.com/TUMFTM/dt-cargo.
