## Benefits of EVs (as listed)

- Several companies report increased driver enjoyment/driving pleasure among employees
- Can charge at home overnight — no more detours to the gas station
- No idle consumption when queuing/idling in traffic
- Less noise and pollution
- Fewer operating and maintenance costs

**Recommendation:** Test-drive EVs via borrowing, renting, or mini-leasing different models before deciding; also recommended to contact companies that already operate EVs to learn from their experience.

## Assessing Your Needs

Key data points a company should know before switching:
- Each vehicle's daily km count
- Each vehicle's annual distance traveled
- Each vehicle's annual fuel consumption

### Economic reallocation with EVs
- EVs are usually more expensive to purchase than comparable petrol/diesel models, but the price difference has fallen significantly in the last two years (both private and commercial vehicles — relative to the document's writing date).
- In most cases, companies should invest in their own charging stations for the fleet.
- Running costs are lower: electricity is cheaper than petrol/diesel and requires less maintenance; charging overnight is cheaper due to typically lower electricity prices at night.
- Financial comparison of petrol vs. electric vehicle referenced on "page 13" of the original document (see TCO section below).

### Technology and climate/environment
- Nearly 1 in 4 (~25%) vehicle registrations in Europe were electric or hybrid as of **July 2020**.
- An EV charged with renewable energy is better for climate/environment than a comparable fossil vehicle:
  - Fewer CO2 emissions → less global warming contribution
  - No local NOx/particulate emissions → improved air quality
  - Less noise → improved local public health
- References a calculation example on "page 14" (see Emissions section below).

### CSR angle
- Green driving is described as a solid contribution to optimizing a company's profile on responsible operations. A defined **CSR** (Corporate Sustainability and Responsibility / Corporate Social Responsibility — both expansions given in source) strategy helps meet future demands for green, responsible service delivery and strengthens competitiveness in future tenders/bids.

## Charging Types and Charging Times

### Charging power standards
- **Almindelig opladning (normal/AC charging):** current standards around **3.7 kW**, up to **7-43 kW** for "hurtigladning" (fast AC charging) — note: in this document's terminology, "hurtigladning" refers to higher-power AC charging, distinct from DC "lynladning."
- **Lynladning (DC rapid/ultra-fast charging):** power already converted to DC, charges directly to the battery bypassing the onboard converter. Standards vary from **50 to 100, 120, and 270 kW**.

### What determines charging time for 100 km
- Vehicle consumption (Wh/km)
- Vehicle's maximum receiving power (kW)
- Charging station's maximum power (kW)
- Charging type (AC/DC)

## Charging Station Installation (Etablering af ladestandere)

- A charging station is described as a permanent investment; companies can request a total bid for establishment/installation, but should watch for caveats/exclusions often buried in "total cost" quotes.
- **Wall-mounted vs. freestanding:** the cheapest default option is typically wall-mounted chargers along a building wall. Underground cable burial is costly; final price depends on surface type (e.g., asphalt vs. grass) and soil condition (e.g., contamination).
- **Power capacity:** suppliers typically recommend a higher delivery capacity (leveringsomfang) than currently needed, reflecting how much power the connection is dimensioned to draw from the grid. More Amperes (A) = faster charging, provided the vehicle's battery can accept the higher power. Amperage is typically settled directly with the electricity distribution company (elselskab); electricity itself can be billed via the charger supplier (if leasing) or directly via the utility (if owned).

## Charging Solution Options (Lease/Own, Subscriptions, Smart Charging)

- **Lease/leasing vs. own installation:** leasing/renting through a supplier or charge point operator brings benefits like intelligent charging; buying your own installation is generally more expensive.
- **Subscription ("flatrate"):** a fixed monthly price gives unlimited charging at no extra cost — optimal for companies expecting high mileage.
- **Refund (Refusion):** when renting/leasing through a supplier, companies can get a refund on the portion of the electricity price made up of taxes/duties (afgifter) — described as one of the key factors making lease/lease cheaper than owning your own installation.
- **Intelligent/smart charging:** allows control over who can access charging stations and at what times of day vehicles should charge; each vehicle gets a charge tag ("ladebrik") for easy access.
- **Access control:** a charging box gives full access control to exclude outsiders while allowing guest charging access (e.g., during customer visits).
- **Public charging:** with a charge tag, direct access to the charge point operator's public charging network network for vehicles needing power before reaching home. Note: public charging prices are higher than a company's own charging stations.

## Professional Advisory Process (4 Stages)

Framed as "Vejen til den grønne flåde" (The road to the green fleet) — engaging an independent/impartial advisor ("uvildig rådgiver") who screens the company, then delivers recommendations, results, and an action plan:

1. **Behov (Needs):**
   - Company profile: industry, activities, employees, green company policy
   - Transport needs: passenger, cargo, and service vehicles and operating intervals
   - Driving patterns: km driven, fuel accounting, routes, and free driving/private use ("frikørsel")
2. **Anbefalinger (Recommendations):**
   - Vehicles: best match based on needs and preferences
   - Charging: type, placement, and installation
   - Energy and payment solution: fixed or consumption-based pricing
3. **Resultater (Results):**
   - Economy: TCO comparison of electric vs. fossil-fuel vehicles
   - Climate: potential greenhouse gas reductions
   - Environment: potential reductions in air pollution, particulates, and noise
4. **Handlingsplan (Action Plan):**
   - Timeline: expected bid receipt, delivery, installation, and start-up
   - Procurement process: loan, purchase, or leasing
   - Tender materials: development of requirement specifications
