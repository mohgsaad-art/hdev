# Hejslet Begravelsesforretning — Fleet Electrification Case Study

**Source file:** `CASE_Hejslet-FINAL.pdf`
**Category:** Case study — fleet electrification (niche/single-vehicle conversion)
**Original language:** Danish (translated to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** A small family-run funeral home in Sæby, North Jutland, spent 3.5 years converting a Tesla into what is described as the world's first Tesla hearse, drawing significant industry attention.

## Company Profile
- **Company:** Hejslet Begravelsesforretning (Hejslet Funeral Home / "bedemandsforretning")
- **Industry/sector:** Funeral services (rustvogn = hearse)
- **Fleet size:** 3 hearses total, of which 1 is electric (Tesla-based)
- **Location:** Sæby, North Jutland (Nordjylland)
- **Person(s) quoted:** John Hejslet — Director (direktør), runs the company together with his brother
- **Publication year of case:** 2021

## Electrification Timeline
- Company has a pre-existing environmental ethos: plants a tree in "Fredskoven" for every coffin purchased, and uses environmentally friendly urns — described as a long-standing "green" orientation predating the EV decision.
- Decision made to explore converting a Tesla into a hearse — described as "a spændende udfordring" (an exciting challenge) to determine feasibility.
- **Conversion duration:** 3.5 years, undertaken jointly by the Hejslet brothers together with Tesla, which approved all their design ideas along the way.
- Key engineering challenge: large side windows and a rear/tailgate door (needed to resemble a traditional Danish hearse) added weight (glass is heavy) combined with an extended roof, making it difficult to stay within the total permitted vehicle weight.
- **Solution:** The vehicle body/roof extension uses the same material aircraft are made from; the window panels are glass-fibre rather than glass, to save weight.
- Also faced a long approval process with the Danish road authority (Færdselsstyrelsen).
- Once complete, the company discovered it needed a proper dedicated charging station and a new main power cable at their premises — a need they had not anticipated because they had not spoken to anyone with prior EV experience before starting.

## Operational Notes
- No fleet-wide depot charging system described (this is a single-vehicle case); a dedicated charger and upgraded main cable were installed at the business premises after the fact.
- The company actively shares its experience with industry colleagues who visit out of curiosity for test drives or advice — positioning itself as an early mover willing to help others in the funeral industry adopt EVs.
- Family members/mourners walking behind the hearse benefit from no exhaust fumes and no engine noise.
