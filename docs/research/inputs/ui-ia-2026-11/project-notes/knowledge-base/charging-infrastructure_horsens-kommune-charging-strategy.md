## Indledning (Introduction)
- Transport = **~1/3 of Horsens Kommune's CO2 emissions**; fossil-fuel cars are a large share of this.
- **Lov om klima af 26. juni 2020** (Danish Climate Act of 26 June 2020) sets a national target of **70% CO2 reduction by 2030**.
- Notes transport-sector emissions have historically been hard to reduce, with only marginal expected change by 2030 absent major intervention.
- Frames EVs as "probably the best climate-friendly alternative currently available" for those dependent on car transport (as opposed to modal shift to bike/transit alone).
- Notes EV technology is relatively new/fast-evolving, creating consumer uncertainty — **fear of insufficient charging availability is a key deterrent** — making well-developed charging infrastructure essential to jumpstarting EV adoption.
- Cites the (national) government's expert group as pointing to municipalities as appropriate active participants in charging-infrastructure buildout; Horsens Kommune states it takes on this responsibility.

## Vi Løfter Ladeinfrastrukturen i Flok (We Lift the Charging Infrastructure Together)
- **Climate plan target: climate neutrality by 2030.**
- **September 2020:** Horsens Kommune joined **DK2020**, committing to the C40 international network's "Climate Action Planning Framework" (CAPF) standard — noted as the **first time** this international standard was adapted for smaller cities/municipalities, with Horsens (and other DK2020 municipalities) described as "writing history" in this regard.
- **National EV target:** the sitting government's target is **775,000 EVs by 2030**, though the government's hope is **1 million EVs (30% of the Danish fleet)**. **For Horsens Kommune, this corresponds to approx. 17,000 EVs.**
- States the goal that all EV drivers in Horsens Kommune should have a charging option — at home, at work/study, or via publicly accessible charging.
- References a **behovsanalyse** (needs analysis) identifying: (a) legally mandated charger locations per the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen, and (b) the municipality's scope of action for public charging-infrastructure rollout, distinguishing where the municipality directly contributes infrastructure vs. where its role is to support other actors.
- States explicitly that Horsens Kommune **cannot secure appropriate public charging-infrastructure rollout alone** — requires collective effort ("løfte opgaven i flok").
- **Udrulningsplan (rollout plan):** based on the needs analysis, with focus on socio-economic efficiency, plus areas where the municipality's facilitating role should support housing organizations and businesses installing chargers for their employees/residents.

## Kommunens Roller (The Municipality's Roles)
- **Overarching role:** driver of the green transition / goal-setter on climate — a cross-disciplinary, strategic role setting ambition levels for the other roles.
- **Bygherre og bygningsejer (Developer and building owner):** subject to the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen — **must install at least 1 charge stand at all non-residential properties with more than 20 parking spaces before 1 January 2025.** These chargers will contribute to the public charging network; must be operated by an external charging operator once/if made publicly accessible.
- **Myndighed (Authority):** secures the planning basis for appropriate rollout via: kommuneplan/lokalplan preparation; building-permit issuance; administering parking rules (incl. reasonable time limits at charge spaces); issuing permits to act as a charging operator on public land. Committed to working to ensure all citizens have good charging options.
- **Arbejdsgiver (Employer):** as one of the municipality's largest employers, wants to help staff switch to EVs by enabling workplace charging.
- **Flådeejer (Fleet owner):** **700+ vehicles**, to be progressively replaced with emission-free vehicles — may mean extra cost for fleet charging facilities, but carries important signaling value to citizens/businesses.
- **Indkøber/bestiller (Buyer/procurer):** Horsens Kommune procures public transit directly, giving direct influence over part of the transport sector's transition; also uses many suppliers of goods/services and can set supplier transport requirements — **this role is noted as not covered further in this strategy.**

## Ladestandere - Typer, Ladetid, Pris (Charger Types, Charging Time, Price)
**Charger comparison infographic (verbatim data, identical structure/values to the Esbjerg Kommune and Køge Kommune documents — likely a shared Rambøll-produced graphic template used across multiple Danish municipal strategies):**
| | Normallader | Hurtiglader | Lynlader |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Power** | <22 kW | 50 kW | >150 kW |
| **Typical charge time** | 4-6 hours | 40-60 min | 10-20 min |
| **Price** | 65,000-75,000 kr | 350,000-450,000 kr | 650,000-750,000 kr |
| **Use case** | Longer stays, e.g. home or workplace | Shorter stays, e.g. shopping | On the go / time-saving at higher price |

- **Normalladere:** up to 22 kW, typically 4-6 hours to charge. The home charge box residents with own-property parking would install is a normallader. Assumption: everyone able to install a home charger will do so if they own an EV, reducing their dependence on public infrastructure. Horsens Kommune will install normalladere at municipal buildings, made publicly accessible where possible — usable by citizens lacking home-charging options. Housing organizations and businesses expected to contribute chargers benefiting residents/employees.
- **Hurtigladere:** typically 50 kW, 40-60 min charge time — fits well with a supermarket visit; particularly suited to retail locations; several retail chains already rolling out fast chargers at stores.
- **Lynladere:** 150 kW+, charge time down to 10-20 min (depends on the EV battery's acceptance rate) — important for through-traffic/long trips, incl. tourists, given short charging time; well-suited to placement along the main road network, for shorter stays. Often installed by petrol stations (already well-placed, with existing infrastructure like a kiosk) and private charging operators; larger rapid-charger parks are being installed along the state road network for long-trip charging.

## Eksisterende Offentligt Tilgængelige Ladestandere (Existing Publicly Accessible Chargers)
- Notes lack of evenly distributed public charging infrastructure is a major adoption barrier.
- **As of late 2021, Horsens Kommune's public chargers are primarily centered around Horsens by (town) and Brædstrup.** Given the expected ~17,000 EVs by 2030, more infrastructure is needed.
- **Home-charging access: 81% of Horsens Kommune residents live with own-property parking** (can typically install a home charge box); **19%** cannot necessarily do so, and cannot all be assumed to have workplace charging either. Additional demand comes from long-trip visitors, traveling salespeople, and tradespeople — all dependent on public charging infrastructure spread across the municipality.
- **Existing public charger inventory (source: plugshare.com, October 2021):**
  - **13 locations**
  - **45 normallader outlets**
  - **3 hurtiglader outlets**

## Eksisterende og Planlagte Kommunale Ladestandere (Existing and Planned Municipal Chargers)
- Existing municipal chargers serve **only the municipality's own vehicles internally** — not publicly accessible, and therefore **not included** in the rollout plan as possible expansion sites (all at non-publicly-accessible locations).
- **Existing municipal charger inventory (source: Horsens Kommune, March 2022):**
  - **6 locations**
  - **9 normalladere**

## Horsens Kommune som Bygningsejer (Building Owner)
- Building owner obligations under the **Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen** (in force **10 March 2020**), an EU-driven measure to accelerate fleet electrification/CO2 reduction and protect consumers from large future cost surprises.
- Implemented under **Byggeloven**; primarily targets buildings requiring building-permit review (new-build, major renovation) but also covers existing buildings.

**"Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen pr. 10. marts 2020" requirements table (Figure, reproduced from source's table structure):**
| Building category | New construction | Major renovation | Existing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beboelse (Residential)**, ≥10 parking spaces (P10) | Preparation of all parking spaces | Preparation of all renovated parking spaces | No requirement |
| **Ikke beboelse (Non-residential)**, ≥10 parking spaces (P10) | Min. 1 charge stand + preparation of 20% of spaces | Min. 1 charge stand + preparation of 20% of renovated spaces | — |
| **Ikke beboelse (Non-residential)**, ≥20 parking spaces (P20) | — | — | **Min. 1 charge stand by 1 January 2025 (SMEs exempt)** |

- **To meet this requirement, Horsens Kommune must install chargers with at least 1 outlet at 76 different locations** across the municipality — e.g. schools, daycare institutions, municipal halls, and administration buildings.

## Horsens Kommune som Arbejdsgiver og Flådeejer (Employer and Fleet Owner)
- Ambition: chargers installed at municipal buildings under the Executive Order should be made **publicly accessible** (usable by citizens, staff, visitors for payment) — requires an external charging operator to take on operations, **tendered on market terms**.
- **As employer**, wants to offer staff charging facilities at municipal workplaces — again requiring an operator to legally enable staff charging.
- **Note on reserved staff parking:** reserving charge spaces specifically for municipal staff would require **an amendment to the (then-proposed) AFI-lov.**
- **Municipal fleet:** ambitious **CO2-neutral by 2030** target under the DK2020 climate plan, covering the municipality's own operations/fleet.
- Since municipal vehicles travel across the whole municipality, **dedicated municipal charge stands** may be needed so staff don't waste time waiting for a free public charger at municipal destinations. Also committed to ensuring **all municipal vehicles can charge at all municipal charge stands across departments** (cross-departmental interoperability).
- **Stated commitments:**
  - Ensure municipal EVs can charge at a dedicated municipal charge stand at relevant municipal destinations, avoiding staff wait time
  - **Convert 15 hjemmepleje (home-care) vehicles to EVs in 2022** — each of the 15 EVs gets its own charger, based at **Plejecenter Præsthøjgården in Horsens**. No fixed plan yet for converting the remaining home-care vehicles.

## Ladestandere på Kommunale Arealer og Det Nye Rådhus (Chargers on Municipal Land and the New Town Hall)
- **Fængslet (The Prison):** known existing demand for public chargers here. Horsens Kommune does not own the prison buildings but **does own all surrounding land**, so only the municipality can undertake charger installation for the prison site. **10 publicly accessible normal chargers** planned for the prison's conference guests and visitors.
- **Hjemmeplejens kommende elbiler (upcoming home-care EVs):** chargers at **Præsthøjgården** included in the rollout plan, but only as **internal-use municipal chargers**. **15 municipal normal chargers** for home-care EVs.
- **Det nye rådhus (the new town hall)** at **Chr. M. Østergaards Vej:** the municipality is a tenant, not building/land owner, here. Still commits to ensuring **13 charge outlets** are eventually installed for the municipality's own fleet, as it progressively electrifies. **13 normalladere for internal municipal EVs.**
- Source: Horsens Kommune, March 2022 (for these three figures).

## Andre Aktørers Ladestandere (Other Actors' Chargers)
- Expects commercial actors (seeing a business case in offering public charging to existing/new customers) to also shape the charging landscape — especially **petrol stations** and **supermarkets/grocery stores**.
- **Petrol stations:** well-placed along the main road network to attract customers; several chains already transforming into "energy stations" offering rapid charging — well-suited to their highway-adjacent locations.
- **Supermarkets:** attract many drivers for grocery shopping; larger supermarkets are themselves subject to the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen. Fast chargers ("hurtigladere") are an obvious fit for retail siting.
- Notes petrol stations and grocery stores are spread across the whole municipality, offering good potential for broad-coverage public charging via this channel. Municipality can engage in dialogue with charging operators/site owners but **cannot directly determine** whether/how many chargers will result.
- **Potential fast/rapid-charger sites identified (source: OpenStreetMap, November 2021):**
  - **36 petrol stations**
  - **50 supermarkets**

## Behovet for Ladeinfrastruktur (The Need for Charging Infrastructure) — 2030 Demand Calculation
- Socio-economic rationale: spreading charging over many hours on normalladere (home, near-home lot, or workplace) reduces grid investment needs, vs. concentrated fast/rapid charging during the "kogespids" period when household electricity demand is already elevated (commute-home hours).
- Residents with own-property parking assumed to self-install home chargers, reducing their public-infrastructure dependence.
- Others will rely on a mix of normal/fast/rapid public chargers, with fast/rapid charging concentrated on the commute-home period, driving larger grid investment needs if usage is high.

**2030 calculated need for Horsens Kommune (verbatim figures):**
- **213 normalladere**
- **14 hurtigladere**
- **9 lynladere**

**Demand-supply reconciliation table (verbatim structure — "Nødvendigt antal offentligt tilgængelige ladestanderudtag" / Necessary number of publicly accessible charge outlets):**
| Charger type | Behov i 2030 (2030 need) | Boligorganisationer (Housing orgs, assumed) | Virksomheder (Businesses, assumed) | Kommunale bygninger (Municipal buildings) | Eksisterende (Existing) | Endelig behov (Final/residual need) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normalladere | 213 | 62 | 103 | 76 | 45 | **-73** |
| Hurtigladere | 14 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 3 | **11** |
| Lynladere | 9 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **9** |

- **Interpretation (per source text):** normal-charger demand is expected to be **more than covered** (residual **-73**, i.e., a projected surplus) once housing-association, business, municipal-building, and existing chargers are accounted for — assuming housing associations and businesses install chargers for residents/staff and make them publicly accessible where possible, and the municipality makes its Executive-Order-mandated chargers publicly accessible too. **Fast and rapid chargers remain undersupplied** (11 and 9 short respectively) and are expected to be addressed by the market (primarily petrol stations and retail).

## Normalladerbehovet på Zoneniveau (Normal-Charger Need at Zone Level)
- Zone-level maps (image content, not extractable as data) show expected normal-charger need by zone: **negative numbers = surplus in that zone; positive numbers = unmet need in that zone.**
- Indicates a case for installing more normal chargers in several zones **in and around Horsens midtby (city center)**, while several midtown zones show a **large surplus**.
- Caveat: zone boundaries don't necessarily align 1:1 with actual housing/road structure, and the analysis doesn't specify exact in-zone siting — indicative only.
- Notes zones with many petrol stations/supermarkets (concentration in certain "blue zones" per the map) are expected to gain fast/rapid chargers from private actors, potentially supplementing/reducing normal-charger need there — but cautions fast/rapid charging is typically **more expensive for the driver** and requires **greater energy capacity** (new electrical connections), so missing normal-charger demand should **not** be replaced solely by fast/rapid chargers.
- **Endelave (island) tourist case:** demand pattern differs from the rest of the municipality — driven mainly by **tourists** rather than residents (most Endelave residents can charge at home and are not public-infrastructure-dependent). Recommends securing publicly accessible charging at a central island location (e.g., the harbor) for visitors, to keep Endelave attractive to tourists.
- **Stated gap to close:** **Horsens Kommune must ensure installation of at least 75 charge outlets** to cover the normal-charger need across the whole municipality [note: this figure of 75 appears as a standalone "manglende ladestandere" callout, while the demand-supply table above shows a residual **-73** (surplus) for normalladere — this is an internal inconsistency in the source document between the zone-level analysis (which shows some zones still underserved even if the citywide aggregate is in surplus) and the aggregate table; reproduced as-is, not reconciled or corrected by Claude, since both figures are explicitly stated in the source].

## Yderligere Ladestandere til Dækning af Behovet (Additional Chargers to Cover Need)
**Horsens Kommune's four stated ground principles for its charging-infrastructure work (verbatim):**
1. Charging infrastructure must help more people switch to an EV
2. There must be publicly accessible charging infrastructure across the whole municipality
3. Chargers the municipality is obligated to install under the Executive Order should, as a starting point, be made publicly accessible
4. Chargers are installed, as a starting point, on market terms

- **Re: principle 1** — residents unable to install a home charger (renters, shared/street parking) should have access to a nearby publicly accessible normal charger.
- **Re: principle 2** — based on the municipality's "hovedstruktur" (main structure plan), will ensure public charging infrastructure in the **9 lokalcenterbyer** (local center towns), the **centerby Brædstrup** (center town), and the **hovedby Horsens** (main town) — ensuring coverage across the whole municipal area while concentrating sufficient infrastructure in Horsens midtby where demand is highest.
- **Re: principles 3 & 4** — mandated chargers tendered on market terms so they become publicly accessible for citizens, staff, and guests, also supporting principle 2.
- Notes some additional public chargers will be added at municipal buildings beyond the strict legal requirement; other relevant **municipal land parcels** have been identified for charger installation to help close remaining gaps — though municipal land isn't always optimally located relative to where public demand actually exists, so the municipality will also engage housing organizations, businesses, and existing charging operators to install infrastructure where the need truly is.
- **Total chargers Horsens Kommune must install (summary callout):**
  - **76 units** per the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen
  - **+ min. 58 additional units** to cover demand, including chargers at the new town hall and housing organizations, to cover need where no municipal land is available

## Udrulningsplan (Rollout Plan)
- Rollout staged for manageability (for both the municipality and charging operators). **Total: 192 charge outlets across 83 locations**, split into **2 rollout stages ("etaper")** comprising **4 and 5 "pakker" (packages)** respectively.
  - **Etape 1: 119 charge outlets across 26 locations.**
  - **Etape 2: 73 charge outlets across 57 locations.**
- Since most of this infrastructure must be established before **1 January 2025** per the Executive Order, rollout stages will be **tendered in the period 2022-2024.**

### Other Actors (Rollout Section)
- Housing organizations and businesses expected to contribute normal chargers, but housing organizations are described as **still hesitant** to install chargers for residents due to **low demand and the economics** of installation.
- Petrol stations (well-placed relative to the main road network) expected to primarily install rapid chargers; supermarkets expected to add fast/rapid chargers at their lots for shopping-trip charging.
- Charging operators (both those already active in the municipality and those seeking entry to private/municipal land) are also key rollout actors.
- Municipality commits to dialogue with both private actors and citizens to ensure timely, strategic rollout.

### Lovgivning (April 2022) — Legal Basis Sidebar
> "Kommunen må ikke etablere og drive offentligt tilgængelig ladeinfrastruktur. Men med den nye AFI-lov (lov om infrastruktur for alternative drivmidler til transport) har Horsens Kommune hjemmel til at udbyde ladestanderne på kommunale arealer på markedsvilkår, så en privat ladeoperatør kan påtage sig opgaven. Hvis ikke udbuddene har interesse for ladeoperatørerne, kan der ydes offentlig medfinansiering, såfremt en ladeoperatør byder ind på opgaven med de forudsætninger. Horsens Kommune skal så vidt muligt bidrage til den offentligt tilgængelige ladeinfrastruktur med den ladeinfrastruktur, som skal etablere jf. ladestanderbekendtgørelsen."
- (Translation: The municipality may not itself establish/operate publicly accessible charging infrastructure. But under the new **AFI-lov**, Horsens Kommune has legal authority to tender charge stands on municipal land on market terms, so a private charging operator can take on the task. If tenders don't attract operator interest, public co-financing can be provided, provided an operator bids on the task under those terms. Horsens Kommune should contribute to public charging infrastructure as far as possible with the infrastructure it must establish per the Executive Order.)

### Statsligt Tilskud (State Subsidy) Sidebar
- Horsens Kommune can apply to a forthcoming **state subsidy pool** to co-finance public chargers — **up to 50% of total municipal payment**, distributed **first-come-first-served**. **Each municipality can only obtain a pool share up to twice its share of the Danish population.**

### Pris for Ladestanderne (Charger Pricing) Sidebar
- **Each charge stand assumed to cost 75,000 DKK to establish**, implying a **total capital investment of approx. 14.4 million DKK** [for the 192-outlet rollout — note: 192 × 75,000 = 14.4 million DKK, consistent with the stated total]. Price can vary considerably by location, especially depending on **excavation distance needed to bring power to the charger**, and whether spare capacity exists at the connection point.

## Handlingsplan (Action Plan)

### Intern i Horsens Kommune (Internal Actions)
- From expected **April 2022**, will run a tender and gauge market interest in installing chargers in Horsens Kommune. If operators can self-finance installation, public chargers on municipal land could be installed **already in 2022**.
- Will apply to the state co-financing pool once open: **50% co-financing, up to 3.16 million DKK potentially available to Horsens Kommune.**
- Will allocate necessary co-financing funds in **Budget 2023 and 2024**.
- **2022: 15 home-care vehicles converted to EVs.** Will progressively convert the rest of the fleet, balancing operational phase-in and economics.

### Samarbejde med El-netselskaberne (Cooperation with Grid Companies)
- Will cooperate/maintain close dialogue with grid companies **N1** and **Konstant Net** on grid-expansion prioritization and building sufficient grid infrastructure for expected EV demand.
- Will facilitate a meeting/event for electrical installers on connecting chargers, heat pumps, etc.
- Will report charger plans to grid companies **Konstant Net and N1** so they can factor this into their own area projects.
- Will inform citizens about the upcoming new electricity-billing model, **"Tarifmodel 3"**, intended to encourage households to spread electricity consumption across the day.

### Information, Kommunikation og Dialog
- General public information via horsens.dk on EVs and the municipality's charging strategy.
- Dialogue with housing organizations to support charger installation on shared lots, with **special focus on housing organizations in areas where municipal-land chargers won't realistically serve rental-housing residents' actual charging needs.**
- Dialogue with businesses to support charger installation for employees/guests, and potentially the public after work hours; will facilitate business-to-business cooperation and guide them through installation.

### Dialog med Tankstationer, Detailhandel og Ladeoperatører
- Will engage commercial actors (retail, petrol stations, charging operators, other interested parties) to ensure diverse charging needs are met across Horsens Kommune.
- Will engage local stakeholders to secure good charging options for **tourists on Endelave**.

## Relevance Notes for HDEV/Depot Tool (Claude's own framing, not from source)
- This is a light-duty/passenger-EV municipal strategy, but it provides a **third independent Danish municipal data point** (alongside Køge and Esbjerg) for public charger installed costs (here: flat 75,000 DKK/stand assumption, vs. Køge's 60,000-70,000 DKK and Esbjerg's 65,000-75,000 DKK normallader figures) — useful for triangulating a representative 2021-2022 Danish public-normallader cost range.
- The **zone-level demand-supply reconciliation table methodology** (calculating net charger need per type, netting out expected housing-association/business/municipal/existing supply against total 2030 demand) is a directly transferable **demand-modeling pattern** for the HDEV tool, even though the specific inputs here are light-duty/passenger-EV-based.
- The **state co-financing subsidy structure** (50% match, first-come-first-served, capped at 2× a municipality's population share of the national pool) is a concrete, sourced example of a Danish public charging-infrastructure subsidy mechanism, relevant background for understanding how Danish public co-financing of charging infrastructure is typically structured (though this specific pool is for public/passenger charging, not confirmed applicable to HD/depot charging).
