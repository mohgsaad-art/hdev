# Copenhagen Action Plan for Charging Infrastructure 2021–2025 (Part 1)

**Source file:** `28000014-40843406-1.pdf` (opaque hash/ID filename — original document title recovered from content: "Handlingsplan for ladeinfrastruktur 2021-2025, Del 1")
**Category:** Municipal policy/action plan
**Original language:** Danish (translated to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** Københavns Kommune's (Copenhagen Municipality's) 2021 Action Plan for EV Charging Infrastructure, Part 1 — setting two new coverage/capacity targets, six guiding principles, and 11 concrete initiatives for expanding publicly accessible charging infrastructure within then-current legal constraints, informed by demand analysis projecting 4,200 charging points needed by 2025.

## Publication Details
- **Publisher:** Københavns Kommune (City of Copenhagen), **Teknik- og Miljøforvaltningen** (Technical and Environmental Administration), unit **Mobilitet, Klimatilpasning og Byvedligehold** (Mobility, Climate Adaptation and City Maintenance).
- **Cover photo credit:** Københavns Kommune. Layout/graphics: TMF MKB.
- **Structure:** This is explicitly **"Del 1"** (Part 1) of a two-part plan. Part 1 covers what can be done within existing (2021) legislation; **Part 2** was planned for autumn 2021, expected to be presented politically in 2022, pending anticipated legislative change and the political decision on road-traffic CO2 reduction levels (tied to the "Tillæg til RoadMap 2021-2025 for KBH 2025 Klimaplanen," expected end of June 2021).

## Background and Rationale
- From 2019 to 2020, the number of electric and plug-in hybrid cars in Copenhagen **nearly doubled**.
- The transition to electric vehicles supports Københavns Kommune's goal of **CO2 neutrality by 2025**.
- December 2020: the Danish government, together with **Radikale Venstre, Socialistisk Folkeparti, and Enhedslisten**, reached a political agreement with an ambition of **1 million electric and plug-in hybrid cars by 2030**, with policy initiatives (including vehicle taxation) expected to achieve **775,000** of these in the same period.
- **7 December 2020:** Teknik- og Miljøudvalget (the Technical and Environmental Committee) decided to expand the framework/quota for reserved parking spaces with associated EV charging stations from **1,000 to 5,000 total**, as the existing quota was nearly exhausted.

## Terminology (Begreber)
- **Ladepunkt** (Charging point): where an EV/PHEV can charge — in practice, the charging outlet
- **Ladestander** (Charging station): a stand delivering power to EVs/PHEVs; can have multiple charging points — this is why Københavns Kommune **typically reserves two parking spaces per charging station**
- **Ladeplads** (Charging space): the area designated for an EV/PHEV to charge; includes a charging point (i.e., a parking space with an accessible charging point)
- **Ladepark** (Charging park): a geographically defined area with multiple (minimum two) charging spaces

## The Plan's Two Parts
The municipality currently (2021) has limited ability to influence the pace or placement of charging-infrastructure rollout, which depends primarily on private charging operators' interest. At the time of writing, it was unclear what legislative changes/national framework conditions would follow from the recommendations of the **Kommissionen for grøn omstilling af personbiler** ("Eldrup-Kommissionen" / Eldrup Commission on the green transition of passenger cars) — key recommendations included enabling municipal tenders and co-financing of charging infrastructure, which could significantly affect what initiatives the municipality could launch and how Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen would apply on municipal land. The two parts were expected to be combined and presented to Teknik- og Miljøudvalget as a unified plan, likely in 2022, timing depending on when legislative change occurred.

**Part 1** sets out 2 goals, 6 principles, and 11 concrete initiatives, forming the basis for the municipality's charging-infrastructure work within current legislation. **Part 2** (planned, autumn 2021/2022) was to build on Part 1 and integrate efforts with the road-traffic CO2 reduction decision and any legislative changes on EV charging infrastructure expected by end of 2021.

## Goals, Principles, and Initiatives Overview (Figur 2)
The plan is structured around the **KBH 2025 Klimaplanen** (Climate Plan) goal of CO2 neutrality.

**Existing goal (KPH2025):** 500–1,000 publicly accessible charging stations.

**Two new goals set by this plan:**
1. **Antal (Number):** Maximum **10** electric/plug-in hybrid cars per charging point on public land.
2. **Afstand (Distance):** Maximum **250 meters** to a publicly accessible charging point from any multi-story residential building ("etagebyggeri").

**Six principles (A–F):**
- **A — Typer (Types):** Ensure sufficient supply of all charging-infrastructure types (normal, fast, rapid)
- **B — Udbredelse (Coverage):** Ensure city-wide coverage, at minimum able to handle current charging demand, with particular focus on traffic hubs, resident/business interest, commercial parking areas, private land, and urban development areas
- **C — Udnyttelse (Utilization):** Ensure optimal utilization of charging infrastructure
- **D — Hensyn (Consideration):** Charging infrastructure installed with consideration for the urban environment and accessibility
- **E — Kommunen går forrest (The municipality leads by example):** The municipality installs charging infrastructure at its own buildings
- **F — Dialog og rådgivning (Dialogue and advisory support):** The municipality takes on a coordinating and advisory role, facilitating dialogue/cooperation with the industry and other actors

**11 initiatives**, grouped into three themes: (1) Parking and installation of charging infrastructure; (2) Dialogue and advisory support; (3) Goals and follow-up. (Full initiative list below in the "Initiatives" section.)

**3 ongoing initiatives** are also listed (already underway at time of publication): (1) implementation of Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen in building-permit case processing; (2) revision of the administrative framework, including design/layout requirements; (3) investigating free parking for plug-in hybrids while charging.

## Principle Details

### A — Types: Ensuring Sufficient Supply of All Charging Infrastructure Types
Important to have sufficient normal, fast, and rapid charging, but Københavns Kommune's **main focus is normal charging**, expected to cover the vast majority of daily charging demand. This choice is based on recommendations from three reports: **"Sådan skaber Danmark grøn infrastruktur til én million elbiler"**, **"Brancheaftale om et enkelt og sikkert ladenetværk i hele DK"**, and **"Veje til en veludbygget ladeinfrastruktur"** (footnoted as the Eldrup-Kommissionen report).

Copenhagen faces a **greater challenge than other municipalities** in ensuring well-developed normal charging, since many residents live in multi-story buildings and only **14%** have access to home-ground charging.

The state (via **Vejdirektoratet**) is expected to plan most rapid charging, particularly needed along the state road network for long trips. The government's **"Infrastrukturplan 2035"** (published 8 April 2021) proposes an overall EV charging-infrastructure rollout plan and would allocate a **0.5 billion DKK** framework for charging infrastructure, rolling out **630 rapid chargers** along the state road network. Some fast/rapid charging will still be needed within the city for business use (including taxis and companies) and visitors.

### B — Coverage: Ensuring City-Wide Charging Infrastructure Distribution
Must account for varying home-charging access by housing type. Priority locations:
- Locations of particular interest to residents/businesses
- Traffic hubs
- Urban development areas
- Private land, e.g. shops, filling stations, and public/subsidized housing developments
- Commercial parking areas

### C — Utilization: Ensuring Optimal Charging Infrastructure Utilization
Good utilization makes it easy for users to find a charging spot and avoids installing unnecessarily large amounts of infrastructure — e.g., ensuring long-duration parking doesn't block others from charging. Contributes to more cost-effective rollout.

### D — Consideration: Installing Charging Infrastructure with Regard to the Urban Environment and Accessibility
With a rising number of charging stations citywide, infrastructure should be integrated into the urban environment without inconveniencing other road users, with attention to both aesthetic and cultural/heritage-environment considerations.

### E — The Municipality Leads by Example
To support EV development and well-developed charging infrastructure, Københavns Kommune will lead by installing charging infrastructure at its own buildings. Under current (2021) legislation, the municipality may only install charging infrastructure for its own vehicle use, but with an expected legal change, the long-term goal is to make some of this infrastructure publicly accessible.
- **As of April 2021: 312 charging stations** (primarily **11 kW**) installed at **94 municipal buildings** for charging the municipality's own vehicles.
- The municipality will also plan for charging infrastructure in local plans (lokalplaner) and engage in dialogue with developers on new urban-development projects.
- The municipality will also lead by engaging in dialogue and potential partnerships on new technology/solutions that can help optimize charging infrastructure and its use.

### F — Dialogue and Advisory Support
Because Copenhagen has extensive multi-story housing, only **14%** of Copenhageners have charging access on their own land — making it especially important for the municipality to coordinate, engage in dialogue, and offer advisory support to key actors relevant to installing publicly accessible charging infrastructure, on both public and private land — e.g., grid companies, housing associations, filling stations, fitness centers, supermarkets etc. Involving private land in the general rollout can also relieve pressure on public space and minimize municipal costs.

**Charging types note:** EV charging occurs via three types — normal, fast, and rapid charging. The plan focuses primarily on **normal charging (up to 22 kW)** and secondarily on **fast charging (50 kW)**, since rapid chargers (150–350 kW) are space-intensive, require good access/egress conditions, and are therefore best suited to areas with shorter vehicle dwell times (e.g., motorway network, filling stations).

## New Targets (Detailed)

### Target 1 — Vehicles per Charging Point: Maximum 10 EVs/PHEVs per charging point on public land
Based on the assumption that **14%** of cars charge on their own land and thus don't use public charging infrastructure. Based on Copenhageners' typical car trip length, a normal charger (11 kW) on public land is assessed as able to cover the daily charging need of approximately **20 EVs/PHEVs**, i.e. approximately **10 cars per outlet/charging point**. Per **Dansk Elbil Alliance**, fast chargers can service **three times** as many cars as a normal charger, and rapid chargers **seven times** as many — but these more efficient charger types carry other drawbacks (see the "Types of Charging Infrastructure" section). The administration assesses these targets can be maintained regardless of whether Part 2 of the plan concludes fewer public charging stations overall are needed (e.g., due to a mix including more fast chargers).

### Target 2 — Distance: Maximum 250 meters to a publicly accessible charging point from any multi-story residential building
Covers all types of publicly accessible charging infrastructure (see Table 1), including infrastructure on private land with public access. Based on an assessment of reasonable walking distance and density needed to achieve city-wide coverage. **Frederiksberg Kommune** has set a similar distance-based target in its own EV strategy. Current average distance to a charging point on public roads varies from **280m to 1,550m** depending on district (per Figur 6). Battery charging efficiency is expected to increase with technology development, meaning each charging point is expected to be able to service more EVs over time.

## The Municipality's Current Limited Scope of Action
As of writing, the municipality **may only finance establishment and operation of charging stations for its own use** (i.e., EVs used for municipal tasks). The municipality's current scope is therefore limited to processing applications for permission to install/operate from private charging operators. **There is currently no legal authority for the municipality to pay for establishing and operating publicly accessible charging stations**, since this is considered a form of business activity — this applies both to underground base installations and above-ground charging-station installation. The municipality also may not pay for base installations and/or charging-station mounting and then tender out operation to private operators.

Under current legislation, the municipality has limited ability to influence the pace of citywide charging-infrastructure rollout. A legislative change could expand the municipality's scope, but the municipality would still remain highly dependent on sustained private-market interest to invest in and operate charging infrastructure in Copenhagen.

### Charging Demand by Location
- **Hjemme (Home):** particular challenge in a dense city like Copenhagen with much multi-story housing, where only **14%** can charge on their own land — a significant share of infrastructure must therefore be integrated into Copenhagen's streetscape/urban space.

### Analysis of Charging Point Demand
Teknik- og Miljøforvaltningen conducted an analysis of future charging-infrastructure demand:
- Expected demand: approximately **4,200 normal-charging points by 2025** and approximately **8,400 by 2030** — this is the **combined public and private** demand (including e.g. private carports/parking spaces, businesses, and parking basements).
- **Dansk Elbil Alliance's** national-level assessment: roughly **1/3** of charging infrastructure will need to be on public land, **2/3** on private land. In Copenhagen, the split between public and private parking spaces is roughly **50/50**, which Dansk Elbil Alliance also assesses as a reasonable split for charging points.
- Based on this, Teknik- og Miljøforvaltningen assesses **2,100 normal-charging points (approximately 1,050 charging stations)** are needed on public land by 2025 to meet expected demand.
- By end of 2021, approximately **1,400 charging points (700 charging stations)** are expected to be installed on public land in Copenhagen.
- If a greater share of publicly accessible charging infrastructure is established on private land, public-land demand could be reduced; similarly, more fast/rapid chargers could reduce the number of normal-charging points needed on public land.
- **Calculation basis:** a reference scenario assuming approximately **775,000 EVs/PHEVs in Denmark by 2030**, per the national political agreement (December 2020), and based on installing **11 kW normal chargers**. The analysis does **not** account for potential fast/rapid charging demand — if more fast/rapid chargers are established, overall demand could be reduced. Per Dansk Elbil Alliance, a fast charger can on average service **three times** as many EVs/PHEVs as a normal charger, and a rapid charger **seven times** as many. Teknik- og Miljøforvaltningen states it will analyze and concretize the normal/fast/rapid charging split in Part 2 of the plan.

## Initiatives (Figur 10 / Table 10 — Ongoing and Part-1 Initiatives)

### Ongoing Initiatives (Already Underway)
1. **Implementation of Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen in building-permit case processing.** The EU building directive sets requirements for member states' establishment/preparation of charging infrastructure; in Denmark implemented via the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen (March 2020), introducing requirements for preparation/establishment of charging stations at construction, e.g. major renovations of existing buildings. The municipality already enforces this in building-permit processing.
2. **Revision of the administrative framework, including design/layout requirements.** The administration is developing design/layout requirements for charging infrastructure on public land, to be incorporated into the administrative framework and new contracts with private charging operators. Installing charging stations on public land today typically involves using sidewalk area, reducing pedestrian space — this initiative also seeks good solutions to this challenge, balancing resident and urban-space considerations.
3. **Investigating possibilities/consequences of introducing free parking for plug-in hybrids while charging.** The administration is investigating advantages/disadvantages and legal aspects of allowing PHEVs free parking while charging at parking spaces with an associated charging station.

### Part 1 Initiatives — Theme 1: Parking and Installation of Charging Infrastructure

**Initiative 1 — Establishing a framework of 2,000 parking spaces for EVs without charging infrastructure.**
A new framework of 2,000 EV parking spaces (without charging infrastructure) is established, in addition to the existing 5,000-space framework with associated charging infrastructure. Spaces can be established both near existing charging-station parking and at other locations where finding parking can be difficult.
- **Effect:** Makes it easier for EV drivers to find parking, both generally and after finishing charging at time-limited charging spaces. Easy EV parking availability can help make switching from a diesel/petrol car to an EV attractive for Copenhageners. Spaces can also be established immediately adjacent to a charging station so each station can service multiple spaces — building on lessons from a trial establishing more parking spaces per charging station on **Blegdamsvej**, initiated by **Miljøpunkt Nørrebro**, which also conducted a screening of possible locations for establishing more spaces per station, potentially forming a basis for broader rollout.
- **Economy:** **11.1 million DKK**, excluding lost parking-revenue ("provenutab").

**Initiative 2 — Implementing time limits on EV charging spaces.**
Teknik- og Miljøudvalget decided on **7 December 2020** to work toward time-limiting all charging-station parking spaces, both existing and new. Implementation builds on the administration's experience trialing time limits on a number of reserved spaces during daytime hours. The time limit will be **three hours**, applying weekdays **08:00–19:00**.
- **Effect:** Ensures better charging-infrastructure utilization by minimizing long-duration parking that blocks others from charging.
- **Economy:** **0.8 million DKK**.

**Initiative 3 — Dialogue on establishing charging infrastructure at commercial parking spaces ("erhvervspladser").**
The administration initiates dialogue with operators to promote charging-infrastructure installation at or near commercial parking spaces, per a protocol note from the TMU meeting on 7 December 2020. Commercial parking spaces in Copenhagen are reserved for commercial vehicles with yellow plates, legally restricted to goods/freight transport, not private use. There are currently **377** commercial parking spaces citywide, with the most (**252**) located in **Indre By**. The municipality currently has limited authority to ensure charging infrastructure is installed at commercial parking spaces, but the administration has contacted all charging-infrastructure providers and encouraged them to install infrastructure at these spaces. With changed framework conditions, the municipality may eventually gain authority to tender charging-infrastructure installation at these spaces too.
- **Effect:** Supports city-wide charging-infrastructure coverage by also supporting installation at Copenhagen's commercial parking spaces.
- **Economy:** Can be launched within the administration's existing operating budget.

**Initiative 4 — Pilot trial with a parking zone for zero-emission vehicles.**
*(This initiative also appears in the companion "Handlingsplan for delebiler" — Action Plan for Car-Sharing.)*
Trial of a zero-emission-vehicle parking zone, where all parking spaces in a defined area are temporarily converted to spaces for EVs and electric car-share vehicles. Through-driving by petrol/diesel vehicles remains possible. Citizen dialogue and trial evaluation will be included.
- **Effect:** Increased focus on the vehicle fleet's green transition and potential reduction in local air pollution.
- **Economy:** **0.85 million DKK**, excluding lost revenue.

**Initiative 5 — Increased enforcement against illegal parking in EV and car-share spaces.**
*(Also appears in the "Handlingsplan for delebiler.")* Parking wardens will pay particular attention to enforcing parking fines at car-share and EV spaces.
- **Effect:** Intended preventive effect, minimizing illegal parking at these spaces.
- **Economy:** **7.25 million DKK**.

**Initiative 6 — Funds for signage and marking of future EV parking spaces.**
No dedicated funds are currently allocated for establishing signage/marking of EV parking spaces that the municipality handles — currently limited to the administration's own operating budget. Broader charging-infrastructure rollout requires dedicated funding for future signage/marking.
- **Effect:** Funding for signage/marking of future EV parking spaces is a precondition for further charging-infrastructure rollout.
- **Economy:** **6.7 million DKK**, excluding lost revenue.

### Part 1 Initiatives — Theme 2: Dialogue and Advisory Support

**Initiative 7 — Dialogue and advisory support on charging-infrastructure and car-share expansion.**
*(Also appears in the "Handlingsplan for delebiler.")* High demand exists for information/help establishing parking and charging infrastructure for car-share and EVs. Københavns Kommune can support rollout by providing information and dialogue with housing associations, businesses, residents, and providers about how to e.g. join a car-share scheme or establish spaces/charging infrastructure. The dialogue process will particularly focus on promoting charging infrastructure for commercial vehicles (vans, taxis) on public and private land, to support these sectors' green transition. Promoting charging infrastructure on private land, and working to make it publicly accessible where possible, will also help make the general rollout more cost-effective for the municipality.

Also requires dialogue/coordination with grid companies to ensure grid capacity keeps pace with charging-infrastructure expansion. Dialogue with grid companies and charging-infrastructure providers can also include promoting innovation — e.g., charging via outlets in streetlamp posts, citizen-driven crowdfunding of charging stations, reservation systems for charging stations, and intelligent charging so future chargers can draw grid power at times when the grid isn't burdened by the city's other electricity consumption. There are also further opportunities to coordinate charging infrastructure with surrounding municipalities and promote car-sharing/ride-sharing through regional cooperation.
- **Effect:** Helps more actors work to promote charging infrastructure and car-sharing; the municipality can play a coordinating role ensuring synergy between projects/schemes.
- **Economy:** **1.75 million DKK**.

**Sidebar: Brancheaftale om ladeinfrastruktur og opladning (Industry Agreement on Charging Infrastructure and Charging)**
In **December 2020**, private charging-station operators and other industry actors entered an industry agreement, committing them to (among other things):
- Ensure all EV drivers, regardless of customer relationship, can start, stop, and pay for charging via **one app** across the entire publicly accessible charging network in Denmark, within the first half of **2021**.
- Provide price information etc. on each charging station and share operational-status information with roaming services.
The industry agreement also includes an ambition to invest in **1,000 rapid chargers, 2,000 fast chargers, and 20,000 normal publicly accessible charging stations by 2025**.

**Initiative 8 — Dialogue and cooperation with Region Hovedstaden, KL, and other municipalities on standardizing/optimizing framework conditions.**
The municipality can facilitate cooperation with **Region Hovedstaden, KL (Local Government Denmark), and other municipalities** — e.g., to influence national framework conditions that could expand the municipality's scope for action on charging infrastructure. This cooperation could beneficially be coordinated with Region Hovedstaden's EV secretariat, **Copenhagen Electric**. Cooperation could also focus on securing better, more uniform framework conditions for charging infrastructure — e.g., technical standards, service requirements, accessibility for all, and pricing (the latter should also involve dialogue with private charging operators).
- **Effect:** Cooperating with other actors to create better framework conditions, making EV ownership/use more attractive, can give Københavns Kommune a stronger voice at the national level and with industry.
- **Economy:** Can be launched within the administration's existing operating budget.

**Initiative 9 — Dialogue with the state on framework conditions** (higher parking fines for illegal parking, possibility of tendering and co-financing, possibility of requiring EV-reserved parking spaces in local plans and urban planning).
Can beneficially be linked with Initiative 8's cooperation with Region Hovedstaden, KL, and other municipalities.

*Higher parking fine for illegal parking:* Municipalities currently cannot independently raise fines for illegally parked cars at EV/car-share parking spaces. The municipality can pursue dialogue with the state to gain authority to increase this fine.
- **Effect:** Raising the parking fine is expected to reduce illegal parking at EV/car-share spaces, improving charging-infrastructure utilization and making EV/car-share ownership/use more attractive in Copenhagen. *(Also appears in the "Handlingsplan for delebiler.")*

*Possibility of tendering and co-financing:* Municipalities currently lack legal authority to tender, co-finance, or install/co-finance charging infrastructure at their own buildings for public use. The municipality can, based on the Kommissionen for grøn omstilling af personbiler's recommendations, engage the state in dialogue to enable this for municipalities.
- **Effect:** A legislative change would give municipalities greater scope regarding rollout pace and city-wide coverage of charging infrastructure.

*Possibility of requiring EV-reserved parking spaces in local plans and urban planning:* The **planloven** (Planning Act) sets the framework for municipalities' ability to e.g. require charging-infrastructure establishment, number of stations etc. in connection with municipal and local plans. The Eldrup-Kommissionen's overall assessment is that municipalities can plan for charging infrastructure and reserve land for it, but lack authority to require its establishment. Københavns Kommune will therefore continue dialogue with the state seeking authority to require EV parking spaces in municipal plans.
- **Effect:** Secures charging infrastructure in new urban areas and urban-development projects.
- **Economy:** All state engagement can be handled within the administration's existing operating budget.

### Part 1 Initiatives — Theme 3: Goals and Follow-up

**Initiative 10 — Ongoing analysis and monitoring of the number of EVs/PHEVs and charging-infrastructure demand.**
Teknik- og Miljøforvaltningen continues analyzing expected charging-infrastructure demand and follows up annually on the goals set in the charging-infrastructure action plans. Further analysis is needed to, among other things, qualify demand on public vs. private land respectively, and demand for fast/rapid charging in Copenhagen.
- **Effect:** Ensures charging-infrastructure rollout proceeds at a sensible pace and with city-wide coverage.

**Initiative 11 — Establishment of a digital tool for monitoring charging-infrastructure demand via citizen and business input.**
A digital tool will be developed allowing residents and businesses to mark on a map where they would like to see EV/PHEV charging infrastructure installed. The map will be accessible via a link from the municipality's EV/charging-infrastructure webpage, with input given by placing a dot on the map. Data is submitted anonymously, avoiding GDPR concerns.
- **Effect:** Gives the administration a way to monitor where interest in charging-infrastructure installation is greatest, supporting optimal rollout. The administration will periodically publish this data via an existing public data platform, e.g. **opendata.dk**, so private charging operators and other interested parties can see where interest/customer base exists for installation. These inputs, and subsequent installation, will also indicate where commercial interest does and doesn't exist — relevant background knowledge if national framework conditions change and the municipality gains tender/co-financing authority.
- **Economy:** Can be established within the administration's existing operating budget.
