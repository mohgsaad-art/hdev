# DFDS — Fleet Electrification Case Study

**Source file:** `CASE_2023_DFDS.pdf`
**Category:** Case study — fleet electrification (heavy-duty electric trucks, long-haul scheduled route)
**Original language:** Danish (translated to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** DFDS operates Denmark's first heavy electric truck on a fixed long-haul scheduled route (Hobro–Slagelse) carrying Arla refrigerated goods, alongside 65 electric trucks in service and 60 more on order.

## Company Profile
- **Company:** DFDS (refrigerated/cold-chain transport division specifically discussed)
- **Industry/sector:** Freight transport / logistics, refrigerated goods distribution
- **Fleet size (electric trucks):** 65 electric trucks in operation, 60 more on order (as of 2023)
- **Key customer/partner:** Arla Foods (dairy) — pilot project customer; Uno-X (charging infrastructure partner)
- **Locations:** Route runs between Hobro and Slagelse (via Nyborg and Horsens); also 2 electric trucks operating in Copenhagen for Arla refrigerated goods near-distribution
- **Person(s) quoted:** Anders Christensen — responsible for DFDS's refrigerated transport (køletransport)
- **Publication year of case:** 2023

## Electrification Timeline
- **Company-wide targets:** Reduce CO2 emissions on the roads by 50% by 2030; be CO2-neutral in logistics by 2040.
- **~2020 (ca. three years before 2023):** DFDS began identifying routes suitable for electric trucks.
- Arla Foods agreed to a pilot project; DFDS deployed one electric truck on fixed scheduled service between Arla's dairies in Hobro and Slagelse — making DFDS the first in Denmark to run heavy electric trucks in regular scheduled service ("fast rutedrift").
- **March 2023:** Uno-X opened Denmark's first publicly accessible fast-charger for electric trucks in Nyborg, enabling the pilot project to launch.
- DFDS also already runs two electric trucks in Copenhagen distributing Arla refrigerated goods to shops (near-distribution / nærdistribution), delivered mainly at night, reducing noise for residents.

**Detailed route/charging example (a randomly chosen day, per source):**
1. Departure Hobro 07:47 — range 279 km
2. Charging in Nyborg 10:58–12:18 (Uno-X Truck charging facility), after ca. 230 km driven — range on arrival: 63 km; new range after charge: 285 km; charged at 250 kW for ca. 80 minutes
3. Unloading in Slagelse 12:53 — remaining range: 240 km
4. Charging in Horsens 16:10–16:56 (45 minutes) — range on arrival: 87 km; new range: 183 km
5. Arrival Hobro 18:34 — range on arrival: 63 km
6. Overnight: truck charges via the depot charger, ready again next morning.

- Charging stops are timed to coincide with the driver's mandatory rest/break periods under driving-time/rest-time rules (køre/hviletid), so "charging time = rest time" ("Ladetid = hviletid").
- The onboard computer guides the driver in real time, precisely indicating remaining range.
- Cold weather and side wind are noted as significantly reducing range, requiring drivers to adapt routines day to day ("Ikke to dage er ens" — no two days are the same). The new Horsens charger gave drivers more flexibility to coordinate charging with rest breaks.

## Financial Figures
- No direct cost/DKK savings figures given in this source (no investment cost, per-km savings, or DKK maintenance savings stated).
- CO2 saving calculation (shown explicitly in source): the electric truck on the Hobro–Slagelse route drives 170,000 km/year. A traditional diesel truck would require 48,000 liters of diesel for the same distance, and at 2.7 kg CO2 per liter this equals 130 tons CO2 (48,000 L × 2.7 kg CO2/L = 130 tons CO2). This is presented as the annual CO2 saving of running the route electrically instead of with diesel.

**Stat callout:** "ÅRLIG BESPARELSE I CO2: 130 TONS" (Annual CO2 saving: 130 tons)

## Operational Notes
- Charging infrastructure: Uno-X public truck fast-chargers in Nyborg and Horsens (250 kW DC) plus a depot charger (50 kW AC) in Hobro for overnight charging.
- Route design deliberately matches charging stops to legally required driver rest breaks, so no additional non-productive downtime is incurred for charging.
- No V2X/smart charging or grid-connection details are mentioned.
- Drivers become more skilled over time at "reading the route" and adapting to range-affecting conditions such as cold and wind.

## Quotes
- Anders Christensen: "For omkring tre år siden gik vi i gang med at udpege de ruter, hvor vi kunne indsætte ellastbiler... Da Arla Foods var villig til at indgå i et pilotprojekt, valgte vi at indsætte en ellastbil i fast rutefart mellem Arlas mejerier i Hobro og Slagelse." (About three years ago we started identifying routes where we could deploy electric trucks... When Arla Foods was willing to enter a pilot project, we chose to deploy an electric truck on fixed scheduled service between Arla's dairies in Hobro and Slagelse.)
- Anders Christensen: "Efterhånden som chaufførerne bliver rutinerede, er de blevet dygtige til at læse ruten... Koldt vejr og sidevind går hårdt ud over rækkevidden, så det er ikke altid muligt at følge den samme rutine." (As drivers become experienced, they've become skilled at reading the route... Cold weather and side wind badly affect range, so it isn't always possible to follow the same routine.)
- Anders Christensen: "Ellastbilerne er ganske enkelt et fantastisk køretøj. Den er støjfri, vibrationsfri og har en vild acceleration. Chaufførerne kan godt være lidt skeptiske første gang, de skal køre i en ellastbil. Men når først de har vænnet sig til den, vil de ikke ud af den igen." (Electric trucks are simply a fantastic vehicle. It's noise-free, vibration-free and has wild acceleration. Drivers can be a bit skeptical the first time they drive an electric truck. But once they've gotten used to it, they won't want to get out of it again.)

## Future Plans
- Reduce road CO2 emissions by 50% by 2030.
- Be CO2-neutral in logistics by 2040.
- Fleet of electric trucks to grow from 65 to 125 (65 in service + 60 on order, as of 2023).
