# Spejlblank — Fleet Electrification Case Study

**Source file:** `CASE_Spejlblank-FINAL.pdf`
**Category:** Case study — fleet electrification (small business, single EV, urban charging constraints)
**Original language:** Danish (translated to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** A Copenhagen window-cleaning company built its very first company vehicle as an electric car in 2016 and used its limited range to trigger a route-optimization overhaul of its whole business, but urban public-charger availability and Copenhagen's charging time-limit rules remain a real operational barrier to fully electrifying its 14-vehicle fleet.

## Company Profile
- **Company:** Spejlblank (vinduespudservirksomhed — window-cleaning company)
- **Industry/sector:** Window cleaning services
- **Fleet size:** 14 vehicles total, of which 1 is electric
- **Location:** Copenhagen (København) — employees drive to/from jobs from home, not from a central office lot
- **Person(s) quoted:** Fredrik B. Grunwald Højer (also spelled Frederik B. Grunwald Højer in source) — founder, established the company in 2016
- **Publication year of case:** 2021

## Electrification Timeline
- **2016:** Spejlblank founded by Fredrik B. Grunwald Højer; the company's very first vehicle was an EV — a used Renault Kangoo Z.E. with a range up to 100 km on a good day — despite the price premium being a significant challenge for a newly started business "where every krone counts."
- The original Kangoo Z.E. was later replaced with a newer Renault Kangoo Z.E., described by the founder as superior on all parameters (comfort, quietness, automatic transmission suited to Copenhagen's dense traffic, and reliable cold-morning starting).
- As of the case's writing (2021), Spejlblank still has only 1 EV among its 14 vehicles, which the founder states is not for lack of will but due to external constraints (price competitiveness and urban charging access).

## Operational Notes
- The EV's limited range forced Spejlblank to fundamentally redesign how it schedules jobs: previously jobs were carried out in the order they were booked; now they are scheduled based on geographic proximity to one another, fully automated via the company's IT/scheduling system. This produced fewer kilometers driven, lower costs, less vehicle wear, and less wasted time on the road — described as a good outcome for both environment and bottom line.
- Key barrier: lack of home/office charging means drivers hunt for available public chargers, which is time-consuming; Copenhagen's rule restricting charger use to 3 hours before 18:00 makes this worse. Spejlblank states it must actually pay employees to drive around the streets after 18:00 searching for an available charging station — described explicitly as a problem the municipality has yet to solve.
- Comfort/experience benefits noted: quieter, automatic transmission works well in dense Copenhagen traffic, and no cold-start issues even on cold winter mornings.

## Future Plans
- Founder's stated ambition/dream: all company vehicles to eventually be electric.
- Explicitly conditional on (a) EV prices becoming more price-competitive and (b) better charging infrastructure/availability in cities — no fixed target year given.
