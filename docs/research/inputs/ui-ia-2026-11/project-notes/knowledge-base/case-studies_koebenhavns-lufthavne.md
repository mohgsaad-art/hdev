# Københavns Lufthavne — Fleet Electrification Case Study

**Source file:** `Casehistorier_Fagfolk_templafy.pdf` (compendium; story: "Københavns Lufthavne tager elbilen under sine vinger")
**Category:** Case study — EV demonstration leading to fleet purchase (airport operations)
**Original language:** Danish (translated to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** Copenhagen Airport tested EVs via the "Mød elbilen" program with 40+ staff (including mechanics) driving the demo vehicles, resulting in the purchase of 2 Volkswagen e-up! pool cars, within a fleet of nearly 200 passenger/light-van vehicles running up to 90,000 km/year, around the clock.

## Company Profile
- **Organization:** Københavns Lufthavne (Copenhagen Airports)
- **Sector:** Airport operations — a "vital part of Danish infrastructure," strategy "World Class Hub" for Northern Europe
- **Fleet:** Just under 200 passenger cars and light vans in operation; vehicles run from 2,000 up to 90,000 km/year, used 24 hours a day, year-round
- **EVs purchased (post-demo):** 2 Volkswagen e-up! (used as pool cars)
- **Person(s) quoted:** Jesper Abery Jacobsen — Senior Projektleder, Miljøafdelingen (Senior Project Manager, Environment Department); Søren Salwin — Sektionsleder, Autoværkstedet (Section Leader, the Auto Workshop)

## Electrification Timeline
- Airport operations include large amounts of electric ground equipment already (e.g. electric baggage carts and passenger stairs — "elektriske bagagevogne og passagertrapper" — described as long-standing).
- Copenhagen Airports participated in the "Mød elbilen" demonstration project as part of its local-environment awareness and air-quality improvement ambitions, balancing ambition, operations, and economics.
- Mød elbilen was primarily tested within the operations department (driftsafdelingen), where more than 40 employees drove the demo vehicles, including the airport's own mechanics.
- Result: Copenhagen Airports purchased 2 Volkswagen e-up! following the trial, used as pool cars, gathering further operational experience.

## Operational Notes
- Airport materiel/equipment (broader than just the road-vehicle fleet) is described as having become "significantly greener" in recent years.
- Given the airport's specialized/heavy-duty usage profile, the stated approach to further EV conversion is explicitly gradual — "hvor vi kan og hvor det er bæredygtigt" (where we can and where it is sustainable), both environmentally and economically.
- The two Volkswagen e-up! pool cars are specifically intended to build further operational experience with EVs.

## Quotes
- Jesper Abery Jacobsen (Senior Projektleder, Miljøafdelingen): "Vi er meget bevidste om vores materiel og påvirkning af lokalmiljøet mht. partikeludledning. Vores materiel er inden for de seneste år blevet væsentlig mere grønt." (We are very conscious of our equipment's impact on the local environment regarding particulate emissions. Our equipment has become significantly greener in recent years.)
- Søren Salwin (Sektionsleder, Autoværkstedet): "Vi stiller store krav til vores materiel og vores brug er specielt og adskiller sig fra andre, omstillingen sker derfor gradvist hvor vi kan og hvor det er bæredygtigt både miljømæssigt og økonomisk." (We place high demands on our equipment and our use is specialized and differs from others', so the transition happens gradually, where we can and where it's sustainable both environmentally and economically.)
- Søren Salwin: "På baggrund af testen har vi anskaffet os to elbiler, der vil give os erfaringer med brug af elbiler i driften. De to elbiler anvendes som puljebiler og de foreløbige erfaringer er meget positive." (Based on the test we have acquired two EVs, which will give us experience with using EVs in operations. The two EVs are used as pool cars and the preliminary experience is very positive.)
