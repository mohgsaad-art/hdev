## 1. Introduction and definitions (Section 1)

### 1.1 Introduction

This Annex describes the certification procedure regarding the **friction losses of wheel ends for non-driven axle applications**. Certification of wheel ends on **driven axles** is included in the procedure laid down in **Annex VII** (i.e., wheel-end losses on driven axles are tested as part of the whole-axle torque loss map in Annex VII; this Annex VIIA is specifically for non-driven axle wheel ends, e.g. steer axles / trailing axles).

Alternatively to wheel-end certification, the **standard friction losses of wheel ends** per **point 6** may be applied for determining vehicle-specific CO2 emissions.

### 1.2 Definitions

For the purposes of this Annex:

1. **Wheel bearing** — the bearings used to support one wheel end in a vehicle.
2. **Wheel end** — the assembly of components establishing the connection between the wheel and the axle; includes wheel bearings, seals and lubricants, as well as the wheel hub (if available) and all other components relevant to rotational friction; **may exclude** the brake disc and wheel flange.
3. **Radial load** — the load applied to the wheel end perpendicular and vertically to the shaft axis.
4. **Axial load** — the load applied to the wheel end in the direction of the shaft axis, considering the dynamic wheel radius.
5. **Load line position** — the position on the wheel end through which the radial load is applied.
6. **Wheel end manufacturer** — the legal entity that produces the wheel end.
7. **Wheel end family** — a manufacturer's grouping of wheel ends which, through design (set out in point 2.3), have similar design characteristics and CO2/fuel consumption properties.
8. **Customer** — the legal entity that sells the vehicle or axle in which the wheel end is installed.
9. **Testing entity** — the legal entity responsible for testing the wheel end: either the wheel end manufacturer or a third party.
10. **Seal** — the part of the wheel bearing designed to prevent intrusion of particles/liquids, or to prevent lubricant leakage.
11. **Clearance** — the total distance through which one bearing ring can be moved relative to the other in the axial direction.
12. **Preload** — the negative operating clearance in the wheel bearing.
13. **Inner ring** — the ring(s) of the wheel bearings with smaller diameter than the outer ring.
14. **Outer ring** — the ring(s) of the wheel bearings with greater diameter than the inner ring.
15. **Measurement** — the measurement of friction losses in the wheel end, expressed as a friction torque in Nm.
16. **Bearing rated load** — the maximum design load as defined in the wheel bearing specifications.
17. **Pitch diameter** — the distance in a wheel bearing between the geometrical centre of two rolling elements when the two rolling elements are diametrically opposed.
18. **Run-in procedure** — the procedure of conditioning an unused wheel end under load to bring it to a state representative of in-use conditions.

## 2. General requirements (Section 2)

### 2.1 Wheel end selection
- Wheel ends used for friction loss verification **shall be new**.
- They shall be the **same** wheel ends as defined by series-production specifications, as will be installed in customer applications.
- Specifications include (non-exhaustively): dimensions, materials, surface quality and treatments, number of rollers, seal, lubricant type/quality/quantity, and any other characteristic relevant to wheel end friction.

### 2.2 Number of wheel ends to test
- For CO2 certification of a wheel end family, **at least four different wheel ends** from the family parent shall be tested per the procedures in points 3 and 4, using the **same speed and load target steps** for each.

### 2.3 Parameters defining a wheel end family

The following criteria shall be **the same** for all members of a wheel end family:
- rolling elements' quantity;
- rolling elements' diameter within **± 0,5 mm** (measured perpendicular and at the centre of the long axis);
- rolling elements' length within **± 1 mm** (measured along the long axis);
- pitch diameter within **± 1 mm**;
- number of rows;
- outer ring contact angle with the rolling elements within **± 1°**;
- the lubricant type: oil or grease;
- load-line position (in the case the family parent is not tested at the position indicated in Figure 2).

### 2.4 Choice of the wheel end family parent
- The family parent shall be the member with the **highest friction**.
- If a family has more than one member, the testing entity shall **justify** the choice of family parent based on component properties.
- The **bearing rated load for the family** shall be the **highest** bearing rated load of all family members.
- For each family member, the testing entity shall provide **quantifiable data** on:
  - seals performance (e.g. friction losses);
  - lubrication (oil or grease) performance (e.g. viscosity);
  - preload / clearance range (e.g. maximum and minimum).
- The approval authority may request additional justification (e.g. simulations or calculations) when it considers the above properties insufficient to justify the family choice.

### 2.5 Run-in
- The testing entity **shall** apply a run-in procedure on the wheel ends, using the **same test set-up and requirements** as for the friction loss measurements.

#### 2.5.1 Run-in procedure — four successive phases

| Phase | Direction | Speed | Radial load | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clockwise | 300 rpm (constant) | 50 % of bearing rated load | 60 ± 2 minutes |
| 2 | Counterclockwise | 300 rpm (constant) | 50 % of bearing rated load | 60 ± 2 minutes |
| 3 | Clockwise | 500 rpm (constant) | 100 % of bearing rated load | 660 ± 2 minutes |
| 4 | Counterclockwise | 500 rpm (constant) | 100 % of bearing rated load | 660 ± 2 minutes |

- The run-in procedure shall be documented (run-time, speed, radial load, bearing temperature) and reported to the approval authority.

*(Note: Phases 3 and 4 run for 660 minutes = 11 hours each at 100% load — this is a substantial duration, roughly 22 hours combined just for the high-load run-in phases, on top of phases 1-2's combined 2 hours. Total run-in alone is ~24 hours, which is consistent with the 55-hour total test ceiling noted in 3.3 below.)*

### 2.6 Lubricant

**2.6.1 Lubricant requirements** — Type, quality and quantity shall match series-production specifications as used in customer applications. If the wheel end manufacturer does not supply the lubricant with the bearing, the customer shall provide the necessary lubricant information for accurate testing.

**2.6.2 Oil lubricant** — If lubricant is oil type, the oil level within the bearing shall be as defined in the axle specifications. Absent a specification, the **maximum geometrically possible oil level** of the axle shall apply.

### 2.7 Operating clearance/Preload
- If adjustable, the clearance/preload used for testing shall be set at the **arithmetic mean** of the specified clearance/preload range, within a tolerance of **± 20 μm**.

### 2.8 Seals
- Seals used for testing shall be the same as defined by series-production specifications/customer applications. If the wheel end manufacturer does not supply seals, the customer shall provide the necessary seal information for accurate testing.

## 3. Testing procedure for wheel ends (Section 3)

### 3.1 Test conditions

**3.1.1 Ambient temperature**
- Test cell temperature: **25 °C ± 10 °C**, measured within 1 m of the wheel bearing's outer ring, documented in the test report. This is a **target temperature** — systematic deviations across tests are **not allowed**.

**3.1.2 Wheel bearing temperature**
- Measured on the bore-side of the inner ring, located on the inner side of the vehicle.
- During measurements, wheel bearing temperature shall be kept at a **maximum of 60 °C**; air cooling may be applied per 3.3.5.

### 3.2 Test set-up

- The test set-up shall be as illustrated in **Figure 1** — **[Figure 1, "Simplified schematic of the test set-up," referenced in source but not visually extracted from this text-only OCR]**.

**3.2.1 Installation of torque, load, temperature, and speed measuring devices**
- Torque measuring devices installed to measure friction losses in the wheel end, minimizing parasitic effects.
- A speed measuring device installed to measure rotational speed of the wheel end.
- A temperature measuring device installed to measure the temperature of the bore-side of the inner ring on the inner side of the vehicle.
- A load measuring device installed to measure the radial load applied on the wheel end.

**3.2.2 Test set-up**
- Consists of an electric machine applying rotational speed to the wheel end, plus a device capable of applying a radial load.
- The wheel end shall be installed so that the **outer ring** of the wheel bearing is rotating and used for speed input, while the **inner ring is not rotating**.
- Gearings and couplings between the electric machine and the wheel end are allowed, provided they do not influence measurement results.

**3.2.3 Measurement equipment**
- Calibration laboratory facilities shall comply with either **IATF 16949**, **ISO 9000 series**, or **ISO/IEC 17025**.
- All laboratory reference measurement equipment used for calibration/verification shall be traceable to national (international) standards.
- Measurement accuracies set in 3.2.3.1–3.2.3.4 concern the **complete measurement chain** (sensors + additional inaccuracy sources). Specified uncertainty tolerances shall not be used for systematic deviations when instruments of higher accuracy are applied.

**3.2.3.1 Friction torque**
- Torque measurement uncertainty for wheel end friction torque **shall not exceed ± 0,2 Nm**.
- If uncertainty is higher, measurements shall be calculated per point **3.4.6**.

**3.2.3.2 Radial load**
- Load measurement uncertainty **shall not exceed ± 1 kN**.
- If radial load is applied as a mass, it shall be converted using the gravitational constant **9,81 N/kg**.

**3.2.3.3 Rotational speed**
- Speed measurement uncertainty **shall not exceed ± 2,5 rpm**.

**3.2.3.4 Temperatures**
- Ambient temperature measurement uncertainty **shall not exceed ± 2 °C**.
- Wheel bearing temperature measurement uncertainty **shall not exceed ± 2 °C**.

**3.2.4 Measurement signals and data recording**

Signals to be recorded:
(a) Input rotational speed [rpm]
(b) Wheel end friction torque [Nm]
(c) Applied radial load [kN]
(d) Bearing temperature [°C]
(e) Ambient temperature [°C]

Minimum sampling frequencies:

| Signal | Minimum sampling frequency |
|---|---|
| (a) Friction torque | 300 Hz |
| (b) Rotational speed | 100 Hz |
| (c) Temperatures | 10 Hz |
| (d) Load | 10 Hz |

*(Note: the lettering in the source's sampling-frequency sub-list does not exactly match the lettering of the signal list immediately above it — e.g. "(a) Friction torque" in the frequency table corresponds to signal "(b)" in the signal list. This mismatch is reproduced exactly as it appears in the consolidated official text; it is a labelling inconsistency in the source, not an error introduced in this transcription.)*

- Raw friction torque data shall be filtered by a suitable **low-pass filter**, such as a **Butterworth 2nd order filter** with a **cut-off frequency of 0,1 Hz**. Filtering of other signals may be applied by agreement with the approval authority; any aliasing effect shall be avoided.
- Raw data **shall not** be reported.

### 3.3 Test procedure

- To determine the torque loss map for a wheel end, grid points of the friction torque loss map data shall be measured per point 3.4.
- A grid point measurement may only be repeated for a **technically justified reason** (e.g. sensor failure); any repetition shall be recorded in the test report.
- **Total testing time** for one wheel end sample — from initiating run-in until concluding the last grid point — shall be **concluded within a maximum of 55 hours**, otherwise the test of that sample is **void**.

**3.3.1 Radial load range**
- Friction loss map measured at radial loads corresponding to **25 %, 50 %, and 100 %** of the bearing rated load.
- Target loads shall be reported together with the actual measured load.

**3.3.2 Radial load line position**
- Radial load applied at the centre of the wheel end, so the load line position is at the centre of the wheel bearing within **± 0,5 mm**.
- Centre of the wheel bearing = the middle of the outside positions of the inner wheel-bearing (WB) rings (see **Figure 2**, "Determination of Load Line Position" — **[Figure 2 referenced in source but not visually extracted from this text-only OCR]**).
- At the manufacturer's request and with approval authority approval, the load line position may be chosen outside the centre, provided the manufacturer provides evidence that this corresponds to the wheel end's application.

**3.3.3 Axial load**
- **No axial load** shall be applied onto the wheel ends for these measurements.

**3.3.4 Rotational speed range**
- The wheel end shall be tested at **250 and 500 rpm**.
- All rotational speed points shall be measured in **clockwise and counterclockwise** directions per the testing sequence in 3.4.1.
- Results **may** be reported as the average of the clockwise and counterclockwise measured values.

**3.3.5 Cooling and heating**
- The wheel end may be **air cooled** by a fan using ambient air at ambient temperature (per 3.1.1). **No other** external cooling or heating is allowed.
- If air cooling is used, the **same cooling condition** shall apply for all tested wheel ends at all grid points.

### 3.4 Measurement of friction torque loss maps

**3.4.1 Testing sequence**

The sequence depends on the measurement configuration:
- If radial load and friction torque are determined **individually by dedicated** torque measurement devices → **Testing sequence A** (3.4.1.1).
- If radial load and friction torque are determined **simultaneously by the same** torque measurement device → **Testing sequence B** (3.4.1.2).
- If the testing entity cannot judge which applies, **Testing sequence A** shall be applied by default.

**3.4.1.1 Testing sequence A**
- Measurements start at the **highest radial load, downward to the lowest**; at each load step, **first the highest, then the lowest rotational speed** is tested.
- Once the grid point at lowest load/lowest speed is measured, the **rotational direction is reversed** and the sequence repeats.
- Shown schematically in **Figure 3** — **[Figure 3, "Testing sequence scheme A," referenced in source but not visually extracted from this text-only OCR]**.

**3.4.1.2 Testing sequence B**
- Measurements start at the **highest radial load and highest rotational speed**.
- Rotational direction reversed; same load/speed point measured again.
- Same load retained; direction reversed again; friction measured at the **lower** rotational speed; this load/speed point is also measured in both directions.
- Sequence repeated for **50 % and 25 %** radial load settings.
- Shown schematically in **Figure 4** — **[Figure 4, "Testing sequence scheme B," referenced in source but not visually extracted from this text-only OCR]**.

**3.4.2 Stabilisation and measurement duration**

- For each grid point, a **stabilisation period of 117 ± 2 minutes** shall be allowed before starting the measurement.
- Additional stabilisation periods:
  - **Testing sequence A**: Before the **1st** grid point and before the **7th** grid point (after rotational direction reversal), stabilisation extended by an additional **60 ± 2 minutes**. (Times indicated in Figure 3.)
  - **Testing sequence B**: Before the **1st** grid point, stabilisation extended by an additional **60 ± 2 minutes**. Before the **5th** and **9th** grid points, extended by an additional **30 ± 2 minutes**. (Times indicated in Figure 4.)
- Friction for each grid point shall be measured during the **last 180 seconds** of the corresponding constant-speed phase.
- If the stabilisation criterion (3.4.3) is not met during the last 180 s of the grid point, the measurement may instead be taken from the **first earlier uninterrupted 180-second segment** where the stabilisation criterion was fulfilled.
- If the test set-up has a wheel-end support bearing required to rotate in both directions during each grid point's measurement, friction shall be measured during the **last 180 s of clockwise rotation** of the support bearing AND during the **last 180 s of counterclockwise rotation** of the support bearing.

**3.4.3 Stabilisation criterion**
- Met when the **standard deviation of friction torque** during measurement does **not exceed 15 % of the mean value or 0,4 Nm**, whichever is **higher**.

**3.4.4 Averaging of grid points**
- For every individual sample, all recorded values for each grid point are averaged to an arithmetic mean over the measurement duration.
- These per-sample arithmetic means for the same grid point are then averaged over **all samples** to one arithmetic mean value per grid point.

**3.4.5 Measurement validation**

For each grid point:
- Wheel end speed (before averaging) shall not deviate from the set value by more than **± 5 rpm**;
- Radial load (before averaging) shall not deviate from the set value by more than **± 2 kN**;
- **No systematic deviation** from set values is allowed.
- If criteria are not met, the grid point measurement is **void**; the entire affected speed and load step shall be repeated, with the reason for voiding recorded in the test report. After passing the repeat, the data shall be consolidated.

**3.4.6 Assessment of total uncertainty of the torque loss**

- If the uncertainty on the measured friction torque is **below** the 3.2.3.1 limit (± 0,2 Nm), the reported friction torque loss **equals** the measured friction torque loss.
- If uncertainty is **higher**, the part exceeding the limit shall be **added** to the measured friction torque losses.

**Formula (as transcribed from OCR):**
```
Treported ¼ Tmeasured þ maxð0; Ut–U limit Þ
```
**Interpreted:**
$$T_{reported} = T_{measured} + \max(0,\ U_t - U_{limit})$$

where:
- T_reported = the calculated friction torque loss at a given speed and load, reported for CO2 certification of wheel ends [Nm]
- T_measured = the measured friction torque loss per section 3.4.4, at a given speed and load [Nm]
- U_t = the absolute value of the torque uncertainty (> 0), expressed in Nm
- U_limit = **0,2 Nm**

### 3.5 Calculation of the friction value for certification

- The grid points of the reported torque loss map are first **averaged across all wheel end samples** (per section 3.4.4 — the source's "section 0" reference appears to be a cross-reference error in the official text; based on context this should refer to 3.4.4), **corrected** per 3.4.6 (if applicable), and then **weighted** per **Table 1** for non-driven axle wheel end applications.

**Table 1 — Weighting factors for non-driven axle applications**

| | 250 rpm | 500 rpm |
|---|---|---|
| 25 % load | 0,4 % | 2,4 % |
| 50 % load | 7,9 % | 35,3 % |
| 100 % load | 9,5 % | 44,5 % |

*(Sum check: 0,4 + 2,4 + 7,9 + 35,3 + 9,5 + 44,5 = 100,0 % — the six weighting factors sum exactly to 100 %, confirming correct transcription of this table.)*

### 3.6 Declaration of the certified friction value

- The wheel end manufacturer **may declare** the weighted average friction calculated in 3.5 as the **certified value** for the wheel end family.
- Alternatively, the manufacturer may declare **any higher** friction value.
- The declared friction value shall be **rounded to 1 decimal place**.

## 4. Conformity of the certified CO2 emissions and fuel related properties

- Every certified wheel end shall be manufactured to conform, with regard to the certification form/annexes description, to the approved type. Conformity procedures shall comply with those set out in **Article 31 of Regulation (EU) 2018/858**.
- Conformity shall be checked based on the certificate description (Appendix 1) and the specific conditions in this point.
- The wheel end manufacturer shall test, **at least every second year** from the date of the family parent's certification, the number of wheel end families shown in **Table 2**. The number of families to be tested depends on production volumes of the **year previous to** the year when conformity-of-production testing is due.
- **At least two wheel ends** of the same family member shall be tested.

**Table 2 — Sample size for conformity testing**

| Production number | Number of wheel end families to be tested |
|---|---|
| 0 – 100 000 | 2 |
| 100 001 – 150 000 | 3 |
| 150 001 – 250 000 | 4 |
| 250 001 and more | 5 |

## 5. Production conformity testing

- The wheel end manufacturer shall apply the **same procedure** as described in point 3, including the run-in procedure and validation criteria.

### 5.1 Conformity of the certified CO2 emissions and fuel consumption related properties test assessment

- A conformity test is **passed** when the weighted average friction value from conformity testing is **lower than or equal to** the declared friction value for the wheel end family, with an **allowed tolerance margin of +10 %**.
- If production conformity testing is **not passed**: **three additional wheel ends** shall be tested using the same procedure. The recorded values of all tested ends (including the three additional ones) shall be averaged per grid point to an arithmetic mean. If the conformity test is **again** not passed, the provisions of **Article 23** shall apply.
- If a family member proves to have **higher friction than the family parent**, that family member shall be **reclassified into another wheel end family**, requiring a **new certification**.

## 6. Standard friction torque loss

> **The standard friction loss for non-driven axle applications shall be 4,8 Nm.**

This is the single generic/default value available as an alternative to the full measurement procedure in points 1–5 — analogous in purpose to Annex VII Appendix 3's standard axle torque-loss tables, but here reduced to **one fixed number** (4,8 Nm) rather than a family of formulas, since non-driven wheel ends have no gear ratio or load-dependent efficiency term — only a near-constant bearing/seal friction torque.

## Electric axles / e-axles — NOT addressed in this Annex, and not clearly addressed elsewhere within the extracted ranges

**Annex VIIA, within the extracted range (lines 12920–13476), makes no reference to electric machines, e-axles, integrated electric drive units, or BEV/FCEV-specific provisions.** This is unsurprising given the Annex's scope: it concerns only **non-driven** axle wheel ends (bearings, seals, lubrication, friction), which is a drivetrain-topology-agnostic component — a non-driven wheel end on a diesel truck's steer axle and on a battery-electric truck's steer axle would, in principle, be governed by the same test procedure, since neither is "driven."

**Important implication for the HDEV sizing tool, flagged explicitly (not an assumption I am asserting as fact, but an inference worth the user's own verification):**
- For a **battery-electric HDV with a conventional, non-driven front/steer axle**, Annex VIIA's wheel-end friction methodology (measured or the 4,8 Nm standard value) would plausibly apply unchanged, since it is purely mechanical and driveline-agnostic.
- For **driven axles** on an electric HDV — i.e., where propulsion comes through a conventional axle with a mechanical gearset fed by an e-motor (as opposed to an **integrated e-axle** where the electric motor and gear reduction are a single unitized assembly) — Annex VII's torque-loss methodology for SR/SP/HR/SRT/HRT axle lines could in principle still apply, since the Annex VII test rig already uses an **electric machine as the dynamometer drive** for ALL axle types (conventional and otherwise) — the test rig's own drive technology is not vehicle-propulsion-dependent.
- However, **neither Annex VII nor Annex VIIA, within the ranges extracted so far, defines or even mentions "integrated e-axle," "electric drive axle," or any axle category beyond the five mechanical axle lines (SR, SP, HR, SRT, HRT)**. If an OEM's e-axle integrates the electric motor, reduction gearing, and differential into one sealed, non-serviceable unit (common in production e-axles today), it is not obvious from this text alone whether:
  (a) it would be certified under Annex VII's existing axle-line taxonomy (treating it as, say, a single-speed HR-equivalent reduction with the "input torque" simply coming from the integrated motor instead of a cardan shaft), or
  (b) it falls under a **different Annex entirely** — most plausibly one of the Annexes dealing with electric machines / electric energy storage / powertrain-level simulation inputs for xEVs, which were added to Reg. 2017/2400 by later amendments (e.g. 2022/1379 ►M3, which is known to have introduced electrified-powertrain provisions into VECTO more broadly) but which **do not appear in the Annex VII/VIIA range assigned to this extraction task**.
- **Recommendation for the user:** if integrated e-axle certification/loss-modelling methodology is needed for the HDEV sizing tool, this should be searched for specifically in the other Annexes of Reg. 2017/2400 (e.g., Annexes on electric machines, batteries/REESS, or the VECTO component/vehicle-level simulation Annexes) rather than assumed to be covered by Annex VII/VIIA. I have not verified this in the current task and am flagging it rather than guessing.
