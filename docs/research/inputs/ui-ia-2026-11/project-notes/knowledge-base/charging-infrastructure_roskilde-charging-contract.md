## Background & Purpose (Section 2)
- Municipality wants to improve EV charging options; ran a project offering multiple operators the chance to install charge points in the municipality.
- Contract follows an **announcement/solicitation process** ("annonceringsproces") — private operators could bid to install charge points on municipal properties and public road areas.
- Contract sets terms for installation, operation, and maintenance of charge points on "Arealerne" (the Areas).
- Areas include both municipal properties and municipal **public road areas**. Road-area installation requires permits under the **Vejloven (Danish Roads Act)**, specifically **§ 80** and **§ 73**. The contract does not limit/regulate the municipality's authority to grant or withhold such road-law permits (to this supplier or others) — permits must still be separately applied for.
- **All investment and operating risk borne by the supplier**, who installs and operates at its own expense and risk.

- Supplier cannot unilaterally decline to install agreed charge points at one or more Areas without valid reason ("saglig grund") — only by mutual agreement.

## Contract Period (Section 4)
- Effective from signature; runs from **first commissioning date, no later than 31 December 2022**.
- Per supplier's tender of [date], first commissioning is [dato1], and the **contract runs 10 years from that date to [dato2]**.
- Municipality may offer a **contract extension of up to 5 years** after this period, on unchanged terms.
- Municipality may terminate for valid reason with **6 months' notice**. Valid reasons include: breach of contract (per point 13), or the area being needed for longer-term/permanent use that would block citizen access to the charge point(s) — in which case supplier must remove the charge point(s) and reinstate the area **at its own expense**.
- If municipality needs to reclaim an Area for longer-term/permanent use during the contract term, municipality must try to designate an **alternative site of comparable value**; supplier is **not obligated to accept** the designated alternative. All costs of establishing charge points at a new site are borne by the supplier, under the same contract.
- Supplier may, with **3 months' notice**, decide to remove one or more installed charge points if there are signs they have not generated / are not expected to generate revenue at the expected level; same applies for repeated cases of vandalism, theft, or vehicle collisions. Supplier must then reinstate the Area (per Section 8).

## Parties' Obligations (Section 5)
- Municipality ensures supplier's access to and use of Areas for installation/operation, as detailed in the requirements spec and supplier's tender.
- Contract = permission to use the named locations under the contract basis terms; for public road areas, a **separate permit must be sought**, assessed independently by the municipality as road authority, which may set reasonable conditions ("saglige vilkår") for the installation.
- **All costs of establishment and operation borne by supplier**, including installation costs and the purchase of ampere (electrical capacity) for grid connection. If existing installations already exist at a site, supplier may contact municipality to clarify if the charge points can use them.
- Parties may negotiate expansion/relocation of charge points. If municipality wants to expand an existing area and the incumbent supplier will not add chargers there, municipality can award the expansion area to another supplier.
- Supplier may request to install additional charge points during the contract term; if municipality permits, these additional charge points are governed by the same contract terms (duration etc.) — i.e., adding chargers does **not** extend the contract term; they must be removed at contract expiry along with the original charge points.
- **Supplier must send relevant raw data to municipality on request, including (not limited to):** (a) when charging occurs, (b) how much electricity is charged, (c) duration of each charging session.
- On contract termination, supplier must provide relevant data for a renewed competitive process (per 5.6) and must not obstruct a successor supplier's takeover preparations, cooperating to minimize disruption to municipality/citizens.

## Installation of Charge Points (Section 6)
- Supplier must install charge points on the Areas **during 2022**, with commissioning no later than **31 December 2022**.
- Must obtain necessary permits from the municipal road authority beforehand, incl. excavation permit ("gravetilladelse").
- **Functional test required before commissioning** — municipality has the right (itself or via a third party) to test the charge points, or may require the supplier to demonstrate their functionality/soundness ("duelighed").
- Must minimize disruption to surrounding locations/users during installation.
- Supplier must present a **timeline/schedule for installation before contract signing**; municipality reserves the right to propose changes to the sequence/order.
- Supplier bears all costs and risk of installation.
- Supplier must reinstate Areas around charge points to the same standard as before installation.

## Operation of Charge Points (Section 7)
- Supplier must operate and maintain charge points during the contract period, ensuring citizens have access to charging **24 hours a day, every day of the year**.
- Must keep charge points well-maintained, well-run, and clean, incl. keeping software updated; must fix faults per the requirements spec.
- Supplier **may not conduct any business other than sale of EV charging** from the charge points, and specifically **may not sell or market retail goods or similar**.
- Municipality handles snow clearance, sweeping, salting, weed control at sites, per standards for similar municipal areas.
- Municipality handles signage and marking at charge points and may set time restrictions on a given Area if desired; **supplier cannot demand this**.

## Obligations on Termination (Section 8)
- Regardless of cause, supplier must leave Areas cleared and reinstated unless otherwise agreed.
- Reinstated Areas (incl. adjoining areas) must appear uniform.
- Reinstatement deadline agreed with municipality, **not to exceed 3 months**; if missed, municipality can have work done at supplier's expense.
- If Areas continue as charging locations for other/future suppliers, **any sale of connection contribution ("tilslutningsbidrag") is a matter between the supplier and the utility company — of no concern to the municipality** (differs from the Lyngby-Taarbæk contract, which had the municipality facilitate such a sale).

## Consumption Costs and Insurance (Section 9)
- Supplier pays all operating, consumption, and maintenance costs related to the charge points.
- Municipality pays any land taxes ("grundskatter") on the Areas, plus interest/repayments on any road/sewer debt ("vej- og kloakgæld") on the Areas.
- Supplier is responsible for insuring the charge points against fire, water damage etc., if desired; supplier liable for any damage to third parties, incl. third-party property.

## Subcontractors and Assignment (Section 10)
- Supplier's rights/obligations cannot be wholly or partly assigned to a third party outside the supplier's corporate group without municipality's written permission.
- Supplier cannot transfer ownership or lease out operation of the charge points without municipality's written approval.
- Supplier is liable to municipality for subcontractor conduct/omissions exactly as for its own; use of subcontractors does not limit supplier's liability for contract performance.
- **Municipality may freely transfer its rights/obligations to another public authority or a publicly-owned company.**

## Prices and Price Regulation (Section 11)
- Supplier sets prices and terms as described in its tender.
- Adjustments to the kWh price are permitted following ordinary/normal regulation practices.
- **Material changes to subscription terms and payment options must be reported to the municipality.**
- (Note: this contract's price-regulation clause is notably less detailed than the Lyngby-Taarbæk contract — no explicit numeric cap such as the 5%/year figure found there.)

## Liability (Section 12)
- Supplier is liable to municipality for all losses attributable to (a) supplier's own conduct, (b) the charge points, and/or (c) operation of the charge points.

## Default / Breach (Section 13)
- Municipality may terminate the contract for **material breach** ("væsentlig misligholdelse").
- **Non-exhaustive list of what constitutes material breach:**
  - Supplier fails to install the charge points, and/or installation exceeds the agreed deadline by **more than 6 weeks**.
  - Supplier ceases operating the charge points as agreed.
  - Supplier is declared bankrupt, placed into restructuring proceedings ("rekonstruktionsbehandling") or similar.

## Ownership (Section 14)
- Municipality retains full ownership of the Areas.
- Supplier retains ownership of the charge points, **including cables and the connection to purchased ampere (electrical capacity)**.
- On termination, supplier must remove the charge points.

## Damages Liability and Insurance (Section 15)
- Supplier liable per ordinary Danish law rules for contractual liability and any special rules applicable to its business; liable for subcontractors.
- (Note: unlike the Lyngby-Taarbæk contract, this version does **not** explicitly require a specific "erhvervsansvarsforsikring" (commercial liability insurance) policy to be maintained/documented — see Section 9.3 instead, which frames insurance as supplier's option ("hvis det ønskes" / "if desired").)

## Changes (Section 16)
- **Supplier bears the risk of legislative changes that increase costs** related to installation and/or operation of the charge points. (Note: this is a notably supplier-unfavorable risk allocation compared to typical infrastructure contracts, and stands in some contrast to the Lyngby-Taarbæk contract's tax/duty pass-through clause.)

## Dispute Resolution (Section 17)
- Governed by **Danish law**.
- Disputes should be resolved amicably where possible; parties commit to a positive, cooperative, and responsible approach to negotiation, escalating within their organizations if necessary. Unresolved disputes go to the **ordinary courts**.
- **Venue: the municipality's home venue ("Kommunens hjemting").**

## Comparison Notes vs. Lyngby-Taarbæk Contract (Claude's own analytical framing, not from source)
- **Contract term:** Roskilde = 10 years (+ up to 5-year extension) vs. Lyngby-Taarbæk = 8 years (+ up to 2×12 months).
- **Municipal termination notice:** Roskilde = 6 months vs. Lyngby-Taarbæk = 9 months.
- **Price adjustment cap:** Lyngby-Taarbæk specifies an explicit 5%/year cap with a documented process; Roskilde has looser language ("almindelige reguleringer" / "normal regulation") without a stated percentage cap.
- **Legislative/tax risk allocation:** Lyngby-Taarbæk passes through documented net tax/duty cost changes to price; Roskilde places legislative cost-increase risk squarely on the supplier (Section 16.1) with no described pass-through mechanism.
- **Termination-for-site-reclaim compensation:** Lyngby-Taarbæk has an explicit compensation formula tied to documented establishment costs pro-rated by remaining contract years; Roskilde instead offers a "comparable value" alternative site (which supplier may decline) with no explicit cash-compensation formula, and supplier bears removal/reinstatement costs when municipality reclaims a site.
- **Supplier walk-away right:** Roskilde uniquely gives the supplier a unilateral right (3 months' notice) to remove underperforming charge points; not present in Lyngby-Taarbæk's text as extracted.
- These are useful contrasting commercial/regulatory-risk templates for a HDEV depot/CPO concession model comparison (municipal on-street public charging, not depot-specific).
