## 1. Background and purpose

- On **10 December 2025**, the European Commission issued new guidance to EU member states on efficient grid connections, as part of the EU's energy infrastructure initiative known as the **"Grids Package."** The specific document cited is: *"Guidance on efficient grid connections C(2025) 8473 final – Energy"* (footnote 1 in the source).
- The EU Commission's guidance responds to rising pressure on grid capacity in several member states, causing delays to grid connection projects. It recommends managing the capacity challenge partly through **well-organized and transparent criteria for prioritizing grid connection requests**, so that available network capacity is used efficiently.
- Energistyrelsen states it is taking note of ("tager til efterretning") the EU guidance because Denmark is also experiencing pressure on electricity grid capacity. Increased prioritization could enable more efficient allocation of the grid capacity available to collective electricity supply companies (Energinet and the netvirksomheder/grid/DSO companies).
- The purpose of this ENS guidance document is to describe the possibilities Danish grid companies have to make increased use of prioritization of grid connection requests, **within the bounds of EU law and the Danish Electricity Supply Act (elforsyningsloven)**, in light of the Commission's new recommendations.

## 2. Legal basis (exact laws/articles cited)

| Legal source | Reference | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| EU Electricity Market Directive | **Article 6, paragraph 1** (elmarkedsdirektivets artikel 6, stk. 1) | Member states must ensure a system of third-party access based on **objective, transparent, and non-discriminatory criteria**. Although third-party access formally concerns transport of electricity, in practice the provision also covers physical grid connection. |
| Danish Electricity Supply Act (elforsyningsloven) | **§ 20** | Implements Article 6(1). Collective electricity supply companies must ensure sufficient and efficient transport of electricity and related services, including maintaining, upgrading and expanding the grid as needed; must connect and make necessary transport capacity available to anyone who submits a request and can pay the costs. This includes a duty to organize operations and capacity allocation in a technically sound and appropriate way. |
| Danish Electricity Supply Act (elforsyningsloven) | **§ 24, stk. 2** | Collective electricity supply companies must not discriminate between grid users — this applies equally when connecting users to the grid. Implements the objective/non-discriminatory-access requirement of Article 6(1). |
| Danish Electricity Supply Act (elforsyningsloven) | **§ 28** | Energinet and the grid companies (netvirksomhederne) are obliged to cooperate with each other regarding grid access and grid transit. |
| EU Commission guidance | *Guidance on efficient grid connections*, **C(2025) 8473 final**, 10 December 2025 | Non-binding recommendations (guidance, not law) on how member states/grid operators can prioritize connection requests. |

## 3. Key legal interpretation: no mandatory "first-come, first-served"

- The Danish implementation of Article 6 means that Danish collective electricity supply companies, as part of their regulated duties, must handle, plan, and potentially prioritize among grid connection requests in a network area **in a way that respects objectivity (saglighed) and non-discrimination**.
- Prioritizing grid connection requests is described as an **established part of the grid companies' existing tasks** — it already involves taking into account efficient use of available capacity and ensuring net- and system-technically sound operation. In practice, grid companies already assess how expected grid load can be managed when processing connection requests.
- **Critically, the EU Commission's guidance confirms that Article 6 does NOT require grid companies to apply the well-known "first-come, first-served" principle** ("først-til-mølle"-princippet) — i.e., the requester who applies first does not automatically go to the front of the queue. **Other criteria may be used.**
- "Non-discrimination," per the Commission's guidance, means that **equal grid users in equal circumstances must be treated without discrimination.** This does **not** preclude establishing new, more differentiated prioritization criteria that treat different grid users differently — provided the differential treatment is based on **objective (saglige) grounds**, as required under applicable EU rules.

## 4. New prioritization criteria recommended by the EU Commission

The Commission's guidance suggests grid companies may apply the following types of criteria (Energistyrelsen presents these as recommendations grid companies "can" draw on, not mandates):

### 4.1 Maturity criterion ("modenhedskriteriet" / "first-ready-first-serve")

- Prioritization depends on an assessment of whether the project is **ready to be connected**, or how far advanced its establishment is.
- Described as a variant of the "first-come-first-served" principle, because it does not differentiate between different types of grid users.
- Considered inherently objective (saglig) and can be applied in a non-discriminatory way to all grid users.
- **Already used by many grid companies (elnetselskaber) and Energinet.**
- Limitation noted: this criterion cannot necessarily secure prioritization **among different types of customers** in cases of limited grid capacity (i.e., it doesn't let a grid operator favor, say, one customer category over another).

### 4.2 Milestone criteria ("milepælskriterier")

- Prioritization depends on whether the project owner can **demonstrate progress**, e.g., documenting — within a deadline — obtained regulatory/authority permits (myndighedstilladelser) or investment decisions (investeringsbeslutninger).
- Maturity and milestone criteria are expected to help counter **"speculative" grid connection requests ("hamstring")** — described in the EU Commission's guidance as cases where grid customers register a project to "stake a claim" on grid capacity without subsequently showing concrete progress toward actually building it.
- These criteria can be combined with the **contractual arrangements** established between the collective electricity supply companies and grid users.

### 4.3 "Grid-friendly" / grid-relieving project prioritization ("net-venlige"/"net-aflastende" projekter)

- Prioritization of projects that help alleviate, or at least do not worsen, **local capacity constraints**, and that may resolve grid bottleneck problems.
- Per the Commission, "grid-friendliness" prioritization can also cover projects that support system needs, deliver system benefits, and contribute to lower costs for grid users — but the **main consideration is relieving local grid capacity constraints**, so prioritization should be based on whether such constraints are actually mitigated.

Specific example criteria given by the Commission for "grid-friendliness":

- Prioritizing **co-located renewable energy (VE) generation and storage technologies, including batteries** — particularly where there is a limitation on feed-in to the grid during periods of high electricity production.
- Prioritizing **consumption facilities in production-dominated areas**, and **production facilities in consumption-dominated areas** (i.e., matching load type to local grid balance).
- Prioritizing **industrial facilities that can deliver flexibility services**.

> **No mention of EV charging or depot charging.** The source document does not reference electric vehicles, heavy-duty EVs, charging depots, or fleet charging anywhere in its text. Any application of these criteria to HDEV depot connections (e.g., whether a depot with smart/managed charging could qualify as a "flexibility service" or "grid-friendly" load) is **not stated in the source** and would be an inference, not a documented fact.

## 5. Constraints and safeguards on new criteria (ENS's own assessment)

- Energistyrelsen's assessment ("Energistyrelsens vurdering"): individual collective electricity supply companies, each on their own, can develop new prioritization criteria — incorporating the Commission's recommendations above — while ensuring efficient capacity allocation.
- **Existing customer base must be protected:** when developing and applying new prioritization criteria, grid companies must also have regard to **maintaining electricity supply for the existing customer base**. Assessments of available capacity for new connections should sufficiently account for the need to accommodate expected future consumption and production growth within the existing customer base.
- Energistyrelsen regards it as an **integral part of the § 20 obligation** that grid dimensioning and capacity allocation account for ordinary and foreseeable **organic growth** in the existing customer base.
- **Objectivity and transparency required (§ 24):** when developing new prioritization criteria, grid companies must place weight on objective (saglige) considerations and **must communicate the criteria to grid users** to ensure adequate transparency.

## 6. Coordinated / common industry approach

- The Commission's guidance also states that new prioritization criteria should be **adapted to the needs of the grid as a whole**, and should be developed through **broad stakeholder involvement**. It recommends member states establish a **common industry-wide approach** to ensure coordinated capacity planning across the grid.
- Energinet and the netvirksomheder are already obliged under **elforsyningsloven §§ 20 and 28** to cooperate with each other on grid access and grid transit.
- Energistyrelsen considers it an integral part of this cooperation duty that **Energinet and the grid companies jointly determine any prioritization criteria**, and ensure that at the interface between the transmission grid (eltransmissionsnettet) and distribution grid (eldistributionsnettet), **sufficient capacity is reserved** to cover expected future needs.
- It would therefore be appropriate to secure a **common industry approach** among the collective electricity supply companies and other relevant parties, so that the future framework for prioritizing between grid connection projects is coordinated across the electricity grid.

## 7. Summary (as stated in the source's own "Opsummering" section)

- Collective electricity supply companies **can** prioritize between grid connection requests, in line with their statutory duties to organize and plan available grid capacity.
- They **should cooperate** and establish a common, holistic industry approach to prioritization.
- In doing so, they can draw on the EU Commission's recommended prioritization criteria described above (maturity, milestones, grid-friendliness).
