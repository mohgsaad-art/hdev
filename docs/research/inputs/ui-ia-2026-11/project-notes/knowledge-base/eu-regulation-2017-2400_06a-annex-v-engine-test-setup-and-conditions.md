## 1. Introduction

The engine test procedure described in this Annex shall produce input data relating to engines for the simulation tool (i.e. VECTO). ▼B

## 2. Definitions ▼M3

For the purposes of this Annex, the definitions in **UN Regulation No. 49** [footnote (1): Regulation No. 49 of the Economic Commission for Europe of the United Nations (UN/ECE) — Uniform provisions concerning the measures to be taken against the emission of gaseous and particulate pollutants from compression-ignition engines and positive-ignition engines for use in vehicles (OJ L 171, 24.6.2013, p. 1)] apply, in addition to the following: ▼M3

1. **"engine CO2-family"** — a manufacturer's grouping of engines, as defined in point 1 of Appendix 3. ▼B
2. **"CO2-parent engine"** — an engine selected from an engine CO2-family as specified in Appendix 3. ▼B
3. **"NCV"** — net calorific value of a fuel, as specified in point 3.2. ▼B
4. **"specific mass emissions"** — total mass emissions divided by total engine work over a defined period, expressed in g/kWh. ▼B
5. **"specific fuel consumption"** — total fuel consumption divided by total engine work over a defined period, expressed in g/kWh. ▼B
6. **"FCMC"** — fuel consumption mapping cycle. ▼B
7. **"Full load"** — the delivered engine torque/power at a certain engine speed when the engine is operated at maximum operator demand. ▼B
8. **"Waste Heat Recovery system" / "WHR system"** — all devices converting energy from the exhaust gas or from operating fluids in engine cooling systems into electrical or mechanical energy. ▼M3
9. **"WHR system with no external output" / "WHR_no_ext"** — a WHR system which generates mechanical energy and is mechanically connected to the engine crankshaft to feed its generated energy directly back to the engine crankshaft. ▼M3
10. **"WHR system with external mechanical output" / "WHR_mech"** — a WHR system which generates mechanical energy and feeds it to other elements in the vehicle's drivetrain than the engine, or to a rechargeable storage. ▼M3
11. **"WHR system with external electrical output" / "WHR_elec"** — a WHR system which generates electrical energy and feeds it to the vehicle's electric circuit or to a rechargeable storage. ▼M3
12. **"P_WHR_net"** — the net power generated by a WHR system, per point 3.1.6. ▼M3
13. **"E_WHR_net"** — the net energy generated by a WHR system over a given time, determined by integrating P_WHR_net. ▼M3

The definitions in points 3.1.5 and 3.1.6 of Annex 4 to UN Regulation No. 49 **do not apply** (superseded by the above). ▼B

## 3. General requirements ▼B

Calibration laboratory facilities shall comply with the requirements of either **IATF 16949**, the **ISO 9000 series**, or **ISO/IEC 17025** ▼M3 (replacing an earlier narrower requirement). All laboratory reference measurement equipment used for calibration and/or verification shall be traceable to national or international standards.

Engines are grouped into **engine CO2-families** per Appendix 3. Point 4.1 (see Part B) specifies which test runs must be performed for certification of a given CO2-family.

### 3.1 Test conditions

All test runs for certification of one specific engine CO2-family (Appendix 3) must be conducted **on the same physical engine**, without changes to the dynamometer/engine-system setup, except for the exceptions in point 4.2 and Appendix 3.

#### 3.1.1 Laboratory test conditions

Ambient conditions must meet the following over the whole test run:

1. The parameter **`fa`** (laboratory test-condition factor), determined per point 6.1 of Annex 4 to UN Regulation No. 49: **0.96 ≤ fa ≤ 1.04**. ▼M3
2. Absolute intake-air temperature **Ta** (Kelvin), per point 6.1 of Annex 4 to UN R49: **283 K ≤ Ta ≤ 303 K**. ▼M3
3. Atmospheric pressure **ps** (kPa), per point 6.1 of Annex 4 to UN R49: **90 kPa ≤ ps ≤ 102 kPa**. ▼M3

If tests are performed in cells simulating barometric conditions other than the actual site atmosphere, the applicable `fa` is determined with the simulated pressure values; the same reference simulated pressure must be used for intake air, exhaust path and all other relevant engine systems, and must stay within the subpoint-(3) limits. ▼B

Where ambient pressure at the test site exceeds 102 kPa, tests may still be performed, using the actual site pressure. ▼M3

Where the test cell can independently control intake-air temperature, pressure and/or humidity, the **same settings** must be used for all test runs within one CO2-family. ▼M3

#### 3.1.2 Engine installation ▼M3

Test engine installed per points 6.3–6.6 of Annex 4 to UN R49.

If auxiliaries/equipment necessary for operation are **not** installed as required by point 6.3 of Annex 4 to UN R49, all measured engine torque values must be corrected for the power required to drive those components, per point 6.3 of Annex 4 to UN R49.

Correction is required only if the **sum of absolute values** of additional/missing engine torque for driving these components, at a given operating point, exceeds the torque tolerances defined in point 4.3.5.5(1)(b) (Part B). For intermittently-operated components, torque is averaged over an appropriate period reflecting actual operating mode (good engineering judgement + approval-authority agreement).

Components in scope for this correction (determined per **Appendix 5**, see Part D):
1. Fan
2. Electrically powered auxiliaries/equipment necessary for operating the engine system

**▼M4 addition:** If an engine of the CO2-family is installed in a vehicle equipped with an **on-board fuel/energy-consumption monitoring device (OBFCM)** per Article 5c(b) of Regulation (EC) No 595/2009, the test engine shall be equipped with this on-board device.

#### 3.1.3 Crankcase emissions ▼B

Closed crankcase: manufacturer ensures no crankcase-gas emission to atmosphere. Open crankcase ▼M3: emissions measured and added to tailpipe emissions per point 6.10 of Annex 4 to UN R49.

#### 3.1.4 Engines with charge air-cooling ▼B

Charge-air-cooling system on the test bed operated to be representative of in-vehicle application at **reference ambient conditions: 293 K air temperature, 101.3 kPa pressure**. Laboratory charge-air cooling should comply with point 6.2 of Annex 4 to UN R49. ▼M3

#### 3.1.5 Engine cooling system ▼B

1. Cooling system on the test bed operated representative of in-vehicle application at reference ambient conditions (293 K, 101.3 kPa).
2. Should use thermostats per manufacturer specification. Non-operational/no thermostat → subpoint (3) applies; cooling-system setting per subpoint (4) regardless.
3. If no/non-operational thermostat: test-bed system must reflect thermostat behaviour under all conditions.
4. ▼M1: Engine coolant flow rate (or pressure difference across the engine-side heat exchanger) and coolant temperature set to a value representative for in-vehicle application at reference ambient conditions, with the engine at **rated speed and full load** and thermostat fully open. This defines the **coolant reference temperature**. This setting is fixed for all test runs within one engine/CO2-family (neither engine-side nor test-bed-side changed). Test-bed-side cooling-medium temperature held reasonably constant (good engineering judgement); it must not exceed the nominal thermostat opening temperature downstream of the heat exchanger.
5. Engine coolant temperature maintained between the declared nominal thermostat opening temperature and the coolant reference temperature (point 4), once the coolant reaches the declared opening temperature after cold start.
6. For the **WHTC cold-start test** (point 4.3.3), specific initial conditions per points 7.6.1/7.6.2 of Annex 4 to UN R49 ▼M3. If thermostat behaviour is simulated (subpoint 3), **no coolant flow** across the heat exchanger until the declared nominal opening temperature is reached after cold start.

#### 3.1.6 Set-up of WHR systems ▼M3

Applies only where a WHR system is present.

**3.1.6.1** — For the parameters in 3.1.6.2, the test-bed installation must **not** give the WHR system better generated-power performance than the in-vehicle specification. Other WHR-related systems on the test bed operated representative of in-vehicle application at reference ambient conditions (293 K, 101.3 kPa).

**3.1.6.2** — Test setup must reflect the **worst case** for temperature/energy-content transfer from excess energy to the WHR system. Parameters to be set (recorded per *Figure 1a* [Figure referenced in source but not visually extracted from this text-only OCR] and reported in the Appendix 2 information document):

- **(a)** Distance from last after-treatment system to WHR heat exchangers/boilers (evaporation), measured downstream (**L_EW**) ≥ manufacturer's max distance for in-use installation (**L_maxEW**).
- **(b)** For WHR systems with turbine(s) in the exhaust-gas flow: distance engine-outlet → turbine entry (**L_ET**) ≥ **L_maxET**.
- **(c)** For WHR systems using a working fluid in a cyclic process:
  - (i) Pipe length evaporator → expander (**L_HE**) ≥ **L_maxHE**.
  - (ii) Pipe length expander → condenser (**L_EC**) ≤ **L_maxEC**.
  - (iii) Pipe length condenser → evaporator (**L_CE**) ≤ **L_maxCE**.
  - (iv) Working-fluid pressure before condenser (**p_cond**) corresponds to in-use application at reference ambient conditions, and in any case not lower than test-cell ambient pressure minus 5 kPa, unless the manufacturer demonstrates a lower pressure is maintainable over vehicle lifetime.
  - (v) Test-bed cooling power for the WHR condenser limited to:
    **P_cool = k × (t_cond − 20°C)**
    where `t_cond` = condensation temperature (°C) of the fluid at `p_cond`; measured on the working-fluid side or test-bed coolant side.
    **k = f0 + f1 × Vc**, where `Vc` = engine displacement in litres (rounded to 2 decimal places), **f0 = 0.6 kW/K**, **f1 = 0.05 kW/(K·l)**.
  - (vi) Condenser cooling may be liquid or air. If air-cooled: same fan as on the vehicle (if applicable), under reference ambient conditions (3.1.6.1); the (v) power limitation still applies, measured on the working-fluid side. If the fan is externally powered, its actual power consumption counts as power delivered to the WHR system when computing net power under subpoint (f).
- **(d)** Other WHR systems taking heat from exhaust/cooling system: set up per (c); "evaporator" = heat exchanger transferring excess heat to the WHR device, "expander" = energy-converting device.
- **(e)** All WHR pipe diameters ≤ in-use diameters.
- **(f)** For **WHR_mech**: net mechanical power measured at the rotational engine speed expected at **60 km/h** (averaged over expected transmission ratios if more than one). Mechanical/electrical power generated must be measured with equipment meeting Table 2 (below).
  - (i) **Net electric power** = electric power delivered by WHR to an external sink/storage **minus** electric power delivered to WHR from an external source/storage; measured as **DC power** (after AC→DC conversion).
  - (ii) **Net mechanical power** = mechanical power delivered by WHR to an external sink/storage (if applicable) **minus** mechanical power delivered to WHR from an external source/storage.
  - (iii) All transmission systems necessary for in-use operation must be set up for measurement during engine testing (e.g. cardan shafts/belt drives, AC/DC converters, DC/DC transformers). If a vehicle-used transmission system is not part of the test setup, the measured net electrical/mechanical power is **decreased** by multiplying with a generic efficiency factor per separate transmission system (Table 1a):

**Table 1a — Generic efficiencies of transmission systems for WHR power** ▼M4/▼M3

| Type of transmission | Efficiency factor for WHR power |
|---|---|
| Gear stage | 0.96 |
| Belt drive | 0.92 |
| Chain drive | 0.94 |
| DC/DC converter | 0.95 |

### 3.2 Fuels ▼B

**▼M4:** The reference fuel for the engine system under test is selected from Table 1 below and must be the **same** reference fuel used for EC type-approval under Regulation (EU) No 582/2011. Fuel properties for the reference fuels in Table 1 are those in Annex IX to Commission Regulation (EU) No 582/2011, and — for hydrogen — Annex 5 of UN Regulation No. 49.

To ensure the same fuel is used for all test runs in one CO2-family's certification, **no refill/tank switch** is allowed, except where it can be ensured the replacement fuel has exactly the same properties (same production batch).

**NCV determination:** two separate measurements, per the respective standard in Table 1, by **two different labs**, independent of the certifying manufacturer, each complying with **ISO/IEC 17025**. The approval authority ensures the fuel sample for NCV determination is taken from the same batch used for all test runs.

If the two NCV values deviate by **more than 440 J/g fuel**, both are void and the measurement campaign is repeated.

**▼M1:** The mean of the two NCV values (where not deviating >440 J/g) is documented in **MJ/kg, rounded to 2 decimal places** per **ASTM E 29-06**.

**▼M3 (gas/hydrogen fuels):** The NCV-determination standards for gas/hydrogen (Table 1) already calculate calorific value from fuel composition. Composition is taken from analysis of the reference-fuel batch used for certification testing, via a **single** analysis by an independent lab (not the mean of two). For gas/hydrogen fuel batch switches (exceptionally allowed), NCV is calculated per batch and the **highest** value documented.

**Table 1 — Reference fuels for testing**

| Fuel type / engine type | Reference fuel type | Standard used for NCV determination |
|---|---|---|
| Diesel / CI | B7 or B100 ▼M4 | at least ASTM D240 or DIN 59100-1 (ASTM D4809 recommended) |
| Ethanol / CI | ED95 | at least ASTM D240 or DIN 59100-1 (ASTM D4809 recommended) |
| Petrol / PI | E10 | at least ASTM D240 or DIN 59100-1 (ASTM D4809 recommended) |
| Ethanol / PI | E85 | at least ASTM D240 or DIN 59100-1 (ASTM D4809 recommended) |
| LPG / PI | LPG Fuel B | ASTM 3588 or DIN 51612 |
| Natural gas / PI or Natural Gas / CI ▼M3 | G25 or GR ▼M1 | ISO 6976 or ASTM 3588 |
| Hydrogen / PI or Hydrogen / CI ▼M4 | Hydrogen | ISO 6976 or ASTM 3588 |

**3.2.1 Dual-fuel engines ▼M3:** reference fuels selected from Table 1; **▼M4** one of the two reference fuels must always be **B7 or B100**, the other must be **G25, GR, LPG Fuel B, or Hydrogen**. The basic provisions of 3.2 apply to each of the two fuels separately.

### 3.3 Lubricants ▼M4

**▼M3:** Lubricating oil for all test runs must be commercially available, with unrestricted manufacturer approval for normal in-service conditions per point 4.2 of Annex 8 to UN R49. Lubricants restricted to special operating conditions, or with unusually short change intervals, are **not allowed**. No modification, no additives. All test runs for one CO2-family's certification use the **same** lubricating-oil type.

### 3.4 Fuel flow measurement system ▼B

All fuel flow consumed by the whole engine system must be captured. Additional fuel flows not directly supplied to the combustion process must be included. Additional fuel injectors not necessary for engine operation (e.g. cold-start devices) must be disconnected from the fuel supply during all test runs.

**3.4.1 Dual-fuel engines ▼M3:** fuel flow per 3.4 measured for **each** of the two selected fuels separately.

Footnotes: (1) "Accuracy" = deviation of the analyzer reading from a reference value traceable to a national/international standard. (2) "Rise time" = difference in time between the 10% and 90% response of the final analyzer reading (t90 − t10). (3) "Max calibration" = 1.1 × the maximum predicted value expected during all test runs for the respective measurement system.

**Dual-fuel "max calibration" determination for fuel mass flow (both liquid and gaseous):**

1. The fuel type whose fuel mass flow is determined by the measurement system subject to Table 2 verification is the **primary fuel**; the other is the **secondary fuel**.
2. Convert the secondary fuel's maximum predicted mass flow to primary-fuel-equivalent:
   **mf*mp,seco = mfmp,seco × NCVseco / NCVprim**
   where `mf*mp,seco` = max predicted mass-flow of secondary fuel converted to primary fuel; `mfmp,seco` = max predicted mass-flow of secondary fuel; `NCVprim`, `NCVseco` = NCV of primary/secondary fuel (point 3.2) [MJ/kg].
3. Maximum predicted overall value:
   **mfmp,overall = mfmp,prim + mf*mp,seco**
4. "Max calibration" values = **1.1 × mfmp,overall**.

`xmin` (for the intercept calculation) = **0.9 × the minimum predicted value** expected during all test runs for the respective measurement system.

**Signal delivery rate:** all Table 2 measurement systems except fuel mass flow: **≥ 5 Hz (≥ 10 Hz recommended)**. Fuel mass flow: **≥ 2 Hz**. ▼B
All measurement data recorded at **≥ 5 Hz (≥ 10 Hz recommended)**. ▼M3

#### 3.5.1 Measurement equipment verification ▼M3

Verification performed for **each** measurement system: ≥10 reference values between `xmin` and the "max calibration" value (3.5) introduced; the system's response recorded. Linearity verification compares measured vs. reference values by **least-squares linear regression** per point A.3.2 of Appendix 3 to Annex 4 to UN R49.
