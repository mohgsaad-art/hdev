# Metro Service — Fleet Electrification Case Study

**Source file:** `Casehistorier_Fagfolk_templafy.pdf` (compendium; story: "Implementering af elbiler kører på skinner hos Metro Service")
**Category:** Case study — fleet electrification (metro rail operations & maintenance)
**Original language:** Danish (translated to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** Metro Service, responsible for keeping the Copenhagen Metro running 24/7, converted more than 25% of its road vehicle fleet to Citroën and Nissan EVs after a "Mød elbilen" trial, and built a fast-charging station plus infrastructure pre-wired for up to 12 EVs.

## Company Profile
- **Company:** Metro Service
- **Industry/sector:** Metro (underground rail) operations and maintenance
- **Fleet featured:** 5 EVs (per Fakta box): 1 Citroën Berlingo Electric (second unit, after the first EV) + 3 Nissan e-NV200 vans, plus the original first EV
- **Website noted in source:** m.dk
- **Person(s) quoted:** Max Brandt — ledende tekniker (lead technician), Metro Service

## Electrification Timeline
- Metro Service had long considered converting its existing diesel-based vehicle fleet to EVs, with feasibility improving as the electric-van market developed.
- Early dialogue on EVs held with Better Place and Renault several years prior, including trialing a Renault Kangoo Z.E. — described as a positive early experience that encouraged further pursuit of EVs.
- Region Hovedstaden's "Mød elbilen" project gave Metro Service the opportunity to test 4 different EVs with charging facilities for 14 days.
- Following a positive analysis/report from the Mød elbilen trial (presented to company leadership), the first EV was purchased shortly after.
- The first EV was quickly supplemented with an additional Citroën Berlingo Electric, and most recently three more Nissan e-NV200 vans were added, encouraged by subsidies via the capital region's EV partnership with Energistyrelsen support.
- Electric vans now make up more than 25% of Metro Service's total vehicle fleet, with plans for the majority of the fleet to convert to electric — the main uncertain cases being a few large vans.

## Financial Figures
- No specific DKK cost or savings figures, and no CO2 tonnage figure, are stated in this source.
- Purchase of the 3 additional Nissan e-NV200 vans was specifically encouraged/subsidized via the capital region's EV partnership with Energistyrelsen support.

## Operational Notes
- Users of the vehicles (including Metro Service's own mechanics) have been very enthusiastic; there is active competition among staff to drive electric rather than the older diesel vehicles.
- EVs are noted as quiet and equipped with automatic transmission, both new relative to the existing vehicle fleet and appreciated by users day-to-day.
- Leadership has consistently backed the EV trial, which the company frames as aligned with its broader CSR (corporate social responsibility) commitments.
- Parking infrastructure is deliberately future-proofed: cabling for up to 12 EVs is already laid, simplifying scale-up.

(We live by electric operations and are responsible for the Metro running as it should, 24 hours a day, so of course it's natural for us to drive EVs, especially now that they've become as good as they are.)

## Future Plans
- Plans for the majority of the remaining fleet to convert to electric, with a few large vans remaining uncertain candidates.
- Continued build-out of on-site charging infrastructure toward the pre-wired 12-EV capacity.
