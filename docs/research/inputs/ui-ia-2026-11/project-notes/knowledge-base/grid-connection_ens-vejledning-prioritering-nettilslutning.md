## 1. Introduction (Indledning)

- On **10 December 2025**, the European Commission issued new guidance to Member States on efficient grid connections, as part of the EU's energy infrastructure package (the **"Grids Package"**). The specific document is cited in a footnote as: **"Guidance on efficient grid connections C(2025) 8473 final – Energy"**.
- This EU Commission guidance is relevant to Danish collective electricity supply undertakings (Energinet and the net companies) because it provides guidance and recommendations on **prioritisation of requests for connection to the electricity grid** ("nettilslutningsanmodninger").
- Background/rationale: several EU Member States are experiencing increasing pressure on grid capacity and a need for new or upgraded grid connections, which is causing delays to grid-connection projects. The Commission recommends addressing the capacity challenge partly through **well-designed and transparent criteria for prioritising grid connection requests**, so that available grid capacity is used efficiently.
- **Energistyrelsen (ENS) takes note of ("tager til efterretning") the EU Commission's guidance**, since Denmark is also experiencing pressure on electricity grid capacity. Increased prioritisation could enable more efficient allocation of the grid capacity available to the collective electricity supply undertakings. Prioritisation of grid connection requests can be a tool to relieve pressure on the grid and to help ensure better planning and expansion of grid infrastructure.
- Purpose of this ENS guidance document: to describe the possibilities available to Danish collective electricity supply undertakings to make **increased use of prioritisation** of grid connection requests, within the framework of **EU law and the Danish Electricity Supply Act (elforsyningsloven)**, and in light of the Commission's new recommendations.

## 2. Applicable legal framework for prioritisation between grid-connection projects (Gældende rammer for prioritering mellem nettilslutningsprojekter)

### 2.1 EU legal basis
- Collective electricity supply undertakings' handling of grid connection requests must occur within the framework of the **Electricity Market Directive, Article 6(1)** ("elmarkedsdirektivets artikel 6, stk. 1") [exact directive number/name not further specified in the extracted text beyond "elmarkedsdirektivet" — likely Directive (EU) 2019/944, but this specific numbering is **not stated in the source text itself**, so it is marked [unclear from source] pending confirmation].
- Article 6(1) requires Member States to ensure a system of **third-party access (tredjepartsadgang)** based on **objective, transparent and non-discriminatory criteria**.
- Although third-party access formally concerns the *transport* of electricity, in practice the provision also covers the **physical grid connection** itself.

### 2.2 Danish implementation — Elforsyningsloven (Danish Electricity Supply Act)
- Article 6(1) of the Electricity Market Directive is implemented in **§ 20 and § 24 of elforsyningsloven** (the Danish Electricity Supply Act).
- **§ 20**: Collective electricity supply undertakings must ensure sufficient and efficient transport of electricity with associated services, including maintaining, upgrading and expanding the grid to the extent necessary, connecting users, and making the necessary transport capacity available to anyone who submits a request and can pay the costs. The obligations under § 20 entail a responsibility to organise operations and capacity allocation in a manner that is **technically sound and appropriate**.
- **§ 24, subsection 2**: Collective electricity supply undertakings must **not discriminate** between grid users ("må ikke forskelsbehandle netbrugerne"). This also applies when connecting users to the grid. This provision implements the Article 6(1) requirement of objective (saglig) and non-discriminatory access to the grid.
- **§ 28**: Cited later in the document in connection with the obligation for Energinet and the net companies to cooperate with each other regarding grid access and grid transit (see Section 4 below).
- **Conclusion drawn by ENS**: The Danish implementation of Article 6 of the Electricity Market Directive means that Danish collective electricity supply undertakings, as part of their regulated task portfolio, must process, plan, and — where relevant — prioritise between grid connection requests within a network area, in a manner that respects the requirements of **objectivity (saglighed)** and **non-discrimination**.
- ENS states that prioritisation of grid connection requests can be seen as an **established part of the undertakings' tasks**. This includes taking into account efficient utilisation of available capacity when prioritising connections, and ensuring net- and system-technically sound operation. This is already reflected in practice, since collective electricity supply undertakings, when processing connection requests, assess how the expected grid load can be managed.

### 2.3 Key clarifications from the EU Commission's guidance (as relayed by ENS)
- The Commission's guidance **supports** the ability of collective electricity supply undertakings to prioritise between grid connection requests.
- The guidance emphasises that **Article 6 of the Electricity Market Directive does NOT require** collective electricity supply undertakings to apply the well-known **"first-come-first-served" ("først-til-mølle") principle** — i.e., that whoever requests grid connection first automatically goes to the front of the queue. **Other criteria may be used.**
- According to the Commission's guidance, "**non-discrimination**" means that **equal grid users in equal circumstances must be treated without discrimination**. This requirement therefore does **not** preclude the establishment of **new and more differentiated prioritisation criteria**, so that different grid users are treated differently. However, it is a requirement under applicable EU rules that any differential treatment of grid users must be based on **objective (saglige) considerations**.

## 3. Establishing new prioritisation criteria (Fastlæggelse af nye prioriteringskriterier)

The European Commission's guidance sets out several possible prioritisation criteria that collective electricity supply undertakings may adopt:

### 3.1 Maturity criterion ("Modenhedskriteriet" / "first-ready-first-serve")
- Prioritisation depends on an assessment of whether the project is **ready to be connected to the grid**, or whether the project's development is **well advanced**.
- Can be described as a variant of the "first-come-first-served" principle, since it does not differentiate between different types of grid users.
- Considered **immediately objective (sagligt)** and can be applied in a non-discriminatory manner towards all grid users.
- **Already used** by many Danish grid companies and by Energinet.
- Limitation: this criterion **cannot necessarily ensure prioritisation among different types of customers** in cases of limited grid capacity (i.e., it does not by itself solve allocation conflicts between different customer segments).

### 3.2 Milestone criteria ("Milepæls"-kriterier)
- Prioritisation depends on whether the **project owner can demonstrate progress** in the project — e.g., documenting, within a deadline, that authority permits/approvals ("myndighedstilladelser") have been obtained, or that an investment decision has been made.
- Maturity and milestone criteria are expected to be able to **counteract so-called speculative grid connection requests ("hamstring")** — referred to in the Commission's guidance — where grid customers register a project in order to "reserve" grid capacity without subsequently showing concrete progress toward actually building the project.
- Maturity and milestone criteria are instruments that can be **combined with the contractual arrangements** established between the collective electricity supply undertakings and grid users.

### 3.3 "Grid-friendly" prioritisation ("net-venlige" / net-aflastende projects)
- The Commission also recommends that collective electricity supply undertakings prioritise so-called **"grid-friendly"** (or **grid-relieving**) projects — i.e., projects that help alleviate, or at least do not worsen, **local capacity constraints**, and potentially resolve bottleneck problems in the grid.
- The Commission also considers that prioritisation by "grid-friendliness" can include projects that support **system needs**, deliver **system benefits**, and contribute to **lower costs for grid users**.
- **Main consideration**: the primary purpose of "grid-friendliness" is to alleviate **local capacity constraints** in the grid — prioritisation should therefore be based on whether such constraints are mitigated.

**Examples of "grid-friendly" prioritisation criteria cited from the Commission's guidance:**

| # | Grid-friendly criterion (as listed in source) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Prioritisation of **co-located renewable energy (VE) and storage technologies, including batteries** — particularly where these come with a limitation on grid feed-in during periods of high electricity production |
| 2 | Prioritisation of **consumption facilities in production-dominated areas**, and **production facilities in consumption-dominated areas** |
| 3 | Prioritisation of **industrial facilities that can deliver flexibility services** |

*(Note: this is presented as a non-exhaustive list of examples — "f.eks. omfatte følgende kriterier" / "may e.g. include the following criteria".)*

### 3.4 ENS's assessment on developing new criteria
- ENS's assessment: each collective electricity supply undertaking can, individually, and while ensuring efficient capacity allocation, **develop new prioritisation criteria**, drawing on the Commission's recommendations above.
- **Important caveat — protection of the existing customer base**: When developing and applying new prioritisation criteria, due regard must also be given to **maintaining electricity supply to the existing customer base**. Assessments of available capacity for new connections should sufficiently account for the need to accommodate the **expected consumption and production development of the existing customer base**.
- ENS regards it as an **integral part of the obligation under § 20 of elforsyningsloven** that grid dimensioning and capacity allocation account for **normal and foreseeable organic growth** in the existing customer base.
- In accordance with **§ 24 of elforsyningsloven**, when developing new prioritisation criteria the collective electricity supply undertakings must place weight on **objective (saglige) considerations** and must **communicate the criteria to grid users** to ensure adequate **transparency**.

## 4. A common industry approach to prioritisation criteria (En fælles branchetilgang til prioriteringskriterier)

- The Commission's guidance also states that new prioritisation criteria should be **adapted to the needs of the grid as a whole**, and that prioritisation criteria should be developed on the basis of **broad stakeholder involvement**.
- The guidance therefore also recommends that an **industry-wide approach** be established in respective Member States to ensure **coordinated planning of grid capacity**.
- ENS notes in this connection that **Energinet and the net companies are obliged to cooperate with each other** regarding grid access and grid transit, pursuant to **§ 20 and § 28 of elforsyningsloven**.
- ENS considers it an integral part of this cooperation that **Energinet and the net companies establish any prioritisation criteria together**, and ensure that at the **interface between the electricity transmission grid and the electricity distribution grids**, sufficient **capacity reservation** has been made to cover expected future needs.
- It would therefore be appropriate to ensure a **common industry approach** among the collective electricity supply undertakings and other relevant parties, so that the future framework for prioritisation between grid connection projects is **coordinated across the electricity supply grid**.

## 5. Summary (Opsummering)

- Collective electricity supply undertakings **may prioritise** between grid connection requests, in accordance with their statutory tasks of organising and planning available grid capacity.
- They **should also cooperate** and establish a **common, holistic industry approach** to prioritisation.
- In doing so, they can draw on the above-mentioned recommended prioritisation criteria from the European Commission (maturity, milestones, grid-friendliness).
