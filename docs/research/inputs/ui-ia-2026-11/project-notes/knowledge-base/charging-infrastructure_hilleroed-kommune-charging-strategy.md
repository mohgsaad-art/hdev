## 1. Hillerød Kommune Kører På El (Hillerød Kommune Runs on Electricity)
- Framing: cars and buses in the municipality should be electric-powered to contribute to reducing CO2 and air-pollution emissions from road transport.
- Notes the EV market is growing rapidly, driven by major global car manufacturers, indicating EVs will drive the green transition in the Danish car market in coming years.
- **Byrådet (city council) has adopted a Klimastrategi (Climate Strategy)** for Hillerød Kommune, with **climate-friendly transport as one of four focus areas**. Vision: **Hillerød Kommune has a fossil-free transport sector by 2050**, and will continue working to limit CO2 emissions from the municipality as an organization/employer.
- **June 2020:** byrådet decided, in connection with a case on tourist-bus drop-off/stop locations, that "the administration shall work on a proposal to make Hillerød a particularly attractive city for EVs, with charging infrastructure and possibly reserved parking spaces through private and municipal investments respectively."
- **October 2020:** Arkitektur, Byplan og Trafikudvalget (Architecture, City Planning and Traffic Committee) received a presentation from **Dansk Elbil Alliance** on how Hillerød Kommune could contribute to EV adoption, and decided the administration should develop a charging strategy — this document is the resulting proposal.
- **December 2020:** a majority in the Folketing (Danish Parliament) reached an ambitious agreement on green transition of road transport, targeting **1 million green cars on Danish roads by 2030**, supporting the **70% CO2 reduction target by 2030 (vs. 1990)**, and an end to sales of diesel/petrol cars by 2030. As of the document's writing, **fewer than 10 in 100 Danes** had chosen an EV; by 2030 the intention is **100 in 100 cars sold** should run on electricity.
- Market expectation (FDM and Dansk Elbil Alliance): **2021** would be the year EVs began meaningfully gaining traction — EVs made up little of new registrations until 2020, then began rising significantly in H2 2020, reaching **15% of new car sales in Q4 2020**.

**Hillerød's local EV numbers:**
- **26,645 cars** in Hillerød Kommune today (at time of writing), of which **269 are EVs/plug-in hybrids**, corresponding to **1.01% of private cars** — roughly at the national average.
- To carry its proportional share of the national 1-million-EV target by 2030, **Dansk Elbil Alliance and DTU estimate Hillerød Kommune will have 9,260 EVs, corresponding to 35% of all private cars**, plus vehicles owned by public/private businesses.

## 2. Behov for Lade-Infrastruktur (Need for Charging Infrastructure)
- Denmark has good general conditions for well-developed charging infrastructure (country size, high home-charging access, relatively short distances, well-developed grid), but flags focus areas: adequate fast/rapid chargers along the state road network for cross-country travel, and the fact that **25% cannot charge on their own land** (national figure cited in this section).
- **Local analysis (Dansk Elbil Alliance and DTU, based on the Transportvaneundersøgelsen — Danish National Travel Survey):** Hillerød's driving demand/car ownership is slightly above the national average. **>7 in 10 (i.e., 75%) of car owners in Hillerød Kommune have access to home parking** (carport etc.) — so **75% can charge at home**, while **23% must charge at shared parking areas** attached to their home (e.g., a housing association), which thus bears significant responsibility for providing charging. The remaining **2%** must find other charging options — "destination chargers" tied to the urban environment, e.g., along major roads/motorways, at dedicated charging stations (petrol-station-like or charge parks), at the workplace, while visiting, or during shopping/leisure activities.
- **DTU estimate for 2030 (based on driving/charging needs and future EVs with average range of 400–500 km):** **196 charge points needed in Hillerød Kommune by 2030**, at residential properties, workplaces, public roads, and petrol/charging stations, split:
  - **85% normalladere (11/22 kW)**
  - **10% hurtigladere (50 kW)**
  - **5% lynladere (>150 kW)**
- Exact future siting not yet determined; general principle: site chargers where people park/spend the most time. Per the DEA/DTU analysis (excluding home charging), the breakdown of away-from-home parking-time destinations:
  - **Workplace: 57%** of away-from-home parking time (largest share)
  - **Family visits and shopping: combined 18%**
  - **Entertainment (cinema, restaurant, etc.) and sport/leisure facilities: combined 8%**
- Excluding family visits (arguably a form of "home charging, just at someone else's home"), the key parking locations for charging-infrastructure planning are: **workplaces, shopping, entertainment, and sport/leisure facilities**.
- **Current charging supply (per elbilviden.dk):** Hillerød Kommune has **8 publicly accessible charge stands** with multiple outlets each, corresponding to **~20 charge points** — meaning **10x more charge stands are needed** than exist today. **7 charge stands are in Hillerød by (town)**, **1 in Ny Hammersholt** (per Figur 1, an image map — exact plotted locations not extractable as text beyond this summary).

### Current Legislative Constraints on Municipalities
- Municipalities today have **limited ability** to influence charging-infrastructure rollout. **Kommunalfuldmagten** (the Municipal Authority Doctrine) does **not** permit municipalities to offer free charging or co-finance publicly accessible charge stands. Municipalities can only **point to sites** where private charging providers wish to install infrastructure — meaning the current market-driven rollout risks **structural, planning, and social imbalance**, since private providers have limited motivation to invest in e.g. smaller towns/rural areas. Municipalities can currently only establish their **own** charging infrastructure for their **own** EVs.
- **Planloven** also does **not** allow municipalities to require a number of charge spaces in municipal/local plans, so municipalities cannot directly influence charger numbers/EV promotion this way — they can currently only **plan and prepare** for charging infrastructure.
- There is **no "ladepligt"** (charging obligation/duty) in Denmark. Municipalities may reserve public road-area parking spaces for EVs/plug-in hybrids but **cannot require the car to actually be charging** while parked there — so charge spaces are often used just for parking, not charging.
- **Kommunernes Landsforening (KL)** and many municipalities are therefore calling for legislation giving municipalities more "levers," including special signage powers, requirements to charge/connect while using a charge space, and better sanctioning options. **Dansk Elbil Alliance and FDM** propose EV spaces be **time-limited via traditional signage and parking discs, with a total 3-hour limit**, but **not applying between 19:00 and 08:00** — so a driver parking at, say, 16:00 (end of typical workday) would not need to move the car until 08:00 the next day (or 11:00 if parked at 19:00).
- Under current rules, municipalities/the state are largely prevented from establishing EV charging infrastructure themselves — installation is bound to private charging providers. **Exception:** municipalities may, after a specific assessment, install charge stands for vehicles used by the municipality itself during work hours, and sell **surplus capacity** on market terms.
- **Possible model described:** charge stands at a lot can be split between dedicated charge spaces for municipal service vehicles and, right next to them, publicly accessible chargers from established private operators. The municipality could fund the underground investment for its own vehicles' chargers, letting private market operators bid on operating/installing the above-ground chargers — those operators would then handle billing for the municipality's electricity use and the commercial activity of selling surplus power to citizens.
- Despite this partial workaround, the document states a **legislative change is still needed as soon as possible**, since municipalities are being asked to support the green transition while citizens/politicians increasingly request more charge stands.

### Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen — Requirements (as described in this document)
- A "lade-bekendtgørelse" under the Bygningsreglementet (Building Regulations) took effect **March 2020**, obligating **larger businesses (including municipalities) with more than 250 employees** to install charge stands for buildings/construction:
  - In **existing** non-residential buildings with **at least 20 parking spaces**: at least **1 charge outlet by 2025**
  - In existing buildings with **at least 10 parking spaces**, undergoing **major renovation**: at least **1 charge outlet**
  - For **new construction** with at least **10 parking spaces**: at least **1 charge outlet**, and remaining parking spaces prepared for future charger installation
- **Hillerød Kommune has 44 buildings with over 20 parking spaces**, obligating the municipality to install 1 charge stand at each **before 2025**, spread across the municipality; **Ejendomme (Properties department)** handles operation/maintenance incl. snow clearance for these buildings (marked in Figur 2, an image map not extractable as text beyond this description).
- Charging-infrastructure installation at these sites is expected to occur gradually alongside municipal fleet vehicle replacement and new construction/major renovations — but the document flags a concern: since current law does **not** allow municipal-building chargers to also be used by the public or municipal employees generally (only the specific building's assigned vehicles), a municipality could end up legally obligated to install a charger at, e.g., a school with **no EV present**, resulting in an idle/unused charge space despite high installation costs. The Executive Order stems from an EU directive apparently aimed at private businesses/their employees, not public institutions — **Kommunernes Landsforening expects the rules to be revised.**
- The Executive Order also affects large private businesses, retail, and cultural institutions — some of which overlap with municipal facilities (libraries, sports halls). Figur 2 depicts where requirements apply and where market-driven charger demand (e.g., from shopping-center/hotel/conference-center customers) is expected.
- Named large local employers already voluntarily installing chargers: **Novo Nordisk, Fujifilm, and Foss** — all cited as already having installed **22 kW (slow) charge stands**, tied to their CSR strategies.
- Multiple stakeholders suggest municipalities should roll out charging infrastructure at a carefully measured pace, prioritizing locations where EVs are actually present, given the mismatch between the Executive Order's building-based trigger and actual municipal fleet EV presence — described as a **"behovsbestemt udrulning"** (needs-based rollout) approach.

## 3. Kommunerne Får Ny Rolle og Større Indflydelse — Forventning om Ny Lovgivning pr. 1. jan 2022 (Municipalities Get a New Role — Expected New Legislation from 1 Jan 2022)
- **Kommissionen for grøn omstilling af personbiler** (the "Bilkommissionen"/Car Commission), established **2019**, tasked with a concrete plan to realize the 1-million-EV-by-2030 target while addressing financing (offsetting lost car-related tax revenue).
- Report's four themes (this summary omits the fourth, congestion charging, as not relevant to Hillerød):
  1. Model for rolling out charge stands in public space
  2. Price of charging
  3. Intelligent/smart charging
  4. (Congestion — omitted here)
- The Commission does **not** expect the public sector to substitute for/spend heavily on charging infrastructure — expects the market to largely handle it in dialogue with public road authorities. Denmark's favorable conditions (size, short distances, developed grid, high home-charging access) are noted, alongside the same focus areas (adequate fast/rapid chargers on the state road network; **1 in 4 cannot charge on own land**).

### Anbefalinger fra Bilkommissionen (Commission Recommendations)
- **Offentlig styring (Public governance):** road authorities should get a significantly larger role — requiring changes to **Kommunalfuldmagten** and **Planloven** so municipalities can (a) set charging-infrastructure requirements in local/municipal plans, and (b) the state/municipalities can run tenders with possible municipal subsidy in areas lacking market interest.
- **Tendering as the key mechanism:** ensures transparency, fairness, increased competition. Municipal urban space should **not** be provided for free — tenders let the state/municipalities make public space available for payment, with subsidy where needed depending on commercial site attractiveness; space goes to the bidder paying most or requiring least subsidy. Standardized tender terms/conditions would ensure market requirements, roaming, uptime, data access, prices, etc. are consistent nationally.
- **New depreciation model proposed:** currently the whole installation is typically depreciated over just a few years matching the contract period. Proposed: **underground infrastructure depreciated over 50 years** (matching expected lifespan), while **above-ground infrastructure continues to be depreciated over 5–10 years**.
- Proposes a **national knowledge center** to capture experience/knowledge on tenders and terms, advising on tender-material design and conditions for the state/municipalities.
- Commission does **not** expect municipalities to invest significantly themselves in charging infrastructure, but to **plan and tender** — which they currently lack legal authority to do; broader powers needed since municipalities hold the local knowledge to manage the process via market dialogue.
- **Parking policy concern:** charging facilities are often used just for parking, undermining private operators' business cases. The Commission cites **Norway/Oslo's challenges**: free parking and bus-lane access for EVs became popular but are hard to roll back once citizens are used to them, and have caused bus-lane congestion given Norway's high EV penetration. Recommendation: **parking fees should be uniform regardless of drivetrain**, and charge spaces should be used for charging, not parking — preserving parking revenue as EV numbers rise. Conclusion: EV owners should **not** have overly favorable conditions, since that would shift more trips to cars (incl. EVs) and away from public transit, worsening congestion; **parking becomes an even more important municipal task**.
- **Employer charging:** Commission proposes municipalities get legal authority to tender charging for employees, including at public workplaces — a large workplace should have chargers just as staff have canteen access, with the same terms at e.g. a rådhus (town hall) as at a private employer. Employee charging billed at market terms.
- **Motorway rapid charging:** Commission identifies a need for **950 rapid chargers** on the primary road network for cross-country travel, proposing **Vejdirektoratet** (Road Directorate) begin tendering now to be ready by 2030. Notes rapid/ultra-rapid chargers are expensive to install, but expects market interest, especially as petrol stations enter the market (reducing the need for public investment, since they already own the land). **A rapid charger costs 1 million DKK today** (per the source, "koster en lynlader en mio. kr.").
- **Tariffs, the grid, and charging (Commission recommendations):**
  - Charging should become **markedly cheaper**; the state should ensure low electricity duty on charging power regardless of housing type/operator.
  - Recommends **reducing the electricity duty from the standard 90 øre/kWh to 0.8 øre/kWh for home charging**, implemented via sub-meters or a schematic solution (similar to the existing electric-heating duty model).
  - Recommends **extending the reduced 0.4 øre/kWh commercial-service charging duty (e.g., via charging providers) to 2030.**
  - Recommends requiring chargers be "intelligent," drawing power when grid capacity allows — but grid regulation should be considered alongside other sectors (heat pumps, data centers, etc.), since transport's share of overall grid demand growth is described as surprisingly small relative to other drivers. Grid should be expanded flexibly; charging should be managed intelligently so EV charging doesn't coincide with the existing afternoon/early-evening consumption peak. Mechanisms: dynamic tariffs and smart home chargers/control, so even if a car is plugged in in the afternoon, the home charger ensures it only actually charges late evening/night when capacity is available and demand/price is low.

### Expected Legislative Timing and Government Infrastructure Plan
- The Commission hoped/expected new legislation from **1 January 2022** giving municipalities new powers; uncertain whether the electricity-duty reduction would have political majority by that date, but a marked duty reduction was expected to significantly boost EV uptake.
- **Government's "Danmark fremad – Infrastrukturplan 2035"** followed up on many Commission recommendations:
  - Proposes an overall plan for EV charging-infrastructure rollout
  - **Allocates 0.5 billion DKK (0,5 mia. kr.) through 2030** for charging infrastructure along the state road network — equivalent to **~50 charge parks with ~630 rapid-charge spaces**, somewhat short (320 rapid-charge spaces short) of the Commission's recommended 950. **Vejdirektoratet assesses that 99.9% of long trips on the state road network can be completed in an EV without detour**, and that waiting time to charge will not exceed **10 minutes** in the year's 100th-busiest hour.
  - Proposes a **tender strategy for charging-infrastructure rollout along the state road network through 2025.**
- **Government proposals aligned with Commission recommendations:**
  - Regulation ensuring charging-infrastructure operators can only be granted rights to install on public land via **announcement/tender**
  - Investigation into whether Planloven should give municipalities legal basis to require charge stands
  - Analysis of whether public employers should be able to facilitate charging infrastructure for employees
- **Further state+municipal proposals:**
  - Standardized tender requirements for charging infrastructure on public land
  - **Roaming access** for EV owners with an e-mobility provider agreement, across all charge stands on public land nationally
  - Investigation into adjustments to the electricity duty for household EV charging
- The government's infrastructure-plan announcement of forthcoming legislation is described as positive, either directly resolving or pointing to concrete investigation of many current municipal charging-infrastructure problems.

- **Public transit (bus) electrification:** the regional transit company **Movia** (Hillerød's own operator) has set targets for **fossil-free bus operation by 2030**, setting functional CO2/NOx/particulate-emission requirements in upcoming bus-line tenders if the municipality supports this.
- Byrådet has a **standing decision favoring emission-free (electric or hydrogen) bus operation** when Movia next tenders bus lines, alongside the 2020 climate cooperation agreement commitment — decided case-by-case per tender given budget constraints. **October 2020:** byrådet decided **line 335** (a cross-municipal line) should be tendered with an emission-free requirement. **October 2021:** byrådet was to decide climate/environment requirements for the tender of **bus lines 301-303, 305, 307, and 337** (operations starting Oct/Dec 2023) — significant for the municipality's public-transit CO2 accounting since these bus lines represent **¾ of all scheduled service hours** in the municipality.

## 5. Målsætninger og Strategiske Indsatser (Targets and Strategic Initiatives)
Proposed targets/initiatives (verbatim structure):
- **Adopt the Bilkommissionen's recommendations:** given the government's announced forthcoming legislation, the administration proposes the municipality plan to be able to tender urban-space charging infrastructure and co-finance charge stands (where no commercial market exists) from **2022**, and to set charging-infrastructure requirements in local/municipal plans once legally permitted.
- **"Behovsbestemt implementering" (needs-based implementation) of the Executive Order:** rather than installing charge stands at all 44 legally-obligated addresses by 2025 regardless of actual EV presence, the administration proposes implementing based on **actual need** at municipal buildings, rolling out as an initial starting point — avoiding investment in charging infrastructure that would sit unused.
- **Fleet and public transit conversion:** reaffirms the byråd's existing commitments (zero-emission new leasing/purchasing; ≥75% CO2-neutral/zero-emission new vehicles) — proposes that **whenever technologically feasible to convert a vehicle to electric, it should be done**. For public transit, commits to emission-free requirements for city buses in all new tenders, and emission-free or CO2-neutral requirements for local/cross-municipal buses (biogas/biodiesel as CO2-neutral alternatives). Notes the **October 2021 decision on bus lines 301-303, 305, 307, 337** as an immediate opportunity to act on this.
- **Proposed capital-budget request for Budget 2022 — chargers for the municipal fleet through 2025:** based on the existing fleet of **124 vehicles** (personbiler + varebiler) as of **early April 2021** — **20 already electric**, rest on petrol/diesel/hybrid. Tractors, mopeds, and trailers **excluded** at this stage (very sparse EV supply in these segments); the municipality's **17 minibuses (up to 9 persons)** also excluded for now.
  - Of the **73 leased** person/van vehicles, **62 personbiler + 4 varebiler (66 total)** expire in **2021-2024** and should convert to EV if technology/price allow.
  - For the **51 municipally-owned** vehicles, proposes ongoing conversion to **100% electric** as departments approach Indkøb for replacement vehicles.
  - Of the 66 expiring leased vehicles, **20 are already EVs** (with chargers already installed) — leaving a need for chargers for **46 new vehicles in 2021-2024**, if all convert to EV. Of these 46, **10 leasing agreements expire in 2021 specifically** — converting these requires installing chargers at **5 addresses within the current year**. **No budget was allocated in 2021 for charger installation**, so the administration points to deciding financing in **BOF2 or BOF3** (budget process rounds — Danish municipal budget-negotiation stages, not further defined in the extracted text).
  - Proposes byrådet direct the administration to prepare a capital-budget request for Budget 2022 covering chargers for the remaining vehicles plus funds for technical charger-procurement assistance.
- **Proposed capital-budget request for Budget 2022 — mapping publicly accessible charge-stand locations:** initial 2030 analysis points to **~200 charge stands needed**, but exact siting undetermined. Proposes a **200,000 DKK budget line** for mapping where publicly accessible charging infrastructure should be sited, including which charger types (normal/fast/rapid) suit a North Zealand/Hillerød context — so the municipality is ready when legislation changes (expected per Commission recommendations/government proposals) from **1 January 2022**. Notes the Commission's urgency message: municipalities should not wait until 2023 to plan, but begin as soon as possible if the 1-million-EV-by-2030 national target is to be met.
- **Municipality as communicator:** proposes the municipality inform residents that updated public-charger information is available via **elbilviden.dk** and charging-provider apps.

## 6. Handleplan (Action Plan)
- Proposed responsibility split across committees:
  1. **Municipal fleet conversion and tendering** → Økonomiudvalget (Finance Committee) / **Indkøb** (Procurement)
  2. **Charging infrastructure at municipal buildings/properties** → Økonomiudvalget / **Ejendomme** (Properties)
  3. **Siting of publicly accessible charge stands, building-authority tasks at new-build/renovation, and public transit conversion** → **Arkitektur, Byplan og Trafikudvalget** (Architecture, City Planning and Traffic Committee) / **By og Miljø** (City and Environment):
     - a. Public charge stands
     - b. Public transit
     - c. Building-authority tasks at new construction/renovation
- **Natur, Miljø og Klimaudvalget** (Nature, Environment and Climate Committee) proposed to be kept informed and to comment before matters go to the Architecture/Traffic Committee, Finance Committee, and byråd.
- Coordination flagged as important since Executive Order compliance is anchored with **Ejendomme**, in dialogue with **Indkøb** and **By og Miljø** — because charger installation at municipal buildings follows needs-based implementation tied to which department/decentralized institution has/gets EVs, and must be considered alongside publicly accessible chargers, since rules are expected to change to allow public access to municipal-building chargers too.

### Competitive Tendering of Vehicles and Charger Installation
- Proposes an **EU tender for a framework agreement on passenger/van vehicles**, since experience shows it's advantageous to acquire vehicles in larger pools rather than one at a time — as seen with the **18-EV home-care tender in summer 2020**.
- Proposes a **combined quote/tender process for charger installation at municipal buildings**, using the **same supplier throughout 2021-2024**. The supplier should help plan the rollout in close cooperation with Ejendomme and Indkøb, ensure the solution is future-proofed, and advise on installing chargers in **pools rather than individually**.

### Internal Working Group
- Proposes establishing an internal working group anchored in **Indkøb and Ejendomme**, plus a representative from each department with one or more vehicles — ensuring cross-departmental dialogue on needs and a platform for discussing fleet standardization, incl. safety and working-environment requirements.
