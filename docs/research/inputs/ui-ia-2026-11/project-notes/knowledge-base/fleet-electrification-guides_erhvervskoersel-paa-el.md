## English Summary (as given verbatim in source, condensed)

Cities and regions are increasingly incorporating zero-emission vehicles into their fleets, setting procurement requirements, and partnering with the private sector to demonstrate real-world use. With this report, the Capital Region of Denmark aims to demonstrate that electric vans are fully usable in everyday business operations with a reasonable TCO, making them an advantageous choice for many companies.

- All 8 participating companies were interviewed; companies are mainly in handcraft/trade and service businesses (electricians, plumbers, couriers) plus one biotech company.
- The first vehicle was commissioned in **September 2015**; vehicle numbers grew substantially during 2018; the last three vehicles were delivered in **February 2020**.
- **51 vehicles total**, three models: **Garia Utility City EC (32 vehicles)**, **Nissan e-NV200 (16 vehicles)**, **MAN eTGE (3 vehicles)**.
- Subsidy: **20,000 DKK per vehicle**, serving as substantial financial leverage.
- **Conclusion (English, verbatim substance):** Private companies are not moving faster toward green fleet alternatives than what is financially worthwhile. Most fleet owners require an EV business case close to that of conventional fossil-fuel operations. The positive business case depends on lower running costs (propellant/fuel costs, driven by mileage need), but liquidity is still highly affected by higher investment costs — hence continued need for subsidy schemes like this one while technology develops and the market matures.

## The Subsidy Scheme

- Funded via **Energistyrelsens elbilpulje** (the Danish Energy Agency's EV subsidy pool) for a demonstration project with large-scale potential.
- Primarily aimed at trade/craft businesses ("håndværkervirksomheder").
- **20,000 DKK per vehicle**, conditional on the company being available for interview (providing experience/operational data for the analysis) and having a genuine incentive effect ("tilskyndelseseffekt") from the subsidy.
- Motivation for the project: high environmental burden of commercial vans (frequent cold starts, poor diesel efficiency per liter). Region Hovedstaden applied for support under Energistyrelsen's EV pool specifically for this demonstration.
- Companies use various financing methods (cash purchase, operational or financial leasing); the higher upfront cost of EVs affects other financing models too.

## Environmental / Rationale Framing

- The three "winning" factors for company adoption identified in the conclusion: reduced running costs from (1) cheaper propellant/energy, (2) lower biannual owner tax (halvårlig ejerafgift), alongside (3) the subsidy — together giving companies economic room to choose the greener option.
- The report notes the EV market developed substantially during the project period — greater vehicle supply, longer range, new technologies — meaning some newer EVs available at time of report-writing would fit companies' needs significantly better than the earliest vehicles used.

## Participating Companies (8) — Brief Profiles

All eight companies were interviewed (phone and written correspondence); longer descriptions are in the report's appendices (bilag 1-8), summarized below including their fact-box (faktaboks) data.

### 1. AFA JCDecaux (AFA) — bilag 1
- **Industry:** Service and maintenance (advertising equipment at bus stops, shelters, train stations).
- **Operating area:** Selected routes in Copenhagen, shorter distances.
- **Vehicles from project:** 7 Nissan e-NV200.
- **Key contact:** Tonny Pagter, Environmental and Quality Manager.
- Has achieved **100% CO2-neutral status for service driving in Copenhagen** through fleet conversion.
- No fixed timeline for full fleet conversion — depends on market/technology pace; still has crane/truck vehicles and higher-mileage-need vehicles that can't yet convert.
- Also participates in related projects: **V2E** and **ProEME** (with CPH Electric as project lead).
- **Top 3 advantages:** (1) Lower maintenance costs, (2) "Greener," (3) Satisfies customers.
- **Top 3 disadvantages:** (1) Lack of larger vehicles, (2) Range, (3) — (none listed).
- Advice to others: test EVs for commercial use, watch for subsidy opportunities.

### 2. Bring — bilag 2
- **Industry:** Logistics and parcel delivery (Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland).
- **Operating area:** Nationwide (Denmark); part of larger Nordic operation.
- **Vehicles from project:** 3 (of 4 ordered MAN eTGE; total Danish EV fleet reaches 5 with these).
- **Total company-wide EV count:** ~1,400 EVs group-wide (mostly Norway) — Bring declined to give national/other-country vehicle totals.
- **Key contact:** Distribution Manager Johnnie Guldbrandt.
- Group-wide goal: **CO2-neutral by 2025.**
- Norway is the Nordic frontrunner within the group; Danish fleet moving more slowly, constrained by needing large vans (up to 18 m³ cargo space) where range remains challenged.
- Vehicles expected to run ~25,000 km/year, primarily city driving; in operation ~250 days/year.
- **Top 3 advantages:** (1) Environmental consideration, (2) Safety, (3) Comfort.
- **Top 3 disadvantages:** (1) Range, (2) Costs, (3) Organizing charging stations at the site.
- Advice: remember all costs when calculating TCO; test multiple models; involve drivers in the project ("listen to the person who'll use the hammer").

### 3. GoEnergi — bilag 3
- **Industry:** Electrical installation specializing in infrastructure for electric transport (delivers many of Denmark's public charging station installations).
- **Operating area:** All of Denmark, mostly Zealand.
- **Vehicles from project:** 1 Nissan e-NV200 (first company to receive a project vehicle — September 2015; 36-month lease ended, vehicle returned autumn 2018).
- **Since then:** acquired a Hyundai KONA for staff/functionary tasks; larger diesel vans still awaited replacement pending longer-range EV options.
- **Key contact:** Owner Jan Darville.
- Challenges: unpredictable daily driving needs (sudden call-outs requiring many km); no towbar option; wanted faster charging.
- **Top 3 advantages:** (1) "Feeling"/driving experience, (2) Easy, (3) Cheap to operate.
- **Top 3 disadvantages:** (1) Range (box vans), (2) Purchase price (box vans), (3) Lack of towbar.
- Advice: test it, try what's currently possible, be positive that daily routines may need slight adjustment.

### 4. Gubra — bilag 4

- **Operating area:** Driving (kørsel) — general.
- **Employees:** 175.
- **Vehicles from project:** 1 Nissan e-NV200.
- **Key contact:** CFO Jan K. Villadsen.
- Gubra earmarks **10% of company profit** for sustainable-purpose investments (not exclusively EVs, but sustainability generally).
- No specific challenges/disadvantages reported; no customer/service visits requiring the vehicle intensively.
- **Top 3 advantages:** (1) Fewer emissions, (2) Sustainable, (3) Less maintenance.
- **Top 3 disadvantages:** None listed (all three rows say "Ingen"/None).
- Advice: "Just do it" — though Jan notes a desire for longer-term EV legislation/rules to allow planning beyond 1-2 years.

### 5. HOFOR — bilag 5
- **Industry:** Utility company (water, wastewater, district heating, town gas, district cooling; also builds wind turbines) serving Copenhagen and **7 surrounding municipalities: Vallensbæk, Hvidovre, Dragør, Rødovre, Albertslund, Herlev, and Brøndby**.
- **Employees:** 1,200.
- **Vehicles from project:** 3 Nissan e-NV200; **36 EVs in HOFOR's total fleet**.
- **Typical replacement cycle:** 96 months.
- **Key contact:** Buyer (Indkøber) Kenneth Bergstrøm.
- Transition described as "overwhelmingly positive," short adaptation period, quick positive employee feedback.
- Benefits: green image boost, noticeable operating-cost savings, reduced emissions, better working environment (lower noise).
- Challenge noted: charging speed limitations on 230V/1-2 phase supply.
- HOFOR's policy suggestions to policymakers: maintain current tax/duty levels on all parameters; EV-only traffic lanes in inner cities; free city parking for EVs; roaming requirements across charge-subscription providers; raise the standard driving-license total-weight limit to **4,200 kg** (as in Germany) to allow more van models on regular licenses.
- Wants: towbar-capable EVs, **minimum 40 kWh battery in all vans**, more special-purpose vehicles (e.g., flatbeds).
- **Top 3 advantages:** (1) Environment, (2) Operating costs, (3) Comfort.
- **Top 3 disadvantages:** (1) Lack of towbar, (2) Charging stations at apartment complexes, (3) None.
- Advice: "Buy the electric vehicle — you'll be surprised by the range, comfort, and low operating costs."

### 6. IntoPit — bilag 6
- **Industry:** Vehicle workshop/leasing company for commercial driving; converted 32 vehicles under this project.
- **Operating area:** Nationwide.
- **Employees:** ~30.
- **Vehicles from project:** 32 Garia Utility City EC (golf-cart-sized vehicles with flatbed/cage for service tasks) — a **segment downsize** from their previous vehicle, the **Fiat Doblò Work Up** (a small van with flatbed body).
- Reported no real challenges; happy with acquisition; main benefit: avoiding fuel and workshop bills.
- Wants EV taxes removed, and state subsidies to continue until manufacturers bring EV purchase prices down to conventional levels.
- **Top 3 advantages:** (1) Environment, (2) Good advertising, (3) Economy.
- **Top 3 disadvantages:** (1) Needs charging, (2) Low top speed, (3) Unknown resale value.
- Advice: "Take the leap."

### 7. Krüdal — bilag 7
- **Industry:** VVS (plumbing/HVAC) company, based in Greve, operating across the Greater Copenhagen area.
- **Employees:** 6 (1 EV + 5 diesel vehicles).
- **Vehicles from project:** 1 Nissan e-NV200, received subsidy **June 2017**, still in use by co-owner Max Krüger for Copenhagen-area tasks.
- Charging has become a growing challenge: rapid EV adoption by the **taxi industry** has intensified competition for public charging spots in Copenhagen/Frederiksberg, since the growth in public chargers hasn't kept pace with growth in EVs.
- No concrete plans to convert more of the fleet at present, due to range and the need for larger vans among the other 5 tradespeople (who cover other parts of Greater Copenhagen and need more range).
- Requirement for further conversion: remaining vehicles need **minimum 300 km mixed-driving range on a winter day**.
- **Top 3 advantages:** (1) Easier to get around the city + parking options, (2) Better operating economics, (3) Better driving comfort and less noise.
- **Top 3 disadvantages:** (1) Limited range, (2) Difficult to access charging (taxis take spots), (3) —.
- Advice: it must always make economic sense to choose an EV — "if I have no economic advantage from driving electric, we'll switch to diesel."

### 8. Røder & Mortensen (R&M) — bilag 8
- **Industry:** Electrical installation company (elinstallatør) with **more than 100 years of experience**, based in Copenhagen; specializes in advisory services on energy optimization.
- **Operating area:** Capital Region including North Zealand and Roskilde.
- **Employees:** 50 (28 vehicles in the fleet).
- **Vehicles from project:** 3 Nissan e-NV200; **5 EVs total** in fleet.
- **Typical replacement cycle:** every 5 years.
- **Key contact:** Managing Director Michael Holm-Christensen.
- Initial challenges: convincing employees it would work; long delivery time (hard to find a supplier); difficulty finding a suitable charging solution.
- Main ongoing challenge: uncertainty about charger availability at customer sites (occupied or too far from where work is performed), requiring more planning.
- **Top 3 advantages:** (1) Easy, (2) Comfort, (3) Better for local environment.
- **Top 3 disadvantages:** (1) The beginning is hard, (2) Charging solutions can be confusing, (3) Supplier information is confusing.
- Advice: "Get started" — no reason to wait.

## Cross-Company Summary (Opsamling)

Common threads across all 8 companies:
- Positive transition experience: happier employees, better driving comfort, no fuel bills.
- Companies feel shared responsibility for reducing greenhouse gases/air pollution and are motivated to convert further as feasible.
- Range remains a shared concern (though severity varies by company); companies are broadly in a "wait and see" position tracking technology/market maturity, especially for the largest/heaviest vehicle classes, where suitable models remain hard to find.
- Companies want more long-term political planning so costs are predictable across a full operating period.
- Companies driving in larger cities report public charging-station growth has not kept pace with EV growth.
- Overall message to other interested companies: get started and test it out.

## Methodology

- Results are organized in three sections: (1) climate/environmental emissions accounting, (2) socioeconomic costs (samfundsøkonomiske omkostninger, "SØ") of those emissions, (3) economic projections in a TCO (Total Cost of Ownership) perspective. **SØ-costs are shown as a standalone line but excluded from the TCO total**, since TCO only includes costs directly attributable to the companies.
- For Nissan e-NV200 and MAN eTGE, a direct comparison is made against their respective fossil-fuel counterparts (Nissan NV200, MAN TGE). For Garia Utility City EC, the comparison is instead a **segment downsize** — Garia is a golf-cart-sized electric service vehicle now doing work IntoPit's Fiat Doblò Work Up (small flatbed van) used to do. Garia's price roughly matched a Nissan e-NV200's at the time, but its smaller size makes it more energy-efficient (**7.75 km/kWh** vs. e-NV200's **5.34 km/kWh**). The report explicitly states this segment downsize gives a **disproportionate advantage to the electric scenario** in the fuel-cost comparison versus a strict one-to-one same-segment comparison, but argues it better reflects the real substitution that occurred than a purely theoretical same-segment comparison would.

### Calculation assumptions and data sources
- Climate/environmental effect calculations based on:
  - **Energistyrelsens samfundsøkonomiske beregningsforudsætninger 2019** (Danish Energy Agency's 2019 socioeconomic calculation assumptions)
  - **DTU Transportøkonomiske Enhedspriser v1.912** (DTU Transport Economic Unit Prices, version 1.9)
  - **Alternative Drivmidler 3.0** (Alternative Fuels 3.0 — a named calculation tool/dataset)
- These same sources are used for the socioeconomic cost (externality) calculations.
- Economic calculations use **list prices** from respective dealers, collected **end of December 2019**. Companies finance fleets via varied methods (purchase, operational/financial leasing); list prices represent total economic value regardless of financing method.
- Fuel/energy, service, and maintenance costs use DTU Transport Economic Unit Prices; insurance costs assumed unchanged between scenarios.
- Costs calculated over an **8-year period**, with vehicles depreciated via the **declining-balance method at 25% per year**.
- Electric-scenario investment costs include a **7,000 DKK per vehicle** charging-station cost.
- Service/maintenance costs **do not** account for battery wear/degradation — could favor the electric scenario somewhat, but expected to be a small effect over 8 years and not considered material.
- **Not included:** the socioeconomic benefit of significantly lower noise emissions from EVs — flagged by the report as a point for further consideration, not quantified here.
- **Footnoted sources (links not fully resolved in extraction, cited as-is):** DTU Transportøkonomiske Enhedspriser; Energistyrelsens samfundsøkonomiske beregningsforudsætninger 2019; Alternative Drivmidler 3.0.

## Emissions Results (Well-to-Wheels)

- Framing: emissions from fuel/energy **production** are "upstream emissions" (opstrømsemissioner / Well-to-Tank, WtT); emissions **during driving** are "downstream emissions" (nedstrømsemissioner / Tank-to-Wheel, TtW). Together: Well-to-Wheels (WtW).
- Climate emissions CO2, CH4, and N2O are combined into **CO2-equivalents (CO2-eq)**. Note: the "Alternative Drivmidler" fossil-fuel dataset does not break out the three climate-emission types separately for fossil fuels.
- Calculations **exclude vehicle life-cycle emissions** (e.g., manufacturing), focusing only on fuel/energy production and driving emissions.

## Socioeconomic Costs (SØ) of Emissions

- Socioeconomic costs in the transport sector are based on how emissions affect public health — factored by illness, lost earning capacity, and mortality attributable to the emissions.
- **CO2 (tons) and PM2.5 (particulates under 2.5 micrograms, in kg) represent the largest health risk per unit emitted.**

## Payback / Break-Even Findings

- For each reference-scenario pair, the classic TCO pattern holds: higher investment cost for the EV, but better marginal (running) economics for all three EV scenarios.
- **Break-even timing** (when cumulative EV costs undercut cumulative diesel costs), based on stated purchase-as-cash-payment calculations:
  - **Nissan e-NV200 vs. Nissan NV200:** break-even after just under **6 years** (with 20,000 km/year usage).
  - **MAN eTGE vs. MAN TGE:** break-even after approximately **3 years and 8 months** (with 25,000 km/year usage).
  - **Garia Utility City EC vs. Fiat Doblò Work Up:** break-even after approximately the **same ~3 years 8 months timeframe**, but at a lower annual need of **12,000 km/year**.
- Break-even timing depends on how long companies keep EVs in operation and how many km are driven in that period: time alone determines tax/service-maintenance savings, while time combined with driving need determines fuel/energy cost savings (greater driving need = greater savings potential).
- The economic case is calculated as **cash purchase**; the report notes that including financing costs would have a marginal effect on overall TCO and would delay total savings by "a few months."
- Figures 10 and 11 show cost projections over 8 years for the combined scenario (with residual value included) and per-model scenario (residual value excluded), axis 0 to 25,000,000 DKK (combined) and 0 to 12,000,000 DKK (per-model).

## Conclusion (Key Points)

- Danish companies broadly are not yet at a stage of large-scale or self-driven adoption of a "larger chapter" of green fleet transition — the project succeeded in introducing companies to hands-on EV testing under normal operations, which was broadly successful; driving enjoyment is high and running costs lower.
- Fuel/energy savings depend on vehicle size/consumption per km and individual driving need.
- Biannual owner tax also contributes to operational savings and depends on vehicle size — **electric vans pay the lowest biannual owner tax rate regardless of size**: both the small Nissan e-NV200 and the large MAN eTGE pay **330 DKK** biannually, while their diesel counterparts (Nissan NV200 and MAN TGE) pay **2,330 DKK** and **6,560 DKK** respectively.
- The 20,000 DKK per-vehicle subsidy has "taken the edge off" higher investment costs, without fully equalizing the purchase-price gap versus a comparable petrol/diesel model. Overall costs across the period are reduced — aided by project subsidy and assuming the electric model retains a higher residual value than its fossil counterpart.
- Companies vary in vehicle turnover frequency and driving needs; using the stated prices/driving needs, an 8-year projection shows: Nissan e-NV200 needs **6 years in operation at 20,000 km/year** to become cheaper than its fossil counterpart; MAN eTGE needs **3 years 8 months at 25,000 km/year**; the Garia/Fiat scenario needs roughly the same timeframe but at only **12,000 km/year**.
- Companies acknowledge shared societal responsibility for reducing emissions but are broadly waiting on market/technology maturation before further green vehicle investment — chiefly due to continued high purchase-price premiums and limited range.
- Over the projection horizon, the project's subsidy funds meaningfully shorten the operating period before EVs become cheaper, plus deliver significant emissions savings and associated socioeconomic benefit, both realized through increased incentive from the subsidy.
- Companies weigh the **liquidity impact** of paying more upfront more heavily than the ongoing savings achieved over the operating period. Regardless of financing arrangement chosen, the higher EV price affects costs. **As long as the market is maturing, continued need for subsidy schemes like Erhvervskørsel på El is expected.**
