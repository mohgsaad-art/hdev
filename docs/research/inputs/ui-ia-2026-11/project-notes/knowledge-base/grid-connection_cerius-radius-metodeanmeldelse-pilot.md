## 2. Purpose of the pilot project (Pilotprojektets formål)

Stated purpose: to help form the basis for developing one or more **standardized market products** for DSOs' procurement of flexibility, developed jointly with Energinet and commercial market actors.

**Primary questions the pilot aims to answer:**
a. Can customers deliver flexibility matching the grid company's needs?
b. How reliable ("sikkerhed") is the flexibility delivery when actually needed?
c. What is the price level of this type of flexibility compared to the cost of grid reinforcement?

**Additional knowledge/learning objectives:**
a. What data and measurements must be made available to Suppliers (Leverandører), and how?
b. What characterizes the hours energy is shifted from and to?
c. How to manage the risk of changes in consumption/production under the station over time?
d. Identify potential regulatory challenges in using flexibility.
e. Are there undesirable rebound effects creating new challenges?
f. Identify and manage contractual challenges to secure a stable, appropriate contractual basis.
g. Are there new challenges in managing market-procured flexibility versus managing agreements with **BNA (Begrænset Netadgang / Limited Grid Access)** customers who have remote-disconnection capability — including ensuring BNA customers are curtailed *before* market-procured flexibility is used.
h. Gather information on how this type of flexibility affects system balance.
i. Gather information on how it affects the supplier's ability to offer **mFRR** (manual Frequency Restoration Reserve) to Energinet.

**Purpose summary:** to determine what economic compensation flexibility-providing customers would require, and to assess whether procuring flexibility on these terms is competitive against the alternative of grid capacity expansion.

**Consultation/socialization prior to filing:**
- TREFOR El-net had dialogue with Energinet; Energinet saw no system-wide issues from the pilot.
- Status briefings given at MSU (Markedssamarbejdsudvalget, the Market Cooperation Committee with Energinet and GPD) — most recently 19 June 2025.
- Presented to other DSOs via a Green Power Denmark ERFA group on flexibility; supportive reception.

## 3. Background (Baggrunden for pilotprojektet)

- Electrification of society and the transition to a renewable-energy-based ("VE-baseret," VE = vedvarende energi/renewable energy) electricity system place new, larger demands on the Danish distribution grid. Flexibility is expected to be able to replace or postpone infrastructure investment.
- The filing explicitly follows up on recommendations from:
  - **Energistyrelsen** (Danish Energy Agency) report: *"Fremme af fleksibilitetsmarked til elnettet"* (Promoting a flexibility market for the electricity grid) — cited with URL `ens.dk/sites/ens.dk/files/El/rapport_fremme_af_fleksibilitetsmarked_til_elnettet.pdf`.
  - **NEKST** (the national energy crisis staff / "den nationale energikrisestab") working group on faster grid buildout — cited with URL `kefm.dk/klima/nekst-den-nationale-energikrisestab/nekst-arbejdsgruppe-hurtigere-udbygning-af-elnettet`.

**Seven criteria the filers state define a "good flexibility solution"** (their own framework, presented as their assessment, not a legal requirement per se):
1. Must be economically sound for society ("samfundsøkonomisk fornuftig") — i.e., not more expensive than grid reinforcement.
2. Must provide a secure investment climate for both flexibility suppliers and grid companies (mutual assurance that investments can be recouped).
3. Must ensure market support/integration with existing electricity markets.
4. Grid companies must have delivery security — to avoid damaging equipment or degrading supply security/quality; supply security must be maintained at an unchanged level.
5. Must support electrification and the green transition — avoiding shifting significant consumption to hours of very low renewable production.
6. Must have low complexity, since many potential flexibility-providing customers are private individuals or small businesses who must be able to understand what they are participating in and see a net economic benefit, or they risk declining to participate.
7. Must have high resilience against the spread of new grid-connected technologies.

**Pilot design implication:** Cerius-Radius will trial offering out a flexibility need under specific main transformer stations (at 10 kV outlets or further out in the grid) as an alternative to expanding grid capacity in the area. The selected stations will **not necessarily have an acute capacity problem** — instead, known/costed reinforcement plans will exist for the same area so cost comparison is possible.

**Post-pilot public evaluation report:** required after project completion, following up on the section 2 objectives. The **economic comparison** between reinforcement cost and the flexibility agreement cost will **NOT be made public** and is reserved for Forsyningstilsynet only, since it contains commercially sensitive information for the supplier and Cerius-Radius. Market-sensitive information or information barred from disclosure by GDPR may also be excluded from the public report but must remain available to Cerius-Radius if relevant to their further flexibility work. **Suppliers must actively participate in the evaluation** and contribute relevant data.

## 4. Method description (Metodebeskrivelse)

**Core concept:** treat flexibility as equivalent to grid buildout — an alternative way to create extra capacity by reducing load during critical bottleneck periods, rather than reinforcing the grid. The DSOs will procure the option to deliver **downward regulation of consumption** ("nedregulering af forbrug") to relieve the grid during an abnormal switching situation ("unormal koblingssituation"). This lets the DSOs directly compare the cost of solving a capacity challenge via flexibility versus via traditional reinforcement, and choose the cheaper solution — flexibility and reinforcement "compete on equal terms."

**Procurement law status:** Because this is a pilot project limited in value and time, contracts awarded under it are **not covered by the Utilities Directive** ("forsyningsvirksomhedsdirektivet"), so there is **no obligation to run a prior formal tender**. However, the DSOs will voluntarily run a **tender-like process** ("udbudslignende proces") mirroring Utilities Directive-style tenders — openly announced, giving all potential actors equal access to bid — to reduce discrimination risk, promote competition, and create a level playing field for large and small actors alike. This is explicitly framed as **preparation/learning for a possible future full tender** if the pilot scales into a larger, Utilities-Directive-covered project.

**Flexibility service nature:** a downward-regulation service, typically activated when an abnormal operating situation occurs at a main transformer station **at the same time as** high grid load.

**Key operational/commercial terms:**
- Cerius-Radius will provide the best possible information (frequency, timing, duration) about the specific flexibility need, but the pilot's defining feature is that **exact timing and duration are not known in advance**.
- Suppliers are told the total need expressed in **MW of downward regulation required**; they may bid for only part of the total desired volume; bid-size units will be specified in the tender materials.
- It is the **Supplier's own responsibility** to find, activate, and settle payment with the end customers delivering flexibility, and to ensure sufficient flexible capacity is available under the relevant station — adequacy is the Supplier's own judgment call.
- **Customers connected under BNA (Begrænset Netadgang / Limited Grid Access) cannot participate** as flexibility sources in the pilot. The pilot will ensure BNA customers are not artificially curtailed as a side effect of the pilot.
- Suppliers bear full responsibility for compliance with electricity market rules; Cerius-Radius is **not liable** for (a) costs from imbalances or operational losses, or (b) sanctions/extra costs to Energinet from failure to deliver services sold to Energinet.
- **Geographic scope:** expected to cover **2 areas** — one in Cerius's and one in Radius's supply area — selected in advance to be as representative as possible of the DSOs' grids. These areas will not necessarily have acute capacity problems but will have known reinforcement plans for cost comparison.

## 5. Sub-processes (De enkelte delprocesser)

### 5.1 Communication
- Planned communication channels: Cerius-Radius websites, contacting **iEnergi** and others, and social media ("SoMe") to alert potential Suppliers to the tender-like process.
- Information will be released as early as possible to give Suppliers preparation time.
- **No special technical requirements** are imposed on potential Suppliers' installations beyond those listed in §5.2 below.
- A **Request for Information (RFI)** process may be opened if potential Suppliers need information not covered by the notification or tender materials.

### 5.2 Tender and requirements for potential Suppliers
- **No prequalification** is conducted — all Suppliers are evaluated against the requirements below at the point of the tender itself.
- The listed requirements are stated as **not discriminatory** between potential Suppliers.

| # | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Assets covered by the bid must be connected to the distribution grid with full delivery scope and be in operation (assets connected under BNA terms **cannot** be bid in) |
| 2 | At time of bid submission, Suppliers must provide a list of meters (POD — metering point numbers) for assets covered by the bid; these are validated against the selected areas to confirm relevance and that the consumption volume plausibly supports the bid-sized downward regulation |
| 3 | Assets must be located within the area defined in the tender |
| 4 | Suppliers must specify whether the bid involves large single assets or multiple smaller aggregated assets (relevant for metering) |
| 5 | Suppliers must designate persons to receive activation notices for the flexibility service |
| 6 | Suppliers must be able to deliver the agreed flexibility **within 15 minutes** of notice being given by Cerius-Radius to the designated contacts |
| 7 | Suppliers must inform Cerius-Radius once flexibility has been activated |
| 8 | Suppliers must, without undue delay, notify Cerius-Radius if contracted assets fail/go out of operation, and again when they return to operation |
| 9 | If the tender specifies a ramp rate for returning to normal load, Suppliers must accept this |
| 10 | Flexibility must be deliverable **24/7**, even though the tender materials will indicate guidance/informational (non-binding) time windows (frequency, timing, duration) for the relevant station/outlet |
| 11 | Suppliers are responsible for maintaining the necessary control/access over flexible customers for the entire contract period |
| 12 | Participating assets may be changed during the contract period only with prior Cerius-Radius approval; added assets must meet the same requirements as at bid submission |
| 13 | Suppliers must appoint a contract-responsible contact person (not for dispatch/delivery matters) expected to have a mandate to participate in evaluating activations |
| 14 | Suppliers commit to preparing an evaluation, jointly with Cerius-Radius, after **each** activation, as a key part of the learning process |
| 15 | Suppliers commit to participating in a final evaluation of the pilot project |
| 16 | Suppliers must accept that delivery and its measurement will be tested after contract signature (see §5.3) |
| 17 | Suppliers must disclose whether they are also a supplier to Energinet on the system or balancing market |
| 18 | Suppliers must accept Cerius-Radius's standard procurement terms |
| 19 | Suppliers must submit the **last 2 years of financial statements**, showing **positive equity** |

### 5.3 Bid evaluation, testing, and contract award
- Award goes to the bid(s) with the **lowest price**, provided that price is **lower than the alternative cost of the specific grid reinforcement** and meets the requirements above and the tender specifications.
- The price comparison is made **only** against the alternative reinforcement cost — it does **not** factor in potential additional costs (e.g., metering — see §9), to avoid distorting bid comparison; supplementary metering needs, if any, are assessed case-by-case by Cerius-Radius after award.
- After award, a **test** is run to confirm the "notice-to-delivery" process is practically workable, or to identify issues Suppliers must resolve before the availability payment is disbursed. This test must occur within a set time window after contract signing; the contract is **terminated** if conditions at the Supplier prevent establishing a workable notice-to-delivery process. Exact test details depend on case specifics (single vs. aggregated assets, delivery method).

### 5.4 Notification, delivery, and validation
- On activation need, Suppliers are notified per the agreement and must deliver the agreed downward regulation within **15 minutes**.
- After delivery, the delivery is validated/evaluated against agreed terms; validation may use Supplier measurements, Cerius-Radius measurements, or agreed calculation methods, depending on circumstances.

### 5.5 Payment model (Betalingsmodel)

Core idea: high-percentage deliveries are paid close to 1:1 with delivery size; payment tapers as delivery percentage falls; deliveries below 30% are treated as **not delivered** and are unpaid, triggering a warning.

**Payment and penalty schedule (Betalings- og bodsbestemmelse):**

| Delivery vs. agreed (%) | Payment as % of activation payment | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 80% and ≤ 100% | 1:1 (100→100, 90→90, 80→80) | Direct 1:1 % relationship between delivery and payment; example values shown (100, 90, 80) — all intermediate sizes trigger proportional payment (e.g. 93%) |
| ≥ 50% and < 79%[^1] | 75→50, 60→50, 50→50 | Example: 50% payment rate applied to a 60% delivery = 30% effective payment. All sizes in this band trigger the flat 50% payment rate (e.g. 64%) |
| ≥ 30% and < 50% | 45→25, 35→25, 30→25 | Example: 25% payment rate applied to a 35% delivery = 8.75% effective payment. All sizes in this band trigger the flat 25% payment rate (e.g. 42%) |
| < 30% | Not paid | Delivery is deemed "not delivered"; Supplier receives a warning. **2 warnings → exclusion from the next activation.** |

[^1]: The source table's band boundaries (e.g., "79%" vs. the "≥80%" band starting point) are reproduced exactly as they appear in the source document, including the apparent small gap/overlap in stated boundaries — this is a direct transcription artifact from the original table, not a correction or estimate.

**Two-part pricing structure** (see §7): Suppliers submit **two prices** in their bid:
- **A.** A price for being on standby/available during the period (one-off "availability" payment — "engangsbetaling")
- **B.** A price for the actual flexibility delivery itself (price per MWh)

## 6. Temporariness and limitations of the pilot (Pilotprojektets midlertidighed og afgrænsninger)

- **Duration:** defined per an addendum dated **27 February 2026** ("tillæg af 27. februar 2026") — the specific duration figure itself is `[unclear from source — the addendum's content/duration figure is not reproduced in this document]`.
- After contract expiry, an evaluation will be conducted, feeding into a possible future **permanent method**, which would itself need to be separately filed and approved.
- **Geographic limitation:** tender-like process to cover 2 main transformer stations — one per supply area (Cerius, Radius) — to be specified in the tender materials. The tender is geographically limited to customers normally supplied from that station under normal switching configuration. Stations will be chosen for a reasonably representative mix of customer types, consumption, and geography, with known reinforcement plans available for cost comparison.
- **Tariff impact:** grid tariffs for consumers and producers under the relevant station will **not** be affected by the pilot during the project period. Settlement of flexibility payments with end-customers (if end-customers are part of the delivery chain) is the Supplier's responsibility, not Cerius-Radius's.
- **Economic ceiling:** the pilot has an upper economic limit comparable to the cost of the alternative grid reinforcement. Total pilot project cost will be reported separately and included in the evaluation.
- **Regulatory accounting:** pilot costs will be included in Cerius-Radius's regulatory accounts ("reguleringsregnskab") alongside other operating costs. The DSOs expect that developing flexibility procurement methods will, longer term, lead to lower and more efficient grid-buildout costs and thus lower tariffs for all grid users.

## 7. Administrative practice in the tender-like process (Administrativ praksis i den udbudslignende proces og pilotprojekt)

- Although not covered by the Utilities Directive, the tender-like process is designed to generate experience with tendering in case the pilot evaluation leads to a larger, more general project that **is** covered by the Utilities Directive (requiring prior formal tendering).
- Selection criteria will be objective, to prevent discrimination. A Supplier's specific role in the electricity market is **not decisive** for Cerius-Radius — Suppliers alone are responsible for acting within applicable market rules.
- **Winning criteria** under Netvirksomhedsbekendtgørelsen § 38 stk. 1 and 3 require the method to ensure:
  - Awareness among all market participants ("Kendskab af alle markedsdeltagere")
  - Free and equal access for potential market participants ("Fri og lige adgang")
  - Technology neutrality ("Teknologineutralitet")
  - Lowest price meeting specifications/terms that is also lower than the reinforcement alternative cost
  - Coordination with Energinet (and involvement of market participants and other grid companies)

**Technology neutrality:** No requirement is placed on any specific technology. Whether Suppliers are customers with their own assets, or market actors aggregating smaller private units such as **electric vehicles (elbiler)**, individual heat pumps, larger batteries, backup power systems, or energy communities, no distinction is made — assessed by the filers as consistent with Netvirksomhedsbekendtgørelsen § 38 stk. 2.

**Tender materials will include:**
a. Description of Suppliers' task and responsibility
b. Total downward-regulation need
c. Bid sizes
d. Contract duration
e. Estimated frequency of downward regulation
f. Estimated duration of downward regulation
g. Notification (response time)
h. Any ramp rates
i. Model for availability payment and per-activation payment
j. Activation period
k. Number of activation periods
l. Geographic scope

**Coordination with Energinet:** No further direct collaboration with Energinet is deemed necessary for this pilot beyond what has already occurred (see §2). Suppliers are expected to operate within existing electricity market rules; Energinet is understood to accept that bottleneck-solving assets can be used in the distribution grid without limiting participation in ancillary-services markets (citing an Energinet news item on "Begrænset netadgangs indvirken i aktørernes evne til at byde ind i systemydelsesmarkederne" — the effect of Limited Grid Access on actors' ability to bid into ancillary-services markets). Suppliers are responsible for coordinating any simultaneous bids into ancillary-services markets. Energinet will be informed before the tender-like process is launched (having already been informed via prior dialogue).

## 9. Economics — payment for flexibility in the pilot (Økonomi)

- For flexibility to have value to the grid company, procured flexibility must always **displace** a grid buildout or reinforcement. Economic value varies case by case (which reinforcement is needed, cheapest way to do it), so value will fluctuate even under a finalized method.
- **Valuation method for the pilot:** an **annuity approach** ("annuitetsbetragtning") is used to convert the avoided investment into the present value of an annual saving from not undertaking a traditional reinforcement project.
- **Key financial parameters used in the calculation:**
  - **TOTEX** (total expenditure) at fixed prices for the grid reinforcement
  - **Depreciation period: 40 years**
  - **Discount rate (WACC): 5%**
- The filers note there is naturally sensitivity in these parameters, to be explored further in a final method, but state that sensitivity is not assessed as materially affecting whether it is worthwhile for market actors/customers to offer their flexibility.
- **Cost treatment:** pilot costs are assessed as operating costs ("driftsomkostninger") for flexibility services and will be reported by Cerius-Radius in the relevant regulatory accounts (per the companion memo referenced in §1). Beyond Supplier payments, costs may also include, e.g., establishing metering at a grid station if Cerius-Radius deems it material for validating delivery — estimated at approximately **DKK 30,000–50,000**, described as immaterial relative to the expected payment for the flexibility agreement itself.
- Cerius-Radius expects to eventually apply for the project's costs to be **exempted from benchmarking** of the grid company; this will be handled separately from Forsyningstilsynet's processing of the method notification. Total project costs will be recorded separately in the accounts.

## 10. Annex 1 (Bilag 1) — Legislative remarks on § 1, no. 30 (§ 73a, stk. 2)

Reproduces legislative commentary ("lovbemærkninger") to **Lov nr. 1677 af 30. december 2024** (amending the Electricity Supply Act, Gas Supply Act, and Penal Code — implementing the EU Electricity Market Directive and future-proofed regulation of grid companies), specifically regarding **§ 73a, stk. 2** ("Approval of experiments with tariffs").

Key points from this legislative history, as reproduced in the source:
- § 73a stk. 2 originated via an amendment in the committee report to bill L 87 (Folketingstidende 2010-2011, Tillæg B, s. 2), allowing Forsyningstilsynet to approve methods for **limited buyer groups and for a limited period** as part of collective electricity utilities' method development, with conditions Forsyningstilsynet may attach.
- The 2024 amendment changes "buyer groups" ("købergrupper") to "**categories of grid users**" ("kategorier af netbrugere") — aligning with the 2021 change (Lov nr. 2605 af 28. december 2021) that replaced "buyer categories" with "categories of grid users" to make clear that **both electricity customers and electricity producers** can give rise to costs that collective electricity utilities may tariff.
- The clarified intent: Forsyningstilsynet's power to approve pilot-basis tariff methods and new cost-allocation principles now explicitly covers **both consumers and producers**, in line with **Article 18 of the EU Electricity Market Regulation** ("Elmarkedsforordningen").
- Purpose: allow Forsyningstilsynet to approve, e.g., a tariff method on a trial basis even where it may be hard to demonstrate in advance which category/categories of grid users give rise to the relevant costs/savings — particularly relevant for developing new tariff products with no established Forsyningstilsynet practice yet. This is intended to prevent protracted development/approval processes for new tariff methods, supporting speed in the green transition and electricity market development, via a **holistic approach** in decisions on projects with new tariff methods.
- The same logic is stated to extend to approval of projects for **new methods of procuring services from grid users** using the collective electricity grid — even where these do not themselves constitute development of a genuine new tariff method, their financing may separately require method approval if it uses, wholly or partly, tariff funds ("tarifmidler").
- Explicit example given: approval can occur as part of developing new tariffing methods, e.g., to promote intelligent electricity consumption, such as a tariffing method for **local collective tariffing** — a tariff solution accounting for the combined grid draw of an association/collective, which by producing and consuming electricity simultaneously can reduce load on the collective grid. **Lov nr. 415 af 25. april 2023** improved the framework for tariffing of, e.g., energy communities via local collective tariffing based on a single metering point set up for that purpose in Datahub (Folketingstidende 2022-23, tillæg A, s. 47).
- Approval can also occur as part of developing **new methods for procuring services from grid users**, e.g., projects testing the establishment of **local flexibility markets** for grid users' market-based delivery of flexibility services to a collective electricity utility — this is the precise legal category this Cerius-Radius pilot notification falls under.
- **EU Electricity Market Reform / Article 18 of the Electricity Market Regulation:**
  - **Art. 18(8):** recognition of network operators' costs should give operators incentives to use flexibility services.
  - **Art. 18(2)(c):** tariff methodologies must support the use of flexibility services.
  - **Art. 18(2)(g):** tariff methodologies must facilitate innovation in consumers' interest, including via flexibility services.
  - The Regulation thereby recognizes in principle the possibility of using tariff funds to finance grid companies' costs for this type of pilot project — projects aiming to promote more efficient use of existing grids (e.g., to defer grid buildout) can lead to cost savings benefiting all grid users in the grid company's area.

## 11. Relevance to HDEV/depot sizing & flexibility revenue modelling

This document is directly relevant to the HDEV sizing & modelling tool's value proposition, for the following explicit reasons found in the source (not extrapolated):

1. **Technology neutrality is explicit and named EVs as an eligible flexibility source category.** The method description states no technology requirement is imposed and lists "elbiler" (electric vehicles) among example aggregatable asset types alongside heat pumps, batteries, backup generators, and energy communities (§7, "Teknologineutralitet"). A fleet depot's flexible/curtailable charging load would plausibly qualify as an eligible "asset" under this framework, either directly (if the depot is the grid customer bidding in) or via an aggregator ("Leverandør"/aggregating market actor).
2. **The mechanism is a downward-regulation (curtailment) service, not a full demand-response dispatch market** — i.e., it pays for the ability and delivery of *reducing* consumption during specific abnormal/high-load situations at a station, not for arbitrary flexible dispatch. For an HDEV depot, this maps to willingness/ability to delay or reduce charging on short notice (15-minute response requirement, §5.2) — a real constraint for fleet operations that would need explicit modelling (e.g., whether depot charging schedules have enough slack to guarantee a 15-minute curtailment response 24/7 without disrupting vehicle readiness).
3. **Two-part pricing (availability payment + per-MWh delivery payment)** gives a concrete commercial structure a depot operator/aggregator could model as a revenue stream: a standby/availability fee plus a volumetric payment for actual curtailment delivered, subject to the tiered payment schedule (§5.5) that penalizes partial delivery below 80% and voids payment below 30%.
4. **Eligibility requirements are non-trivial and quantifiable** for depot modelling: full-scope grid connection (not BNA/Limited Grid Access), metering data (POD numbers) sufficient to validate that consumption volume plausibly supports the bid size, 2 years of positive-equity financial statements, and 24/7 availability commitment (even though indicative time windows are given).
5. **Value ceiling is explicitly tied to avoided grid reinforcement cost**, calculated via a 40-year depreciation period and 5% WACC annuity — giving the tool a concrete, source-verified methodology (not the tool's own assumption) for estimating the maximum economic value a DSO would pay for depot flexibility in a comparable Danish context.
6. **Geographic/temporal scope is narrow and pilot-specific** (2 named station areas, defined pilot duration per the Feb 2026 addendum) — this is **not yet a generally available revenue mechanism** for depots; it is a limited pilot whose evaluation will determine whether a permanent, more broadly accessible method is later filed and approved. Any tool feature suggesting depots can access this revenue stream today should flag it as pilot-stage and geographically/administratively restricted, pending Forsyningstilsynet's future decisions.
7. **Ancillary-market interaction is called out**: the pilot explicitly investigates how supplying DSO-procured flexibility affects a supplier's ability to also offer **mFRR** to Energinet (§2i), and requires bid disclosure of whether the Supplier is also active in Energinet's system/balancing markets (§5.2) — relevant if the tool ever models stacked revenue streams for a depot (DSO flexibility + TSO ancillary services).

**Explicit gaps / items NOT in this source that a complete depot-flexibility model would still need:** the actual named pilot station locations, the actual pilot start/end dates (only the referencing addendum date of 27 Feb 2026 is given), the actual €/MW or DKK/MW bid sizes or price levels from any real tender round, and the content of the companion cost/accounting memo. These are `[unclear from source]` and must not be assumed.
