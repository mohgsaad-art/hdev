## Foreword / Policy Framing
- Esbjerg Kommune has set an ambition of **CO2 neutrality by 2030**.
- Transport sector identified as historically hard to decarbonize, partly due to car dependency.
- Fleet electrification (fossil → electric) framed as necessary; requires charging infrastructure.
- Municipality commits to converting its own fleet and installing chargers at municipal buildings; an electrified fleet will also become a **selection parameter when choosing future suppliers**.
- Explicitly acknowledges Esbjerg Kommune **cannot reach its ambition alone** — dependent on cooperation with businesses, citizens, and housing associations.

## Fra Fossile Brændstoffer til El (From Fossil Fuels to Electric)
- **Vision 2025** — political ambition to be a "grøn foregangskommune" (green pioneer municipality).
- **November 2020:** Esbjerg Kommune joined **DK2020**, committing to the C40 international city network's climate-planning standard.
- **Budget 2021:** a unanimous byråd adopted the **CO2-neutral by 2030** target.
- **Transport = ~1/3 of Esbjerg's CO2 emissions; passenger cars = almost half of transport-sector emissions.**
- **CO2 distribution by sector in Esbjerg Kommune, 2018 (pie chart, verbatim percentages):** Landbrug (Agriculture) 39%, Energi (Energy) 34%, Transport 24%, Kemiske processer (Chemical processes) 2%, Affaldsdeponi (Waste disposal) 1%.
- **CO2 emissions in the transport sector (pie chart):** Vejtrafik (Road traffic) 66%, Fly (Aviation) 16%, Non-road 14%, Færge og fiskeri (Ferry and fishing) 3%, Tog (Trains) 1%.
- **CO2 emissions within "Vejtrafik" (road traffic) (pie chart):** Personbiler (Passenger cars) 71%, Andre køretøjer (Other vehicles) 26%, Busser (Buses) 3%.
- **Esbjerg Kommune's roles (four listed):**
  1. **Bilflådeejer (Fleet owner):** over **600 vehicles**, to be gradually replaced with emission-free vehicles
  2. **Bygningsejer (Building owner):** owns many buildings, several covered by the ladestanderbekendtgørelsen
  3. **Myndighed (Authority):** plans and can set requirements/possibilities for charging infrastructure in kommuneplan, lokalplaner, and byggesagsbehandling
  4. **Indkøber og bestiller (Buyer and procurer):** uses many suppliers of goods/services, can require e.g. emission-free transport from them

## Behovet for Ladeinfrastruktur (The Need for Charging Infrastructure)
### EU/National Pressure Points (verbatim list)
- **EU requires car manufacturers to limit new cars to max. 95 grams CO2/km.**
- **EU's Clean Vehicles Directive**, effective **August 2021** — sets minimum thresholds for the share of public vehicle procurement that must be "green vehicles."
- **Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen**, from **10 March 2020** — requires building owners to prepare for/install charging infrastructure.
- **Aftale om 'Grøn omstilling af vejtransporten'** (Agreement on Green Transition of Road Transport), dated **4 December 2020** — via changed tax/duty structures, supports an expectation of **775,000 EVs in Denmark by 2030**.

### Local EV Target
- The parties behind "Grøn omstilling af vejtransporten" hope for **1 million EVs in Denmark by 2030 (30% of the fleet)**.
- **For Esbjerg Kommune, this means 20,000 EVs by 2030.**
- **The byråd has earmarked a 5 million DKK pool (2021-2022) to establish more charge stands in Esbjerg Kommune.**
- Cites the **Bilkommissionen's 2nd partial report**, published **12 February 2021**: charging infrastructure must be established **ahead of** demand, not reactively — this strategy aims to have public charging infrastructure ready by 2025 that can support the expected 2030 demand.

### Esbjerg Kommune Does Not Act Alone
- Citizens able to do so are expected to install their own home charger (most convenient/cheapest).
- Ambition: as many as possible should charge on **normalladere** — whether installed by housing associations on shared lots, by employers for staff, or as publicly accessible normal chargers near home — to reduce pressure on fast/rapid chargers and on the grid (especially strained in late afternoon/early evening).
- **Hurtigladning (fast charging)** particularly relevant for **30-60 minute stops**, e.g. shopping — retail plays a role here.
- **Lynladning (rapid charging)** primarily for minimizing charging time on long trips/commercial driving — expected to be installed by private actors on commercial terms.

## Esbjerg Kommune som Bilflådeejer (Fleet Owner)
- Esbjerg Kommune has joined the government's **klimasamarbejdsaftale** (climate cooperation agreement), with targets for public transit, the municipal fleet, and municipal road vehicles.
- Committed to **leasing/buying zero-emission vehicles from 2022**, if economically realistic and technologically feasible.
- Committed to preparing a **2021 plan** for when at least **75% of newly leased/purchased municipal road vehicles** will use a CO2-neutral or zero-emission fuel.
- **Fleet: 600+ vehicles**, only a small share currently zero-emission.
- **Benefits of fleet electrification cited:** lower maintenance (EVs need little servicing); electricity is much cheaper than diesel/petrol as a fuel, yielding significant operating savings.
- **CO2 savings potential:** if the municipality progressively replaces all passenger cars and vans with electric equivalents from **2022 to 2030**, total CO2 savings potential is **approx. 2,800 tonnes** (actual savings depend on conversion speed).
- **Figure (described, chart not fully extractable as data):** shows annual CO2 emissions of the current fleet (orange line) vs. expected annual emissions under progressive electrification (purple line), annual CO2 reduction (green line), and accumulated CO2 savings over time (blue line), for years 2021-2030, on a Y-axis scaled 0-3,000 tonnes CO2 equivalent.
- **Stated commitments:**
  - Prepare a detailed plan for how/when the fleet can convert to zero-emission vehicles
  - Work toward a **zero-emission fleet by end of 2030**, to the extent economically realistic and technologically feasible

## Esbjerg Kommune som Bygningsejer (Building Owner)
- As building owner, subject to the **Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen** (in force from 10 March 2020, tied to Byggeloven).
- Requires: preparation for chargers at all new construction/major renovations; if non-residential, at least 1 charge stand must be installed. Existing non-residential buildings with **more than 20 parking spaces** must install at least 1 charge stand.
- **Esbjerg Kommune currently owns 88 buildings** triggering this last requirement — at least 1 charge stand must be installed at **88 different locations** across the municipality (map referenced, not extractable as text — locations listed by town name: Ribe, Gredstedbro, Store Darum, Jernved, Gørding, Bramming, Tjæreborg, Vejrup, Grimstrup, Esbjerg, Mandø, Egebæk, Andrup, Vester Nebel, Tarp, Guldager, Hjerting, Roager, Vester Vedsted, Øster Vedsted, Hunderup, Myrtue, Skads, Esbjerg Lufthavn, Korskro).
- **All chargers installed under the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen by Esbjerg Kommune will be normalladere.** Costs expected to be primarily borne by Esbjerg Kommune.
- **Deadline: 1 January 2025** — a foundational constraint for the municipality's charging-infrastructure rollout.
- **Siting ambition:** these Executive-Order-mandated chargers should be made **publicly accessible** (usable by citizens for payment), partially covering public charging need.
- Notes some chargers (e.g. at kindergartens, schools, halls) will be in areas of **limited expected demand** (e.g. single-family-home areas where most residents can charge at home) — contributing only marginally to public charging coverage there, though possibly meeting staff demand.
- Other areas — **Esbjerg midtby (city center)** and **Ribe middelalderby (medieval town)**, where many park on the street, plus older single-family-home areas like **Rolfsgade in Esbjerg** (typically no on-property parking) — will need **more** charging infrastructure than the Executive Order alone provides.
- Esbjerg Kommune will work to ensure clusters of publicly accessible chargers on nearby parking lots to meet this need. **As a default, the municipality does not want curbside charging ("kantstensopladning")** due to various urban-space problems it causes.
- **Stated commitments:**
  - Progressively install chargers at municipal buildings with >20 parking spaces per the Executive Order, through 2025
  - Ensure Executive-Order-mandated chargers are made publicly accessible for both municipal staff and citizens generally

## Esbjerg Kommune som Myndighed (Authority)
- Responsible for setting requirements/possibilities for charging infrastructure in kommuneplan/lokalplaner and byggesagsbehandling.
- **Additional normal-charger need identified:** existing/planned 2021 chargers plus Executive-Order-mandated chargers will **not alone** meet public normal-charger demand, and are not always sited where demand is highest — namely the **midtbyer (town centers) of Esbjerg, Ribe, and Bramming**.
- **Recommendation: install at least 22 additional normal chargers in the town centers** to meet expected demand there.
- Rationale for prioritizing normal chargers: longer charging duration (workday/overnight) spreads grid load more evenly, reducing grid investment needs — a socio-economic benefit.
- **Equal charging opportunity regardless of housing type:** not all citizens can install a home charger; some may charge at work but not all will have that option. Citizens dependent on public charging infrastructure (e.g. fast chargers at retail, rapid chargers commuting to/from work) risk **queueing/long wait times**, as currently observed in **Norway** — and charging would concentrate in the "kogespids" (cooking peak — the period of highest household electricity demand), requiring more grid investment. The 22 extra town-center normal chargers are intended to give street-parkers a nearby normal-charging option instead.
- Siting of the 22 chargers shown on referenced maps (not extractable as text); expected to be installed alongside the Executive-Order-mandated chargers, with **costs primarily borne by the charging operators** (contrast with the Executive-Order chargers, whose costs fall primarily on the municipality).
- **Stated commitments:**
  - Secure the planning basis for appropriate charging-infrastructure rollout
  - Ensure **at least 22 additional charge stands** are installed by 2025 to meet expected 2030 demand
  - Prioritize additional charge stands primarily where need is greatest — the town centers of Esbjerg, Ribe, and Bramming

## Esbjerg Kommune som Bestiller og Indkøber (Procurer and Buyer)
- **January 2021:** Esbjerg Kommune adopted a new procurement policy and strategy with strong climate/sustainability focus, aligned with the government's **"Strategi for offentlige indkøb"** (October 2020), which states the public vehicle fleet must be emission-free by 2030.
- Municipality intends to require suppliers use, e.g., **emission-free vehicles for goods transport**, or **electric/emission-free vehicles for service transport like taxi rides**, assessed per tender.
- **Already-taken steps (predating the new procurement policy):**
  - Since **September 2020**, Esbjerg Kommune's new **refuse/waste collection trucks run on biogas.**
  - **2020:** Esbjerg Kommune tendered its public bus service. **From December 2021, all city buses will be emission-free electric buses** — a **70% reduction** in total CO2 emissions from bus operations.
- **Stated commitments:**
  - Only emission-free city buses from **December 2021**
  - Investigate requiring emission-free vehicles from suppliers in tenders
- Photo captions reference: "first spadeful of earth" ceremony for Esbjerg Kommune's new **bus depot for emission-free electric buses**, and refuse trucks running on biogas.

## Dialog med Borgere, Virksomheder og Boligforeninger (Dialogue with Citizens, Businesses, and Housing Associations)
- Citizens/businesses/housing associations generally want the municipality to take a **leading role**, facilitating rollout via knowledge-sharing, dialogue, and inspiration.
- **Housing associations:** key challenge is serving residents without home charging. Barriers: general parking-space shortage, cost of establishing charging infrastructure, and difficulty understanding the practical measures involved in shared charging infrastructure. Municipality commits to dialogue and guidance.
- **Businesses:** face fleet-conversion tasks, Executive Order compliance, and rising employee demand for workplace charging. General desire for more information; municipality to act as a knowledge broker on EVs, fleet conversion, charger installation, and relevant legislation (incl. the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen).
- **Stated commitments:**
  - Disseminate general information to citizens, businesses, and housing associations on EVs and public charging infrastructure
  - Engage housing associations in dialogue, helping answer their charger-installation questions
  - Work with **Business Esbjerg** (named local business organization) to spread knowledge among businesses about the Executive Order, EVs, fleet conversion, and charger installation

## Mange Aktører i Udrulningen af Ladeinfrastruktur (Many Actors in the Rollout)
- Reiterates Esbjerg Kommune's leadership intent, while acknowledging dependence on other actors (retail chains installing chargers to attract customers, growing number of charging providers).
- **"Robust" planning approach:** because technology, the EV market, and the charging-infrastructure market are evolving rapidly, and future legislation/lade-patterns are uncertain, Esbjerg Kommune adopts a **stepwise ("trinvis") approach**: meet the Executive Order's requirements by 2025, while installing public charging infrastructure ahead of demand, closely monitoring rollout/usage to stay ahead of demand.
- **Chargers on pure commercial terms:** fast/rapid chargers needed for commuters, tradespeople, traveling salespeople, and through-traffic, also serving citizens unable to install home chargers. Esbjerg Kommune will actively engage with interested charging operators and help identify suitable sites. **An initial screening has already identified suitable rapid-charging-station sites**, considering traffic volumes, publicly-owned land, and electricity supply.
- **Stated commitments:**
  - Closely monitor charging-infrastructure and charging-pattern developments, supporting appropriate public charging-infrastructure rollout
  - Engage interested charging operators in dialogue on suitable station sites
