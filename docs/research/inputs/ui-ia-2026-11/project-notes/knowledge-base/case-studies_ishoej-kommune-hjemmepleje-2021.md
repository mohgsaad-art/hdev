# Ishøj Kommune (Hjemmepleje) — Fleet Electrification Case Study (2021)

**Source file:** `CASE_Ishoej-FINAL.pdf`
**Category:** Case study — fleet electrification (municipal home-care fleet)
**Original language:** Danish (translated to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** Ishøj Kommune used a 1 million DKK sustainability grant to add 8 new electric vehicles to its home-care (hjemmepleje) fleet, bringing it to 12 EVs, with strong employee adoption after initial skepticism.

**Note on related case:** The project's compendium document (`Casehistorier_Fagfolk_templafy.pdf`) contains a separate, earlier-dated case story about Ishøj Kommune's home care service ("Gode erfaringer med elbiler i hjemmeplejen"), describing 3 Renault Zoe out of a 13-vehicle fleet (25%), first EVs acquired late 2014. That is a materially different snapshot (fewer vehicles, earlier date, different named contact — Rikke Kragh Iversen) and is filed separately as `claude/knowledge-base/case-studies/ishoej-kommune-hjemmepleje-2016.md`. This file covers only the 2021 case (`CASE_Ishoej-FINAL.pdf`), which reflects Ishøj's later, larger-scale EV adoption.

## Company Profile
- **Organization:** Ishøj Kommune (Municipality of Ishøj) — specifically the Hjemmepleje (home care/home nursing) department, plus broader municipal vehicle fleet context
- **Sector:** Public sector — municipal home care services
- **Fleet size (home care):** 12 electric vehicles (up from 4 previously, after 8 new EVs added)
- **Fleet size (municipality-wide):** ca. 70% of the municipality's cars are electric, as of the case's writing; remainder to be replaced over time "where it makes sense"
- **Location:** Ishøj Kommune, Denmark
- **Person(s) quoted:** Martin Nielsen — one of the municipality's vehicle-responsible staff (bilansvarlig), tasked with purchasing the home-care vehicles; Kirsten Christensen — Development Consultant (udviklingskonsulent), Ishøj Kommune
- **Publication year of case:** 2021

## Electrification Timeline
- Ishøj Kommune has a stated goal to grow its vehicle fleet with as many EVs as budget allows.
- Municipality received a **1,000,000 DKK (1 million kroner)** grant/allocation specifically to make its vehicle fleet more sustainable.
- Martin Nielsen was tasked with purchasing vehicles for the home-care service; initial consideration was given to buying used EVs with low mileage (noted as good value for money), but a local dealer offered new vehicles at a sufficiently favorable price that buying new made more sense in this instance.
- Result: home care fleet expanded by 8 brand-new EVs, bringing the total to 12 EVs in the home-care fleet.

## Operational Notes
- Charging is depot-based with a deliberately staggered/rotating schedule (every 2-3 days per vehicle) managed so that staff time is spent on residents/citizens, not on managing EV charging — a stated priority from Kirsten Christensen.
- Responsibility for plugging in vehicles is assigned explicitly to the evening shift, ensuring vehicles are charged and ready for the morning shift.
- Employee onboarding/training program nicknamed "Martins Minikøreskole" ("Martin's Mini Driving School") — Martin personally walks each employee through the vehicle's functions and offers to take a test drive with anyone who wants one, specifically to build comfort/trust with skeptical staff.
- Employee sentiment shifted from initial reluctance to enthusiasm: staff report the EVs drive better, offer more comfort, are quieter, and are less stressful to drive (no gear changes). Several employees have considered or already switched their personal vehicles to EVs as a result.
- Municipal approach to fleet-wide electrification is explicitly described as pragmatic and gradual, not a wholesale one-time replacement — vehicles are replaced with EVs as they come up for renewal "where it makes sense," balancing sustainability, economic responsibility, and common sense.

(As a municipality we have a responsibility to lead the way in sustainable development. But we take a pragmatic approach. We don't just replace the entire fleet at once, but do it gradually, where it makes the biggest difference. Sustainable development, financial responsibility, and common sense must go hand in hand.)

## Future Plans
- Continued gradual replacement of remaining non-electric municipal vehicles as they reach end of life, "where it makes sense" rather than on a fixed timeline.
- No specific target year for 100% fleet electrification stated in source.
