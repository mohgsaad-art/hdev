## 1. Introduction (Section 1)

This Annex describes the **certification provisions regarding the torque losses of propulsion axles** for heavy duty vehicles.

Alternatively to certification of axles, the **calculation procedure for the standard torque loss**, as defined in **Appendix 3** to this Annex, can be applied for the purpose of determining vehicle-specific CO2 emissions. (i.e., manufacturers have a choice: measure the real axle, or use generic/standard loss values.)

---

## 2. Definitions (Section 2)

All of the following are defined "for the purposes of this Annex":

1. **Single reduction axle (SR)** — a driven axle with only one gear reduction, typically a bevel gear set with or without hypoid offset.
2. **Single portal axle (SP)** — an axle that typically has a vertical offset between the rotating axis of the crown gear and the rotating axis of the wheel, due to the demand for higher ground clearance or a lowered floor (low-floor concept for inner-city buses). ►M3 Typically, the first reduction is a bevel gear set, the second a spur gear set (or helical gear set) with vertical offset close to the wheels. ◄
3. **Hub reduction axle (HR)** — a driven axle with two gear reductions. The first is typically a bevel gear set with or without hypoid offset; the other is a planetary gear set, typically placed at the wheel hubs.
4. **Single reduction tandem axle (SRT)** — a driven axle basically similar to a single driven axle, but which also transfers torque from the input flange, over an output flange, to a further axle. Torque can be transferred with a spur gear set close to the input flange (generating a vertical offset for the output flange), or via a second pinion at the bevel gear set that takes off torque at the crown wheel.
5. **Hub reduction tandem axle (HRT)** — a hub reduction axle with the added possibility of transferring torque to the rear, as described for SRT.
6. **Axle housing** — the housing parts needed for structural capability and for carrying the driveline parts, bearings and seals of the axle.
7. **Pinion** — part of a bevel gear set (which usually consists of two gears). The pinion is the driving gear, connected to the input flange. In an SRT/HRT, a second pinion can be installed to take off torque from the crown wheel.
8. **Crown wheel** — part of a bevel gear set; the driven gear, connected to the differential cage.
9. **Hub reduction** — the planetary gear set commonly installed outside the planetary bearing at hub reduction axles. Consists of three gear types: the **sun** (centre), the **planetary gears** (rotate around the sun, mounted to the planetary carrier fixed to the hub — typically 3 to 5 of them), and the **ring gear** (not rotating, fixed to the axle beam).
10. **Planetary gear wheels** — the gears that rotate around the sun within the ring gear of a planetary gear set, assembled with bearings on a planetary carrier joined to a hub.
11. **Oil type viscosity grade** — a viscosity grade as defined by **SAE J306**.
12. **Factory fill oil** — the oil type viscosity grade used for the factory oil fill, intended to stay in the axle for the first service interval.
13. **Axle line** — a group of axles that share the same basic axle function as defined in the family concept.
14. **Axle family** — a manufacturer's grouping of axles which, through design (as defined in Appendix 4), have similar design characteristics and CO2/fuel consumption properties.
15. **Drag torque** — the required torque to overcome the inner friction of an axle when the wheel ends are rotating freely with 0 Nm output torque.
16. **Mirror inverted axle casing** — an axle casing mirrored with respect to the vertical plane.
17. **Axle input** — the side of the axle on which torque is delivered to the axle.
18. **Axle output** — the side(s) of the axle where torque is delivered to the wheels.

## 3. General requirements (Section 3)

- ►M3: **The axle gears and all bearings shall be new** for the verification of axle losses, while **wheel end bearings may already be run in and may be used for multiple measurements.** ◄
- ►B: On request of the applicant, different gear ratios can be tested in one axle housing using the same wheel ends.
- Different axle ratios of **hub reduction axles and single portal axles (HR, HRT, SP)** may be measured by exchanging the hub reduction only — subject to the provisions of **Appendix 4**.
- **Maximum total run-time** for the optional run-in plus measurement of an individual axle (excluding the axle housing and wheel-ends) shall **not exceed 120 hours**.
- The torque loss map for each ratio of an individual axle shall be measured; however, axles can be grouped into **axle families** following Appendix 4.

## 4. Testing procedure for axles (Section 4)

### 4.1 Test conditions

**4.1.1 Ambient temperature**
- Test cell temperature: **25 °C ± 10 °C**, measured within 1 m of the axle housing.
- Forced heating of the axle may only be applied via an external oil conditioning system (see 4.1.5).

**4.1.2 Oil temperature**
- Measured at the centre of the oil sump, or any other suitable point per good engineering practice.
- For external oil conditioning: may alternatively be measured in the outlet line from the axle housing to the conditioning system, within 5 cm downstream of the outlet.
- In both cases, oil temperature **shall not exceed 70 °C**.

**4.1.3 Oil quality**
- Only recommended factory fill oils as specified by the axle manufacturer. ►M3: When testing different gear ratio variants in one axle housing, new oil shall be filled for each single measurement of the whole axle system. ◄

**4.1.4 Oil viscosity**
- If multiple viscosity grades are specified for factory fill, the manufacturer shall choose the **highest viscosity grade** for the parent axle measurement.
- If more than one oil within the same viscosity grade is specified within one axle family, the applicant may choose one for the certification-related measurement.

**4.1.5 Oil level and conditioning**
- Oil level/fill volume set to the **maximum level** per the manufacturer's maintenance specifications.
- An external oil conditioning and filtering system is permitted; axle housing may be modified to include it.
- The conditioning system shall **not** enable changing oil levels in a way that raises efficiency or generates propulsion torques artificially (contrary to good engineering practice).

### 4.2 Test set-up

**4.2.1 Axle installation**
- For a tandem axle, each axle shall be measured separately. The first axle with the longitudinal differential shall be locked. Output shaft of drive-through axles shall be installed freely rotatable.

**4.2.2 Installation of torque meters**
- 4.2.2.1 — Two-electric-machine setup: torque meters installed on the input flange and on **one** wheel end, with the other wheel end locked.
- 4.2.2.2 — Three-electric-machine setup: torque meters installed on the input flange **and on each wheel end**.
- 4.2.2.3 — Half shafts of different lengths are permitted in a two-machine setup, to lock the differential and ensure both wheel ends turn.

**4.2.3 Test set-up "Type A"**
- Consists of a dynamometer on the axle input side and **at least one** dynamometer on the axle output side(s). Torque measuring devices installed on input- and output-side(s).
- ►M3: For Type A setups with only one dynamometer on the output side, the freely rotating end on the output side shall be rotatably locked to the other end (e.g., activated differential lock, or any other mechanical differential lock implemented solely for measurement). ◄
- To avoid parasitic losses, torque measuring devices shall be positioned as close as possible to the axle input/output sides, supported by appropriate bearings.
- Mechanical isolation of torque sensors from parasitic shaft loads may additionally be applied (e.g., extra bearings and a flexible coupling or lightweight cardan shaft between sensors and a bearing).
- ►M3: Figure 1 shows an example Type A setup in a two-dynamometer layout. ◄ — **[Figure 1 referenced in source but not visually extracted from this text-only OCR]**
- For Type A configurations, the manufacturer shall provide an analysis of parasitic loads; based on this, the approval authority decides the **maximum influence of parasitic loads, i_para**, which **cannot be lower than 10 %**.

**4.2.4 Test set-up "Type B"**
- Any other test set-up configuration. Maximum influence of parasitic loads **i_para = 100 %** for Type B.
- Lower i_para values may be used by agreement with the approval authority.

### 4.3 Test procedure

- The basic torque loss map data shall be measured and calculated per paragraph 4.4.
- ►M1: Torque loss results shall be complemented per 4.4.8 and formatted per **Appendix 6** for processing by the simulation tool (VECTO). ◄

**4.3.1 Measurement equipment**
- Calibration laboratory facilities shall comply with either ►M3 **IATF 16949** ◄, **ISO 9000 series**, or **ISO/IEC 17025**.
- All laboratory reference measurement equipment used for calibration/verification shall be traceable to national (international) standards.

- **4.3.1.1 Torque measurement** — Uncertainty calculated per 4.4.7. Sample rate per 4.3.2.1.
- **4.3.1.2 Rotational speed** — Uncertainty of input/output speed sensors shall **not exceed ± 2 rpm**.
- **4.3.1.3 Temperatures** — Ambient temperature sensor uncertainty **≤ ± 1 °C**; oil temperature sensor uncertainty **≤ ± 0,5 °C**.

**4.3.2 Measurement signals and data recording**

Signals to be recorded for torque loss calculation:
(i) Input and output torques [Nm]
(ii) Input and/or output rotational speeds [rpm]
(iii) Ambient temperature [°C]
(iv) Oil temperature [°C]
(v) Temperature at the torque sensor ►M3 [°C] (optional) ◄

**4.3.2.1 Minimum sampling frequencies**

| Sensor | Minimum sampling frequency |
|---|---|
| Torque | 1 kHz |
| Rotational speed | 200 Hz |
| Temperatures | 10 Hz |

**4.3.2.2** — Recording rate for arithmetic mean determination at each grid point: **10 Hz or higher**. Raw data need not be reported. Signal filtering may be applied by agreement with the approval authority; any aliasing effect shall be avoided.

### ►M3 4.3.3 Torque range

The torque loss map extent to be measured is limited to the **least** of:
- either an **output torque of 10 kNm** for heavy lorries and heavy buses, or **2 kNm** for medium lorries;
- or an **input torque of 5 kNm** for heavy lorries and heavy buses, or **1 kNm** for medium lorries;
- or **the maximum engine power tolerated by the manufacturer** for a specific axle, or (for multiple driven axles) in accordance with the nominal power distribution. [this third bullet is ►B, unmarked for amendment continuation]

**4.3.3.1 (►B)** — The manufacturer may **extend** the measurement up to **20 kNm output torque** by:
- linear extrapolation of torque losses, OR
- performing measurements up to 20 kNm output torque in **2 000 Nm steps**, using an additional output-side torque sensor rated at 20 kNm (2-machine layout) or two 10 kNm sensors (3-machine layout).

If the smallest tire radius is reduced (e.g., product development) after completing measurement, or the test stand's physical boundaries are reached, missing points may be extrapolated by the manufacturer from the existing map, subject to:
- extrapolated points **shall not exceed 10 %** of all points in the map;
- a **penalty of 5 % torque loss** shall be added to the extrapolated points.

**►M3 4.3.3.2 Output torque steps to be measured:**

*Heavy lorries and heavy buses:*
| Range | Step |
|---|---|
| 250 Nm < Tout < 1 000 Nm | 250 Nm steps |
| 1 000 Nm ≤ Tout ≤ 2 000 Nm | 500 Nm steps |
| 2 000 Nm ≤ Tout ≤ 10 000 Nm | 1 000 Nm steps |
| Tout > 10 000 Nm | 2 000 Nm steps |

*Medium lorries:*
| Range | Step |
|---|---|
| 50 Nm < Tout < 200 Nm | 50 Nm steps |
| 200 Nm ≤ Tout ≤ 400 Nm | 100 Nm steps |
| 400 Nm ≤ Tout ≤ 2 000 Nm | 200 Nm steps |
| Tout > 2 000 Nm | 400 Nm steps |

### 4.3.4 Speed range (►B)

- Test speeds range from **50 rpm wheel speed** to the maximum speed.
- Maximum test speed = whichever of the following is reached **first**:
  - **4.3.4.1** — Maximum applicable axle input speed, which may be limited to the axle's design specification.
  - **4.3.4.2** ►M3: Maximum wheel speed, measured considering the smallest applicable tire diameter at a vehicle speed of **90 km/h** for medium and heavy lorries and **110 km/h** for heavy buses. ◄ If the smallest applicable tire diameter is not defined, 4.3.4.1 applies instead.

### ►M3 4.3.5 Wheel speed steps to be measured

- **50 rpm** step width for heavy lorries and heavy buses.
- **100 rpm** step width for medium lorries.
- Intermediate speed steps are permitted.

### 4.4 Measurement of torque loss maps for axles

**4.4.1 Testing sequence (►M3):** For each speed step, the torque loss shall be measured for each output torque step **starting from the lowest torque value upward to the maximum and downward to the minimum**. Speed steps may be run in any order. ►M1: The torque measurement sequence shall be performed and recorded **twice**. ◄ Interruptions for cooling/heating are permitted.

**►M3 4.4.2 Measurement duration:** Minimum **5 seconds**, maximum **20 seconds** per grid point.

**4.4.3 Averaging of grid points**
- ►M1: Recorded values for each grid point within the 5–20 s interval (per 4.4.2) shall be averaged to an arithmetic mean. ◄
- ►B: All four averaged intervals of corresponding speed/torque grid points from both sequences (upward and downward) shall be averaged to an arithmetic mean, resulting in **one torque loss value**.

**4.4.4 Torque loss calculation (at input side)**

As transcribed from source (OCR-corrupted symbols retained):
```
Tloss ¼ Tin Ä X Tout / igear
```
**Interpreted formula** (my reading of the OCR'd equation — flagged explicitly as interpretation, not verified against a clean PDF rendering):

$$T_{loss} = T_{in} - \frac{T_{out}}{i_{gear}}$$

where:
- T_loss = Torque loss of the axle at the input side [Nm]
- T_in = Input torque [Nm]
- i_gear = Axle gear ratio [-]
- T_out = Output torque [Nm]

**4.4.5 Measurement validation**
- ►M1 **4.4.5.1** — Averaged speed values per grid point (5–20 s interval) shall not deviate from the setting values by more than **± 5 rpm** for output speed.
- ►B **4.4.5.2** — Averaged output torque values (per 4.4.3) for each grid point shall not deviate more than **± 20 Nm or ± 1 %** from the torque set point, **whichever is higher**.
- **4.4.5.3** — If criteria are not met, the measurement is **void**; the entire affected speed step shall be repeated, and after passing, the data shall be consolidated.

**4.4.6 Uncertainty calculation**

Total uncertainty U_T,loss of the torque loss is calculated from:
i. Temperature effect
ii. Parasitic loads
iii. Uncertainty (incl. sensitivity tolerance, linearity, hysteresis, repeatability)

Based on sensor uncertainties at **95 % confidence level**, calculated per applied sensor (e.g., three-machine layout: U_T,in, U_T,out,1, U_T,out,2) as the square root of the sum of squares ("Gaussian law of error propagation").

**Formulas (as transcribed from OCR, symbols corrupted):**
```
UT;in=out ¼ 2 Ü sqrt( U²TKC + U²TK0 + U²cal + U²para )
UTKC ¼ (1/sqrt(3)) Ü (wtkc/Kref) Ü ΔK Ü Tc
UTK0 ¼ (1/sqrt(3)) Ü (wtk0/Kref) Ü ΔK Ü Tn
Ucal ¼ 1 Ü (wcal/kcal) Ü Tn
Upara ¼ (1/sqrt(3)) Ü wpara Ü Tn
wpara = senspara * ipara
```

**Interpreted formulas** (my cleaned reading — flagged as interpretation):

$$U_{T,in/out} = 2 \times \sqrt{U_{TKC}^2 + U_{TK0}^2 + U_{cal}^2 + U_{para}^2}$$

$$U_{TKC} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \times \frac{w_{tkc}}{K_{ref}} \times \Delta K \times T_c$$

$$U_{TK0} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \times \frac{w_{tk0}}{K_{ref}} \times \Delta K \times T_n$$

$$U_{cal} = \frac{w_{cal}}{k_{cal}} \times T_n$$

$$U_{para} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \times w_{para} \times T_n, \quad w_{para} = sens_{para} \times i_{para}$$

**Variable definitions (verbatim from source):**
- U_T,in/out = Uncertainty of input/output torque loss measurement separately for input and output torque; [Nm]
- i_gear = Axle gear ratio [-]
- U_TKC = Uncertainty by temperature influence on current torque signal; [Nm]
- w_tkc = Temperature influence on current torque signal per K_ref, declared by sensor manufacturer; [%]
- U_TK0 = Uncertainty by temperature influence on zero torque signal (related to nominal torque) [Nm]
- w_tk0 = Temperature influence on zero torque signal per K_ref (related to nominal torque), declared by sensor manufacturer; [%]
- K_ref = Reference temperature span for tkc and tk0, declared by sensor manufacturer; [°C]
- ΔK = Absolute difference in sensor temperature measured at torque sensor between calibration and measurement; if sensor temperature cannot be measured, default value ►M3 **ΔK = 15** ◄ shall be used [°C]
- T_c = Current/measured torque value at torque sensor; [Nm]
- T_n = Nominal torque value of torque sensor; [Nm]
- U_cal = Uncertainty by torque sensor calibration; [Nm]
- w_cal = Relative calibration uncertainty (related to nominal torque); [%]
- k_cal = Calibration advancement factor (if declared by sensor manufacturer, otherwise = 1)
- U_para = Uncertainty by parasitic loads; [Nm]
- w_para = sens_para × i_para — relative influence of forces and bending torques caused by misalignment
- sens_para = Maximum influence of parasitic loads for specific torque sensor declared by sensor manufacturer [%]; if not declared, default **= 1,0 %**
- i_para = Maximum influence of parasitic loads for specific torque sensor depending on test set-up (per 4.2.3 and 4.2.4)

| Parameter | Limit |

| U_T,out (one dynamometer on output side) | **15 Nm or 0,25 %** of measured torque, whichever allowed value is higher |
| U_T,out (two dynamometers, each output side) | **7,5 Nm or 0,25 %** of measured torque, whichever allowed value is higher |

### 4.4.8 Complement of torque loss map data

- **4.4.8.1** (►B) — If torque values exceed the upper range limit, **linear extrapolation** shall be applied, using the slope of linear regression based on all measured torque points for the corresponding speed step.
- **►M3 4.4.8.2** — For output torque range values **below** the lowest measured grid point (per 4.3.3.2), the torque loss value of the **lowest measured grid point** shall be applied.
- **4.4.8.3** (►B) — For **0 rpm** wheel speed, the torque loss values of the **50 rpm** speed step shall be applied.
- **4.4.8.4** (►B) — For **negative input torques** (e.g., overrun, free rolling), the torque loss value measured for the related **positive** input torque shall be applied.
- **►M1 4.4.8.5** — For a **tandem axle**, the combined torque loss map for both axles is calculated from the single-axle test results at the input side, and the input torques are also added:
  ```
  Tloss,rep,tdm = Tloss,rep,1 + Tloss,rep,2
  Tin,tdm = Tin,1 + Tin,2
  ```

## 5. Conformity of the certified CO2 emissions and fuel consumption related properties (CoP)

**5.1** — Every approved axle type shall be manufactured to conform to the description in the certification form/annexes, conforming to the approved type. ►M3: Conformity procedures shall comply with those in **Article 31 of Regulation (EU) 2018/858**. ◄

**5.2** — Checked based on the certificate description (Appendix 1) and the specific conditions of this paragraph.

**5.3** — The manufacturer shall test **annually** at least the number of axles indicated in **Table 1**, based on annual production numbers. Only axles falling under this Regulation's requirements count toward production numbers.

**5.4** — Each tested axle shall be representative of a specific family.

**5.5** — Table 1 gives the number of families of SR axles and other axles to be tested.

**►M3 Table 1 — Sample size for conformity testing**

| Production number | Number of tests for SR axles | Number of tests for other axles than SR axles |
|---|---|---|
| 0 – 40 000 | 2 | 1 |
| 40 001 – 50 000 | 2 | 2 |
| 50 001 – 60 000 | 3 | 2 |
| 60 001 – 70 000 | 4 | 2 |
| 70 001 – 80 000 | 5 | 2 |
| 80 001 and more | 5 | 3 |

**5.6** — The **two** axle families with the **highest production volumes** shall always be tested. The manufacturer shall justify the number/choice of tests (e.g., by sales figures) to the approval authority. The remaining families to be tested are agreed between manufacturer and approval authority.

**5.7** — The approval authority, together with the manufacturer, identifies the axle type(s) to be tested, and ensures the selected type(s) are manufactured per the same standards as serial production.

**5.8** — If a test result per point 6 is **higher** than specified in point 6.4, **three additional axles** from the same family shall be tested. If **at least one fails**, provisions of **Article 23** apply.

### 6. Production conformity testing

**6.1** — Methods, applied upon **prior agreement** between approval authority and applicant:
- (a) Torque loss measurement per this Annex, full procedure, limited to grid points per 6.2.
- (b) Same as (a), excepting the run-in procedure. A corrective factor may be applied for run-in characteristics, determined per good engineering judgement and agreement with the approval authority.
- (c) Measurement of drag torque per paragraph 6.3. Manufacturer may choose a run-in procedure (good engineering judgement) up to **100 h**.

**6.2** — For CoP under 6.1(a) or (b): grid points limited to **4 grid points** from the approved torque loss map.

**6.2.1** — The full torque loss map shall be segmented into **three equidistant speed ranges** and **three torque ranges**, forming **nine control areas** (Figure 2 — **[Figure referenced in source but not visually extracted from this text-only OCR]**, titled "Speed and torque range for conformity of the certified CO2 emissions and fuel consumption related properties testing", ►M1).

**6.2.2** — For **four** control areas, one point each shall be selected, measured, and evaluated per the full procedure (section 4.4):
(i) Control areas selected depending on axle line:
  - **SR axles** (incl. tandem combinations): Control areas **5, 6, 8, 9**
  - **HR axles** (incl. tandem combinations): Control areas **2, 3, 4, 5**
(ii) The selected point shall be at the **centre** of the area (speed range and applicable torque range for that speed).
(iii) To allow comparison with the certification loss map, the selected point shall be **moved to the closest measured point** from the approved map. ►M3: If the selected point is midway between two approved points, the **higher** point shall be used. ◄

**6.2.3** — Efficiency calculation for each measured/corresponding point:

```

```

- η_avr,high speed = average efficiency for high speed

**6.2.5** — For CoP under 6.1(c): the drag torque of the **parent axle** of the tested axle's family shall be determined during certification. ►M3: This can be done before or after run-in (per point 3.1), or by extrapolating all torque map values for each speed step down to 0 Nm — extrapolation shall be **linear or polynomial second order**, whichever gives the lower standard deviation. ◄

### 6.3 Determination of drag torque

**6.3.1** — Simplified test set-up: one electric machine, one torque sensor on the input side. ►M3: For a single portal axle with different-length output shafts, a test setup with **two** electric machines and two torque sensors (one on each output) is also permitted, with both output shafts driven synchronously in the driving direction; final drag torque = sum of both output torques. ◄

**6.3.2** — Test conditions per paragraph 4.1 apply. Torque uncertainty calculation may be omitted.

**6.3.3** — Drag torque measured in the speed range of the approved type per paragraph 4.3.4, under the speed steps per 4.3.5.

### 6.4 Conformity of the certified CO2 emissions and fuel consumption related properties — test assessment

**6.4.1** — A CoP test is **passed** when one of the following applies:

(a) ►M1: If torque loss measurement per 6.1(a) or (b) is conducted — the tested axle's average efficiency during CoP shall **not be lower than**:

## Appendix 3 — Calculation of the standard torque loss

The standard torque losses for axles are given in **Table 1**. The standard table values consist of the sum of a **generic constant efficiency value** (covering load-dependent losses) and a **generic basic drag torque loss** (covering drag losses at low loads).

**Tandem axles** shall be calculated using a combined efficiency for an axle including drive-thru (SRT, HRT) plus the matching single axle (SR, HR).

**Table 1 — Generic efficiency and drag loss**

| Basic function | Generic efficiency η | Drag torque (wheel side) Td0 = T0 + T1 × i_gear |
|---|---|---|
| Single reduction axle (SR) | 0,98 | T0 = 70 Nm, T1 = 20 Nm |
| Single reduction tandem axle (SRT) / Single portal axle (SP) | 0,96 | T0 = 80 Nm, T1 = 20 Nm |
| Hub reduction axle (HR) | 0,97 | T0 = 70 Nm, T1 = 20 Nm |
| Hub reduction tandem axle (HRT) | 0,95 | T0 = 90 Nm, T1 = 20 Nm |
| All other axle technologies | 0,90 | T0 = 150 Nm, T1 = 50 Nm |

*(Note: the source text groups "Single reduction tandem axle (SRT) / single portal axle (SP)" under one row with one efficiency value, 0,96 — transcribed exactly as laid out in the source, even though SRT and SP are distinct axle lines per the Section 2 definitions above.)*

**Basic drag torque (wheel side):**
$$T_{d0} = T_0 + T_1 \times i_{gear}$$
(using the T0, T1 values from Table 1)

**Standard torque loss (input side):**

As transcribed:
```
Tloss;std ¼ Td0 þ Tout/η Ä Tout/igear
```
**Interpreted:**
$$T_{loss,std} = T_{d0} + \frac{T_{out}}{\eta} - \frac{T_{out}}{i_{gear}}$$

where:
- T_loss,std = Standard torque loss at the input side [Nm]
- T_d0 = Basic drag torque over the complete speed range [Nm]
- i_gear = Axle gear ratio [-]
- η = Generic efficiency for load-dependent losses [-]
- T_out = torque [Nm]

**Corresponding input-side torque:**

As transcribed:
```
Tin ¼ Tout/igear þ Tloss;std
```
**Interpreted:**
$$T_{in} = \frac{T_{out}}{i_{gear}} + T_{loss,std}$$

where T_in = Input torque [Nm].

> **This is directly relevant to the HDEV sizing tool**: Appendix 3 is the "generic/standard values" shortcut that lets a simulation proceed without a manufacturer-measured axle loss map. For an auditable tool, it would be worth flagging to the user whether they intend to support both the measured-map path (Annex VII main body, Appendix 6 parameters) and this standard-values fallback.

## ►M3 Appendix 4 — Family Concept

**1.** The applicant submits an application for a certificate for an axle family, based on family criteria in paragraph 3. An axle family is characterized by common design and performance parameters. The manufacturer may decide family membership as long as paragraph 4 [sic, likely refers to paragraph 3] criteria are respected; additional, more-restrictive criteria may be introduced by the manufacturer (not necessarily performance-relevant). The axle family must be approved by the approval authority, with appropriate performance information on family members provided to the authority.

**2. Special cases** — Where there is interaction between parameters, this must be considered to ensure only axles with similar characteristics are grouped. Such cases shall be identified by the manufacturer and notified to the approval authority, to be taken into account as a criterion for creating a new family. Parameters not listed in paragraph 3 but with a strong influence on performance shall likewise be identified (per good engineering practice) and notified.

**3. Parameters defining an axle family:**

**3.1 Axle category:**
(a) Single reduction axle (SR)
(b) Hub reduction axle (HR)
(c) Single portal axle (SP)
(d) Single reduction tandem axle (SRT)
(e) Hub reduction tandem axle (HRT)
(f) Same inner axle housing geometry between differential bearings and horizontal plane of centre of pinion shaft per drawing specification (exception for SP). Geometry changes from an optional differential lock integration are permitted within the same family. Mirror-inverted axle casings can be combined in the same family as the origin axles, provided bevel gear sets are adapted to the other running direction (change of spiral direction).
(g) ►M1: Crown wheel diameter (**+1,5 % / –8 %** ref. to the largest drawing diameter) ◄
(h) Vertical hypoid offset pinion/crown wheel within **± 2 mm**
(i) For SP: Pinion angle with respect to horizontal plane within **± 5°**
(j) For SP: Angle between pinion axle and crown wheel axle within **± 3,5°**
(k) For HR, HRT, FHR*, SP: same number of planetary gear and spur wheels *(note: "FHR" appears here in the source without being defined in Section 2's definitions list within this extracted range — possibly "Full Hub Reduction" or a typo; flagged as a term not defined within this extraction range)*
(l) ►M1: Gear ratio of every gear step within an axle in a range of 2 [unit unspecified in source — likely meant as a ratio range/tolerance factor], as long as only one gear set is changed ◄
(m) Oil level within **± 10 mm** or oil volume **± 0,5 litre**, referring to drawing specification and vehicle installation position
(n) Same oil type viscosity grade (recommended factory fill)
(o) ►M3: Type of bearings (inner diameter, outer diameter, width) at corresponding positions (if fitted), within **± 1 mm** of drawing reference ◄
(p) ►M3: Type of sealing ◄

**4. Choice of the parent axle:**
- **4.1** — The parent axle is the one with the **highest axle ratio**. If more than two axles share the same ratio, the manufacturer must provide analysis to determine the worst-case axle as parent.
- **4.2** — The approval authority may require additional axle testing if it concludes the worst-case torque loss is better characterized that way; the manufacturer must submit information to determine the axle with the likely highest torque loss.
- **4.3** — If other features within the family may affect torque losses, they shall also be identified and considered in parent axle selection.
