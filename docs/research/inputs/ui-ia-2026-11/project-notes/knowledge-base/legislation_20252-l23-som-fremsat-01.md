## 3. General Remarks (*Almindelige bemærkninger*) — Section 1: Introduction

**Stated purpose (formål) of the bill**, as given verbatim in substance:

> The bill aims to ensure a more expedient and efficient utilization of capacity in the collective electricity supply grid, in a situation where grid capacity has become a scarce resource and demand for electricity is rising. The bill is expected to be implemented through executive orders that will help ensure faster access to green, secure and affordable energy for households, businesses and society's critical functions.

**Stated diagnosis of the problem:**
- Demand for grid connection has risen markedly in recent years due to electrification, expansion of renewable energy, and establishment of new types of electricity consumption and energy storage.
- Existing grid capacity is insufficient to meet all connection requests — Denmark faces a "new energy-policy reality" in which grid capacity has become a scarce resource, risking to challenge electrification, new green technologies, a competitive business sector, and secure conditions for consumers.
- Under current regulation, connection requests are in practice largely processed on an ongoing basis, generally in order of receipt and according to project maturity ("first-come, first-served" in practice, though not a formal legal principle). In a situation of scarce capacity, this risks that capacity is not sufficiently directed to society's most important needs, and grid build-out has in practice been planned to accommodate whatever demand arises.

**Stated aim of the bill:** to establish a basis for more *active prioritization* of access to grid capacity, so that capacity is allocated in line with societally prioritized needs and grid planning/expansion is likewise based on such prioritized needs rather than simply on aggregate demand.

**The bill's three main legislative components** (as summarized in the introduction):
1. A new legal authorization (*hjemmel*) to set rules on **prioritization** of grid-connection requests, including grid-friendliness (*netvenlighed*), applicant information/documentation duties, and **refusal** of requests when capacity is insufficient. Prioritization must be based on objective and legitimate (*saglige*) criteria within overarching categories reflecting societal priorities, the green transition, and efficient grid operation. The authorization may also be used to regulate **disposition of already-allocated capacity** (i.e., treating certain changes to existing connections as new connection requests for prioritization purposes). **It will not be possible to strip customers of capacity already allocated to them.**
2. A new authorization requiring collective electricity supply undertakings, when refusing connection, to provide information on measures that could improve the project's prospects of obtaining connection (including on improving grid-friendliness).
3. An expansion and clarification of the existing § 67 authorization to cover connection of **all types of** electricity/CHP production facilities (including those using fossil fuel), plus electricity consumption facilities and energy storage facilities — folding in, as a simplification, facilities currently regulated under the VE Act. Purpose: to prevent circumvention of the above prioritization measures and support expedient, efficient connection arrangements for all grid users.

## 4. Section 2.1 — Prioritization of Capacity in the Collective Electricity Grid

### 2.1.1 Current Law (*Gældende ret*)

- Access to the grid is regulated in the Electricity Supply Act, which implements EU third-party access rules. § 20(1)(2)-(3) requires transmission and grid (distribution) undertakings to connect suppliers/purchasers of electricity and provide necessary transport capacity/access.
- § 24(1)-(2) (current): everyone has a right to use the collective grid for electricity transport against payment, and collective electricity supply undertakings may not discriminate between users or categories of users.
- "Collective electricity supply undertakings" (*kollektive elforsyningsvirksomheder*) = transmission and distribution undertakings (§ 5 no. 23). **Energinet** is currently the transmission undertaking; grid companies (*netvirksomheder*) are the distribution undertakings.
- The combination of §§ 20 and 24 today creates, in effect, **a right to grid connection** for those who request it. This implements Article 6(1) of the Electricity Market Directive on third-party access.
- **The Electricity Supply Act currently contains NO provision allowing refusal of connection requests**, including for lack of capacity. An express refusal right (former § 25(5)) existed but was **repealed in 2004** as part of implementing the EU's 2nd liberalization package, and has not been reintroduced since.
- The Act also currently contains **no authorization for the Minister to set rules on prioritizing connection requests**.
- The European Commission's guidance document **C(2025) 6703 of 19 December 2025**, "Guidance on efficient and timely grid connection" (*Vejledning om effektiv og rettidig nettilslutning*), is cited as confirming that Member States, within the Electricity Market Directive framework, may set detailed rules for handling connection requests, including prioritization among requests, provided this is done on objective, transparent and non-discriminatory criteria — and that **EU law does not require a "first-come, first-served" principle**.
- Grid companies today **can, to some extent**, prioritize among connection requests as part of their operational duties, on objective/legitimate criteria and subject to the non-discrimination prohibition — but this **does not extend to** establishing a general priority order between different types of grid users or capacity needs based on broader energy-policy or societal considerations.
- In practice, prioritization today is based on administrative/technical criteria: order of receipt, project maturity, documented progress, and considerations of efficient grid operation/loading. Methods for network access must be approved by the **Forsyningstilsynet** (Danish Utility Regulator/Danish Energy Regulatory Authority).
- **§ 24(3)** (current) authorizes rules on capacity-reservation agreements and their administration, but **does not** authorize rules on subsequent disposition of capacity already allocated. It is noted that grid companies' existing connection terms already permit temporary reduction of delivery volume if capacity is unused (e.g., a non-consumption-based charge), without this constituting an actual loss of the entitlement.
- **§ 68 a(1)** (current) allows the Minister to require collective electricity supply undertakings to make decisions (*afgørelser*) on matters covered by specified provisions — but **§ 24 is not among the provisions currently cross-referenced there**, meaning connection/access decisions under § 24 are not currently formalized as appealable administrative decisions in the same way.
- A Forsyningstilsynet ruling of **9 May 2025** (case no. 24/02409) on a complaint against an Energinet connection decision is cited: the Ministry considers that this ruling created uncertainty about grid companies' competence to decide whether a facility should be connected as one or multiple installations, and takes the position that this competence follows implicitly from §§ 20, 22 and 28 of the Act.
- **VE-lovens § 30** (current, VE Act) is the parallel/overlapping authorization for connection of wind turbines, solar-PV, wave power, and hydropower — historically established because these were not covered by Electricity Supply Act § 67 (limited to "environmentally friendly electricity and CHP production facilities").
- Current § 6 e (compensation scheme) and § 12 b(2) (100kV+ transmission reinforcement cost allocation) are described as **no longer relevant/current** (see rationale below).

### 2.1.2 The Electricity Market Directive (*Elmarkedsdirektivet*, Directive (EU) 2019/944, as amended by (EU) 2024/1711)

- **Article 6(1):** Member States must ensure a third-party access system to transmission/distribution systems, based on published tariffs applied objectively and without discrimination between system users; tariffs/methodologies must be approved per Article 59 before entry into force and published.
- **Article 6(2):** A TSO/DSO may refuse access if the system lacks necessary capacity. Refusal must be duly justified based on objective, technically and economically sound criteria, having particular regard to Article 9. The regulatory authority must ensure such criteria are applied consistently and that the refused party has access to a dispute-resolution procedure. **When access is refused for EV charging points, information about measures needed to reinforce the network MUST always be given.** The party requesting such information may be charged a reasonable fee reflecting the cost of provision.

### 2.1.3 Ministry's Considerations (*Klima-, Energi- og Forsyningsministeriets overvejelser*)

Key points:
- Demand growth is driven by electrification of transport, heating and industry, plus new large consumption facilities: **data centers, PtX (power-to-X) plants, and batteries**.
- Capacity in parts of the grid is under significant pressure; demand in several places substantially exceeds available and planned capacity — a rapid, significant challenge requiring short-timeframe capacity-allocation decisions.
- Current allocation by order-of-receipt/maturity is judged **not appropriate** where demand markedly exceeds capacity — risk that capacity disproportionately goes to a few large projects without adequate regard to overall grid utilization and societal priorities.
- The Ministry concludes it should use the discretion the Commission's guidance confirms exists under Article 6, and set **overarching categories** for prioritizing connection requests reflecting: ordinary consumption needs of households/businesses, and the green transition/electrification.
- **Grid-friendliness (netvenlighed)** should be a factor in disposing capacity within categories — this presupposes Energinet and grid companies develop relevant "grid products" (*netprodukter*) supporting flexible grid use.
- Regulation should **not be limited to new requests** — dispositions regarding already-allocated capacity that create a new/changed capacity need should, in appropriate circumstances, be captured, to prevent circumvention via changes to existing connections. **The proposed regulation will not cause a user to lose the right to already-agreed capacity**, but may involve **postponement** of the ability to bring unused capacity into use.
- **Expropriation assessment:** The measure is, as a starting point, assessed as **not** constituting expropriation. However, the Ministry **cannot rule out** that in certain situations, on a concrete assessment, the scheme could have the character of expropriation — in which case the affected undertaking would be entitled to compensation under **§ 73(1) of the Danish Constitution (Grundloven)**.
- Prioritization should be supplemented by a right to **refuse** connection requests when capacity constraints and the set prioritization mean a request should not be granted. Refusal decisions are to be made by collective electricity supply undertakings and appealable to the Forsyningstilsynet.
- The § 67 clarification (connection competence) is explicitly linked to and meant to work together with the § 24 prioritization/disposition rules — together intended to support more effective and expedient use of grid capacity.

### 2.1.4 The Proposed Scheme (*Den foreslåede ordning*) — THE CORE OF THE BILL

This is the most detailed and substantively important section for grid-connection queue reform purposes.

**Legal mechanism:** New § 24(4) authorizes the Minister to set rules on (a) prioritization of connection requests, including grid-friendliness and applicant information/documentation duties, and (b) disposition of already-allocated capacity. New § 24(4), 2nd sentence, separately authorizes rules on **refusal** of connection requests. New § 24(5) authorizes rules requiring undertakings that refuse connection to provide improvement-measure information, optionally fee-based.

**Pooled processing:** Rules are expected to require **larger connection requests to be processed jointly in periodic pools** (*periodiske puljer*), to give sufficient overview of competing requests within the relevant grid area for consistent prioritization. **Smaller requests (ordinary households, smaller businesses) are expected to continue to be processed on an ongoing/ad hoc basis.**

**Applicant documentation:** Rules are expected on what information/documentation applicants must provide to enable categorization, grid-friendliness assessment, and administration of the model, aiming for a uniform, adequate information basis.

**Scope — new requests AND existing-capacity dispositions:** The model will apply not only to new connection requests but also to dispositions regarding already-allocated capacity that create a new/changed capacity need — e.g., **re-establishing a decommissioned connection point, increasing a previously reduced delivery volume, or activating previously unused capacity**. Such dispositions are expected to be treated, for prioritization purposes, as new connection requests. This does **not** change the customer's existing entitlement to allocated capacity — the prioritization applies only to how the *new or changed* need is accommodated given the current capacity situation, and does not constitute a final decision (*afgørelse*) on whether/when the disposition can proceed. Conversely, dispositions that do **not** create a new/increased draw on capacity (e.g., facilities reducing/smoothing a customer's load, or storing self-generated electricity for later self-consumption within already-allocated capacity) will **not**, as a starting point, be treated as new connection requests.

**Four priority categories** (expected first implementation, to be detailed in executive orders):

| Category | Description (as stated) |
|---|---|
| **Category 1 — Protected needs** (*beskyttede behov*) | Ordinary and foreseeable consumption growth in the existing customer base (e.g., a household installing a heat pump or acquiring an EV — NOT major reconfigurations/changes of supply type); new households; smaller businesses (expected to be delimited by a capacity/power-draw threshold); societally critical functions (hospitals, defense, emergency services, and similar, including data centers tied to critical functions); **certain publicly accessible EV charging-infrastructure projects for road vehicles may also fall in this category**. |
| **Category 2 — Other projects** | The general/default category for business and electrification projects not falling under the other categories — notably projects supporting electrification and the green transition (industry, transport, heat electrification; renewable generation, CCS, hydrogen, biogas, PtX); functions as a catch-all for everything not in categories 1, 3 or 4. |
| **Category 3 — Energy storage facilities** | Batteries and other forms of energy storage, and storage capacity in co-located facilities established **beyond** the user's ordinary production/consumption need. Key test: whether storage capacity is added *on top of* ordinary needs — mere ability to shift load or participate in ancillary-services markets does not itself place a project in Category 3. |
| **Category 4 — Certain large power consumers** | Characterized by very large capacity need and material impact on the grid capacity situation — initial implementation expected to cover **data centers** (defined by reference to Directive (EU) 2023/1791 on energy efficiency and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1364 on data centre sustainability: a building or group of buildings housing premises/technical infrastructure primarily for data processing, storage, and related IT functions, incl. servers, network equipment, storage systems and support functions). **Not all data centers fall in Category 4** — those tied to critical functions fall in Category 1 instead. |

**Non-sequential application:** Categories are NOT applied strictly sequentially — lower-category projects can still obtain connection where compatible with the capacity situation; requests across categories can be processed in parallel.

**Capacity reservation for Category 1:** Rules are expected to reserve a portion of available/future capacity on an ongoing basis for Category 1 needs (based on grid companies' estimates of demand development, fed to Energinet), so that ordinary/foreseeable consumption growth and other protected needs can be met even under capacity constraints. For critical functions, reservation presupposes a concrete identified project.

**"Netvenlighed" (grid-friendliness) as the governing allocation principle *within* categories (except Category 1):** Defined as a project's impact on the grid capacity situation and its contribution to appropriate/efficient use of available capacity. Assessed by the collective electricity supply undertaking based on objective/technical factors: load profile, flexibility, contribution to grid robustness, technical characteristics, and interaction with other grid use; also weight given to willingness to shift consumption/production to periods of spare capacity, reduce peak loading, or otherwise support smoother/more efficient grid use. **If grid-friendly solutions/products cannot be offered to all relevant projects due to operational/capacity constraints, they will first be offered to higher-priority categories.** The Forsyningstilsynet will **not** need to approve which factors undertakings may weigh in assessing grid-friendliness generally, but **will continue to approve concrete grid products, methods, terms and tariffs** under ordinary rules (e.g., an interruptibility product's content must be approved, but not the fact that use of that product feeds into the grid-friendliness assessment).

**Refusal rules — the key operative logic:**
- Categories 1 and 2 requests **cannot be refused for lack of capacity** — if they cannot be immediately accommodated, they instead enter the ongoing capacity-disposition queue under the prioritization model and await available capacity (i.e., they queue, they are not rejected).
- Categories 3 and 4 requests that **cannot be accommodated through a sufficient degree of grid-friendliness without limiting the ability to allocate capacity to higher-priority needs** are, depending on circumstances, expected to be **refused**.
- Refusal thus requires: (i) capacity constraints exist; (ii) the request falls in Category 3 or 4; AND (iii) the project cannot, through sufficient grid-friendliness, be accommodated without constraining higher-priority needs. Mere existence of a capacity shortage is **not by itself sufficient** grounds for refusal.
- If a grid-friendly product/solution that could accommodate the project is not yet established or (where required) not yet approved by the Forsyningstilsynet, the request may be **kept in the queue** pending the product becoming available — not automatically refused, though this doesn't grant a right to connection either.
- Refusals must be made **by decision (afgørelse)** of the collective electricity supply undertaking (linked to the § 68 a(1) amendment adding "24," — see § 1 no. 11) and are **appealable to the Forsyningstilsynet**.
- A refused request is meant to **drop out of the demand baseline used for forward grid planning** — i.e., refused/unaccommodated Category 3/4 demand will not, by itself, justify future network expansion.
- Where the capacity constraint concerns the transmission grid or the transmission/distribution interface, the grid company's capacity assessment is expected to be based on information from **Energinet** (coordination between Energinet and grid companies is expected).

**Information-on-refusal duty (§ 24(5)):** Undertakings refusing connection must, on request, provide information on measures that could improve the project's prospects (e.g., changes to consumption pattern, flexibility, use of relevant grid products; broad information on network-reinforcement needs where relevant). This information does **not** constitute a commitment to future connection. The duty **may be satisfied via general guidance/website information** where adequate for the specific case — undertakings are **not** required to prepare bespoke solution proposals or provide project-development/consulting assistance, though must (without separate charge) give information sufficient for the applicant to understand the refusal grounds and assess appeal prospects. A **fee** may be charged for bespoke information (capped at actual cost of providing it).

**Oversight:** The Forsyningstilsynet is expected to (a) handle complaints against refusal decisions, and (b) supervise undertakings' compliance with the information duty and administration of certain rules issued under the authorization.

**Constitutional/legal-nature note:** The prioritization applied to individual requests is described as **preliminary and indicative** (not a final decision / "afgørelse") — it creates no legally protected expectation of connection; an actual right to connection arises only once a binding connection agreement is concluded or a connection decision is made. Only the *refusal* itself is a decision.

## 5. Section 2.2 — Connection to the Collective Electricity Grid (§ 67 expansion)

### 2.2.1 Current Law
- § 67 (current) authorizes the Minister to set rules on connection of "environmentally friendly electricity and combined-heat-and-power production facilities" only.
- § 68 a(1) currently allows delegating decision-making competence on § 67 matters (among others) to collective electricity supply undertakings.
- Executive Order no. 1343 of 29 November 2024 on grid connection of electricity production facilities ("nettilslutningsbekendtgørelsen") — issued under both § 67 (Electricity Supply Act) and § 30 (VE Act) — currently covers environmentally friendly electricity/CHP facilities, wind turbines, solar-PV, wave power and hydropower.
- **§ 67 currently contains NO authorization** for connection rules regarding electricity consumption facilities, energy storage facilities, or fossil-fuel-based production facilities — a regulatory gap.
- Energinet/grid companies are noted to have a degree of discretion (*skøn*) regarding connection-relevant matters; Energinet is subject to the Public Administration Act (*forvaltningsloven*) per § 17 of the Energinet Act; grid companies (not part of public administration) are expected by the Ministry to observe administrative-law principles of objectivity, equality and proportionality.
- The **9 May 2025 Forsyningstilsynet ruling** (case 24/02409) assessed a complaint under the connection order's § 3(1) and the EU **RfG Regulation** ((EU) 2016/631, "Requirements for Generators") — creating the uncertainty referenced above about competence to decide single- vs. multi-facility connection treatment.

### 2.2.2 Ministry's Considerations
- The manner of connecting facilities affects the grid, including security of supply; it is important Energinet/grid companies can connect facilities in the most expedient way for the collective grid.
- Ministry concludes § 67 should be widened to cover electricity consumption facilities, energy storage facilities, and fossil-fuel-based production facilities.
- Simplification: fold the two existing separate authorizations (Electricity Supply Act § 67 for environmentally friendly generation; VE Act § 30 for wind/solar/wave/hydro) into **one consolidated authorization** under § 67.
- § 6 e (compensation scheme) and § 12 b(2) (100kV+ cost allocation) are assessed as **no longer current/relevant** — background: the compensation scheme related transitionally to the 2022 phase-out of an equalization scheme (*udligningsordning*) for renewable-connection costs, financed via the PSO system (abolished end of 2021); a temporary state-budget-financed compensation scheme covered the 2022 transition year until geographically differentiated connection charges/injection tariffs for producers could be introduced from 1 January 2023. No pending applications or complaints remain under this scheme. Similarly, producer connection costs to the 100kV+ transmission grid are now governed by producer-payment methodologies approved by the Forsyningstilsynet under § 73 a, consistent with **Article 18 of the Electricity Market Regulation** (*elmarkedsforordningen*) — making § 12 b(2)'s allocation rule obsolete.

### 2.2.3 The Proposed Scheme
- § 67 is broadened to cover: **all** electricity production facilities (environmentally friendly and fossil-fuel-based electricity/CHP plants, wind turbines, solar-PV, wave power, hydropower) **plus** electricity consumption facilities and energy storage facilities.
- **Examples of "electricity consumption facilities" given:** electric boilers/heating elements (*elpatroner*), heat pumps, EV charging stations (*ladestandere*), and electrolysis facilities — but explicitly **excluding** ordinary household electrical appliances (e.g., washing machines).
- "Energy storage facilities" (*energilageranlæg*) — defined by reference to Electricity Supply Act § 5 no. 16 [definition itself not reproduced in the extracted bill text — **[unclear from source]**, refer to § 5 no. 16 of the consolidated Act directly].
- The broadened § 67 automatically falls within existing § 68 a(1) (decision-delegation to grid undertakings), § 68 a(1) 2nd sentence (administrative-task delegation), § 68 a(2) (appeal to the Minister, subject to Forsyningstilsynet carve-outs), and § 90 no. 1 (rules potentially barring ministerial appeal for certain decisions). The Forsyningstilsynet remains the appeal body for decisions under the connection order's §§ 3–4, and remains the appeal body under **RfG Regulation Art. 7(8)** and **DCC Regulation ((EU) 2016/1388) Art. 6(8)**.
- It is expected future executive orders will explicitly confirm Energinet/grid companies' competence to decide whether a facility is connected as one or multiple installations — **but they will NOT gain power to force merging/coupling of already-existing separately-connected facilities or projects with existing connection agreements.**
- VE Act § 30 is repealed as duplicative; VE Act § 2(1)(4) is clarified to state the VE Act's scope also covers solar-PV installations etc., not only wind turbines (a clarification, not a substantive change).
