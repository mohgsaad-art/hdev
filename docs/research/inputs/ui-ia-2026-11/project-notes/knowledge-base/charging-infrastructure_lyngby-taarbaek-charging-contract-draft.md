## Contract Period (Section 3)
- Effective from signature; runs from **[dato1]** (expected commissioning date, per supplier's tender) to **[dato2]**.
- **[dato2] is explicitly defined as dato1 + 8 years.**
- Extension option: up to **2 × 12 months** on unchanged terms.
- Municipality can terminate for cause ("saglig grund") with **9 months' notice**.
- **Early-termination compensation formula (verbatim):**
  > "De samlede dokumenterede etableringsomkostninger x ((den resterende periode målt i år) / 8 år)"
  (Total documented establishment/installation costs × (remaining period in years / 8 years))

## General Obligations (Section 4)
- Contract covers supplier's installation and operation of charge points per requirements spec, price list ("tilbudslisten") and the supplier's tender, at locations to be inserted ("[indsæt lokationer]" — placeholder, not filled in this draft).
- Contract also functions as permission ("tilladelse") to use/control the named locations under contract terms.
- Mutual obligation of loyal cooperation; both parties must allocate necessary time/staff resources.
- Municipality must ensure supplier has access to and control of the sites.
- **Compensation for restricted site access:** If municipality limits supplier's full control of a site (e.g., site given to a third party), supplier is entitled to compensation calculated from:
  > Supplier's kWh sales revenue **minus** supplier's costs, over the **most recent 3 months** prior to the access restriction, scaled by the extent (time and area) of the restriction. Supplier must calculate/document the compensation amount.
- Parties may negotiate expansion or relocation of sites. If municipality wants to expand an existing site and the incumbent supplier declines to add chargers there, municipality can award the expansion area to another supplier.
- On relocation during the contract term, supplier is compensated as if the contract were terminated (per Section 3 formula).
- If rule/regulatory changes affect the contract, supplier must promptly inform municipality of the consequences.
- **Supplier reporting obligations (data to be sent to municipality on request), listed verbatim categories:**
  - When charging occurs ("Hvornår der lades el")
  - How much electricity is charged ("Hvor meget el der lades")
  - Duration of each individual charging session
  - Which payment method customers (possibly split by customer segment) choose, e.g. subscription, mobile payment, or cash
  - What the customer/segments pay to charge. **Weekly updated kWh list prices for non-subscription customers must be published on the operator's website.**
  - Actual uptime/downtime ("Reelle oppe-/nedetider")
- On contract termination, supplier must make relevant data available to support a new competitive tender, and must not obstruct a successor supplier's transition preparations, cooperating to minimize disruption to the municipality and its citizens.

## Installation Obligations (Section 5)
- Supplier must install charge points at all locations named in Section 4.
- Must obtain necessary permits from the road authority before installation (e.g., excavation permit — "gravetilladelse").
- Must minimize disruption to surrounding sites/users during installation.
- Supplier bears **all costs and risk** of installation at its own expense.
- Must restore site appearance to pre-installation condition as far as possible ("så vidt muligt").

## Operations & Maintenance Obligations (Section 6)
- Supplier must operate/maintain charge points and ensure citizen access to charging **24 hours a day, every day of the year**.
- Must keep charge points well-maintained, well-run, clean, and keep software updated.
- Must handle fault/error correction per the requirements specification.
- **Supplier may not conduct any other business activity from the charge points besides sale of EV charging** (e.g., no retail sales).
- Municipality handles snow clearance, sweeping, salting, and weed control at sites, per its standard for similar areas.
- Municipality commits to publicizing charge point locations on its website, GIS map, and via signage near each site.

## Obligations on Termination (Section 7)
- Regardless of cause of termination, supplier must leave sites cleared and reinstated, unless otherwise agreed.
- Reinstated areas (and adjoining areas) must appear uniform/consistent.
- **Reinstatement deadline: agreed with municipality but must not exceed 3 months.** If missed, municipality may have the work done at supplier's expense.
- If a site continues to function as an EV charging/parking location after contract end (for a successor supplier), the outgoing supplier has the right to **sell its grid connection contribution ("tilslutningsbidrag")** to the incoming supplier, at documented current market price ("gældende dagspris"). Municipality will assist in incorporating that price into a new agreement.

## Pricing (Section 8)
- Charging prices per supplier's tender (to potentially be inserted into contract text — not filled here).
- Prices charged to municipality's citizens must cover **all** of supplier's contract-related costs; supplier's costs are "of no concern" to the municipality (i.e., municipality does not subsidize/reimburse).
- **Municipality provides the sites free of charge ("gratis") to the supplier for the whole contract period.**
- Prices are **fixed for the first year** of the contract.
- After year 1, either party may request price adjustment based on documented normal/customary price regulation, **capped at a maximum of 5% upward per year**.
- Supplier may request adjustment beyond 5% if electricity prices rise extraordinarily in a period — must include documentation of the price increase and an account of how the supplier will track the electricity price development so the per-kWh price returns to the originally agreed level (incl. the max 5% adjustment).
- Price changes require **mutual agreement**.
- Supplier must keep municipality informed of price regulations affecting contract services and document, on request, what constitutes "customary" regulation.
- If changes to Danish taxes/duties (incl. refund/rebate schemes) unavoidably affect supplier's contract costs to a non-trivial degree, prices for affected services are adjusted by the documented net consequence — in either direction (up or down).

## Breach / Default (Section 9)
- Breach ("misligholdelse") = failure to fulfil the contract per the contract basis, unless prevented by municipality's own circumstances and/or force majeure.
- Covers both late/non-delivery of the main service and failure of ancillary obligations.
- Supplier must immediately notify municipality of actual or foreseeable breach, explaining: cause, remedial actions, expected resolution timing, and prevention of recurrence; must also flag options and ask how municipality wants the situation resolved.
- Supplier must then remedy per municipality's instructions within the given or a municipality-set reasonable deadline.
- If supplier foresees risk of non-performance due to municipality's or a third party's circumstances, must alert municipality to the risk and possible consequences.
- Municipality has standard Danish law breach remedies available upon supplier default.
- Supplier bears all costs of remedying its own breach.
- Supplier is liable for subcontractor-caused breaches to the same extent as its own.
- On **material breach ("væsentlig misligholdelse")**, municipality may terminate immediately or with reasonable notice depending on circumstances.
- Section 7 (termination obligations / reinstatement) also applies when contract is terminated for breach.

## Ownership (Section 10)
- Municipality retains full ownership of the **sites/land**.
- Supplier retains ownership of the **charge points** themselves.
- On termination, supplier must remove the charge points and reinstate the site (per Section 7).

## Liability & Insurance (Section 11)
- Supplier liable per ordinary Danish law rules on contractual liability, plus any special rules applicable to supplier's business; liable for subcontractors.
- Supplier must maintain **professional/commercial liability insurance** ("erhvervsansvarsforsikring") on customary terms/coverage during the contract period; must produce valid policy or other documentation on municipality's request.

## CSR Terms (Section 12)
- Supplier must comply with municipality's CSR terms (Bilag 3).
- Supplier is **not** required to employ apprentices/trainees ("lærlinge/elever") under this contract.

## Assignment (Section 13)
- Supplier may not fully or partially assign/transfer rights and obligations to a third party without prior written agreement with the municipality.

## Subcontractors (Section 14)
- Supplier may subcontract specific parts of the contract, but only after municipality is informed and has received a signed "tro- og loveerklæring" (declaration of good faith/no debarment) from the subcontractor.

## Disputes / Governing Law / Venue (Section 15)
- Contract governed by **Danish law**.
- Disputes should first be resolved amicably.
- Failing that, resolved in the ordinary Danish courts unless otherwise specifically agreed.
- **Venue: the municipality's home venue ("Kommunens hjemting").**
- If a dispute concerns several/all municipalities in the purchasing cooperative ("Indkøbsfællesskabet" — procurement consortium), venue for a combined lawsuit is the "tovholderkommune" (lead/coordinating municipality)'s home venue.
