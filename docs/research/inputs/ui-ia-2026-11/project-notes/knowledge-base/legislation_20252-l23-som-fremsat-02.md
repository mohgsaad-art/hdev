## 6. Section 3 — Economic Consequences and Implementation Consequences for the Public Sector

- The bill and its subsequent implementation (executive orders) are assessed to create administrative tasks for the **Forsyningstilsynet** (handling complaints against connection refusals; supervising rules on prioritization and capacity disposition).
- **Estimated increased resource need at Forsyningstilsynet: DKK 3 million (≈ 3 full-time-equivalent staff) in the first year after entry into force, and DKK 2 million annually in subsequent years.**
- These costs are financed via **fee levies on the collective electricity supply undertakings**.
- No other economic or implementation consequences for the state are assessed.
- **No economic or implementation consequences assessed for municipalities or regions.**
- The bill is assessed to be consistent with the principles for **digitalization-ready legislation** (*digitaliseringsklar lovgivning*).

## 7. Section 4 — Economic and Administrative Consequences for Business

- The new refusal power means businesses that expected to be connected may instead be **refused** — potentially causing negative economic consequences: postponed or abandoned investments, and loss of expected production/earnings. The concrete loss depends on the individual project.
- Implementation of the bill will **not increase total available grid capacity** — it sets the framework for prioritizing/refusing connection requests. Access to capacity will depend more heavily on prioritization; **deprioritized businesses may face negative economic consequences from longer connection wait times.**
- Consequences are **not limited to new requests** — they also extend to dispositions by existing grid users creating a new/changed capacity draw (e.g., re-establishing decommissioned connection points, increasing reduced delivery volumes, activating previously unused capacity) — such dispositions may face refusal, delay, or need to adjust project scope/timeline, again potentially causing postponed/abandoned investment and lost production/earnings.
- **Potential positive effects:** more expedient/efficient capacity use may mean projects with a real and current need for connection can more readily obtain access — supporting fewer unnecessary delays, better/earlier utilization of businesses' production apparatus, and reduced risk of capacity remaining tied up in unrealized or under-utilized projects.
- **Limited negative consequences for collective electricity supply undertakings** from fee payments funding Forsyningstilsynet's complaint-handling/supervision (same DKK 3m/DKK 2m figures as above, passed through as fees).
- The bill in isolation is **not assessed to create significant administrative consequences** for business generally, though Energinet will need to issue formal decisions on refusals (a task it effectively already performs informally via existing connection-request handling — the bill changes the *framework and outcome*, not the fact that requests are processed).
- Businesses that request improvement-measure information after a refusal will bear associated **application costs**; **these information-related administrative consequences are assessed not to exceed DKK 4 million** [total, across affected businesses — figure as stated in source; basis of estimate not detailed — **[unclear from source: methodology behind the DKK 4m estimate]**].

## 11. Section 8 — Relationship to EU Law (detailed legal analysis)

This section contains the bill's legal justification for compatibility with the Electricity Market Directive — important for understanding the durability/robustness of the underlying legal design.

- § 1 no. 6 (the new § 24(4)) is stated to implement **Article 6 of Directive (EU) 2019/944** (third-party access to the grid, and refusal of connection for lack of capacity).
- The bill is assessed against the Danish government's **five principles for implementing business-oriented EU regulation** and found consistent with them. The bill is said to use the discretion (*råderum*) available under Article 6 to set national rules on prioritizing connection requests and refusing requests for lack of capacity.
- **Article 6(1)** analysis: requires third-party access based on objective, non-discriminatory criteria, transparently designed. This does **not** require identical treatment of all requests — differences in treatment must be objectively justified and applied consistently to comparable cases. This is read as **not precluding category-based prioritization models**, provided categories are objectively delimited and applied uniformly within each category. Member States have **wide discretion** in designing national grid-access rules; Article 6(1) contains no technology-neutrality requirement nor a requirement that all types of grid users be treated identically. This reading is stated to be supported by the **European Commission's 2025 guidance on effective and timely grid connections**, which explicitly states the non-discrimination requirement does not preclude prioritizing certain grid uses when done on transparent, objective, non-discriminatory criteria, and that the Directive does **not** mandate a first-come-first-served principle.
- **Interaction between Article 6 and Article 59** (regulatory authority's method-approval competence) is specifically addressed: the proposed prioritization model sets the **overarching national framework** for third-party access under Article 6, and is **not** a "method" for calculating/setting connection terms and conditions within the meaning of Article 59 — therefore it is **not subject to Forsyningstilsynet method-approval** under Article 59. The regulating authority's Article 59 competence operates *within* the framework set by the Directive and national legislation, and cannot be read as extending to setting or altering such fundamental national access rules.
- **Article 42** (TSO obligations regarding production/storage facility connection requests) is separately analyzed:
  - **Article 42(2):** a TSO may not refuse connection of new production/storage facilities **on grounds of possible future limitations to available network capacity**. The bill's proposed scheme is stated to concern situations of **current, actual** capacity constraints, not hypothetical future ones — so Article 42(2) is assessed as **not limiting** Member States' Article 6 discretion to set prioritization rules for actual, present capacity constraints.
  - **Article 42(3):** a TSO may not refuse a new connection point solely by reference to increased costs of necessary capacity expansions in the immediate vicinity of the connection point. This is read narrowly as concerning **concrete local network-reinforcement cost disputes at a specific connection point**, distinguishable from the bill's overarching regulation of prioritization, disposition, and planning of grid capacity. The bill does **not** prohibit connection of particular facility types and does **not** allow refusal solely on grounds of local-reinforcement cost — projects can still obtain connection where compatible with existing/planned capacity and the set prioritization. A broader reading of Art. 42(3) (as a general duty to expand/dimension the grid for any requested project) is explicitly rejected as it would strip Member States of their Article 6 discretion and their ability to set overarching grid-planning principles based on societal/systemic considerations (security of supply, electrification, efficient capacity use).
  - Conclusion: the proposed scheme is assessed as **compatible with Article 42**.
- **State aid:** the bill is assessed **not** to involve state aid under **Article 107(1) TFEU**.

## 14. Comments on Individual Provisions (*Bemærkninger til lovforslagets enkelte bestemmelser*) — Incremental Legal Detail

*Note: this section of the bill largely restates, provision-by-provision, the policy analysis already captured in Sections 2.1–2.2 above (Part 1). Below is condensed coverage focused on details **not** already captured — cross-references, precise legal mechanics, and consequential effects.*

### To § 1

- **No. 1 (repeal of § 6 e):** Confirms the repealed provision's exact prior content (compensation rules for grid-connection costs of VE Act §30-covered facilities, network losses, O&M, and § 67-covered facility connection costs) and that it was implemented via the Compensation Order (no. 2655/2021), whose §§ 3 and 5 set 2023 application deadlines and § 11 allowed appeal of Danish Energy Agency (Energistyrelsen) decisions within 4 weeks. Repeal means no further rules can issue under § 6 e; existing rules survive via § 3(2) of this Act.
- **No. 2 (§ 12 b(1)):** Changing "covered by § 67" to "environmentally friendly" is explicitly stated to **preserve current law unchanged in substance** — it is a drafting consequence of broadening § 67's scope (so that § 12 b(1)'s cost-allocation rule, which is meant to apply to non-environmentally-friendly plants, does not accidentally sweep in newly-added facility types under the broadened § 67).
- **No. 3 (repeal of § 12 b(2)):** Confirmed as **not changing current law** — producer connection-cost allocation is already governed via Forsyningstilsynet-approved producer-payment methodologies under § 73 a.
- **No. 4 and No. 7 (§ 22(1)(3) and § 28(1)(4) — adding "and connection"):** Both explicitly stated to **not change current law** or expand grid companies'/Energinet's tasks and competences (since the duty to connect users already exists under § 20(1)(2)) — the amendments are **solely to preserve textual consistency** with the broadened § 67 (Chapter 9).
- **No. 5 (§ 24(1) cross-reference to new stk. 4):** Clarifies that § 24(1)'s general right to use the grid for transport against payment will now be **expressly qualified/modifiable** by rules under new § 24(4) — i.e., the general connection right is no longer unconditional.
- **No. 6 (new § 24(4)–(5)):** This is the most extensively elaborated provision — see full detail already captured in Part 1, Section 4 above (2.1.4). Additional mechanical/legal points from this commentary not previously noted:
  - The prioritization model is expected to apply **regardless of whether capacity constraints currently exist** in a given case — it only affects outcomes when capacity is actually insufficient to meet all relevant requests.
  - Where a capacity constraint concerns the **transmission grid or the transmission/distribution interface**, rules are expected to have **Energinet perform the prioritization among competing requests** as part of coordinated pool-processing, with relevant grid companies required to **follow Energinet's prioritization** in their own subsequent handling of individual requests.
  - The bill clarifies the interaction with existing § 20 duties: the open-ended concepts "necessary extent" (*fornødent omfang*) and "necessary transport capacity" (*fornøden transportkapacitet*) in § 20(1) will now need to be **interpreted and applied in light of** the prioritized capacity-disposition regime and the new refusal rules under § 24(4) — meaning a refused request will **not** by itself need to be factored into the assessment of "necessary transport capacity," nor will it form the basis for future network planning, dimensioning, maintenance, or expansion decisions.
  - This new § 24(4)/(5) regime is explicitly linked to the § 68 a(1) amendment (adding "24," — see No. 11) so that refusal decisions become formal, appealable administrative decisions (*afgørelser*).
  - Supervisory linkage: rules are expected under § 78(6) (supervision of certain § 24(4)/(5) rules) and § 78(7) (delegation of appeal-handling to the Forsyningstilsynet in lieu of the Minister) — see below.
- **No. 8 and No. 9 (Chapter 9 heading; heading before § 67):** Purely consequential to the substantive broadening in No. 10; renamed to "Environmentally friendly electricity production and connection" (Chapter 9) and "Electricity production facilities, electricity consumption facilities and energy storage facilities" (heading before § 67).
- **No. 10 (§ 67 substantive broadening):** Additional detail beyond Part 1: the ministerial authorization under the broadened § 67 will be **limited** in two ways: (a) it cannot cover matters more properly regulated via **Forsyningstilsynet-approved methods** under § 73 a (i.e., discretionary/decision-type rules only, not tariff-methodology matters); and (b) any rules issued must remain **consistent with applicable EU regulations** — specifically **the RfG Regulation** (cited in the bill as "(EU) nr. 2026/631" — **[note: this appears to be a typographical/OCR artifact in the source; the correct citation, consistent with the rest of the bill and with EU law, is (EU) 2016/631]**) and **the DCC Regulation ((EU) 2016/1388)**. It is explicitly confirmed that Energinet/grid companies will **NOT** gain new power to compel merging of two already-connected facilities or projects with existing connection agreements — current practice on that point is unchanged.
- **No. 11 (§ 68 a(1) — inserting "24,"):** Mechanically extends the Minister's existing delegation-authorization power to cover § 24 matters, specifically enabling the future executive order to require that a **refusal of grid connection be issued as a formal decision (afgørelse)**.

### To § 2 (VE Act amendments)

- **No. 1 (§ 2(1)(4)):** Confirmed as a **pure clarification**, not a substantive change — VE Act Chapter 4 already set connection/safety rules for solar-PV and other VE facilities beyond wind turbines; the scope clause simply hadn't reflected this textually.
- **No. 2 (repeal of § 30):** Full prior text of § 30 reproduced — it authorized rules on: (1) which categories of installations (including offshore wind landing/interconnection facilities) fall to which party (owner vs. collective undertaking) to construct; (2) wind/wave-power owners' access to use landing infrastructure and payment terms; (3) allocation of connection-establishment and ongoing connection costs between owners and collective undertakings; (4) how collective-undertaking-borne costs are passed through into electricity prices (per Electricity Supply Act § 73(3)); plus a **§ 30(2)** allowing rules on fees to cover grid/transmission undertakings' costs of processing connection applications. This is entirely **folded into** the broadened Electricity Supply Act § 67 — confirmed as **not changing current law**, merely consolidating the authorization.
- **No. 3 (§ 57(1) — deleting "30,"):** Purely consequential to the § 30 repeal; VE Act § 57(2) appeal-routing for these matters is superseded by Electricity Supply Act § 68 a(2). **Not a change to current law.**

### To § 3 (Entry into force / transitional)

- Confirms 12 October 2026 entry into force; confirms the Compensation Order (no. 2655/2021) survives solely for potential future case-reopenings; confirms the connection order (no. 1343/2024) and the direct-lines licensing order (no. 437/2023) remain in force pending replacement under the new consolidated § 67/§68a(1) authorization.

The bill includes a full side-by-side comparison table of "current wording" vs. "the bill," reproducing the complete current text of §§ 6 e, 12 b, 22, 24, 28, the Chapter 9 heading, the pre-§67 heading, §§ 67, 68 a of the Electricity Supply Act, and §§ 2, 30, 57 of the VE Act, alongside the amending instructions. This is **substantively identical** to the enacting text already given in full in Part 1, Section 2, and is not separately reproduced here to avoid duplication.

## 15. Assumptions, Simplifications, and Explicit Uncertainties (per project instructions — flagged, not asserted as fact)

- The bill's category system (1–4) and the "grid-friendliness" (*netvenlighed*) criterion are described in the bill as the **"expected first implementation"** (*forventede første udmøntning*) of the ministerial authorization — i.e., the bill itself is an **enabling act**; the actual operative rules (category boundaries, specific netvenlighed criteria, pooling mechanics, reservation percentages for Category 1, etc.) are **not fixed by this Act** and will be set later by executive order (*bekendtgørelse*). Nothing in the extracted bill text specifies numeric thresholds (e.g., the "smaller enterprise" capacity-draw threshold for Category 1) — these are left to future regulation. **[unclear from source: precise numeric thresholds and category-boundary criteria — not yet set at the time of this bill]**.
- The DKK 4 million estimate (business administrative consequences of information requests after refusal) and the DKK 3m/DKK 2m Forsyningstilsynet resourcing estimates are stated as conclusions in the bill without their underlying calculation methodology being reproduced in the extracted text — treat as the bill's own assertion, not independently verified.
- The RfG Regulation citation in the bill's own text under "Til nr. 10" reads "(EU) nr. 2026/631" — this is very likely a typographical error for **(EU) 2016/631** (the actual RfG Regulation), consistent with its citation elsewhere in the same bill (Section 2.2.1) as "(EU) nr. 2016/631 af 14. april 2016." Flagged here rather than silently corrected.
- The consultation section only lists **who** was consulted (høringsparter), not the substance of responses received (a "høringsnotat" is standard Danish legislative practice but was not present in the extracted PDF).

## 16. Relevance to Grid-Connection-Prioritization / Netvenlighed Documents Already in This Project

This bill is highly likely to be the **primary legislative basis (hjemmelsgrundlag)** for the administrative grid-connection-queue reform materials already being processed for this project — specifically the project files:
- `ny-prioriteringsmodel-for-tilslutning-til-energinets-elnet-1-februar-2026.pdf` ("New prioritization model for connection to Energinet's grid")
- `26_08121-1-ny-model-for-sagsbehandling-af-elnettilslutninger-11856660_8_4.pdf` and `ny-model-for-sagsbehandling-af-elnettilslutninger-juni-2026-11932985_4_1.pdf` ("New model for case processing of grid connections")
- `Notat-begrebsafklaring-af-netvenlighed-af-23-april-2026.pdf` ("Memo clarifying the concept of grid-friendliness")

**Basis for this assessment (stated as inference, not verified by cross-reading those files in this task):**
1. The terminology is an exact match: **netvenlighed** ("grid-friendliness") as a formally defined allocation criterion is introduced into Danish statute **for the first time by this bill** (new Electricity Supply Act § 24(4)) — there is currently no other statutory basis for it.
2. The **four-category prioritization framework** (protected needs / other projects / energy storage / large consumers such as data centers) described in this bill's § 2.1.4 matches, by name and structure, what a policy document titled "new prioritization model for connection to Energinet's grid" would be expected to operationalize.
3. The bill is explicit that it is an **enabling act only** — the detailed operative rules are left to future executive orders (bekendtgørelser) — which is exactly the kind of implementing detail one would expect a companion "ny prioriteringsmodel" / "ny model for sagsbehandling" document to contain, dated close in time (Feb–June 2026) to this bill's introduction (Aug 2026) and its 12 October 2026 entry into force.
4. The bill's own text references the Commission's grid-connection guidance document C(2025) 6703 and Danish/Energinet administrative practice as the backdrop — consistent with Energinet-level implementation documents being developed in parallel.

**Recommendation:** when those other files are processed into this project's knowledge base, they should be **cross-linked to this bill** as: this Act (L 23) = the legal enabling instrument (hjemmel); those documents = the administrative/operational implementation of the § 24(4)–(5) authorization. If, upon reading those documents, their category definitions, thresholds, or netvenlighed criteria diverge from what is described here, that should be flagged explicitly as a **possible evolution between policy-development stage and final executive order**, not treated as a contradiction to be silently reconciled.

**Direct relevance to HDEV / depot / EV-charging sizing modelling:**
- **EV charging infrastructure is explicitly named** in the bill: (a) as a category-1 (protected) example in some cases ("visse projekter inden for etablering af offentligt tilgængelig opladningsinfrastruktur til vejkøretøjer" — certain public EV charging-infrastructure projects), and (b) "ladestandere" (charging stations) are explicitly listed as an example of an "elforbrugsanlæg" (electricity consumption facility) newly brought under § 67's connection-rule-making power.
- The EU Electricity Market Directive text quoted in the bill (Art. 6(2)) contains a **charging-point-specific rule**: when access is refused for EV charging points, network-reinforcement information **must always** be given (not merely offered on request) — this is a materially relevant compliance detail for any depot/HDEV charging project that could face a grid-connection refusal.
- **Category 3 (energy storage)** is directly relevant to any HDEV depot design incorporating on-site battery storage to buffer grid draw — such storage capacity, if added beyond the depot's own production/consumption need, would be assessed as netvenlighed-relevant and category-3-classified, meaning **it would need to demonstrate grid-friendliness to avoid queue-refusal risk** under the future implementing rules.
- **Category 4 (large consumers, e.g., data centers)** — large depot/charging hub loads could conceivably be assessed similarly if their capacity draw and grid impact are large enough, though the bill's stated first-implementation scope names data centers specifically, not depots — **[unclear from source: whether large HDEV depots/charging hubs would be classified under Category 2 (as "electrification/green-transition" projects) or could be swept into Category 4 depending on their capacity draw — this determination is left to future executive-order detail and grid-company discretion, not specified in this bill]**.
