## Chapter 6 — Main Open Work for Follow-Up Activities After SR7

This chapter is explicitly intended "to support the Commission in planning the next steps," ranked from short-term/already-in-progress items to long-term strategic items. Reproduced as a full topic table.

### Table 32 — List of open topics after SR7 (reproduced in full)

| Topic | Description | Actions necessary |
|---|---|---|
| **Update of VECTO cycles (trucks)** | ACEA and subcontractor still reviewing "Urban delivery" and "Construction" cycles; update proposals expected "still in 2017." | Review updated cycles and implement into VECTO software. |
| **Update of tyre-dimension list in VECTO** | Tyre industry noted some future dimensions are not covered by the current list. | Recommend regular table updates; list of required info already sent to **ETRMA**; since valid entries also appear in the HDV CO2 TA, updates require legislative text updates too (or wording allowing simpler software-only amendments). |
| **Fuel properties for Natural Gas engines in VECTO** | All Natural Gas engine types are currently combined into a single "NG" fuel type using **typical CNG** properties; LNG's different properties are not considered. | Decide whether to split NG into separate CNG and LNG fuel types; if yes, survey typical LNG fuel properties and implement. **Note:** VECTO currently considers only Tank-to-Wheel (TTW) emissions — this especially understates LNG's full GHG impact, which also includes Well-to-Tank (WTT) CH4 losses during long-distance transport and liquefaction energy consumption (cross-referenced to the "Well to tank emissions" topic below). |
| **Generic gear-shift strategies in VECTO for AMTs and AT vehicles** | Current AMT gear-shift strategy is optimised for long-haul/regional-delivery and has shortcomings in low-speed cycles (urban delivery, municipal). ACEA investigating an improved approach. | Review ACEA's new approach once mature (tested successfully at multiple OEMs); implement in VECTO if agreed by the VECTO maintenance board; further testing needed; **updating AMT strategy will also require re-reviewing AT gear-shift rules** (AMT-vs-AT ranking issue). |
| **Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS)** | ADAS can significantly improve fuel-efficient driving but are not currently modelled in VECTO CO2 certification. ACEA has drafted approaches for simulating **"Engine Stop-start," "Eco-roll,"** and **"Predictive Cruise Control"** and combinations thereof. | (1) Review ACEA's system definitions/parameter settings via a market survey and inquiry into typical real-world ADAS usage/settings; (2) implement into VECTO, test, iterate with industry; (3) incorporate ADAS-declaration provisions into the legislative text (definitions, Ex-Post verification methods). |
| **Ex-post test procedure** | Several tasks remain open for legislative implementation (see file 05, section 4.4.1.8). | Topics/actions as listed there; a DG GROW project on this topic is ongoing. |
| **Methods for buses and coaches** | Several tasks remain open (see file 06, section 5.1.5). | Topics/actions as listed there. |
| **Hybrid electric vehicles (HEV)** | HEVs are **not currently covered by the VECTO approach.** | Possible options and necessary steps were analysed in detail in a separate **DG CLIMA study [ref 12]** (the same Silberholz & Hausberger feasibility study cited in file 06). |
| **Advanced engine technologies** | Several near-future engine technologies not yet covered by VECTO: **Dual Fuel engines (CNG with Diesel injection)** and **Waste Heat Recovery (WHR).** | Develop appropriate test procedures, VECTO simulation modules, and legislative text, in close cooperation with industry. |
| **Incorporation of specific bodies, trailers, and semi-trailer designs into CO2 certification** | All trucks currently certified with "standard bodies"/"standard-trailers" — **no incentive to optimise real body/trailer designs.** | Options/tasks as listed in file 06, section 5.3.2. |
| **CdxA test procedure (CFD)** | CFD might suit determination of *relative* air-drag differences for HDV/trailer design variants; more code/settings harmonisation needed before certification use; ACEA/CLCCR activities ongoing to assess suitability and boundary conditions. If successful, "the approach is promising." | Decide whether to pursue CFD simulation for a 2nd phase of HDV CO2 legislation; if yes, cooperation between ACEA, Commission, and possibly a consultant to elaborate the method in detail. |
| **N2 and M2 vehicles with max GVW <7.5 tons** | Several tasks remain open (see file 06, section 5.2.3). | Options/tasks as listed there. |
| **"Eco-features"** | Not all new technologies can be quickly integrated into the full CO2 test procedure (new component test + VECTO simulation + validation is slow). An alternative assessment method may be needed for new/niche technologies before (or instead of) full VECTO implementation — e.g., vehicle testing with a measured with/without ratio, or VECTO interfaces allowing adapted values (e.g., alternator power demand reduced if waste-heat recovery supplies electric energy). | Decide if necessary; if yes, define a timeline and set up a development project. |
| **Incorporation of OEM-specific control strategies** | Considering OEM-specific control strategies (e.g., gear-shift strategies, HEV controllers) in VECTO CO2 certification is seen as worthwhile for several vehicle systems. Possible solutions: **SIL (Software-in-the-Loop)** or **HIL (Hardware-in-the-Loop)** interfaces to VECTO. | Estimated as a **complex, multi-year** development: define/implement VECTO SILS/HILS interfaces (input/output signals) compatible with all OEM software; potentially extend VECTO modules for additional required signals; proof-of-concept and further optimisation; elaborate a type-approval approach for "black-box" software/dataset or ECU; verify system behaviour on a defined number of vehicles by TAA; elaborate legal text. **It is estimated that implementing SILS/HILS does not necessarily require switching VECTO from "backward" to "forward" simulation.** |
| **Well to Tank (WTT) emissions (all vehicles)** | VECTO currently considers **only Tank-to-Wheel (TTW)** emissions. Neglecting WTT "does not correctly rank the real GHG impact of different propulsion technologies (different fuels or **electricity from the grid if PHEVs and EVs might be covered by VECTO in future**)." | A **political decision** is needed; discussing WTT factors with stakeholders "might be a long process." |

**★ Direct textual quote on electrification (most explicit statement in the entire report on this project's core topic area):** "Currently VECTO considers only Tank to Wheel emissions (TTW). Neglecting the Well to Tank (WTT) chain does not correctly rank the real GHG impact of different propulsion technologies (different fuels or electricity from the grid if PHEVs and EVs might be covered by VECTO in future)." As of this report (Oct. 2017), **VECTO/HDV CO2 certification had no electric-vehicle modelling, no WTT/grid-emissions accounting, and no defined method for BEV/PHEV energy consumption** — these were acknowledged open, politically-undecided topics for future work, with the only concrete related activity being the separate hybrid-feasibility study (ref [12]).

## Chapter 7 — Summary and Outlook (key points not already covered in other files)

This chapter largely restates Chapter 1/2's objectives and Chapter 3–6's findings in condensed form; points not otherwise captured in files 01–06:

- **Overall assessment:** "The method was 100% completed within SR7 for all truck groups as scheduled to be CO2 certified in the current HDV CO2 legislation." Some improvements/updates are still recommended for the near term (updated urban-delivery/construction cycles and construction-cycle generic vehicle data, both under ACEA elaboration at report time).
- **Recommended near-term (by 2018) priorities explicitly named:** improved generic gear-shift strategies for AMT and AT transmissions; incorporation of ADAS into HDV CO2 certification.
- **Medium-term goals explicitly named:**
  - Inclusion of Hybrid Electric Vehicles (with possible required adjustments to the "advanced auxiliary model" to avoid double-counting of energy-recuperation effects).
  - Methods for advanced engine technologies (e.g., CNG-with-Diesel-injection dual-fuel engines) and Waste Heat Recovery.
- **Long-term vision explicitly named:** an option to incorporate OEM-specific control strategies into VECTO CO2 certification via SIL or HIL interfaces — "estimated to be a complex process and requiring several years of lead time." The report reiterates that **a shift to forward simulation is not seen as a prerequisite** for this.
- **Closing framing statement (verbatim, describing VECTO's intended societal role):** "Under the boundary condition of ongoing maintenance and improvements to keep up with latest vehicle technologies, the VECTO method shall be a very well suitable basis to inform customers on real world fuel consumption, monitor CO2 emissions from the HDV sector and give OEMs the opportunities to demonstrate the improvements in environmental impact of the HDV sector."
