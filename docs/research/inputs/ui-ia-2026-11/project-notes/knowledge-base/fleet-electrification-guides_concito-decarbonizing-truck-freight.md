# Decarbonizing Truck Freight Transport (CONCITO PIXI Brief 1)

**Source file:** `CONCITO_Dekarbonisering-af-godstransport-med-lastbiler_PIXI1_A4.pdf`
**Category:** Fleet electrification guide / policy report
**Original language:** Danish (translated to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** A CONCITO policy brief (funded by Energifonden) evaluating four decarbonization pathways for Danish road freight (efficiency, liquid fuels, gaseous fuels, electrification) and concluding that direct electrification is the most promising route, with recommendations for national policy.

## Policy Context / Climate Targets

- Denmark's national targets: **70% CO2 reduction by 2030**, **net-zero by 2050**.
- Heavy truck transport currently accounts for **6%** of Denmark's total CO2 emissions.
- Truck freight emissions are projected to **increase their share of Denmark's total CO2 emissions by over 20%** by 2030 (i.e., the sector's relative contribution grows even as overall emissions should fall) — freight transport is growing and expected to continue growing.

### Fleet composition and emissions concentration
- Denmark has approximately **42,000 lastvogne (trucks)**, and **90%** of domestic freight transport is carried out by them, almost exclusively diesel-powered.
- The largest trucks and **sættevognstrækkere** (semi-trailer tractor units) make up **60%** of the truck fleet but are responsible for **85%** of the sector's CO2 emissions.
- A pie/bar chart in the source shows sector CO2 shares: Godstransport (freight transport) context figures shown are 10%, 85%, 60% alongside categories "Øvrig" (other), "Anden vejtransport" (other road transport), "Øvrig trafik" (other traffic), "Energisektor" (energy sector), "Landbrug" (agriculture), "Godstransport" (freight transport) — the exact chart layout/axis could not be fully disambiguated from the extracted text; the key readable figures are the 60% (share of fleet) / 85% (share of CO2) statistic for large trucks and semi-trailers, and the 10% (national freight transport CO2 share, consistent with the companion PIXI2 brief).

## Barriers to Decarbonization (identified via CONCITO analyses, meetings, and workshops)

1. **Uncertainty about future "winning" green solutions** — which technologies, fuels, and methods will be recognized as part of a green, climate-neutral future.
2. **High costs of switching** to new green technologies without a corresponding willingness-to-pay from customers, which matters greatly in a competitive market.
3. **Lack of political attention** to freight transport's climate challenges — EU regulation has been slow and unambitious, and Denmark has not taken significant initiatives to support green transition of road freight specifically. Lack of political goals/frameworks reinforces the other barriers.

Note: the report explicitly states the lack of transition is NOT due to general unawareness of climate challenges or widespread resistance — awareness and interest in sustainability is described as high across the freight sector.

## Four Decarbonization Pathways Analyzed

### 1. Efficiency (Effektivisering) — better capacity utilization

**Opportunities:**
- Reduce empty/half-empty running.
- **Modulvogntog (modular combination vehicles / EMS — European Modular System):** two extra-large trucks can in principle replace three smaller ones. Theoretically can reduce up to **20%** of CO2 for the individual transport company.

**Challenges:** Many structural and practical obstacles to fully matching goods volume with transport systems. Cannot always "conjure up" new goods for a return trip. Transport companies are competitors without always sharing customers/tasks. Modular solutions only fully usable for a minority of task types.

**Potential:** No large decarbonization potential from efficiency alone. Modular combination vehicles unlikely to reduce national truck transport emissions by more than **2-3%**. Some efficiency measures may not even reduce CO2 since transport becomes cheaper (rebound effect). Recommended levers: economic incentives such as higher CO2-based transport taxes, and IT systems enabling hauliers to share transport tasks and unused capacity.

### 2. Liquid fuels (Flydende brændstoffer)

**Types:** Advanced biofuels from residual biomass (e.g., **HVO diesel** — Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil diesel) and **electrofuels** (synthetic fuels made from green hydrogen + captured CO2). Advantage: can be used directly in existing tanks/engines/infrastructure without vehicle replacement.

**Challenges:**
- Advanced biofuels require access to sustainable feedstocks, which are and likely will remain scarce and expensive; increased biomass demand risks land-use pressure and negative climate/environmental side effects. Denmark's dependence on biomass energy is already described as "extremely high" and should be reduced, not increased.
- Electrofuels require large amounts of green (wind/solar) electricity plus CO2 capture (from smokestacks or direct air capture); neither will be commercially available at scale for many years. Major inefficiency: much green energy is wasted converting to electrofuel and then burning it in an inefficient combustion engine.

**Potential:** Cannot deliver a marked or fast decarbonization by keeping current trucks and merely switching fuel. Biofuels' overall climate effect is too uncertain beyond small niches of residual products with no other use. Electrofuels require significant additional investment in renewable energy, infrastructure, and CO2-capture technology before delivering meaningful fuel volumes to trucks. Electrofuels seen as relevant longer-term, particularly where direct electrification is not possible, but **will not deliver significant decarbonization contribution to road freight by 2030**.

### 3. Gaseous fuels (Gasformige brændstoffer)

**Types:** Fossil natural gas (usable in specially built truck engines; liquefied as **LNG**) — little climate benefit versus diesel. **Biogas** — produced from livestock manure, crop residues, and biological waste; does not necessarily pressure agricultural land the way many liquid biofuels do. Biogas production utilizes methane (itself a potent greenhouse gas) and improves fertilizer utilization in agriculture. Can theoretically deliver low or even negative CO2 emissions, and is typically cheaper than advanced liquid biofuels — though today's price is kept artificially low through **massive state subsidy schemes** (not named specifically in source).

**Challenges:** Significant uncertainty about availability of sustainable feedstock for increased production; feedstock type largely determines actual greenhouse-gas savings. Methane leakage across the production-to-fueling chain can, in the worst case, cancel out the entire climate benefit. Biogas may deliver more climate value in other sectors (e.g., heavy industry needing high combustion temperatures) than in road freight.

**Potential:** Very difficult to determine due to many uncertainties. Recommendation: stricter regulation of biogas to ensure real climate benefit and direct it to the sectors where it delivers the most climate value.

### 4. Electrification (Elektrificering)

**Mechanism:** Freight transport shifts to green electricity as motive power via electric motors, which have far higher technical efficiency than combustion engines. Three ways to deliver electricity to the truck:
- **Batteries** (as in ordinary electric cars)
- **Overhead power lines (el-veje / "electric roads")** — known from railways
- **Fuel cells** — requiring green electricity first be converted to liquid hydrogen that is tanked on the truck

**Strategic framing:** Most logical to start with electrifying local freight transport with battery trucks (short trips), while electric roads or possibly hydrogen could serve long, heavy transports responsible for a large share of CO2. Notable point: **approximately half of all freight travels on just 2% of the road network**, which significantly limits the amount of new infrastructure (e.g., electric roads) needed. Key enabler: Danish electricity will within a few years be produced by wind and solar with almost no CO2, and electric motors are simultaneously the most economical and environmentally friendly technology.

**Challenges:**
- Electrification requires a fundamental technology shift — entirely new truck types must replace existing ones.
- Price of electric and hydrogen trucks is still very high — **up to 3-4 times the price of diesel trucks**.
- Supply of trucks, charging options, and green hydrogen production is still very limited.
- Electric operation can create logistics challenges due to charging wait times.
- If choosing the hydrogen route: requires significantly more wind energy, since **up to 75% of energy is lost in the hydrogen supply chain**, versus only **25-30% loss with direct electrification**.
- Hydrogen route would also require building an entirely new infrastructure for producing, distributing, and safely storing cooled/liquid hydrogen.

**Potential:** With the transition to CO2-neutral electricity in a few years, electrification has the potential for near-full decarbonization of road freight well before 2050, without needing to overcome fundamental technological barriers. However, due to the need for vehicle replacement, infrastructure build-out, and logistics adaptation, electrification is **far from able to deliver a 70% reduction by 2030**, but might contribute **15-20%** by 2030.

## Conclusion

None of the four pathways alone can deliver anything resembling a 70% CO2 reduction by 2030 — due to the time needed for technology transition, uncertain/limited climate benefit of some technologies, and the risk that different pathways compete with and slow each other down.

**Direct electrification is identified as the most promising pathway**, for four reasons:
1. Wind/solar-powered electric transport enables genuine zero emissions.
2. Electric vehicles use energy more efficiently than other technologies.
3. Other co-benefits: low noise and low pollution.
4. Development trends point to electrification becoming the cheapest solution over time.

Battery vehicles are expected to dominate local and regional distribution; it remains more open how long-haul transport will be electrified. CONCITO's analysis points to establishing **electric roads with overhead catenary lines over a small part of the motorway network** as a feasible option toward 2030.

Combining electrification with other solutions (e.g., logistics efficiency improvements) may bring further benefits; supplementary green fuels/technologies may be needed for tasks that cannot be solved with electric vehicles due to practical or economic barriers.

## CONCITO's Recommendations

1. **A future political strategy should clearly set electrification as the central agenda for road freight transport.**
2. **The strategy should be implemented through tax and subsidy policy** that effectively promotes zero-emission vehicles, and **infrastructure policy** ensuring necessary investment in shared systems to supply trucks with green power (e.g., charging stations, overhead catenary lines).
3. **Denmark should also work for electrification within the EU** and together with its two nearest neighboring countries, where large programs for road freight electrification are already underway (countries not named in the extracted text — **not clearly stated in source**).
4. **Further study needed on the right technical solutions for long and cross-border truck transport.** Specifically mentioned: important to complete the analysis of a possible **electric road between Øresund and Femern**, which the Danish government (regeringen) has announced.
5. **Clarification needed on the role of other technologies** — hydrogen, biogas, and electrofuels — in road transport, considering which sectors most need these resources and which transport tasks are not realistically electrifiable by 2030.

**Three headline recommendation categories (as presented in the source's closing graphic):**
1. Dekarboniseringen af sektoren skal på den politiske dagsorden (Decarbonization of the sector must be put on the political agenda)
2. En målrettet national elektrificering i samarbejde med nabolandene (Targeted national electrification in cooperation with neighboring countries)
3. En supplerende indsats i andre teknologiske og organisatoriske spor (A supplementary effort in other technological and organizational tracks)
