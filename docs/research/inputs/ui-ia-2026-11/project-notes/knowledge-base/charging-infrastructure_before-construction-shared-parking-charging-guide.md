# Before Construction: Charging Infrastructure on Shared Parking Areas (Guide for Municipalities and Politicians)

**Source file:** `Før_byggeriet_Ladeinfrastruktur_på_delte_parkeringspladser.pdf`
**Category:** Charging infrastructure — guide (planning/pre-construction, municipal)
**Original language:** Danish (translated/summarized to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** A guide ("Før byggeriet" = "Before construction") by Region Hovedstaden's Center for Regional Udvikling (Copenhagen Electric) aimed at municipal politicians and local planners, covering the legislation, planning tools, and technical/dialogue considerations for enabling EV charging infrastructure on shared parking areas before residential buildings are constructed. First in a three-part series (Before / During / After construction).

## Glossary (Ordforklaring) — as defined in source
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| EMSP | E-Mobility Service Provider, aka "platform provider"/"platforms-operatør". Software/service company managing charge facilities; does not supply chargers themselves, only the software solution. Often used when housing organizations/companies want to operate their own chargers. |
| Hurtiglader (Fast charger) | Also called DC charger. High-power charger delivering **50–100 kW**. Converts AC→DC in the charger itself. Larger/more expensive than a normal charger; usually requires expanding the existing electrical installation/capacity. |
| Ladeboks (Charge box) | A wall-mounted EV charger, typically with 1 or 2 outlets. |
| Ladeinfrastruktur | Collective term for the entire EV charging infrastructure. |
| Ladeoperatør (Charging operator) | A company that establishes, installs, services and operates chargers; may include fuel/energy stations adding chargers (typically fast chargers). |
| Ladeplads | The area designated for an EV to charge; includes a charge point. |
| Ladepunkt (Charge point) | A place where an EV can charge — in practice, the charging plug. |
| Lader | Collective term for various charger types (charge box, charge stand, etc.); also called "ladefacilitet". |
| Ladestander (Charge stand/post) | A stand delivering power to EVs; can have multiple charge points/plugs. |
| Ladepark | A geographically defined area with many chargers, typically fast chargers, often by motorways/major roads. |
| Ladeøer (Charging islands) | Multiple normal chargers grouped in clusters/"islands" so EV drivers can easily find a free charger. |
| Lastbalancering (Load balancing/sharing/management) | Technical solution ensuring chargers on a parking lot share available power, with total load never exceeding a predefined cap — slowing other EVs' charging rather than overloading the local fuse, if a new EV connects when capacity is fully used. |
| Lynlader (Ultra-fast/rapid charger) | Also DC charger, delivering **at least 150 kW**. AC→DC conversion happens in the charger, not the EV battery. The most expensive charger type, offering the highest charging speed; usually requires expanding the existing electrical installation/capacity. |
| Normallader (Normal charger) | Also "destinationslader"/AC-lader. Low-power charger delivering AC power **between 3.7–22 kW**. Most common, cheapest-to-install type; used where cars park for extended periods (home, workplaces, larger shopping areas); can often be installed with minimal additions to the existing electrical installation. |
| Udtag (Outlet) | The plug in a charge stand that the car connects to. |
| V2G / Vehicle to Grid | Technology/method using bidirectional charging so an EV functions as a grid battery, able to (for payment) deliver power back to the grid during peak-load situations, reducing grid overload risk. |

## Introduction / Policy Context
- References **Bilkommissionen** (the Car Commission)'s **Delrapport 2: "Veje til en veludbygget ladeinfrastruktur"** (Partial Report 2: "Paths to a well-developed charging infrastructure"), published **February 2021**. Cited source URL: fm.dk/media/18388/delrapport-2_veje-til-en-veludbygget-ladeinfrastruktur_web.pdf
  - The report flags a need for the public sector to take an active role in charging infrastructure buildout, with focus on EV owners who cannot install a charger on their own property — notably residents of housing organizations with shared parking (no individually owned parking space).
- Guide purpose: help municipal politicians and local planners identify tools the municipality can use to create the best possible framework for charger installation in areas with shared-parking housing.
- Benefits of enabling home charging on shared parking, as stated in the source:
  - **For residents (time & economics):** overnight charging uses otherwise-idle parked time, avoiding queueing for a free charger and the ~30-minute charging session typical of a fast charger ("lynlader"); normal chargers at the property typically achieve **lower kWh prices** than fast/rapid chargers.
  - **For EV battery health:** AC (normal) charging is gentler on the battery than DC (fast/rapid) charging.
  - **For grid load:** less severe consumption peaks if multiple EVs charge evenly overnight.
  - **Reduces need for publicly accessible charging infrastructure** — fewer public parking spaces need conversion to charge spaces.

## Relevant Legislation Before Construction (Section: "Relevant lovgivning")
Four laws/executive orders identified as relevant in the pre-construction planning phase:
- **Planloven** (the Danish Planning Act)
- **Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen** (the Charging Point Executive Order)
- **Vejloven** (the Danish Roads Act)
- **Privatvejsloven** (the Private Roads Act)
- Also mentions **AFI-loven** (Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Act) and related executive orders as less critical for municipal planning of shared-parking housing specifically, but relevant to public procurers/publicly accessible chargers.

### Planloven (Planning Act)
- Ensures coherent planning reconciling societal land-use interests. Municipalities must prepare kommuneplan (municipal plan) and lokalplaner (local plans) under this act.
- **No legal basis ("hjemmel") in the Planning Act to require the establishment of charging infrastructure.** The municipality can only secure the planning basis (i.e., the *possibility* of establishing larger/coherent charging facilities).
- The municipality **may** set requirements for how parking areas are laid out regarding EVs and car-sharing vehicles (citing the "Overliggeraftale om klimasamarbejdsaftaler om grøn kollektiv trafik" — an overarching agreement on climate cooperation agreements for green public transport between the national government and Københavns, Aarhus, Odense, Aalborg, Vejle, and Frederiksberg Kommuner, dated **25 June 2020**).
- Chargers themselves do not require a local plan ("lokalplan"), but depending on installation size (kW and/or number of outlets), a charging station may trigger a local-plan requirement (traffic volume, ancillary installations like canopies/kiosks, and interface with road authority for access). **Normal chargers (≤22 kW)** generally will not be classified as a "technical installation" and thus do not require the municipality's planning/access assessment.
- Municipality can initiate charger installation on its own land if not contrary to the local/municipal plan framework.

### Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen (Charging Point Executive Order)
- **Entered into force 10 March 2020.** Retsinformation link: retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2020/181
- Implements parts of the EU **Energy Performance of Buildings Directive** (bygningsdirektivet), issued under authority of **Byggeloven** (the Building Act). EUR-Lex reference: CELEX:32010L0031
- Sets requirements on building owners for preparation and/or installation of chargers in specific cases (illustrated in a table/figure, "Figur 1," credited to **Rambøll**, categorizing by residential vs. non-residential and by new construction / major renovation / existing buildings). **Note: the figure's exact cell-by-cell table values were not extractable as text from this PDF — it is an embedded image; the surrounding text describes its structure and key thresholds, reproduced below, but the precise per-category numeric requirements shown in the figure itself could not be transcribed. Treat that specific figure as "not clearly stated in extractable text."**
- Building owner = companies, housing organizations, municipalities, regions, and others owning buildings. **SMEs (Små og Mellemstore Virksomheder)** are exempt from the requirement for **existing** buildings, unless a public-sector entity rents space in the building.
  - **SME definition (per source footnote, citing "Vejledning til ladestanderbekendtgørelsen," 1 January 2021):** fewer than **250 employees** and annual turnover under **€50 million**, or total annual balance sheet under **€43 million**.
- Requirements are **minimums**, split by residential/non-residential and new-build / major-renovation / existing-building categories.
- Shared parking areas at a residential building typically only trigger a **preparation** requirement (not full installation). Shared parking at businesses, or mixed-use (residential + business) parking, triggers **both** installation and preparation requirements.
- A dedicated guidance document ("vejledning") to the Executive Order exists, covering edge cases like buildings with "mixed users" — link: bygningsreglementet.dk/Vejledninger/Andre_vejledninger/Vejledning/Vejledning-om-laderstander
- Recommendation (guide's own framing): treat the Executive Order as a **minimum** — residential buildings, "tæt-lav" (dense-low) housing, tower blocks, etc. with shared parking will likely fall behind actual user demand if only "prepared for" rather than actually fitted with chargers from the start.
- **"Larger renovation" ("større ombygninger") trigger criteria — ALL of the following must be met:**
  1. More than **10 parking spaces** attached to the building after the renovation
  2. Construction work must be occurring on the building
  3. The parking facility and/or the building's electrical infrastructure must be renovated
  4. Costs of installing chargers and preparing for chargers are **under 7%** of total renovation costs
- No requirement in the Executive Order that chargers be publicly accessible and/or operated by charging operators.
- Charger must be located on parking spaces attached to the building — not necessarily consolidated on a single parking area or the same land parcel ("matrikel").
- **Expected revision:** as of **autumn 2023** (document's writing time), a proposal to rework the EU Parliament and Council's Energy Performance of Buildings Directive had been tabled (EUR-Lex EP:P9_TA(2023)0068), and a revision of the Danish Executive Order with tightened requirements was anticipated on this basis.
- **Building permit processing (Byggesagsbehandling):** the Executive Order's requirements are part of the authority review of building applications and thus part of the building permit for new construction/expansion/major renovation. Requirements **cannot be tightened** during permit processing, and for residential buildings there is currently only a **preparation** requirement. The guide suggests municipal building caseworkers can, via dialogue with developers, encourage actual charger installation (not just preparation), on topics such as:
  - Recommended number of charge spaces at commissioning and at a later expansion stage
  - Specific placement of chargers and their cabling
  - **Width of charge parking spaces** — recommend more space so users can move around the car with the cable plugged into a side-mounted outlet
  - **Accessible/disability-friendly charge spaces** — how wheelchair users, etc. can also use the charger
  - Placement of the power supply/transformer station for the chargers, planned from the start including possible future expansion

### Vejloven (Roads Act) and Privatvejsloven (Private Roads Act)
- Publicly accessible roads fall into two categories: **offentlige veje** (public roads — municipal/state roads, under **Vejloven**) and **private fællesveje** (private shared/community roads, under **Privatvejsloven**, LBK nr. 1234 af 01/11/2015 with later amendments).
- Previously, a municipality (as road authority) could permit charger installation on public road land under Vejloven **§ 80**. **With the AFI-loven (retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2022/412), this power lapsed** — the right to act as a charging operator on municipal land and road areas must now be **tendered on market terms** per the AFI Act.
- Road authority retains limited powers regarding charging infrastructure: must grant permits for new road access (e.g., to charging stations), ensure sensible parking restrictions and signage at charge spaces. Municipality has authority under **Færdselsloven** (the Traffic Act) to determine where EV (and other electric-vehicle) parking spaces are established, and can set restrictions in its parking policy.
- Signage guidance: **Vejdirektoratets vejregelhåndbog "Færdselstavler, Oplysningstavler"** (Danish Road Directorate's road-rules handbook on traffic/information signage), pages 36–38, describes applicable signage for various situations on public road land.
- On **private fællesveje**, the municipality is the road authority but generally does **not own** these roads — they're typically owned by private landowners with road-usage rights ("vejret") alongside other road-entitled parties. Municipality has no obligation to ensure charging infrastructure here, though as planning authority it may have an interest in it. Because the municipality doesn't own these roads, it **cannot itself tender the right to operate as a charging operator** on them. Only the road owner(s)/entitled parties/property owners' association, with road authority permission, can install charging infrastructure on a private fællesvej, per **§ 66 of Privatvejsloven**. The municipality (as road authority) can only grant a public-law permit per **Privatvejslovens § 56**, meaning it won't object to the permitted use — whether the permit recipient can actually exercise it is a private-law matter (interpretation of the road-rights agreement). This can make establishing charging infrastructure difficult if some road-entitled parties object.
- Some municipalities **own** many private fællesveje — on these, the municipality may tender charging-infrastructure areas under the AFI-loven, if other legal conditions under Privatvejsloven/Vejloven allow it.

## Charging Infrastructure in Municipal & Local Planning
### Kommuneplanlægning (Municipal Plan)
- Political goals on charging infrastructure can be embedded in the **hovedstruktur** (main structure — overarching goals/guidelines for development and land use), **retningslinjer** (guidelines — governing land use and considerations per area; guiding, can include land reservations/protections), and **rammebestemmelser** (framework provisions — binding provisions on what each area may be used for and how it may be built; city council can adjust via a "kommuneplantillæg," a municipal-plan addendum, usually alongside a new local plan).
- Example guideline formulations suggested: initiatives to secure infrastructure when planning new urban areas; preparation for charging infrastructure per new dwelling beyond the Executive Order's requirements (e.g., empty conduit, space for technical installations); concrete studies of whether to reserve area for fast/rapid chargers as a supplement/replacement to normal chargers serving a whole area/adjacent areas.
- Framework provisions are binding for land use, but normal chargers on shared parking generally won't require special framework provisions (minor installation, no increased noise/traffic).
- Recommendation: label chargers as "lav-" or "højeffektsladere" (low- or high-power chargers) if the municipality wants to specify which infrastructure type is allowed within a given framework area — but **avoid writing specific kW figures**, since rapid technology change can quickly make written figures obsolete and constrain future buildout.

### Lokalplanlægning (Local Plan)
- Local plans allow detailed planning of specific areas' use, access, buildings, supply installations, etc. Can create the *possibility* for charging infrastructure but **cannot require** its establishment. Requirements on parking-space layout for EV/car-sharing vehicles are possible though (per the same Overliggeraftale reference as above).
- Because technology/legislation change quickly, and local plans often have a lifespan of **~40 years** and are rarely amended once realized, the guide recommends **technology-neutral** local plans — e.g., securing only "preparation" via required empty-conduit routing and space for technical installations, rather than fixed technology specs. Alternatively, omit charging-specific clauses from the local plan entirely, leaving all case handling to the building-permit process.
- Early dialogue with the developer before a new local plan is an opportunity to raise the topic and encourage appropriate charging infrastructure from the start — cannot be mandated, but can be influenced.

## Number and Placement of Charge Points
- No one benefits from over-installing charging infrastructure, especially where charge spaces exclude non-charging cars from parking — important to hit the right level.
- Demand depends on: number of residents, car ownership, EV share, vehicle range, daily km driven, etc.; should be future-proofed for expected demand.
- **Plug-in hybrids** require disproportionately more charge points since their batteries accept only limited power (typically **3.7 kW AC**) and are smaller (typically **~50 km electric range**), requiring near-daily charging.

### Method for Simple Calculation of Number of Charge Points (as given in source)
Formula: **A × B / (C/D)**
- A = number of cars to park = 100 (example)
- B = share of EVs in 2030 = **31%** [footnote: 31% corresponds to 1 million EVs out of a vehicle fleet of 3.26 million in 2030]
- C = average EV range = **350 km** [footnote: average range has been continuously improved; older EVs will have shorter range than indicated; cites FDM tests showing significant improvement in new EVs' range since 2020, fdm.dk]
- D = average km driven per passenger car per day = **45.5 km/day**
- Example given: if 100 cars are expected in a housing area, or the municipality's parking norm requires 100 spaces, it is reasonable to assume a need for **4 outlets / 2 double-outlet charge stands**.
- **Assumptions underlying the formula (explicitly stated):** the EV does not need to be moved after charging but stays overnight at the charger (one EV per 24h per outlet assumed); different EVs' charging needs don't coincide on the same day but spread across the week. In practice this can result in a perceived shortage of chargers, so the guide recommends slightly **overdimensioning** — and overdimensioning the **installation** (conduit, cables) in particular, since it's much cheaper to add the next charger later if this is done. Recommends monitoring for significant changes to the above inputs.
- Placement recommendation: best to place chargers in "ladeøer/-grupper" (charging islands/clusters) so users don't need to drive around searching; also cheapest to install together, ideally near the connection point. Accessibility/visibility can also factor into placement decisions.

## Choice of Charger and Usage Restrictions
### Charger Power (kW)
- Two broad categories: **normalladere (AC-ladere)** up to **22 kW**, and **højeffektsladere (hurtig-/lynladere = DC-ladere)** typically delivering **50 kW or more**.
- Shared parking chargers expected to predominantly be normal chargers (long-duration overnight parking/charging). A number of normal chargers (≤22 kW) can usually be accommodated within existing local planning and installed without a building application.
- If a developer/resident organization considers one or more fast chargers or an ultra-fast charging station, the local plan must be able to accommodate this. An ultra-fast charging station can cause noise nuisance (citing **"Kortlægning af støj fra lynladestandere til elbiler," DJ-MG for Region Hovedstaden, 2022** — a noise-mapping study of EV rapid chargers) and, if publicly accessible, increased traffic load; may also involve canopies, lighting, etc.; high-power chargers often require a new transformer station. Recommendation: clarify in the planning phase whether an ultra-fast station is desired on shared parking.

### Lastbalancering (Load Balancing)
- If all chargers connect to a shared supply point, this enables power-capacity distribution (load balancing); recommends using a charging operator to manage correct billing per user under such systems.
- Dedicated parking spaces (e.g., in a parking garage tied to a specific unit) may require one charge point per space, typically increasing ampere-purchase costs since power capacity must be bought per point to ensure correct billing.
- A shared solution (single operator/EMSP or shared load-management system with the same charger box) enables load balancing while still billing users correctly — recommended to have a shared charging solution ready at building commissioning. Cross-references the "Under byggeriet" guide.
- **Sidebar box "Om lastbalancering":** Ampere must be purchased from the grid company when installing chargers; the faster the desired charging, the more power/ampere needed, so connection fees to the grid company can become expensive without load balancing. With load balancing, power is distributed among cars based on available supply — the more cars charging simultaneously, the lower the power (kW) delivered to each; as cars finish, power is reallocated to the rest. **Without load balancing, the power from each charge point must be counted with a simultaneity/coincidence factor of 1 (a requirement per DS/EN60364).**

### Usage Restrictions
- Housing organizations may worry about outside/non-resident use blocking residents. Signage can help discourage this. Parking restrictions (time-limits) can be imposed by the developer or later property board/resident association — but **any signage regulating behavior on roads governed by the Traffic Act requires approval from the road authority and police.**
- If signage alone is insufficient, chargers can be locked to outsiders via charge cards/user cards; consider having one or a few dedicated guest charge spaces.
- Simultaneous-demand and availability problems can be addressed with **booking systems** — usable on private land but **not permitted on public roads** per Vejloven.

## Dialogue with Grid Companies (Elnetselskaber)
- Growing share of consumption tied to electricity; the grid faces pressure from broad electrification and needs significant expansion over the **next 10–20 years** to meet demand.
- Grid companies increasingly need early visibility into upcoming plans requiring grid expansion (urban development plans, electrification of heat sources, ultra-fast charging stations). **Fællesregulativet 2022** (Common Regulation/Code 2022 — issued under the Executive Order on authorization and operation of businesses in the electrical, plumbing and sewer installation field) requires registration with grid companies when installing chargers, to help register electricity demand (Fællesregulativet 2022, point 2.1). This only covers **existing** demand — insight into future demand is also needed.
- **Sidebar "Om elnetselskaber":** Grid companies own the electrical infrastructure from Energinet's overarching grid to the individual consumer/building. Under **Elforsyningsloven** (the Electricity Supply Act), grid companies must operate, maintain and, as needed, continuously expand the grid within their respective concession areas. Each grid company holds a **monopoly** in its concession area and is subject to a range of rules, including a **connection obligation** for both new consumption and new production. Grid expansion (transmission and distribution level) should occur at the lowest socio-economic cost, cost-effectively, in the right place and at the right time — requiring good visibility into future activities.
- In dense urban areas (e.g., tight city centers), limited space often means hard prioritization; more EV chargers can require new transformer stations in the urban space, which take up physical space and sometimes require removing/discontinuing parking spaces. Transformer stations are a **prerequisite** for local charger establishment; more extensive grid expansions may require the grid company to work/install new electrical installations further from the charging facility if the connection requires a larger expansion of the upstream grid. Closer dialogue with the grid company can, to some extent, yield special agreements on integrating transformer stations into the urban space (final placement, visual appearance, necessary access roads, screening vegetation, etc.).
- Recommends discussing with grid companies other ways to manage grid buildout well — e.g., theme meetings on charging infrastructure for electrical installers/electricians covering grid/capacity considerations, potentially arranged with the local business council/network.

## Chargers at Existing Housing with Shared Parking
- Installing chargers at existing housing organizations is challenging but achievable. Networks of housing organizations can address the green agenda including charger installation, sharing knowledge on what works/doesn't across organizations. **Municipality can act as facilitator** of such networks.
- Housing organizations could run **joint tenders** for chargers to get better offers and/or share experience/tender materials, reducing individual-organization resource/time burden and potentially raising overall quality compared to each organization working alone.
- Experience-sharing builds confidence; municipality can share its own experience with charger rollout, tender processes, and post-installation charging management.

## Considerations on Electric Car-Sharing (Delebiler på El)
- Municipalities can plan/reserve area for charging infrastructure in municipal/local plans but have **no legal basis** to require its establishment (per Planloven).
- Municipalities **may** set requirements on parking layout regarding EVs and car-sharing vehicles, per **Erhvervsstyrelsen** (the Danish Business Authority) — citing the same Overliggeraftale reference.
- Municipality has authority under **Færdselsloven** to determine EV/electric-vehicle parking locations; may need to define restrictions for EV parking/charge spaces in its parking policy.
- Car-sharing (possibly electric) can let some residents forgo or delay buying a car; benefits: economic/health for individuals, less parking pressure/urban congestion, local/global environmental benefits, and can make the housing organization more attractive to live in.

## Relevance Notes for HDEV/Depot Tool (Claude's own framing, not from source)
- This document is entirely about **light-duty/passenger EV residential charging planning law**, not HD trucks or depots, but the planning-law analysis (Planloven's inability to mandate charger installation, only enable it; the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen's preparation-vs-installation triggers; AFI-loven's market-tender requirement for public land) is a directly relevant regulatory-environment reference for any Danish depot-siting or public charging-hub project that involves municipal land or road access.
- The load-balancing sidebar (coincidence factor = 1 without load management, per DS/EN60364) is a useful, citable technical reference point relevant to depot electrical sizing methodology, though stated in a light-duty EV context.
