# Forsvaret (Danish Defence) — Fleet Electrification Case Study

**Source file:** `Casehistorier_Fagfolk_templafy.pdf` (compendium; story: "Forsvaret i fronten om at mindske energiforbrug og klimapåvirkning")
**Category:** Case study — fleet electrification (defence ministry, administrative pool vehicles)
**Original language:** Danish (translated to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** The Danish Ministry of Defence's Materiel and Procurement Agency added 2 electric Renault Zoe pool cars, part of a broader energy-reduction strategy targeting at least a 20% cut in energy consumption by end of 2020.

## Company Profile
- **Organization:** Forsvaret (the Danish Defence Forces) — specifically Forsvarsministeriets Materiel- og Indkøbsstyrelse (the Ministry of Defence's Materiel and Procurement Agency)
- **Sector:** Defence / public administration
- **Fleet featured:** 2 Renault Zoe (electric pool cars), replacing 2 petrol-driven "microbiler" (microcars)
- **Website noted in source:** forsvaret.dk

## Electrification Timeline
- Forsvaret operates a wide range of vehicles and equipment; alongside fighter jets and armored personnel carriers, it also runs a large stock of ordinary passenger vehicles used as pool cars for staff meetings and visits to agencies/suppliers around the country.
- As "something new," 2 electric Renault Zoe pool cars were acquired for the Materiel- og Indkøbsstyrelsen.
- Broader context: Forsvarsministeriet (Ministry of Defence) is one of Denmark's largest workplaces; it runs internal campaigns encouraging climate- and energy-conscious behavior — covering not just saving water and electricity but finding better, more energy-efficient solutions generally.
- A two-stage training initiative was launched: first, executives, managers and "energy ambassadors" are trained in basic energy management; later, an "energikørekort" (energy driving license) is rolled out for all employees under the Ministry of Defence's authority, regardless of role. This is one of the initiatives supporting the Ministry's climate and energy strategy goal of reducing energy consumption by at least 20% by end of 2020.

## Operational Notes
- The introduction of EVs had high novelty/news value internally; many employees were curious and wanted to get to know the new vehicles.
- Many employees now prefer the electric vehicles over diesel cars, particularly for short trips not requiring mid-trip charging.
- Staff appreciate the absence of gears and the quiet, brisk acceleration of the EVs.
- Ongoing evaluation of whether further EVs can be implemented.
