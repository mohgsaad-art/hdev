# Køge Kommune Charging Infrastructure Strategy 2022-2026

**Source file:** `Ladestanderstrategi_Koege_Kommune.pdf`
**Category:** Charging infrastructure — municipal strategy
**Original language:** Danish (translated/summarized to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** Køge Kommune's (municipality, Zealand, Denmark) official "Ladestanderstrategi" (Charge Point Strategy) for 2022-2026, published February 2022 by the Teknik- og Miljøforvaltningen (Technical and Environmental Administration), setting out the municipality's roles as fleet owner, building owner, authority, and cooperation partner in rolling out EV charging infrastructure.

## Administrative / Issuing Body
- **Issuer:** Køge Kommune (municipality), Torvet 1, 4600 Køge; Teknik- og Miljøforvaltningen (Technical and Environmental Administration)
- **Publication date:** February 2022 ("Februar 2022")
- **Strategy period:** 2022–2026

## 1. "Fremtiden er elektrisk" (The Future is Electric) — Policy Context
- Køge Kommune was one of the **first 20 Danish municipalities** to adopt a **DK2020 klimaplan** (climate plan) aligned with the Paris Agreement, in **2020**, targeting **climate neutrality by 2050**.
- Transition to electric passenger cars is described as **contributing approx. 70% of the transport-sector CO2 reduction target for 2030** in Køge Kommune's climate plan.
- Also framed as supporting the **national target of a 70% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2030 relative to 1990**.
- Notes grid-load concern: rising electrification will increase pressure on the grid, especially **late afternoon after work hours** when household electricity use rises — the same period most people would want to charge. From a socio-economic perspective, it's desirable that most EV charging power come from **smart normal chargers**, which spread charging over time and are less burdensome on the grid, reducing grid investment needs and ultimately lowering consumer charging costs.
- **Strategy's four focus areas ("indsatsområder"):**
  1. Køge Kommune as **bilflådeejer** (fleet owner) — fleet electrification and charger installation
  2. Køge Kommune as **bygningsejer** (building owner) — must install charging infrastructure
  3. Køge Kommune as **myndighed** (authority) — ensuring installation requirements are followed and public charging infrastructure is installed on public land
  4. Køge Kommune as **samarbejdspartner** (cooperation partner) — supporting workplaces, housing associations, and retail in charger installation

## 2. Situationen Nu (The Current Situation)

### 2.1 EVs in Køge Kommune
- **National figures (2021):** 32,000 EVs and 30,000 plug-in hybrids in Denmark. **Klimarådet** (the Danish Council on Climate Change) estimates **1 million EVs needed in Denmark by 2030**, corresponding to **33% of the entire Danish car fleet** (citing Klimarådet (2020), "Kendte veje og nye spor til 70 procents reduktion").
- **Local figures:** as of **1 January 2021**, **29,000 cars** registered in Køge Kommune (Danmarks Statistik, 2022). A third of Køge's fleet corresponds to **~9,000 EVs**.
- **Greenhouse gas accounting (Figure 1, Køge Kommune 2020 DK2020 Klimaplan):** Transport = **42%** of emissions in Køge Kommune; other categories shown: El-forbrug (electricity use, incl. heating) = **15%**, Varmeforbrug (heat use) = **37%**, Landbrug (agriculture) = **4%**. - Expectation: those able to install their own charging facilities will do so (most convenient/cheapest); others should ideally charge at publicly accessible normal chargers or at work. Under current price structures, charging at fast/rapid chargers is more expensive per person than at normal chargers.

### 2.3 Ladestandere (Charge Stands) — Current State
- **9 locations with publicly accessible charge stands** in Køge Kommune, with **between 1 and 26 charge plugs** per location (Kort 1). Located in the Køge Nord industrial quarter near Skandinavisk Transportcenter, at Køge Campus, at Køge Universitetshospital, in Den Hvide By, and near Køge by at Blegdammen and Vestergade. Locations updated continuously at **elbilviden.dk/ladekort**.
- More charge stands are continuously added as companies/shops install them. A **Clever rapid-charging station with 16 outlets** had just opened at exit 32 in Køge; **COOP** announced (autumn 2021), in partnership with **OK**, plans to install charge stands at **500 of their stores** nationally (citing a ritzau.dk press release). Some businesses fall under the ladestanderbekendtgørelsen and must install at least 1 charge stand at their buildings **before end of 2025**.

### 2.4 Public Co-financing of Charging Infrastructure
- Per **Kommunalfuldmagten** (the Danish Municipal Authority Doctrine, an unwritten body of law governing what activities municipalities may undertake) and **Planloven**, there is currently **no legal basis for municipalities to co-finance publicly accessible charging infrastructure**.
- **A new law effective from 1 April 2022** enables public authorities to co-finance charging infrastructure on their own locations where the market cannot deliver the task itself — co-financing on own land permitted **through and including 2024**. Municipalities also gain the ability to finance chargers for their own fleet and make them publicly accessible outside the hours used by municipal cars.
- **A state subsidy pool for charging-infrastructure co-financing was established in 2022.** Per the **Transportministeriet** (Ministry of Transport), municipalities can "apply for funds from the pool for co-financing of the municipal payment need arising from specific tenders with private operators to install charging infrastructure. Municipalities can obtain co-financing of **50 percent of the total municipal payment**. The pool is distributed on a **first-come-first-served basis until 1 November 2022** or until funds are exhausted." Citation: Transportministeriet (2022), trm.dk/nyheder/2021/150-millioner-kroner-til-endnu-flere-ladestandere-i-2022 — **the pool is described as 150 million DKK** (per the URL/title cited, "150 millioner kroner til endnu flere ladestandere i 2022").

## 3. Ladestanderbekendtgørelsens Krav (Charging Point Executive Order Requirements)
- The Executive Order, in force from **1 March 2020**, covers preparation and installation of charge stands, with requirements for both private and public building owners, covering existing buildings, new construction, and major renovations.

**Table 5 — Requirements and their meaning for Køge Kommune as building owner (verbatim structure):**
| Building/work type | Requirement | Meaning for Køge Kommune |
|---|---|---|
| New residential building, >10 parking spaces | Prepare all parking spaces for chargers | Relevant at new construction |
| New non-residential building, >10 parking spaces | Install min. 1 charge stand + prepare min. every 5th parking space | Relevant at new construction |
| Residential building undergoing major renovation, >10 parking spaces | Prepare all renovated parking spaces for chargers | Relevant for **17 buildings** owned by Køge Kommune, if they undergo major renovation |
| Non-residential building undergoing major renovation, >10 parking spaces | Install min. 1 charge stand + prepare min. every 5th renovated parking space | Relevant for **32 buildings** owned by Køge Kommune, if they undergo major renovation |
| Existing non-residential building, >20 parking spaces | Install min. 1 charge stand before 1 January 2025 | **20 buildings** in Køge Kommune covered by this requirement — see map/Bilag 1 for list |

- **"Larger renovation" ("større ombygning") defined in the Executive Order (footnote, verbatim):** construction work where costs of installing/preparing for chargers are under **7%** of total renovation costs, resulting in more than **10 parking spaces** after renovation, and where the renovation covers: (a) the building's electrical infrastructure and/or the parking facility itself, when the parking facility is located inside the building, or (b) the electrical infrastructure at the parking facility and/or the parking area itself, when the parking facility is attached to the building.
- The Executive Order will affect other building owners beyond Køge Kommune, so more charge stands will appear at businesses and housing as major renovations occur — but implementation of the Executive Order alone is assessed as **not sufficient to cover the full future charging need**.

## 4. Køge Kommune as Fleet Owner (bilflådeejer)
- Per Køge Kommune's climate plan, the **entire municipal fleet is targeted to be electric-powered no later than 2030.**
- Considerations for fleet-charger siting: whether the parking lot is municipally owned, and expected parking duration of municipal vehicles there. Sometimes it's better for the lot owner to invest in chargers itself; other times the municipality may invest in charging infrastructure on non-municipally-owned lots.
- Fleet chargers will **not, as a starting point, be publicly accessible** — municipal employees must always be able to charge when needed — though sharing with citizens/visitors when not in use by municipal cars may be possible in some situations.
- **Køge Kommune's stated commitments (verbatim intent):**
  - Convert the fleet at a pace that makes good economic and environmental sense — **but no later than 2030**
  - Expand charging infrastructure at a pace matching the fleet conversion

## 5. Køge Kommune as Building Owner (bygningsejer)
- Implementing the Executive Order will result in charge-stand installation at Køge Kommune buildings. **20 municipal buildings are covered by the requirement to install at least 1 charge stand before 1 January 2025** (see Kort 2, Bilag 1 Table 1 for the location list).
- To optimize charger utilization, Køge Kommune wants these chargers to be **publicly accessible**, serving both building staff and nearby residents — e.g., non-staff access in evenings/nights when the lot is otherwise unused.
- After market dialogue with operators, Køge Kommune will put these locations **out to tender in 2022**. If not all locations are covered in the first round, a new tender will follow so all are established **by end of 2024**. These locations will be tendered together with public-charger locations (Kort 3), to maximize municipal coverage while letting operators finance and operate the stands. Whether to install more stands than the Executive Order strictly requires at individual locations will be assessed.
- **Stated commitments:**
  - Meet the Executive Order's requirements, installing min. 1 charge stand at parking lots attached to municipal buildings **by end of 2024**
  - Tender all or most locations in **2022**
  - If not all locations are tendered in 2022, a new tender no later than **2024**

## 6. Køge Kommune as Authority (myndighed)
Two roles: (1) building-permit authority (byggesagsbehandling), ensuring Executive Order compliance; (2) authority over charge-stand siting on public land.

### 6.1 Publicly Accessible Charge Stands
- The **~7%** of Køge residents unable to park on own land/housing-association lots (per DEA/DTU) especially need public charge stands.
- Due to accident risk and aesthetic considerations, **the municipality does not allow cables run across sidewalks** for EV charging; also **not permitted to install private chargers on public roads**. As a default, the municipality **does not allow public chargers on public roads** for traffic reasons.
- Public charging infrastructure will also serve visitors to the municipality.
- **Site-selection criteria (verbatim list):**
  - Goal: residents without on-property/housing-association parking should be able to charge **within 1 km of their home**
  - Locations chosen expecting housing associations will install their own chargers for residents, reducing public-charger need for those residents
  - A publicly accessible charge stand in **every larger town** (Køge, Herfølge, Ølby, Ejby, Borup, Lille Skensved, Bjæverskov, Vemmedrup, and Algestrup)
  - Chargers can be placed on a municipally-owned public parking lot; **not placed along public roads by default**, for traffic reasons
  - Distance to shopping, culture, sport, leisure facilities
  - Proximity to existing chargers and to chargers required at municipal sites under the Executive Order
  - Distance to public transport, supporting park-and-ride
  - Broad geographic spread across the municipality
  - Possibility of installing multiple chargers at the same site — **cheaper to install chargers in groups than individually**; also more convenient for EV drivers since the chance of finding a free charger is higher
- **Tendering process:** dialogue with citizens (public meeting, social media) to gather location preferences; based on this and "Kort 4" (the combined map), the municipality will prepare a **tender for locations in 2022**. Tendering all mapped locations together supports more competition, better prices, higher citizen service, and allows smaller operators to bid, helping ensure charge points even in smaller towns. A market dialogue will precede the tender. By tendering a combined package, operators are expected to bear the installation costs.
- **Stated commitments:**
  - Ensure the Executive Order is followed for renovations/new construction
  - Tender all or most selected locations in 2022
  - If not all locations tendered in 2022, a new tender no later than 2024

### 6.2 Principles for Charge-Stand Siting on Parking Lots
**Køge Kommune will work to ensure (verbatim list):**
- Chargers installed in **clusters**, to reduce the chance of EV drivers driving in vain looking for a charger
- Chargers placed close to existing power outlets
- Chargers placed, as far as possible, to serve drivers who genuinely need to charge — i.e., not necessarily placed "at the front" of the lot
- Empty conduit ("tomrør") pulled for additional future chargers whenever excavation is already occurring
- Multiple charging operators present at larger parking lots, to ensure better competition
- "Charging petrol stations" ("ladetankstationer") sited away from town centers should have kiosk and toilet facilities

## 7. Køge Kommune as Cooperation Partner (samarbejdspartner)

### 7.1 Citizens
- Citizens have been and will continue to be involved in identifying relevant charge-stand locations. A notably successful event: **"Kør Grøn Dag"** (Drive Green Day), where residents actively shared where they thought chargers should be sited. Further citizen input on siting was planned for **spring 2022**, ahead of tendering the locations shown on Kort 4.

### 7.2 Boligforeninger (Housing Associations)
- **20% of housing in Køge Kommune** is in housing associations (defined here as ejer/andel/lejerforeninger — owner/cooperative/tenant associations — and private rental properties). Many have shared parking lots; some will be covered by the Executive Order, but only at major renovations. Outside renovations, it's up to the individual association to install its own chargers. Køge's housing associations are described as positive toward this development, with several planning charger installation. The municipality will hold **information meetings** to advise and inspire associations.

### 7.3 Virksomheder (Businesses)
- Of time parked away from home, **57% is at workplaces** — the longest away-from-home parking duration (source: DEA/DTU 2019). Workplaces/businesses are therefore key partners. Some businesses in the municipality fall under the Executive Order and must install at least 1 charge stand at existing buildings **before 1 January 2025**.
- **SME exemption** (footnote, verbatim): the exemption applies if the building is owned/used by small or medium-sized enterprises. If a public authority, private individual, or large company is among the owners/users, the exemption does not apply. **SME definition: fewer than 250 employees and annual turnover under €50 million, or total annual balance sheet under €43 million** — per the European Commission's Recommendation of 6 May 2003 on the definition of micro, small and medium-sized enterprises. Source: "Vejledning til bekendtgørelse om forberedelse til og etablering af ladestandere i forbindelse med bygninger" (ladestanderbekendtgørelsen guidance).
- Businesses in Køge Kommune have shown general interest in installing chargers for customers/employees. Together with **Connect Køge** (a named local business network), the municipality has organized meetings with businesses about charger installation; similar informational meetings planned for the future.

### 7.4 Cooperation with Retail (Detailhandlen)
- Per DEA/DTU: **5.5%** of time parked away from home is for shopping, and ~**4%** for cinema/café/restaurant visits, typically **1–2 hours** per visit.
- The municipality will cooperate with local businesses and retail in town centers to support charger installation, based on identified private-lot locations that would benefit shoppers/visitors (Kort 5, Bilag 1 Table 3).
- **Stated commitment:** support, through dialogue, housing associations, businesses, and retail installing chargers where citizens have need.

## Relevance Notes for HDEV/Depot Tool (Claude's own framing, not from source)
- This is a light-duty/passenger-EV municipal strategy document, but the **charger cost table (Table 3, sourced from COWI 2020)** giving concrete 2020-era Danish DKK price points for normal/fast/rapid public chargers (including specific connection-fee amperage assumptions — 35A reduced fee for a 22kW double charger, 125A reduced fee for a 50-75kW fast charger, 250A full connection fee for a 150kW rapid charger) is a useful, sourced cost-reference baseline, even though these are passenger-vehicle-scale chargers, not HD truck chargers.
- The municipal fleet electrification framing (Section 4, "Køge Kommune as fleet owner") — including the practice of not making fleet chargers publicly accessible by default, and the option to share excess capacity outside fleet-use hours — is a directly relevant governance pattern for public-sector depot/fleet charging in Denmark.
