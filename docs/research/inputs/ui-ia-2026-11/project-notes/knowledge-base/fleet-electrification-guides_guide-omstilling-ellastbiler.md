# Guide to Transitioning to Electric Trucks (Region Hovedstaden / Copenhagen Electric)

**Source file:** `Guide til omstilling til ellastbiler.pdf`
**Category:** Fleet electrification guide / policy report
**Original language:** Danish (translated to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** A November 2023 practical guide from Region Hovedstaden's Copenhagen Electric knowledge center, condensing a fuller GoGreen Denmark report, offering a 10-step process for converting a truck fleet to electric, with CO2/TCO tool references, tax changes, and three real-world route case examples (DFDS, Unicon, Dansk Cater).

## Why Invest in Electric Trucks Now (Key Arguments)

- Danish government ambition for Denmark to be a green frontrunner will increasingly pressure diesel-truck operators — driven by public-sector green procurement requirements and private companies' emission-free freight goals for climate accounting.
- The new kilometer-based road charge (see Taxes section) makes now an opportune time to consider electric trucks.
- **Diesel trucks have a 766% higher CO2 emission** than electric trucks over the vehicle's full lifetime (source cited: regionh.dk, "Tung transport" — Region Hovedstaden webpage; footnote 1).
- Diesel trucks emit no particulates or NOx (when electric) and are quieter, benefiting human health.
- Electric trucks provide a competitive/sales argument for companies and a quieter, calmer workday for drivers.

### Listed benefits of switching to electric drive
- Runs silently — enables night-time deliveries in cities
- Matches new legal requirements and customer/municipal demands for emission-free driving
- Cheaper to operate and service
- Few "teething problems" despite being new technology
- Markedly better cab working environment
- An extra sales argument for company sales staff
- Creates internal pride
- Future-proofs the company

### Pace of development
- Electric trucks are described as being where electric passenger cars were 10 years ago, but developing faster.
- Range up to **300-400 km** on one charge is already being discussed.
- **By 2025**, truck manufacturers expect: range to increase by approximately **50%**, charging power to **more than double**, and charging time to correspondingly **halve**.

## Upcoming Taxes — Kilometer-Based Road Charge from 2025

- From **2025**, the existing **vejbenyttelsesafgift** (road usage charge / Eurovignette-type charge) is replaced by a **kilometerafgift** (kilometer-based charge) that scales with the CO2 emissions a truck causes.
- Charge is calculated per kilometer traveled.
- **From 2025**, it initially applies only to the overordnede vejnet (the primary road network) — state roads (statsveje) and parts of the municipal road network. Government intention is for the **entire public road network to be covered from 2028**.
- **Sund og Bælt** administers the scheme and publishes which roads are subject to the charge from January 2025 vs. from January 2028.
- The charge is calculated based on a new classification system: **CO2-emissionsklasser** (CO2 emission classes). **Motorstyrelsen** (Danish Motor Vehicle Agency) and **Færdselsstyrelsen** (Danish Road Traffic Authority) are developing the model and classification, which will appear in **Motorregisteret (DMR)** (the Danish Motor Register) for all Danish trucks.
- This CO2-emissionsklasse system has **no correspondence with the current Euro emissions standard (Euronorm)**.
- Trucks with an **electric drivetrain will be placed in emissionsklasse 5** (the top/cleanest class).
- The charge is also differentiated by whether driving occurs within **miljøzoner** (environmental/low-emission zones). Miljøzoner currently exist in **København (Copenhagen), Frederiksberg, Aarhus, Odense, and Aalborg**. Driving within these zones costs **50% more** than the rest of the road network.
- The kilometer charge initially applies to trucks **over 12 tons**; the intention is to extend it to trucks **over 3.5 tons from 2027**.
- Per the original political agreement (referenced on Skatteministeriet's website), rates were originally planned to increase from 2028, but **there is currently no clarification on whether this will happen** ("der er dog pt ikke nogen afklaring").

## Work Environment (Arbejdsmiljø)

- Diesel trucks expose drivers to constant noise and vibration throughout the day, causing fatigue.
- Electric trucks eliminate noise/vibration in the cab and during outside-vehicle work; no gear shifting; drivers report being able to concentrate better on traffic.
- Quote: *"De chauffører, der har været så heldige at få tildelt en ellastbil, vil meget nødigt ud af den igen. Det er simpelthen en fornøjelse at arbejde støjfrit uden at høre på en diesel- og kølemotor, der står og banker hele tiden."* — **Flemming Nielsen**, director, AB Catering København AS.
- Case vignette: **Kurt Pedersen**, 73-year-old driver at **Unicon**, tested Unicon's new electric concrete-mixer truck and delayed his retirement by 5 years as a result, citing no vibration, no engine noise, and reduced fatigue after a workday.

## Off-Peak Delivery (Varelevering i ydertimerne)

- Electric trucks' lower noise level enables deliveries during off-peak/night hours ("ydertimerne").
- Benefits: routes completed faster outside rush-hour traffic (potential savings on driver wages and fuel/energy); customers benefit from goods being delivered before stores open, enabling faster restocking.
- **Caveat:** equipment used for off-peak delivery (e.g., roll cages/"bure") must be certified under the **PIEK-certificering** (PIEK certification — a Dutch-origin noise standard for quiet urban/off-peak delivery equipment).
- Further reading referenced: Copenhagen Electric's report **"Grøn Bylogistik"** (Green City Logistics) — likely available separately; not one of the 8 files processed in this batch, but appears to be a related Copenhagen Electric resource (title matches a project file "Grøn byggelogistik i praksis.pdf" / "GrønBylogistik_rapport - final.pdf" listed among the project's files — worth cross-referencing if not already processed).

## Real-World Examples (Case Vignettes)

### Range as a perceived barrier
- Range is described as one of the biggest perceived challenges/concerns with electric trucks — an expectation that capacity won't meet operational needs.
- Counterpoint fact cited: **approximately 40% of all road-freight trips on European highways are shorter than 300 km** (source: Eurostat, "Road freight transport by distance class, 2018-2020 (million tkm)"; footnote 3, ec.europa.eu/eurostat).
- Fast chargers ("lynladere") for trucks are now available at multiple locations in Denmark, enabling mid-route charging for longer range needs.

### Weather effects
- Range is always affected by weather. Rain, cold, and especially crosswind/sidewind increase energy consumption and reduce electric truck range.

### Driving technique
- An experienced driver trained in energy-efficient driving can use **approximately 20% less energy** than a less experienced driver, purely through optimized driving technique.
- Key technique: smooth, minimal braking/acceleration — even more important in electric trucks because braking energy is recuperated and stored in the battery, extending range.

### Charging time = rest time
- Route planning should align charging time with mandatory driver rest periods (køre/hviletid — driving/rest time regulations).
- A fast-charge stop mid-route typically takes **45 minutes to 1 hour**, which the driver can use for administrative tasks, eating, and rest — integrating charging into a new drive-rest-work routine.

### Route Example 1 — 540 km (DFDS)
- DFDS drives daily from **Arla's dairy in Hobro** to **Slagelse** and back — a 540 km round trip.
- Example single-day sequence:
  1. Electric truck starts nearly fully charged at 8:00 AM in Hobro, heading to Slagelse.
  2. After ~230 km, arrives at **Uno-X Trucks'** charging facility in **Nyborg** with ~21% battery remaining. Charges at **250 kW** for ~80 minutes while the driver eats/rests.
  3. Continues to Slagelse and back, this time with a 45-minute charging stop in **Horsens**, enough charge for the trip home.
  4. Back in Hobro, charged overnight via a depot charger, ready for the next morning.
- "Figur 4" (Figure 4): Route diagram of the DFDS example (visual map/route diagram — exact graphic content not reproducible from extracted text beyond the sequence described above).
- Further reading referenced: DFDS's case on Elbilviden.dk.

### Route Example 2 — 200 km (Unicon)
- Unicon has invested in electric concrete mixer trucks, fitted with an electrified concrete drum requiring significant energy.
- Trucks have range up to **300 km**, sufficient for Unicon's needs since each concrete truck rarely drives more than **200 km/day**.
- Charged via a depot charger overnight at the factory, during periods when factory production is idle anyway — no need to invest in increased electrical capacity.
- Further reading referenced: Unicon's case on Elbilviden.dk.

### Route Example 3 — 100 km (Dansk Cater)
- Dansk Cater transports chilled/frozen goods to professional kitchens, day and night.
- Each electric truck runs two trips per day; trucks have a large cooler and lift used frequently; route is approximately **100 km**.
- Further reading referenced: Dansk Cater's case on Elbilviden.dk.

## Methodology / Framework: "Omstilling til ellastbil i 10 trin" (Transition to Electric Truck in 10 Steps)

This is the guide's core methodology, summarized from the fuller underlying report (with section references to that report, e.g. "afsnit 5.1"):

1. **Establish a working group** (section 5.1) — form a cross-organizational working group so different competencies can contribute to the process.
2. **Map requirements for the future electric truck and your needs** (section 5.2) — the battery is by far the most expensive single component, so avoid oversizing it; aim for a tailored solution matching concrete operational needs and specifications, while also considering longer-term flexibility if the truck may need to handle other future tasks.
3. **Map the route** (section 5.2) — determine how many kilometers the route covers.
4. **Enter market dialogues with truck manufacturers** (section 5.2) — once the route is known, engage manufacturers to learn what products fit your needs.
5. **Map battery capacity requirements** (section 5.2) — all energy for driving and auxiliary functions comes from the battery; account for cooling/freezing capacity, cranes, lifts, or other equipment/tasks the truck must be sized for.
6. **Clarify the charging plug location on the electric truck** (section 5.3.1) — no standard exists for plug placement; it can be on either the right or left side of the vehicle — important for planning the charging bay layout.
7. **Clarify whether the electric truck can charge with both AC and DC** (section 5.3.1) — some manufacturers offer both AC and DC charging, others DC-only; not a technical problem, but there are significant economic differences.
8. **Map the charging window for the electric truck** (section 5.3.2) — determine how many hours the truck has to charge at the depot after finishing the day's route(s) until the next day's route(s) begin.
9. **Explore the best charging solution for your depot** (section 5.3.2) — determine whether AC or DC is needed, whether multiple outlets are needed, whether the charger needs to distribute power across multiple vehicles, what power level is needed, and whether the charger should be portable or fixed-mounted.
10. **Clarify whether there is sufficient power capacity at the depot** (section 5.3.2) — check local grid substation loading via **Radius** (DSO) to see if there's room for charging infrastructure on the current installation or if the grid needs to be expanded.

**Follow-up step after the 10 steps:** Perform a total-cost-of-ownership (TCO) calculation combined with an LCA calculation (referencing sections 4.1 & 1.2 of the underlying report) to assess economic and climate impact. This should account for:
- Establishment costs for the vehicle and charging infrastructure
- Operating costs: fuel/energy, service, insurance, and taxes
- Using the TCO tool on Region Hovedstaden's website, adaptable to the specific company's circumstances
