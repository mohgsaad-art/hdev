## Section 1: Definitions
Key defined terms (translated):
- **Dag** (Day): a calendar day
- **Parterne** (The Parties): Ordregiver and Operatøren jointly
- **Kontrakten** (The Contract): this agreement with its appendices, signed by both parties
- **Ydelser** (Services): the Operator's obligations under the Contract
- **Ladepark** (Charging Park): all charging stations and other installations the Operator must establish/operate on the leased area under the Contract
- **Ladestander** (Charging station): a device for transferring electrical energy to electric vehicles
- **Normalladestander/Normallader** (Normal charger): a charging station transferring electricity at the power level specified in the Kravspecifikation
- **Hurtigladestander/Hurtiglader** (Fast charger): as above, at fast-charger power level
- **Lynladestander/Lynlader** (Rapid charger): as above, at rapid-charger power level
- **Ladepunkt** (Charging point): a single charging outlet on a charging station to which one EV can connect
- **Ladeplads** (Charging space): the parking space where an EV can wait while using a Ladepunkt
- **Slutbrugere** (End users): road users who use charging stations to charge an EV
- **Slutbrugerpris** (End-user price): the price an end user pays to use the Operator's charging services for a specific charging session — can be an Ad hoc-pris or an Abonnementspris
- **Ad hoc-pris** (Ad hoc price): the price paid for a specific charging session without needing to register, sign a written agreement, or enter a longer-term relationship with the Operator
- **Abonnementskunde** (Subscription customer): an end user who, beyond simply purchasing the charging service, registers, signs a written agreement, or enters a longer-term relationship with the Operator
- **Abonnementspris** (Subscription price): the price a subscription customer pays for a specific charging session

## Section 2: Background, Purpose, and General Conditions
The Contract is entered between the contracting authority (Ordregiver) and the Operator with the purpose of the Operator establishing and making available charging points for EV road users at locations specified in the tender material. The contract follows a competitive tender process and grants the Operator use of designated areas. Contract covers a specific "delkontrakt XX" (sub-contract) and may include options.

Purpose also includes ensuring charging points are made available on terms that let as many EV users as possible use the charging stations; the contracting authority aims for a technology-neutral or generic solution suited to as many EV users as possible.

At a specified location, a specified number of charging points is established, reserved for passenger cars and vans up to **3,500 kg**.

**Alternative financing models built into the template (mutually exclusive, filled in per project):**
- **Model A (municipality prepares site):** the contracting authority establishes and builds the area where charging stations are located, arranges electricity access, and pays the grid connection fee; the Operator bears all investment and operating risk for the charging stations themselves.
- **Model B (Operator pays the municipality):** the Operator pays a one-off lump sum to the contracting authority for the right to establish/operate the charging stations (see Section 13); the Operator is otherwise remunerated only through end-user payments.
- **Model C (municipality pays the Operator, "Støttebeløb"):** the Operator receives a one-off lump sum from the contracting authority to establish/operate the charging stations, in addition to being remunerated through end-user payments.

### 2.1 Operator's Rights
Upon signing, the Operator gains the right to operate charging stations at the specified locations, per the technical specifications in the Kravspecifikation (Requirements Specification).

## Section 3: The Leased Area and Charging Station Locations
The contracting authority leases "Lejearealer" (leased areas) to the Operator; their locations appear in Bilag 1 (Appendix 1). For each location, the Operator and contracting authority discuss the precise placement of charging stations. The Operator is responsible for adequate signage so end users can locate the charging stations. Signage must comply with the Danish road regulations ("vejreglerne") — specifically sign **E 33 Parkering** with under-sign **UE 33,4**, or an under-sign reading "Kun opladning af elkøretøjer" (EV charging only), plus any time restriction. A signage plan must be submitted for road authority approval before installation.

## Section 4: Establishment of the Charging Stations
Once the precise placement is agreed, the Operator sends an order letter ("bestillerbrev") to the grid company. Once a date for the necessary electricity supply capacity is confirmed, the Operator must submit a timeline per location; any subsequent changes must be promptly notified to the contracting authority with an updated timeline.

The Operator, acting as developer/client ("bygherre"), must establish the charging stations so they can be used by end users **no later than 28 days** after the grid company confirms sufficient capacity at the connection point. Charging stations must be function-tested for adequacy within this 28-day window; minor outstanding items without real impact on functionality are accepted after the 28 days. The contracting authority has the right to test the charging stations itself or via a third party, or to request the Operator document functionality.

Establishment and technical installation may only begin once the contracting authority has given written approval of detail drawings and the signage plan. The responsibility split is further detailed in the Kravspecifikation; the Operator bears, at its own cost and risk, all expenses related to establishing charging points/stations, including groundworks, connection fee, laying of any ducting and cables to the grid company's designated connection points, and foundations for charging stations. The contracting authority must ensure the Operator gets access to and use of the leased area sufficient to begin establishment activities.

## Section 5: Regulatory Approvals
All necessary regulatory permits/approvals for establishing and operating the charging stations that fall under the Operator's obligations (per the Kravspecifikation) are obtained by the Operator.

## Section 6: Requirements for the Charging Stations

### 6.1 Overall
The Operator must operate the charging stations under this Contract no later than 28 days after the grid company provides sufficient supply capacity at the connection point. From that date, throughout the contract period, the Operator is responsible for ensuring the charging stations meet all requirements under applicable legislation, including the **Lov om infrastruktur for alternative drivmidler til transport (AFI-loven)** (Law on infrastructure for alternative fuels for transport).

### 6.2 Number of Charging Points
The Operator must establish the number of charging points specified in Bilag 1 for each location covered by the awarded sub-agreements and options. The Operator must inform the contracting authority of any signs of supply-capacity scarcity at the leased areas (e.g., high utilization rate) and must engage in dialogue about any need to reduce or scale up the number of charging points/stations (see also Section 20).

### 6.3 End-User Prices
The total price the end user pays per kWh (including fees, costs, VAT, and all taxes) must be clearly displayed on the charging station or on the Operator's website/app, so the end user can see it before starting a charging session.

**If Model B applies (Operator pays a lump sum to the contracting authority):** revenue under this Contract may not form the basis for paying bonuses, discount shares, or other compensation to the municipality's employees. The price of electricity sold from the charging stations may not exceed the price level for comparable services in the relevant market area, determined per the principles of **Konkurrencelovens § 5 a** (Danish Competition Act, section 5a).

#### 6.3.1 Maximum Discount for Subscription Customers
The Operator may offer subscription agreements but may not require end users to hold a subscription to use the charging stations — non-subscribers must always be able to use and pay for charging. If offering a subscription, the Operator must ensure it complies with applicable law and has reasonable terms, including a reasonable notice period for cancellation. Subscribers may be offered special discounts, bonuses, etc.

**The following applies only where the contracting authority assesses limited competition at the location**, per **BEK nr. 1073 af 28/06/2022** (Executive Order on contracting authorities' conclusion of agreements on the right to operate publicly accessible charging points): if the Operator is the sole supplier of charging services at a leased area, the Operator may grant subscription customers a **maximum 10% discount** compared to the Ad hoc price at that location.

Discount calculation methodology, per subscription type:
- **(A)** For subscriptions where customers pay only per use, with a discount on the Ad hoc price (typically %) or on consumption (typically DKK/kWh): the discount is the difference between the Ad hoc price at the time of charging and the final invoiced price after discount.
- **(B)** For other subscription types (e.g., a fixed fee regardless of consumption, or a fixed fee plus consumption-based payment): the discount is the difference between the average Ad hoc price at the location during the billing period and the average effective kWh price paid by subscribers under the Operator's most common subscription type during that billing period (e.g., a calendar month).

#### 6.3.2 Documentation
The Operator must submit an annual **tro- og loveerklæring** (statement of honor/declaration on oath) to the municipality confirming subscription customers have not received a discount exceeding 10%, first submitted by [date] and then every following December during the Contract's term.

The Operator must, within a reasonable time upon the municipality's request, provide documentation of monthly average kWh prices for ad hoc vs. subscription customers per leased area, and documentation of discount sizes — the municipality may request documentation covering at most the **preceding 12 months**. If the Operator fails to provide requested documentation by the deadline, the municipality may impose a daily penalty of **<5,000> DKK** per working day until the documentation is provided.

If the municipality judges it cannot itself assess whether submitted documentation sufficiently proves compliance, it may send the documentation to an independent third party (e.g., an auditor) for assessment; the third party's fee is paid by the municipality if the documentation is found sufficient, otherwise by the Operator.

Both parties are obliged to continuously assess the competitive situation to determine whether charging points under the agreement are subject to limited competition (and thus the discount cap); this can be assessed based on statistical data and discussed at status meetings. The Contract should specify how this obligation is handled by both parties. The municipality must set an appropriate walking or driving distance from charging points under the Contract to the nearest publicly accessible competing operator's charging point, to determine whether a point is under limited competition — this distance is influenced by factors like topography, expected end-user age, and road surface/pavement.

## Section 7: Statistics
Upon the contracting authority's request and at no cost, the Operator must provide statistical data on charging history and charging-point data within **30 days**. The Kravspecifikation specifies what data the contracting authority can, at minimum, request. The contracting authority **cannot request statistics more than 4 times per year**. Statistics must be prepared per the contracting authority's specified format in **Excel format** and sent electronically, unless otherwise agreed.

## Section 8: Operation of the Charging Stations
The contracting authority is responsible for cleaning the area where charging stations are located, including winter service (snow clearing, sweeping, salting) at a level matching the contracting authority's cleaning of the rest of the area. If the Operator wants a higher cleaning level, it must arrange this itself. **Chemical pesticides may not be used** in area cleaning. The Operator must ensure compliance with functionality, uptime, and response-time requirements per the Kravspecifikation. The Operator has no right to conduct any business from the charging stations other than selling charging services — e.g., no right to sell retail goods.

## Section 9: Consumption Costs and Insurance
The Operator pays all operating, consumption, and maintenance costs within the area boundaries. The contracting authority pays any land tax on the area plus interest/installments on any road/sewer debt, plus winter service per Section 8. The Operator must insure the charging stations against fire, water damage, etc., and must carry third-party liability insurance. The Operator must document insurance compliance upon the contracting authority's request.

## Section 10: Road Works
The contracting authority, as road authority, may carry out any work on the area and adjacent road areas without this giving rise to compensation claims from the Operator for resulting inconvenience; work must be carried out with maximum consideration for the Operator. If end-user access to the area is fully cut off for a period, the Operator may close the charging stations during that period. The contracting authority may only cut off access after **6 weeks' prior notice**, except for urgent/emergency works, which can occur without notice if necessary; interruptions can only occur when absolutely necessary and for the shortest possible period.

## Section 11: Labor Clause and Corporate Responsibility
A standard Danish public-procurement **arbejdsklausul** (labor clause), requiring:
1. **Clause:** the Operator must ensure its workers and any sub-advisors' workers involved in fulfilling the agreement receive pay (including special benefits), working hours, and other conditions no less favorable than those applying to work of the same kind under a **collective agreement** entered by the most representative labor market parties in the relevant occupational field in Denmark, applicable across the whole Danish territory. Covers workers employed in performing the task **in Denmark**. Workers, including those of subcontractors in any chain, must be informed of these working conditions. All wage payments must go through a **digital payment system** (e.g., bank payment system). The contracting authority or a third party, including representatives from **Statens Kontrolenhed for Arbejdsklausuler** (the Danish State's Control Unit for Labor Clauses), may make unannounced workplace visits during the agreement to verify compliance.
2. **Documentation:** the contracting authority can at any time request relevant documentation of compliance, in Danish, Norwegian, Swedish, or English (with authorized Danish translation if the original is in another language). Upon written demand, the Operator must provide relevant documentation within **10 working days**, including pay/time records with daily start/end times, e-income receipts, pension contribution receipts, occupational injury insurance, payroll accounts, residence/work permits, employment contracts, worker contact information, and the reference framework used to set pay/working conditions — plus, for posted workers, documentation of board, lodging, transport, and incidentals. The Operator must retain this documentation for the full agreement term and must comply with data protection law before handing over material.
3. **Sanctions:** failure to provide documentation within the deadline incurs a daily penalty of **2,000 DKK** per working day until provided. If the Operator breaches the labor clause and this leads to a legitimate worker claim, the contracting authority can withhold payments to cover such claims (up to the level owed under the clause), plus the Operator is fined **25,000 DKK per worker** with a legitimate claim; withheld amounts also cover reasonable costs of assessing, verifying, and paying out compensation. The contracting authority may seek advice from relevant employer/employee organizations to assess compliance.

## Section 12: Cooperation and Subcontractors

### 12.1 Cooperation and Confidentiality
The parties must cooperate and act loyally toward each other to fulfill the Contract's purpose. The Operator, its employees, and any subcontractors/their employees must maintain confidentiality regarding information about the contracting authority's and others' affairs learned through the Contract. Public-administration confidentiality rules apply to the contracting authority. The Operator may use the contracting authority as a reference but may not, without prior written permission, issue public statements about the Contract or publicize its content — any publication, exhibition, or public disclosure (press, trade press, radio, TV, internet, social media) must go through or be pre-approved by the contracting authority. The Operator must ensure staff assigned to the Contract have the necessary competencies at all times.

### 12.2 Use of Subcontractors
The Operator is liable for subcontractors' performance exactly as for its own; using subcontractors does not limit the Operator's responsibility for meeting the Contract's requirements.

## Section 13: Payment
Amounts stated exclude VAT.

### 13.1 Lejeafgift (Rental Fee) — applies under Model B
The Operator pays a one-off lump sum for leasing the areas, per the Tilbudsliste (Price List). Payment is due at commissioning of the charging stations, at latest 28 days after the grid company provides sufficient supply capacity; if the delay is attributable to the contracting authority, the payment deadline is postponed accordingly. Late payment incurs default interest per the **Renteloven** (Danish Interest Act).

### 13.2 Støttebeløb (Support Amount) — applies under Model C
The contracting authority pays the Operator a one-off lump sum to establish and operate the charging stations for the Contract's duration, per the Tilbudsliste (Price List); payment is due at commissioning.

## Section 14: Ownership and Transfer Upon Termination

### 14.1 Ownership
The contracting authority retains full ownership of the area. The Operator retains ownership of the charging stations and all other equipment it installs, plus the cables run from the connection point to the charging stations, until Contract termination.

### 14.2 Possible Cable Transfer Upon Termination
If a location will continue serving as a charging location after Contract termination (i.e., the contracting authority continues using it as a charging space for EVs/hybrids), the Operator may sell its connection contribution, supply cables, and any additional equipment to a future operator, with cables transferred fully functional; the Operator must assist a smooth handover, including providing necessary background information free of charge. As compensation for transferring cable ownership, the Operator can receive at most an amount equal to the cables' value based on **linear depreciation without price adjustment of the acquisition/installation cost over 20 years**. The contracting authority may require the Operator to provide a certified public accountant's statement confirming the amount's accuracy.

### 14.3 Reinstatement Upon Termination
If the contracting authority does not enter a new agreement with the Operator, and the Operator does not arrange a sale to a new operator for the connection contribution, supply cables, charging stations, and related equipment, the contracting authority may require the Operator to reinstate the area at the Operator's own cost. The Operator must generally remove cables during reinstatement, unless the contracting authority consents (not obligatory) to leaving them, in which case the Operator must secure them properly per applicable law. If reinstatement is required, the Operator must remove all installations/equipment above and below ground and reinstate the area at its own cost before Contract termination; in case of early termination (e.g., due to Operator breach), the Operator retains at minimum **10 working days** for reinstatement regardless of the termination notice period. If reinstatement is not completed within the deadline, the contracting authority may have the work done at the Operator's expense.

The Operator cannot, without the contracting authority's written approval, transfer ownership or sublease operations wholly or partly to others — though the contracting authority will look favorably on such transfer/sublease provided service levels aren't reduced, the contracting authority's obligations/risk aren't increased, and it's compatible with procurement rules.

### 14.4 Termination Due to Operator's Breach or Bankruptcy
Upon Contract termination due to the Operator's material breach (per 15.3, including bankruptcy), the contracting authority may (but is not obliged to) transfer ownership to a third party, with charging stations transferred fully functional; the Operator must assist a smooth handover. As compensation, the Operator receives an amount equal to the value of the charging stations based on **linear depreciation without price adjustment of the acquisition cost of charging stations and technical cabinets over 10 years**, and the acquisition/installation cost of cables **over 20 years**. The contracting authority may require an accountant's certification of the amount.

## Section 15: Operator's Breach

### 15.1 Breach Related to Establishment
Constitutes breach if: the Operator fails to send an order letter to the grid company within **10 working days** of agreement on precise charging-point placement; establishment is not completed within the **28-day** window from confirmed supply capacity; or the Operator entirely fails to establish the charging stations. If the Operator proves the failure is not attributable to it (e.g., force majeure), a new timeline is agreed and the breach provisions apply to the new timeline.

### 15.2 Breach Related to Operation
Constitutes breach if operation of the charging stations — including end-user pricing per Section 6.3 — is not conducted per the Contract, including the Kravspecifikation's requirements.

### 15.3 Material Breach
Material breach includes (non-exhaustively):
- Operator fails to establish the charging stations, or establishment exceeds the agreed timeline by more than **6 weeks** without valid, documented, notified reason
- Operator repeatedly fails to meet the availability (uptime) requirement
- Operator ceases operating the charging stations
- Operator reduces the agreed service level to a non-trivial degree
- Operator's repeated breach of the same contractual matter, including (non-exhaustively): repeated failure to meet end-user pricing requirements, or repeated failure to submit end-user price documentation
- Operator's bankruptcy, restructuring proceedings, opening of composition negotiations (or equivalent for a foreign company), or materially deteriorated financial condition threatening proper Contract performance, to the extent Danish bankruptcy law allows — if bankrupt, the estate must within **14 days** decide and notify the contracting authority whether it will step into the Contract
- Operator is subject to compulsory dissolution by the **Erhvervsstyrelsen** (Danish Business Authority) or equivalent foreign authority, unless it documents within 10 working days that dissolution grounds don't apply
- Operator found to have acted contrary to the corporate-responsibility requirements in Section 11, having previously breached the Contract similarly
- Operator fails to ensure compliance with applicable law regarding establishment/operation
- Operator fails to comply with the Contract's regulatory-approval provisions
- Operator's continued failure to remedy a matter for which the maximum penalty (per Section 16.1) has been charged
- (Model B only) Operator fails to pay the agreed rental fee when due

If material breach exists, the contracting authority may terminate the Contract immediately and contract with a new operator. The listed breach scenarios are non-exhaustive but indicative of what counts as material breach.

## Section 16: Contracting Authority's Remedies for Breach
Where not expressly stated otherwise, Danish law's general rules on remedies for breach apply, including proportional reduction (forholdsmæssigt afslag).

### 16.1 Penalty (Bod)
**16.1.1 Establishment of the Charging Stations:** if the commissioning deadline (Section 4) is exceeded, the contracting authority is entitled to a daily penalty of **<5,000> DKK per day** until charging stations are established and available to end users, capped at a maximum penalty of **<50,000> DKK**.

**16.1.2 End-User Prices:** if the Operator is found to have invoiced end-user prices in the Charging Park contrary to Section 6.3, penalties apply as follows: **1st instance: <10,000> DKK**; **2nd instance onward: <20,000> DKK**.

**16.1.3 Payment of Penalty:** accrued penalty amounts are paid to the contracting authority on the 1st banking day of each month.

### 16.2 Damages
In case of breach, the Operator must compensate the contracting authority for any loss resulting from the breach per Danish law's general damages rules; the parties have agreed **indirect losses are not compensated**. If the contracting authority terminates per Section 15.3, the Operator must pay the contracting authority's costs of contracting with a new operator. If a third-party damages claim is brought against the contracting authority due to matters the Operator is responsible for, the Operator must indemnify the contracting authority.

### 16.3 Termination (Ophævelse)
If the contracting authority terminates the Contract, the Operator must reinstate the area and dispose of cables per Section 14.

## Section 17: Contracting Authority's Breach
Beyond its other obligations, the contracting authority must ensure the Operator gets access to and use of the area where charging stations are to be established. Failure constitutes breach by the contracting authority.

## Section 18: Operator's Remedies for Breach
Danish law's general rules on remedies for breach apply where not expressly stated otherwise, including proportional reduction. The Operator may claim damages under Danish law's general rules but **may not claim indirect losses, including lost profit**, from the contracting authority.

## Section 19: Force Majeure
Defined as circumstances arising without a party's fault and beyond its control, e.g., war, unusual natural events, and fire. Neither party is liable for force majeure circumstances they could not reasonably have anticipated at Contract signing. The Operator cannot incur a penalty during force majeure. Force majeure can only be invoked for as long as the situation persists.

## Section 20: Changes

### 20.1 Changes in Legislation
The Operator bears the risk of legislative changes that increase its costs of establishing/operating the charging stations. If legislative changes make establishment/operation under this Contract impossible, the concession lapses unless the parties agree otherwise.

### 20.2 Changes in Utilization Rate
The Operator bears the **full economic risk** for a decline in charging-point utilization rate at the leased areas. The Operator cannot reduce the number of charging points/stations without the contracting authority's prior written permission; the Operator may discuss reduction possibilities, providing documentation of a utilization rate supporting reduction — only possible where other legislation (including the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen) can still be met with fewer charging points/stations. Conversely, a high utilization rate may support scaling up; the Operator must engage in dialogue on this (initiated by either party), providing utilization documentation. If the contracting authority wants more charging points/stations at a location but the Operator does not, the contracting authority may tender that opportunity to other operators.

### 20.3 Other Changes
The contracting authority, subject to procurement law, has the right to require changes to the Contract. The Operator can only object if changes would "decisively" alter the Contract's character, including associated risks — meaning the contract's subject matter changes such that deliveries meet a different need of the contracting authority or end users than what naturally falls within the originally awarded Contract's scope. The Operator must promptly provide information on any economic consequences of requested changes; once informed, the contracting authority decides whether to proceed, and if so both parties sign an "allonge" (addendum) that becomes part of the Contract.

## Section 21: Assignment of the Concession Contract

### 21.1 Contracting Authority's Assignment
The contracting authority cannot, without the Operator's prior written permission, assign its rights/obligations wholly or partly to a third party.

### 21.2 Operator's Assignment
The Operator's rights/obligations cannot, without the contracting authority's written permission, be assigned wholly or partly to a third party (see also Section 14).

## Section 22: Termination of the Contract
This Contract runs for X years from [date] and terminates without notice on [date]. Upon termination: (1) cable rights may be transferred per Section 14.2; (2) the Operator must clear all encumbrances before Contract expiry so cables can be transferred to a new operator/third party unencumbered; (3) each party bears its own transfer costs. Termination does not affect the validity of provisions (e.g., confidentiality) intended to survive termination. Regardless of the reason for termination, the Operator must reasonably assist the contracting authority in preparing grounds for a possible new tender of the covered service, and must cooperate as needed with a new operator regarding transition of Services — the Operator receives no separate remuneration for this assistance.

## Section 23: Notice of Termination
The Contract is non-terminable by the Operator during its term, except where grounds for termination-for-breach exist. The contracting authority may terminate with **x months' written notice**, effective end of a month.

### 23.1 Termination Upon Annulment of the Award Decision
The contracting authority may terminate with at least **one month's notice**, effective end of a month, if its award decision is annulled by **Klagenævnet for Udbud** (the Danish Complaints Board for Public Procurement) or the courts. The Operator's potential compensation claims following such termination are generally settled per Section 16.2, and the Contract's termination-right provisions are factored into any loss calculation. If the Operator knew or should have known, at Contract signing, of the factual/legal circumstances causing annulment, it cannot claim compensation for this termination.

## Section 24: Disputes
The Contract is governed by **Danish law**. Disputes are first sought resolved through good-faith negotiation, escalating to senior organizational levels if necessary. Unresolved disputes are settled by **arbitration under the rules of Det Danske Voldgiftsinstitut** (the Danish Institute of Arbitration), with each party naming one arbitrator and the Institute appointing the chair — alternatively, parties may agree disputes go to the ordinary courts with venue in **Copenhagen**.
