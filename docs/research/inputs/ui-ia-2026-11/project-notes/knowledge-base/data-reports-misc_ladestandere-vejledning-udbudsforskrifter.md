## Purpose
This guidance document explains how to approach a charging-point tender and how the individual documents in the tender-template set relate to one another. Cross-references the companion handbook **"Planlægning og etablering af ladeinfrastruktur til elbiler"** for broader guidance on planning, considerations for tendering, and establishing publicly accessible charging points.

## Section 1: General

### 1.1 Content
The set of tender templates ("sættet af forskrifter for udbud af ladepunkter") consists of:
1. **Paradigme - Forskrift for Bestemmelser om udbud og tilbud, BUT** (Template - Regulation for Terms of Tender and Bid)
2. **Paradigme - Forskrift for Kravspecifikation** (Template - Regulation for Requirements Specification)
3. **Paradigme - Forskrift for Tilbudsliste (TBL-P)** (Template - Regulation for Price List)
4. **Paradigme - Forskrift for operatørens serviceniveau** (Template - Regulation for Operator's Service Level)
5. **Paradigme - Udkast til koncessionskontrakt** (Template - Draft Concession Contract)

**Target audience:** primarily **municipalities (kommuner)**, and prepared specifically for tendering charging points installed on **municipal land made available for public use**. The templates cannot be directly used for other procurements, e.g., tenders for charging points used exclusively for the municipality's own vehicle fleet.

**Explicit caveat on currency:** the EV market is developing rapidly; the templates were prepared in the second half of 2022 based on the market, legislation, and general recommendations/trends known at that time. Users are cautioned that conditions may have changed, affecting how tender materials should be designed and how requirements for the operator should be described.

Cross-reference: the handbook **"Planlægning og etablering af ladeinfrastruktur til elbiler"** is referenced for further description of how to plan for charging infrastructure and what considerations are recommended when planning, tendering, and establishing publicly accessible charging points.

### 1.2 Scope Limitation (Afgrænsning)
The tender templates apply to tendering the establishment of charging points **for passenger cars**. The templates **cannot be directly used** for tendering charging infrastructure for the municipal fleet, e-bikes, or heavy vehicles. However, they can provide inspiration for structure, requirements, and competition parameters for tendering other types of charging infrastructure; it is also recommended to jointly consider and coordinate planning and tendering of charging infrastructure across vehicle types.

### 1.4 Terminology
Supplements the terminology defined in the draft-contract template with an additional table (partly overlapping the terminology table in the companion Handbook document). Key terms (English gloss):
- **AC-lader** — low-power charger delivering alternating current; conversion to DC happens in the car; typically up to 22 kW
- **Ampere (A)** — unit of current
- **DC-lader** — high-power charger delivering direct current after conversion happens in the charger itself; typically 50 kW or more
- **Elbil (BEV)** — battery electric vehicle, charged via cable
- **Hurtiglader** — DC charger charging at 50–100 kW
- **Kilowatt (kW)** — unit of power, quantifying charging facility output
- **Kilowatt-time (kWh)** — unit of energy/electricity consumption, quantifying battery size
- **Ladeboks** — wall-mounted charging facility, typically 1–2 outlets
- **Ladefacilitet** — umbrella term for various charging units (ladeboks, ladestander, ladestation, ladepark etc.)
- **Ladeinfrastruktur** — umbrella term for the entire EV charging infrastructure
- **Ladeoperatør** — the actor responsible for establishing, installing, servicing, and operating charging points; can include filling stations supplementing with charging stations (typically rapid chargers)
- **Ladepark** — geographically defined area with multiple charging spaces
- **Ladeplads** — the area designated for an EV to charge; includes a charging point
- **Ladepunkt** — where an EV can charge — in practice, the charging plug/socket
- **Lader** — umbrella term for charging units (ladeboks, ladestander etc.)
- **Ladestander** — a stand delivering power to EVs; can have multiple charging points/plugs
- **Ladeøer** — clusters ("islands") of multiple normal chargers, so EV drivers can easily find a free charger
- **Lynlader** — DC charger charging at a minimum of 150 kW
- **Ladestation** — geographically defined area with many charging points, typically rapid chargers, often at motorways/major roads
- **Normallader** — AC charger charging up to 22 kW
- **Operatør af ladepunkter** — synonym for ladeoperatør
- **Plug-in hybrid (PHEV)** — vehicle with both electric and combustion motor; battery charged both via driving and via charger; typically ~50 km electric range before the combustion engine engages; limited (AC-only) charging speed, so occupies collective charging infrastructure for a long time
- **Udtag** — a charging point's equivalent of a filling station's fuel nozzle

## Section 1.5: Tender Strategy (Udbudsstrategi)

- The contracting authority must conduct the tender **on market terms**, per the AFI-loven.
- The tender must be conducted as a **concession tender (koncessionsudbud)**.
- The contracting authority must first calculate the **value of the concession**, per the objective method in **Koncessionsdirektivets artikel 8, stk. 3** (EU Concessions Directive), which lists the following factors to be taken into account (reproduced in full per source Figur 1.1):
  a) the value of any options and any extensions of the concession's duration
  b) revenue from fees and fines paid by users of the works or services, beyond amounts collected on behalf of the contracting authority
  c) payments or financial advantages of any kind made by the contracting authority or another public authority to the concessionaire, including compensation for fulfilling a public-service obligation and public investment grants
  d) the value of grants or other financial advantages from third parties for performing the concession
  e) revenue from the sale of assets that are part of the concession
  f) the value of all goods and services made available to the concessionaire by the contracting authority, provided they are necessary for performing the works or services
  g) premiums or payments to applicants or bidders
- The calculated concession value must be published alongside the tender.

### Concession Value Thresholds and Legal Basis (per Bekendtgørelse nr. 1073 af 28/06/2022)
| Concession value | Legal basis |
|---|---|
| > 40,100,744 DKK | Tender conducted per the **Koncessionsdirektivet** (EU Concessions Directive) |
| < 40,100,744 DKK but > 1,000,000 DKK | Announcement on **udbud.dk** per **udbudslovens § 192** |
| < 1,000,000 DKK | On market terms per **udbudslovens § 193** |

**This entire tender template package is designed only for the middle tier** — announcement per **udbudslovens § 192**, meaning the Contract must be concluded based on an announcement per udbudslovens § 192 and rules issued under udbudslovens § 191, stk. 2, per Bekendtgørelse nr. 1073 af 28. juni 2022.

Private operators generally self-finance establishment, operation, and removal costs. Municipalities, regions, and inter-municipal partnerships (kommunale fællesskaber) may exceptionally pay operators if, following a tender or market-conformity check per udbudslovens § 193, it's established the agreement cannot otherwise be concluded, per bekendtgørelse nr. 414 af 4. april 2022. **Information about the size of payment the municipality/region is willing to offer for a specific agreement may not be disclosed in advance.**

### 1.5.1 Tender Process
When conducted per udbudslovens § 192, the contracting authority must be aware of:
- Must establish an award procedure complying with **udbudslovens § 2** (equal treatment, transparency, proportionality principles)
- No requirement for detailed descriptions of the tendered item, but bidders must be able to determine the content of the tendered service, and the description must identify key elements, e.g., minimum requirements
- No specific minimum deadlines apply, but deadlines used must account for the required bid materials, contract complexity, and time needed to prepare a bid
- Must establish award criteria and ensure bids are submitted on a uniform basis for equal assessment; no obligation to use award criteria per udbudslovens § 162, but criteria used must respect treaty-based principles, be objective and non-discriminatory, and sub-criteria must be disclosed per the transparency principle
- No obligation to weight or prioritize criteria, but bidders must be able to understand how bids will be assessed
- Must award the contract per the established procedure and criteria
- Must notify all bidders in writing and simultaneously of the award decision, including justification for any procedure cancellation
- Must notify a bidder of the decision's justification upon request; no statutory obligation for notification at prequalification stage, but this obligation is assessed to be the same per procurement-law principles and good administrative practice
- **No obligation to hold a standstill period**
- May restrict the tender to a limited number of applicants (prequalification); selection criteria must be transparent, non-discriminatory, and objective, with no special requirements otherwise; no obligation to invite a specific number of applicants, just enough to ensure effective competition
- Udbudslovens exclusion rules don't apply, but the contracting authority may still weigh such factors if published with the announcement; **no requirement to use the ESPD** (European Single Procurement Document — footnote explains: a self-declaration businesses submit for public tenders, serving as preliminary proof of meeting exclusion/suitability/selection criteria; typically only the tender winner submits full documentation, source: Konkurrence- og Forbrugerstyrelsen)
- The contracting authority has broad freedom to design an appropriate process, including processes resembling known tender forms (restricted tender, open tender, negotiated tender)

### 1.5.2 Division into Sub-Contracts (Inddeling i delkontrakter)
When there are many locations, the contracting authority can consider bundling them into sub-agreements (delaftaler), with advantages:
- Attractive locations' advantages can offset less-attractive locations' disadvantages, so the combined package is more attractive, achieving a better overall price
- Can ensure broad geographic distribution among multiple operators, so a single operator doesn't dominate e.g. a city center, achieving better competition among operators

Conversely, operators generally prefer to bid on locations they find attractive individually, rather than being forced to take some locations as part of a package.

## Section 2: The Set of Tender Templates

Because of Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen's requirements, municipalities will largely tender locations for **normal chargers**, reflected throughout the templates. Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen requires non-residential properties with **more than 20 parking spaces** to install at least one charging outlet by **1 January 2025** (SMEs excepted). This means municipalities must install chargers at buildings like schools, daycare institutions, municipal halls, etc. — many of which won't be commercially attractive to operators, potentially requiring public co-financing. These locations also typically have longer parking durations, so lower charging power (kW) suffices, making normal chargers relevant (cheaper to install than higher-power charging points).

### 2.1 Bestemmelser om udbud og tilbud (BUT)
Describes the tender's terms — purpose, timeline, minimum requirements, award criteria, and any weighting/prioritization. Purpose: to equip bidders to understand how the tender is designed and create transparency in how criteria are weighted, and thus how tender winners are determined. Must be tailored to the chosen tender process. If a restricted-tender/prequalification procedure is used, selection criteria must be stated in the announcement (and can be repeated in the BUT); the contracting authority can draw inspiration from EU-tender selection criteria and minimum suitability requirements in the Udbudsloven, and may also require applicants not be subject to the exclusion grounds in the Udbudsloven. The BUT paradigm also includes an example description of a negotiation process, if relevant.

### 2.2 Kravspecifikation
Lists a set of minimum requirements the bidder must be able to meet (without reservation) to bid. If requirements are set too strictly relative to where the market is, the contracting authority risks a limited bidder pool, reducing competition — against the contracting authority's interest. Conversely, the contracting authority has an interest in setting some minimum requirements to ensure good product/service quality — a balancing act between market reality and the contracting authority's wishes. The market is expected to continuously adapt to contracting authorities' wishes, so the Kravspecifikation should be adapted based on what the market can deliver at tender time; **the template's Kravspecifikation is calibrated to market knowledge as of autumn 2022.**

### 2.3 Tilbudsliste
An Excel file where the bidder states either: (1) what the bidder is willing to **pay** to gain the right to operate charging points at a given location, or (2) what **public co-financing** the bidder requires to operate charging points at a given location. The contracting authority must decide whether locations are tendered individually or in sub-agreements (per section 1.5.2), reflected in the Tilbudsliste design. The Tilbudsliste must include per-location price fields even when working with sub-agreements or a single combined price, since a municipal co-financing share **per location** is required to apply for the state pool for co-financing charging points on municipal land.

### 2.4 Operatørens serviceniveau
The bidder gives a qualitative description of the offered service level across two sub-criteria: **Functionality, payment and data**; and **Workflows and processes**. The response section is structured as a table with pre-defined assessment criteria. Descriptions are qualitatively assessed by the contracting authority. These are **competition parameters** where quality is assessed, but it is **not a requirement** that the operator fill in text/descriptions — a blank response doesn't disqualify the bid or make it non-conforming; it simply doesn't score positively. The greater the credible quality demonstrated, the more positive weight given. The bidder's completed appendix is attached to the contract with the winning operator. The contracting authority can choose to weight criteria differently if some competition parameters are judged more important than others.

### 2.5 Udkast til kontrakt
Included so the operator knows what contract the contracting authority expects to sign with winning operators, setting out the terms the bidder can expect at contract signing. The contract is just an example, and the contracting authority must adapt it to the specific tender. **Important limitation:** the contract **cannot subsequently be materially amended**, since its content was a fundamental condition of the tender — major changes, or changes judged material to bidders' general interest in the tender, cannot occur afterward without re-running the tender.

## Section 3: Execution (Udførelse)

The operator's usual focus is on establishing the charging point itself and associated supply cables. It's therefore recommended the contracting authority carefully considers, during tender planning, where the interface should lie between what the contracting authority itself establishes/provides and what the supplier must deliver.

### 3.1 Division of Services Between Contracting Authority and Operator
The Kravspecifikation template's default proposal: the operator undertakes all establishment work for the charging stations, potentially against public co-financing.

**Alternative model** described in the Kravspecifikation, where the contracting authority instead handles:
- Ensuring necessary capacity at the connection point, including the supply unit
- Groundworks (excavation)
- Ducting (tomrør) for supply cables to the charging stations
- Physical layout of the location where charging stations will be established, including surfacing
- Any covering/shelter of the charging space

Under this alternative model, the contracting authority becomes owner of this infrastructure and bears a large share of the initial cost — often a barrier for many operators, since it requires a large upfront investment.

### 3.2 Supplementary Services from the Operator
If the contracting authority wants the operator to deliver more services than described above, this requires an additional description in the Kravspecifikation beyond what the template provides.
