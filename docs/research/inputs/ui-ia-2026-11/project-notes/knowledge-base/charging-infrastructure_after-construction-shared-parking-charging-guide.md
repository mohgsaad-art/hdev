# After Construction: Charging Infrastructure on Shared Parking Areas (Guide for Housing Organizations and Property Boards)

**Source file:** `Efter_byggeriet_Ladeinfrastruktur_på_delte_parkeringspladser.pdf`
**Category:** Charging infrastructure — guide (retrofit/existing-building, for housing organizations & property boards)
**Original language:** Danish (translated/summarized to English below; proper nouns kept in Danish)
**One-line summary:** The third guide in Region Hovedstaden/Copenhagen Electric's three-part series ("Efter byggeriet" = "After construction"), aimed at housing organizations and property boards ("boligorganisationer og ejendomsbestyrelser"), covering the decision-making/stakeholder process, legislation, sizing, charger technology, ownership models, payment structures, **subsidy programs**, and installation requirements for retrofitting EV charging infrastructure onto existing shared parking areas.

## Introduction
- Cites data from **De Danske Bilimportører** (the Danish Car Importers' Association) showing EV sales accelerating strongly in **2023**; if the trend continues, Denmark is on track to reach (possibly exceed) the goal of **1 million EVs by 2030**. Source citation: bilimp.dk/nyregistreringer/
- Notes that, unlike home charging on private property (where the EV owner alone decides and bears financial responsibility for their own charger), establishing chargers on shared parking areas involves more planning complexity — but with informed decision-making, nothing prevents a successful rollout.
- Offering EV charging as an amenity at existing buildings can be a **strategic decision** for housing organizations/property boards: benefits residents, supports the green transition, and can be a **competitive parameter** for attracting/retaining residents.
- Same benefits list as the other two guides (time/cost savings, lower kWh prices at normal chargers, gentler battery charging, reduced grid peak, reduced need for public charging infrastructure) — reproduced identically.
- Guide purpose: help housing organizations and property boards plan, establish, and operate charging infrastructure at their **existing** properties with shared parking.

## Legislation on Charger Installation
- States explicitly: **as of autumn 2023, there is no legislation requiring the installation of chargers on shared parking at existing housing.** Nonetheless, organizations/boards should be aware of the two most relevant pieces of legislation:
  - **Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen** — sets requirements for preparation and/or installation of chargers
  - **AFI-loven** — sets requirements especially for the chargers themselves if publicly accessible

### Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen
- Same core content as the other two guides: in force since **10 March 2020**; implements parts of the EU Energy Performance of Buildings Directive; issued under Byggeloven; building-owner categories and SME exemption identical (SME definition: <250 employees, <€50M annual turnover or <€43M annual balance sheet, per the Executive Order's own guidance dated 1 January 2021); Figure 1/Rambøll table referenced (again as an embedded image, not extractable as text); "larger renovation" 4-criteria trigger test identical (>10 parking spaces, construction work occurring, parking/electrical infrastructure renovated, costs <7% of total renovation cost).
- **New/distinct point emphasized in this guide (not stated this explicitly in the other two):** the Executive Order places **no requirement on existing buildings classified as residential ("beboelse")**. Whether a building is classified as residential or non-residential appears in the building's **BBR-meddelelse** (Bygnings- og Boligregistret — the Danish Building and Housing Register record). This means **most housing organizations and property boards are not legally required to install chargers for residents**, since their buildings are used for residential purposes and are thus exempt (for existing buildings).
- Despite this exemption, the Executive Order is described as a major driver of Danish charging-infrastructure buildout (especially normal chargers), and organizations are advised to stay updated on its content and any changes.
- **Same "expected revision" discussion** as the other guides (autumn 2023 EPBD recast proposal), including the same **"right to plug"** provision described (residents on shared parking would gain the right to have a charger installed on request, making the housing organization/property board responsible for meeting that request if enacted).

### AFI-loven
- Same core content and the same **Figure 2** list of five underlying executive orders as in "Under byggeriet" (BEK 1073/2022, BEK 414/2022, BEK 1010/2022, BEK 688/2023, BEK 2635/2021) — see that guide's knowledge file for the full list with titles.
- Framed here as relevant for housing organizations/property boards to know, **especially if their chargers become publicly accessible**.
- Same AFIR-directive/compatibility-standard note: newer European EVs can use Type 2 (low-power) and CCS Combo (high-power) connectors.

## Vejen til beslutning (The Path to a Decision) — Unique Section Not in the Other Two Guides
This section, specific to the "after construction" retrofit context, gives **five numbered recommendations** for how existing housing organizations should approach the charging-infrastructure decision process:

1. **Indsaml data om parkeringsforhold og beboernes køretøjer** (Collect data on parking conditions and residents' vehicles) — Before sizing the charger need, determine available parking area and number of cars using the spaces. Some residents may already own or plan to acquire an EV soon. Usage patterns matter too — some organizations (typically in urban/city-adjacent areas) may have high parking occupancy without much actual charging need, e.g., if residents have limited daily transport needs but keep a car for leisure/holiday use. A solid mapping exercise helps determine charger need and optimal placement.

2. **Inkluder alle relevante interessenter** (Include all relevant stakeholders) — Board, property administration, and residents should all be part of the decision process, since a permanent installation like chargers creates change and financial consequences affecting everyone with an interest in the area. Recommends forming a **working group** with representatives from different stakeholder groups to ensure progress and that all viewpoints/concerns are incorporated during the investigation phase.

3. **Foreslå en komplet plan for opstilling af ladestandere** (Propose a complete plan for charger installation) — A plan covering number of chargers, placement, cost analyses, and timeframe clarifies project scope and requirements. Ensures efficient, holistic rollout, but may require resources depending on property size/ambition — for larger/more complex properties, engaging a relevant consultant/advisor for the planning work may be worthwhile.

4. **Informer og indgå i dialog om ladestanderne og elbiler** (Inform and engage in dialogue about chargers and EVs) — Plan information meetings or workshops for residents and board members on charger benefits and available options. Cable/network establishment and future-charger preparation should be framed as a **shared investment** that increases the housing organization's property value. Communicating pros/cons and solution considerations helps inform decision-makers and reduce concerns — **"Uvidenhed er ofte kilde til størst modstand."** (Lack of knowledge is often the biggest source of resistance.)

5. **Indgå i netværk med andre boligorganisationer** (Join networks with other housing organizations) — via e.g. **AlmenNet** or **BL** (Boligselskabernes Landsforening / Danish Federation of Social Housing Organisations), to build shared understanding of charging-infrastructure rollout; share knowledge about good/bad solutions, drawing inspiration from **AlmenNet's toolbox**; present the knowledge-sharing to residents/board members.

## Number and Placement of Charge Points
### Calculating the Number of Charge Points
- Same core statement as the other guides: no definitive formula exists (uncertainty about future technology, car ownership, residents) — only estimates/rules of thumb.
- **Identical calculation method to the other two guides:** formula A × B/(C/D), with A=100 (example car count), B=31% EV share by 2030 (1M EVs / 3.26M fleet), C=350 km average EV range, D=45.5 km/day average driving distance → suggests ~4 outlets/2 double-outlet stands for 100 parking spaces, with the same assumptions (one EV overnight per outlet, staggered weekly demand) and the same recommendation to slightly overdimension, especially the underlying installation (conduit, cables), and to monitor for significant changes in the input assumptions.
- Same plug-in hybrid note: disproportionate charger demand due to limited charging power (typically 3.7 kW AC) and smaller battery/range (~50 km electric), requiring near-daily charging.

### Placement of Chargers
- Same core content as "Under byggeriet": recommend ladeøer/-grupper (charging island/clusters); cheapest to install together near the connection point; wall-mounted charge boxes on the building facade often minimize cost (though usually single-outlet, requiring more boxes); a single charge outlet can effectively serve ~3 EVs if staggered across the day if free parking spaces are available for fully-charged cars to move to; **Figure 3 (credited to Rambøll)** shows the same two-outlet-charger-at-a-four-bay-corner concept serving up to 4 cars over 24 hours without moving cars.
- If every unit has a dedicated space, guest parking/other shared areas can serve collective charging; equipping every dedicated space individually is possible but significantly raises total cost.
- Placement must respect local plans and road law; private fællesvej changes need road-authority/police approval; rapid-charging stations require considering noise/traffic and regulatory approval.

**"Parkeringsrestriktioner" (Parking Restrictions) — subsection:**
- To maximize charging-infrastructure utilization and possibly limit charging duration, consider parking restrictions, administered by the developer or later property board/resident association — restrictions must align with road-authority/police approval before signage is installed.

**"Sikring mod uautoriseret brug" (Protection Against Unauthorized Use) — subsection:**
- Shared parking often raises concern about outsiders using chargers, preventing optimal resident use. Solvable via clear signage or locking chargers to outsiders. Consider dedicated guest parking spaces with chargers for residents' visitors — weighed against the economic considerations of offering publicly accessible chargers (possibly only part of the day) — cross-references the Payment Structures section.

## Charger Choice and Economic Considerations
### Types of Chargers and Boxes
- Content essentially identical to "Under byggeriet": first decision is normal (AC) vs. high-power (DC) vs. combination, depending on charging-speed needs; normal chargers are the right solution for most residential buildings and cheapest to install.

**Normallader details** — identical to "Under byggeriet": AC, up to 22 kW, primarily European 3-phase Type 2 connector; most solutions = free-standing 2-outlet stand, 1-2 outlet charge box, or two boxes on a shared post (**Figure 4, credited to DTU Wind**); free-standing/post-mounted via empty conduit, wall-mounted via cable tray; **same recommendation of 22 kW (32A/3-phase) per outlet** with the same rationale (not all EVs support 22kW — many 3-phase EVs capped at 11kW, single-phase at 3.7/7kW per ev-database.org — but cost difference between 11kW/22kW is modest, and full utilization requires load balancing / smart chargers); same internal load-balancing/power-sharing note for 2-outlet chargers.
- **Same sidebar "Smartladning - Intelligente ladestandere"** with identical content on smart-charger capabilities (power adjustment/load balancing, time-shifting to cheap/green power, grid/building capacity communication enabling an operator/EMSP to adjust kW dynamically).

**Højeffektslader details** — identical to "Under byggeriet": 50kW DC charger, **up to 450,000-600,000 DKK install cost (2022 prices)**, up to **125 amps** capacity needed; appropriate in special cases (shared car-share vehicle, frequent visitor traffic, or rule-of-thumb >20 normal chargers on site); **50kW charger delivers up to 250 km range per hour of charging**; recommend **1-hour** time-limited parking if made publicly accessible, to maximize utilization and improve overall establishment/operation economics.

### Eje eller leje ladestanderne? (Own or Lease the Chargers?) — Adapted for Existing-Building Context
- Same framing as "Under byggeriet" on public charging as a backup for long trips (operator app payment; credit card for ≥50kW chargers installed post-AFI-loven).
- **This guide frames the decision as the housing organization's/property board's own choice** (rather than the developer's, as in "Under byggeriet"), since the building already exists.

**Owning the chargers:**
- Organization/board can handle all establishment and operations itself, or contract a charging operator/EMSP for some/all operations. Same points on service agreements enabling tax refund, operator/EMSP handling back-end/administration/billing.
- Same cost trade-off: higher establishment cost (chargers, excavation, ampere) and resourcing needs if self-managed, vs. typically lower resulting per-kWh price for residents; same tax-exemption/refund exclusivity note requiring a dedicated settlement meter with own "aftagenummer" if the building has electric heat or other tax-exempt power/solar.
- Same footnote: **2 September 2020** amendment to the "sideaktivitetsbekendtgørelsen" enabling housing organizations to establish/operate EV chargers.

**Leasing the chargers:**
- Same framing as "Under byggeriet": operator typically handles establishment and ongoing operations, often free/low-cost to the organization but recouped via higher resident kWh price (bounded by market alternatives); organization can alternatively self-fund excavation/ampere for more flexibility and lower resident prices; same single-vs-multiple-operator trade-off discussion (competition vs. inability to load-balance across operators).
- (Note: this guide does **not** include the "choosing before/after building commissioning" subsection found in "Under byggeriet," since the building already exists and commissioning has already occurred — logically omitted, not a gap.)

### Betalingsstrukturer (Payment Structures) — Adapted for Existing-Building/Housing-Fee Context
**When chargers are owned and operated by the housing organization:**
- Establishment costs/charger purchase can be taken from the **shared/common budget, collected via rent/common charges ("huslejen/fællesudgifterne")** — a solidarity-financed model benefiting a growing future user base, though imposing cost on all units regardless of near-term benefit. Alternative: keep charging-infrastructure economics separate, charging **more per kWh for ongoing repayment of the initial investment** — meaning early EV adopters bear more of the shared-infrastructure financing cost. A balanced middle-ground between the two models is also possible.

**When chargers are owned by the organization and operated by a charging operator:**
- Before the agreement is made, a payment structure both parties can accept must be chosen — e.g., a percentage of the kWh price to the operator for running operations, rest to the organization; kWh pricing can follow the spot price or an average spot price over a preceding period (e.g., a month). Alternative: fixed monthly price per charger to the operator regardless of usage.
- **New model mentioned here, not in "Under byggeriet":** the **"andelsforenings-model"** (cooperative-association model) — a model where a cooperative entity manages the charging solution for, e.g., the housing organization for a fee, and all surplus returns to the members (incl. the housing organization).
- Same payback-timeline discussion (investment can pay back within a few years depending on establishment cost and resident/guest kWh pricing; publicly accessible chargers at certain times can accelerate payback via higher guest pricing).
- Same discussion of operator business-model variety, hardware-ownership reluctance limiting competition, and the need to find a new operator (or buy newer chargers) at contract renewal.

**When the charger is leased/owned by the operator:**
- Same content as "Under byggeriet": operator funding excavation/installation/ampere needs to recoup via higher resident kWh price; same caution about typically **~10-year** contract terms; same points on lower operator risk (and thus potentially lower kWh prices) when excavation/conduit/ampere are handled separately by the organization, easier operator-switching when the organization (not the operator) owns the buried infrastructure, and the importance of planning for what happens to operator-funded buried infrastructure post-contract (including possible resale to a successor operator).
- Same discussion of tender/multi-quote price competition, possible fixed markup per kWh above spot price, residents typically preferring chargers closed to non-residents (via charge cards/app), and the potential upside of allowing limited public access to boost operator revenue and enable lower resident prices — dependent on the organization's location and nearby workplaces/other shared-parking housing.
- Same closing point: few operators currently offer all-inclusive subscription solutions; not the most economical choice for most typical-driving-pattern EV owners, though some drivers value simplicity or benefit from a fixed price regardless of charging location.

### Tilskud (Subsidies) — Unique Section, Not Present in the Other Two Guides
- Housing organizations should stay informed about subsidy schemes to reduce charger installation costs.
- **In 2021, the Social-, Bolig- og Ældreministeriet (Ministry of Social Affairs, Housing and Senior Citizens) made a pool of 33.6 million DKK available** for installing chargers in shared parking facilities belonging to housing associations etc. Citation: retsinformation.dk/api/pdf/225722
- **As of autumn 2023, there is no subsidy pool for charger installation at housing organizations**, but the guide recommends monitoring statens-tilskudspuljer.dk (the Danish government's subsidy-pool portal) for updates.

## Installation of Charging Infrastructure
- Content essentially identical to "Under byggeriet": all charge-box electrical work is subject to authorization; recommend an experienced installer since EV-charging dimensioning differs from standard dimensioning; first step is assessing the building's electrical capacity, determining how much extra power/amps can be drawn without overload.
- Same guidance: avoid buying excess amps; determine total power demand based on desired kW and number of stands; consider a load management system for load balancing, time scheduling, and power control; check charger technical specs/manufacturer guidance for future-proofing and intelligence.
- Same electrical requirements: own electrical panel and supply from the grid company's designated connection point/transformer; minimum sub-meter to separate charging consumption; each outlet protected by its own RCD with **max. 30 mA trip current**; **Type B RCD** required unless the charger has built-in DC-fault protection; **earth transition resistance under 50 ohm**; **transient overvoltage protection** required if publicly accessible.
- **Same "Opladning på 3 faser" sidebar** on 3-phase installation requirement and phase rotation recommendation for single-phase-limited EVs (e.g., plug-in hybrids at 3.7/7kW).
- **Added recommendation unique to this guide:** "Det anbefales at gå i dialog med elinstallatøren eller evt. ladeoperatøren, hvis boligorganisationen entrerer med en sådan for at sikre, at der er forberedt til flere ladestandere i fremtiden." (Recommend dialogue with the electrical installer or charging operator, if the organization contracts with one, to ensure preparation for more chargers in the future.)

### Kabeltrækning (Cable Routing)
- Same core content as "Under byggeriet" on lower installation-phase cost vs. later retrofit cost, oversized conduit/routing paths, cable wells for inspection/future pulling — **but this guide's version does not repeat the specific numeric rules of thumb (110mm conduit per 4×11kW outlets, 15° bend/500mm, 50mm tray width per 4 chargers) that appeared in "Under byggeriet."** Instead, it stays at the general-recommendation level and refers to dialogue with the installer/operator (see above). **Flagged: the specific tomrør/nedgravning numeric sub-sections present in "Under byggeriet" (Tomrør, Nedgravning headings with BEK 1082 §41 depth rules) are not present as separate content in this "Efter byggeriet" document's extracted text — this appears to be a genuine content difference between the two guides, not a missed extraction, since the "Kabeltrækning" section here is shorter and lacks those subheadings.**

### Tilslutning af ladestandere til elnettet (Grid Connection)
- Identical content to "Under byggeriet": grid companies' connection obligation within their supply area upon fee payment; connection = purchasing delivery scope (ampere) from the grid company; **~1,000-1,500 DKK per ampere incl. VAT (2023 price)**; registration must precede installation work, following the grid company's procedures, stating expected power demand/consumption pattern; grid company responds on connection options; actual offer/timeline only after a formal connection request; **new transformer station lead time up to 3 years** if needed (especially relevant for multiple fast/rapid chargers).
- Same **"Behov for ampere" sidebar**: 11kW charger on 3 phases needs 16A; 22kW charger on 3 phases needs 32A; extra amps purchased from the local grid company if no spare capacity; recommend planning for future expansion (conduit etc.) from the start for cost-effectiveness.

### Anlæg/etablering (Construction/Establishment)
- Identical content to "Under byggeriet": establishment scope depends on building-mounted vs. free-standing chargers; excavation requires LER.dk (Ledningsejerregistret) checks to prevent damage to existing lines even in ostensibly new areas; general caution needed since line-position accuracy carries uncertainty; applicable environmental disposal requirements for excavated material must be followed; new lines must be reported to LER.dk after work; charger installation requires grid-company registration even with existing connection/capacity, per **Fællesregulativet 2022 point 2.1**.

## Considerations on Electric Car-Sharing (Delebiler på El) — Expanded Section with New Subsidy Content
- Opens with a framing unique to this guide: in housing areas already considering charger installation, it may also be sensible to investigate including electric car-sharing as an integrated part of the mobility solution.
- Same core benefits list as the other guides: car-sharing lets some residents forgo car ownership, reducing tenant vehicle numbers and parking pressure; economic/health benefits for individuals; reduced urban parking pressure/congestion; local/global environmental benefits; can strengthen the housing organization's green profile and attractiveness.

### Implementering af el-delebil (Implementing an Electric Car-Share Vehicle) — Unique Subsection
- Various options exist: some companies deliver an electric car-share vehicle to the housing organization's parking lot each morning and collect it for charging at day's end; organizations can contract specialized car-sharing companies, or invest in their own electric car-share vehicle.
- Organizations should assess costs of acquiring/maintaining electric car-share vehicles: purchase or leasing, insurance, regular service/maintenance, charger installation. A suitable financing model must be established to ensure service profitability. Options include:
  - **User-based payment** — e.g., via an app where the user registers each trip/usage and is billed afterward
  - **Subscription-based payment** — user billed a fixed monthly sum for unlimited use when the car is free; can be billed through housing rent for simplicity as part of the resident's fixed costs
- Important to clarify liability allocation in case of accidents/damage.
- Success requires informing/engaging residents about the option, its benefits, how it works, and how to use it effectively. Organizations must be prepared to scale the service up or down per demand — potentially requiring flexible contracts with suppliers/partners. Ongoing service evaluation is important, using resident feedback and quantitative metrics (number of users, number of trips) as useful inputs.

### National Subsidy Pool for Electric Car-Sharing (Unique Numeric Content)
- **In 2023 and 2024, a state subsidy pool for promoting electric car-sharing totaling 103.544 million DKK was allocated.**
- **51.772 million DKK allocated in each of the two years**, with separate application rounds.
- Applications submitted via **virk.dk** by companies, associations, or partnerships between them.
- **2023 application round closed 6 October 2023.**
- **2024 application round open 3 June – 30 September 2024.**
- Citation: **Bekendtgørelse om pulje til fremme af el-delebiler**, retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2023/821

**Criteria for subsidy allocation (per source, § 8 and § 9 of the cited Executive Order — quoted/paraphrased structure):**
- **§ 8, primary weighting criteria** — projects promoting one or more of:
  1. The link between electric car-sharing and public/collective transport
  2. Use of electric car-sharing in new construction and dense residential development
  3. Use of electric car-sharing in sparsely populated areas
- **§ 8 Stk. 2, secondary weighting criteria:**
  1. Geographic coverage relative to existing electric car-share schemes
  2. Need for electric car-share schemes that would not otherwise be covered on market terms
  3. Project maturity
  4. Number of potential users benefiting from the project
- **§ 9:** Subsidy can cover **up to 75%** of approved subsidy-eligible costs.
  - **§ 9 Stk. 2:** the subsidy recipient's own co-financing cannot be covered by other state subsidy pools or EU subsidies.

## Relevance Notes for HDEV/Depot Tool (Claude's own framing, not from source)
- Like its companion guides, this document is about light-duty/passenger residential EV charging, but two elements are distinctively useful beyond that context: (1) the **five-step stakeholder decision process** (data collection → stakeholder inclusion → complete plan → information/dialogue → peer network) is a transferable organizational-change methodology pattern potentially adaptable to fleet/depot electrification decision processes at housing or business organizations; (2) the **Danish public subsidy program structure** (percentage-of-cost caps, primary/secondary weighting criteria, application windows via virk.dk) is a concrete example of how Danish subsidy schemes for alternative mobility infrastructure are structured, useful as a reference pattern when researching whether comparable depot-charging or HD-EV subsidy schemes exist or follow similar structures.
- Cross-reference: this guide's "andelsforenings-model" (cooperative-association charging-operator model) is a specific commercial/ownership structure variant not seen in the other charging-infrastructure contract documents in this knowledge base, worth noting for the range of CPO business models encountered in the Danish market.
