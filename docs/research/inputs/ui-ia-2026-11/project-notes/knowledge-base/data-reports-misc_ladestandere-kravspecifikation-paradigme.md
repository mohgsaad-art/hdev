## Section 1: Background and Framework
Template text explains that with the AFI law's entry into force, municipalities can tender the right to operate as a charging operator on municipal land, on market terms. Template placeholders indicate the municipality should state: its adopted "Strategi for ladeinfrastruktur," the number of charging outlets and locations it is obligated to install under the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen before 1 January 2025, and that at most of these locations it expects **normal chargers (≤22 kW AC)** to be most appropriate, while a further set of "øvrige kommunale arealer" (other municipal areas) may be suited to normal, fast, and/or rapid chargers. Guidance notes suggest separating legally-mandated locations (per the Ladestanderbekendtgørelsen) from other/optional locations, since bidding requirements or co-financing willingness may differ between the two location sets.

## Section 2: Division of Responsibilities Between Contracting Authority and Operator

### Operator's (Leverandør/Operatør's) Responsibilities
- All formalities related to establishing and connecting to the public electricity grid
- Order letter ("bestillerbrev") to the grid company and connection fee ("tilslutningsbidrag") payment to the grid company
- Supply cables from the connection point to the charging points
- Establishment of a ground-mounted meter cabinet ("terrænmålerskab") if the billing meter cannot be placed in the charging stations themselves
- Groundworks — both excavation and reinstatement work
- Foundations for charging points
- Installation, operation, and maintenance of the charging stations, including ongoing software updates to keep them fully functional throughout the contract period
- Preparation of a signage plan for road authority approval and installation of signage

### Municipality's (Kommunens) Responsibilities (in the template's default allocation)
- Establishment of any transformer, distribution panel, ducting ("tomrør"), and foundation for charging points
- Ensuring the grid company establishes power to the charging stations, and payment of connection fees
- Establishing the charging spaces on the site plus foundations on which charging stations/rapid charging stations are built
- Ensuring access roads and paved/hardened surface area at the location where charging stations will be established

**Guidance note:** the template flags that it must be decided, as part of the tender strategy, whether the municipality itself bears the cost of and carries out groundworks and grid connection fees, or whether this work and its costs are shifted to the operator in exchange for extra payment — if the alternative model is chosen, all tender documents must be reviewed and rewritten accordingly.

## Section 3: Requirements for the Supplier's Services

### 3.1 General Requirements
- Minimum specified number of charging outlets per location must be installed (per Bilag 1).
- Operator must mount charging stations on a suitable/prepared foundation.
- Operator must connect charging points to the grid company's designated connection point.
- Where multiple charging points are installed at one location, they must support **load sharing** (load balancing) when multiple vehicles are connected simultaneously, ensuring all outlets can deliver the same charging power under simultaneous use — **CHAdeMO outlets are explicitly exempted from this requirement**.
- Operator must handle all formalities for establishing/connecting charging points to the public grid via the established routing path ("føringsvej").
- Operator must participate in detailed planning of charging point installation in dialogue with the municipality and any local building-responsible parties, and help coordinate interfaces between the parts of the project each party is responsible for.

### 3.2.1 Requirements for Normal Chargers (Normalladere)
1. Freestanding charging stations with **1 or 2** charging outlets to be delivered (template variable).
2. Each outlet must deliver a minimum of **11 kW** charging power.
3. Outlets must be **Type 2**.
4. Maximum dimensions: **500 / 500 / 1200 mm** (width/depth/height).
5. Must be **IK10**-rated (impact/vandalism resistance).
6. Must have a minimum **IP54** ingress-protection rating.
7. Must be rated for operation in ambient temperatures between **-20°C and +50°C**.
8. Must comply with all applicable rules/regulations for Type 2 charging.
9. Must be equipped with surge protection (transient protection) able to withstand lightning-induced overvoltage, etc.
10. AC input voltage must be **3x400V (+N) ±4%**.
11. Must be able to communicate via internet over **4G/5G or Ethernet** (Ethernet Cat 6A cable), and must be remotely monitorable and controllable by the operator.
Guidance notes: a wall-mounted charge box or freestanding charging station can be supplied; in many locations a business case may not support charging power above 22 kW (2×11 kW), which is why 22 kW is the default normal-charger baseline in the template.

### 3.2.2 Requirements for Fast Chargers (Hurtigladere)
1. Each outlet must deliver a minimum of **50 kW** charging power.
2. Outlets must be **CCS (Combo 2)**; if the operator also offers CHAdeMO charging, it must be immediately visible which charger(s) offer this.
3. Cable length minimum **4 meters** for CCS outlets, **3.5 meters** for any CHAdeMO outlets; cables must not be able to lie on the ground before, during, or after charging.
4. Charging cables must be rated to handle the current corresponding to the charging power (see point 5 below).
5. Fast chargers must be able to charge at a minimum of **900V**.
6. Must be equipped with a digital info screen with (or a link to) user instructions and current electricity sale price, at minimum in **Danish and English**; screen minimum **7 inches**, legible regardless of ambient lighting conditions.
7. Maximum dimensions: **1100 / 2000 / 2500 mm** (width/depth/height).
8. **IK10**-rated.
9. Minimum **IP54** rating.
10. Rated for **-20°C to +50°C** ambient operation.
11. Must comply with all applicable CCS (and any CHAdeMO) charging regulations.
12. Equipped with surge protection.
13. Efficiency per fast charger must be **0.94 or higher**.
14. Power factor (cosphi) must be **0.94 or higher**.
15. AC input voltage: **3x400V (+N) ±4%**.
16. Frequency input: **50Hz ±1Hz**.
17. THDi (harmonic distortion): **5% or less**.
18. Must communicate via internet (4G/5G or Ethernet Cat 6A) and be remotely monitorable/controllable by the operator.

### 3.2.3 Requirements for Rapid Chargers (Lynladere)
1. Each outlet must deliver a minimum of **300 kW** charging power; CCS outlets must have minimum **150 kW**; CHAdeMO outlets (if any) minimum **75 kW** (note: these two sub-figures appear to describe minimum floors that are lower than the general 300 kW headline figure — reproduced exactly as stated in source).
2. Outlets must be **CCS (Combo 2)**; visible indication if CHAdeMO is also offered.
3. Cable length minimum **4 meters** (CCS) / **3.5 meters** (CHAdeMO); cables must not lie on the ground before/during/after charging.
4. Charging cables must be rated for **400A**.
5. Minimum charging voltage: **900V**.
6. Digital info screen (Danish/English minimum, 7-inch minimum, legible in all lighting), as for fast chargers.
7. Maximum dimensions: **1100 / 2000 / 2500 mm** (width/depth/height) — same envelope as fast chargers.
8. **IK10**-rated.
9. Minimum **IP54**.
10. Rated for **-20°C to +50°C**.
11. Compliance with all applicable CCS (and CHAdeMO) regulations.
12. Surge protection equipped.
13. Efficiency **0.94 or higher**.
14. Power factor (cosphi) **0.94 or higher**.
15. AC input voltage **3x400V (+N) ±4%**.
16. Frequency input **50Hz ±1Hz**.
17. THDi **5% or less**.
18. Internet communication (4G/5G or Ethernet Cat 6A) and remote monitoring/control by operator.

**Guidance rationale notes accompanying the charger requirement sections:**
- IK10 rating ensures chargers are robust/vandal-resistant.
- IP54 rating ensures relative dust- and water-tightness.
- The AC input voltage requirement ensures the voltage level at the charging point stays within an acceptable range.
- The ≥0.94 efficiency requirement for fast/rapid chargers is set to ensure as efficient power transfer as possible between charger and vehicle.
- The power factor (≥0.94), frequency input (50Hz ±1Hz), and THDi (≤5%) requirements together aim to ensure good integration of the chargers into the electricity grid.

### 3.3 Charging Spaces and Access
Operator must ensure, with reference to **EN 301 549** (Accessibility requirements for ICT products and services, section 8.3 on reach and range), that the physical design of charging points and payment systems is adapted so that people with reduced functional capacity, including wheelchair users, can independently use the charging stations.

### 3.4 Payment and Payment Systems
1. Charging stations must be equipped with a payment module so payment for electricity can be settled directly between the operator and the end user, via credit card, SMS/smartphone app, and/or a charging tag ("ladebrik") or similar.
2. The payment function must be equipped with easily understood instructions, at minimum in Danish and English, or pictograms, ensuring simple and fast operation for end users.
3. The operator is responsible for establishing and administering payment services for charging use and ensuring correct invoicing of end users.
4. The operator must ensure the end user's own charging data is accessible (e.g., via app, website, or the charging point's display) both during and after charging, including ongoing charging data (State of Charge, Ampere, Volt, Watt) and final consumption.
- Cites **BEK nr. 1010 af 27/06/22** as the source of rules governing payment methods and user information requirements.

### 3.5 Service Requirements
1. Operator commits to operating and maintaining charging stations professionally so installed equipment appears fully functional, well-kept, and clean.
2. Operator commits to installing any information signage and instructions (e.g., via QR code) to ensure correct use of charging stations, at the operator's own expense.
3. Operator must maintain a fault-reporting system (e.g., web- or mobile-based) enabling fault reporting 24 hours a day, allowing users to (i) report a faulty charging station, (ii) report other problems with the charging point, and (iii) leave a comment.
4. Operator must have an online call center/service center available at minimum **8:00–16:00 on weekdays**, staffed in **Danish and English**, where end users can report faults and get personal assistance with charging point operation and payment handling.
5. Operator must ensure charging point availability (uptime) of at least **98%**, measured monthly. Periods of software updates are excluded from the uptime calculation. Operator must perform ongoing operation and maintenance per manufacturer instructions, but must at minimum perform **annual service inspections** of all charging infrastructure including charging points, cables, technical cabinets, etc.

### 3.6 Data Exchange
Operator must, within a reasonable deadline (set with regard to the scope of the data delivery, and at maximum within **30 days**), upon the municipality's request and at no cost to the municipality, give the municipality access to the following data:
- Total kWh delivered to end users per charging point (per day, per month, per year)
- Capacity utilization per charging point (hours in use per day/month/year)
- Duration per charging session, i.e. how long each vehicle is connected to the charging point
- Breakdown of payment methods used (per month)
- Average prices per kWh on a monthly basis, separately for ad-hoc customers and subscription customers, plus documentation that any discount exceeding **10%** is justified per the criteria stated in the Contract's section 6.3.2
- Average availability (uptime) on a monthly basis per charging point

## Section 4: Documentation of Services
Operator commits to delivering:
- Detailed data sheet for charging points
- User manual for all charging equipment
- Instructions for operation and maintenance
Operator must also submit required information to the **Ledningsejerregistret (LER)** (Danish register of underground utility/cable owners). Operator must take photos during installation showing all cable connections, and make these photos available to the municipality.
Guidance note: it may be useful to obtain data sheets and user manuals for the charging equipment for internal/external question-answering, and it may be worth developing an FAQ for the municipality's website.
