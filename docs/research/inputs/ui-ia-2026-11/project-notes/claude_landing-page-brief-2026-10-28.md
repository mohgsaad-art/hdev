# EV Scope landing page — expectations brief (28 Oct 2026)

Written from Bob's feedback on four rejected versions. This lists what the page must meet and what failed before. It does not prescribe a layout. The designer is expected to bring their own idea.

## What the page is for
Sell EV Scope to people who sign off on electric trucks and depots: fleet owners, truck makers (OEMs), municipalities, and charge operators / grid companies. A visitor should leave knowing what decision EV Scope helps them make, what they get, and why they can trust it.

## Expectations

**1. Brand, not a template**
- Same visual identity as the tool (workbench 10): white cards on a light grey sheet, one accent colour (parking-sign blue #1F4E9E), IBM Plex Sans / Mono, the EV parking-sign logo.
- Tasteful neumorphism where it means something: recessed fields, raised buttons that press in.
- No dark "space" hero, no rainbow or gradient text, no glows, no emoji icons, no generic SaaS/AI-startup look ("candy land", "AI slop").

**2. Soul and personality, without gimmicks**
- Earlier versions were "soulless, empty, blank" (too plain) and then "goofy" (too flashy). Both failed.
- Personality comes from the subject itself: trucks, depots, routes, the grid, real engineering detail. It does not come from decoration.
- Clear visual hierarchy: headline, then body text, then quiet grey notes used sparingly. Normal font weight is the default; not everything bold, not everything faded.

**3. Motion only with purpose**
- Movement happens in response to the visitor (scrolling into view, hover, click). Nothing loops on its own forever.
- Motion should explain something (e.g. a result appearing, a step being highlighted), never just decorate.

**4. Truthful proof, no fake advertising**
- Anything shown as "the tool" must be what the tool really shows: real screens or faithful reproductions of the actual overview/result cards, labelled as the sample case with illustrative numbers.
- No invented demos, features, numbers, customers, testimonials, logos or prices.
- Any claim must be supportable from the project docs (VECTO grounding, sources per input, Radius/Cerius grid products, tender and grant findings, the six document templates).

**5. Real selling points for each audience**
- Fleet owners, OEMs, municipalities and charge operators / grid each get their own visible pitch. Nothing hidden behind tabs where only one can be seen at a time.
- Each pitch: the decision they face, what EV Scope gives them, and which document they walk away with.
- Use the real questions and pain points from the research, not weak slogans. Rejected example: "sized for the hardest day" as a headline. Strong material includes:
  - grid lead times of 9 months to 6+ years (Radius process);
  - Radius's own pre-work list matching EV Scope's scope;
  - tenders scoring battery capacity, delivery and 8–10-year service;
  - the Vejdirektoratet grant form fields;
  - FNA vs BNA;
  - year-8 battery guarantees.

**6. Show the deliverables**
- The six document templates (feasibility report, DSO application, tender annex, financial model, grant data, client presentation) are a core selling point and must be presented convincingly.
- Badges (Fleet Ready, Grid Friendly, Cost Positive, Flex Ready, Verified Data) only once the rules are decided; until then, mark them as in draft or leave them out.

**7. Practical**
- Landing page only, one self-contained HTML file. The tool is not embedded and there is no full demo inside the page.
- Lightweight, offline except fonts.
- Works at 1440 px and on a laptop. Nothing overlaps or cuts off, and there is no sideways scroll.

## What failed before (do not repeat)
| Version | Bob's verdict |
|---|---|
| v1 | Looked just like the tool: empty, blank, hollow, still. |
| v2 | Embedded the whole tool and a slider "live demo". Wrong: the page should not be the tool. |
| v3 | Dark hero, gradient headline, endlessly looping animations, fake cards not matching the tool, weak copy, audience questions hidden in tabs. "Goofy", "AI made slop", "fake ads". |

## Done means
Bob looks at it and sees a credible, distinctive engineering company page in EV Scope's own style. He'd send it to a municipality or an OEM without embarrassment, and every claim on it is true.
